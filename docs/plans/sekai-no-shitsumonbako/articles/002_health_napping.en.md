# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about napping?｜Japan Questions #2

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/napping` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Around the world, some people call napping lazy while others say you should nap on purpose. Japan's official sleep guidance answers the question **by age group**. Here is what it says, and where the common beliefs go wrong.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the longer you sleep, the more you recover." The national guidance sums naps up in two words: "short" and "early."

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) **Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023** [健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023] (adopted February 2024) gives separate recommendations for adults, children and older people. **For older people, it describes it as desirable to "avoid long naps during the day and stay active."**

## Misunderstanding 1｜The longer, the better

Sleep moves into deeper stages as time passes. Waking from a deep stage can leave you feeling foggy for a while.

The MHLW's earlier guidance, the **Sleep Guidelines for Health Promotion 2014** [健康づくりのための睡眠指針2014], listed **"a short nap of 30 minutes or less in the early afternoon"** as a way of coping with daytime sleepiness.

> **[Verify]** The wording "30 minutes or less" and "early afternoon" is based on the 2014 Sleep Guidelines. **Check the original texts, including how the 2023 edition handles this point.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It makes no difference when you nap

Sleeping in the late afternoon or evening can affect how easily you fall asleep at night. **If you nap, do it early in the afternoon** — this is the approach set out in the guidance above.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A nap can make up for lost night-time sleep

A nap does not fill in sleep you missed at night. **For adults, the same guide says to make sure you get the sleep you need, "using six hours or more as a guide."** If you are chronically short of sleep at night, fixing your nights is the surer route.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Older people should nap more

The opposite. **For older people, avoiding long naps is described as desirable.** The concern is the balance with night-time sleep: time spent in bed should not become too long.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If you cannot fall asleep, lying down is a waste

A short rest, or time spent with your eyes closed, has value as rest in itself. **Do not regard not falling asleep as a problem in itself.**

## How VV sees it

VV wants to move away from thinking that links rest with slacking off. **Short, and early.** That alone makes napping easier to handle.
