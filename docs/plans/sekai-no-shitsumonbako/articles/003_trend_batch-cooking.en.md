# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about batch cooking?｜Japan Questions #3

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/batch-cooking` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Batch cooking — tsukurioki (作り置き), cooking several dishes ahead to keep in the fridge — is practised everywhere, but ideas about storage differ widely between countries and households. The Japanese government sets out food-poisoning prevention at home as **six situations with specific temperatures**. If you shop and cook in Japan, these are the numbers to know.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "once it is in the fridge, it is safe." Low temperatures do not stop food going off; they only slow it down.

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) pamphlet "Six points for preventing food poisoning at home" gives **10°C or below for the fridge and −15°C or below for the freezer** as a guide. Even so, it warns not to rely on the cold, because **"bacteria do not die."**

## Three principles, six situations

The three principles of food-poisoning prevention are **"don't contaminate, don't let it multiply, kill it off"** (つけない・増やさない・やっつける). The "six points" apply these to six situations at home.

| Situation | Key point |
|---|---|
| 1. Shopping | Buy fresh food last and go straight home |
| 2. Storing at home | Fridge **10°C or below**, freezer **−15°C or below**. Do not overfill |
| 3. Preparation | Wash your hands. Wash utensils used for raw meat or fish each time |
| 4. Cooking | Heat until the **centre reaches 75°C for at least 1 minute** |
| 5. Eating | Keep hot food hot and cold food cold |
| 6. Leftovers | Cool quickly, use clean utensils. **If too much time has passed, throw it away** |

Batch cooking is an activity that concentrates on **points 2, 4 and 6**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜In the fridge it keeps for a week

How long a dish keeps varies completely from dish to dish. The more moisture it holds, the more it was handled after cooking, and the more raw ingredients it contains, the shorter it lasts. **Do not think in terms of a single fixed number of days.** The government material itself says of leftovers: "if too much time has passed, be decisive and throw it away."

## Misunderstanding 2｜You can put food in the fridge while it is still hot

Hot food raises the temperature inside the fridge and **breaks the storage temperature (10°C or below) for everything else.** Divide it into shallow containers, cool it quickly and then put it in — the method given under "leftovers" in the six points.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Once it has cooled, you can touch it freely

Bacteria get in from the chopsticks or spoons you serve with. **Use clean utensils and take out only what you will eat.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Reheating lightly is enough

When reheating leftovers, too, the guidance is to **heat thoroughly, aiming for 75°C or above.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Smell and appearance always tell you

Food can be unsafe even when it looks and smells unchanged. **Put day-counting first, and if anything at all seems wrong, do not eat it.**
