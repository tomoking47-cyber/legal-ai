Visitors often plan a trip to a Japanese drugstore before they even land. Most of the advice they read is about *what to buy*, and very little about *how the shops actually work*.

## A. The most common misunderstanding is treating a drugstore as a pharmacy. In Japan, the two overlap but are not the same thing.

Many shops sell cosmetics, food, drinks and household goods alongside medicine — and some do not dispense prescriptions at all.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Every drugstore can fill a prescription

A shop that sells over-the-counter medicine is not necessarily a dispensing pharmacy. **If you need a prescription filled, look for a dispensing counter, and expect to need a prescription issued in Japan.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Anything on the shelf can be bought freely

Some categories of medicine are placed behind a counter and require a member of staff to hand them over and explain them. **This is a legal requirement, not unhelpfulness.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜English packaging means English instructions

A product may carry English on the front and Japanese only on the back, where the actual usage and warnings are. **Ask staff, or photograph the back and translate it, before using anything.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Tax-free applies automatically

Tax-free purchases require a passport, a minimum spend at one shop on one day, and a specific counter or till. **Rules change — check the current requirements before relying on them.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜What you buy can always be taken home

Import rules for medicine vary widely between countries, and some ingredients that are ordinary in Japan are restricted elsewhere. **We cannot advise on the rules of your destination — please check with your own country's authority.**
