# Q. What do people get wrong about taking shoes off indoors in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #5

- Status: **Draft — legal review pending**

Almost every visitor knows shoes come off at the door. Fewer people notice that the habit shapes how the whole home is built and cleaned.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that this is only about politeness. It is mostly about where dirt is allowed to stop.

The entrance is treated as a boundary. Everything beyond it is a surface people sit, sleep and put food on.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is a rule about respect alone

Removing shoes keeps street dirt at the door. In homes where people sit and sleep close to the floor, **the floor is furniture as much as it is a surface to walk on.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The entrance is just a hallway

The `genkan` is usually a step lower than the rest of the home. That step is the line: shoes below it, socks above it. **The height difference is doing the work, not a sign on the wall.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any indoor footwear is fine anywhere

Many homes keep separate slippers for the bathroom. Wearing them back into the living space defeats the purpose. **If a different pair is waiting at a door, it is waiting there for a reason.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜It only applies to homes

Some clinics, schools, traditional restaurants, temples and fitting rooms follow the same rule. **Watch what the people ahead of you do at the entrance.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is an inconvenience

Once dirt stops at the door, the rest of the home needs less cleaning. **The effort moves to one place instead of spreading across every room.**
