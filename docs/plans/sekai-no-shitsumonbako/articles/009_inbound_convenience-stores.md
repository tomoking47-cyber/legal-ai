# Q. What do people get wrong about convenience stores in Japan?｜Japan Questions #9

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/convenience-stores` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Visitors arrive expecting a shop. What surprises most of them is how much of daily life runs through these counters — and how large the network is.

## A. The common misunderstanding is thinking of them as somewhere to buy snacks. For many people here they are closer to a small utility counter, and there are more than 50,000 of them.

## The scale

The Japan Franchise Association's annual survey for 2025 reported **convenience-store sales up 1.9% on the year, the fifth consecutive annual increase**, with average spend per visit rising for the twelfth year. Store counts nationwide exceed **50,000**.

> **[Verify]** Sales figures are from the Japan Franchise Association's 2025 annual tabulation. The nationwide store count is drawn from secondary reporting; confirm the current JFA figure before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is only a shop

Many branches handle parcel drop-off and collection, utility-bill payment, ticket printing, photocopying and cash withdrawal. **A lot of everyday admin happens at that one counter.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The food is an emergency option

Fresh items are restocked through the day and dated tightly. **For many people here it is ordinary food, not a last resort.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Allergen information will be in English

Japan's mandatory allergen labelling covers **eight specified ingredients: shrimp, crab, walnut, wheat, buckwheat, egg, milk and peanut.** Walnut was added in 2023, with labelling made compulsory after a transition period ending in March 2025. A further set of ingredients is recommended for labelling but not mandatory.

**Labelling is in Japanese.** If you have an allergy, do not guess from the picture — translate the ingredient panel or ask staff.

> **[Verify]** Confirm the current list of specified ingredients and the number of recommended ingredients with the Consumer Affairs Agency before publication.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Everything can be eaten on the spot

Some branches provide a counter or seating; many do not. **Eating while walking is not usual in most places — look for a bin or a seating area first.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Bins are always available

Bins at the front are intended for items bought there. **Public bins are scarce, so many people carry their rubbish until they get back.**
