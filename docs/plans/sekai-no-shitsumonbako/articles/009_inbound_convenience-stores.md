# Q. What do people get wrong about convenience stores in Japan?｜Japan Questions #9

- Status: **Draft — legal review pending**

Visitors arrive expecting a shop. What surprises most of them is how much of daily life runs through these counters.

## A. The common misunderstanding is thinking of them as somewhere to buy snacks. For many people here, they are closer to a small utility counter.

Food is only part of what happens there.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is only a shop

Many branches handle parcel drop-off and collection, bill payment, ticket printing, photocopying, printing and cash withdrawal. **A lot of everyday admin happens at that one counter.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The food is an emergency option

Fresh items are restocked through the day and dated tightly. **For many people here it is ordinary food, not a last resort.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Everything can be eaten on the spot

Some branches provide a counter or seating; many do not. **Eating while walking is not usual in most places — look for a bin or a seating area first.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Bins are always available

Bins at the front are intended for items bought there. **Public bins are scarce, so many people carry their rubbish until they get back.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Allergen information will be in English

Ingredient and allergen labelling is normally in Japanese only. **If you have an allergy, do not guess from the picture — translate the label or ask staff.**
