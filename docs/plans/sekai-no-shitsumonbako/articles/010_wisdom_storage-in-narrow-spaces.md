# Q. What do people get wrong about storage in narrow spaces in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #10

Small-space storage is often presented abroad as a set of clever tricks. Seen from inside a Japanese home, it looks more like a set of decisions.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that it is about buying more storage. It is mostly about deciding what does not come in.

Containers move the problem. They rarely remove it.

## Misunderstanding 1｜More boxes means more storage

Every container takes up space itself, and hides what is inside. **Adding storage often increases what a household keeps rather than what it can find.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Gaps are wasted space

The narrow gap beside a fridge or a washing machine is a shape, not a fault. **Products exist for exactly those widths — which is why so many household items here are unusually narrow.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Storage should be permanent

Bedding is put away in the day. Seasonal clothing rotates twice a year. **Space is reused across the day and across the year rather than assigned once.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Everything should be hidden

Items used daily are often left within reach on purpose. **Hiding something you use every day just adds a step to every day.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is a design problem

Much of it is a habit: things return to one place after use. **The habit does more work than the furniture does.**
