# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing cosmetics?｜Japan Questions #11

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/storing-cosmetics` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

How to store cosmetics is debated around the world, and it is one of the points where household habits differ most. Cosmetics sold in Japan often carry no expiry date, which puzzles many newcomers. Once you know the Japanese rules, the basis for deciding becomes clear.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "keeping it cold makes it last longer." Products are made to keep their quality for at least 3 years under the conditions shown on the label.

Under Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (the country's cosmetics and drugs law), **an expiry date is not required except on cosmetics containing ascorbic acid or enzymes, and on those that could deteriorate within 3 years of manufacture.** So a product with no date printed is, by design, one that **"keeps its quality unopened for 3 years if stored properly."**

Rather than chilling, the surer thing is that **"proper storage": keeping the product away from direct sunlight, heat and humidity.** Always follow the storage instructions on each product's label.

## How the rules work｜Three types that must carry a date

Under a 1980 (Shōwa 55) notice of the then Ministry of Health and Welfare, the following cosmetics must show an expiry date:

1. Those containing ascorbic acid (vitamin C) or its esters or salts
2. Those containing enzymes
3. Those **at risk of changing in character or quality within 3 years** of manufacture or import under proper storage conditions

> **[Verify]** Several specialist websites present the above as a summary based on Article 61 of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act and the 1980 Ministry of Health and Welfare notice. **Before publication, confirm against the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association's Cosmetics Q&A and the original legal texts.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜In the fridge it is safe

Chilling a product that is not labelled for refrigeration can change its emulsion or make ingredients separate out as crystals. **Refrigerate only products whose labels say to.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It does not matter how often you take it in and out

Each time it comes out of the fridge, it meets a temperature change and condensation. **"In and out repeatedly" is a harsher condition than "kept cold throughout."**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The bathroom is a good place to store cosmetics

In a Japanese home, the washbasin area (洗面所, senmenjo) and the bathroom have both humidity and temperature swings. **They are in fact unsuitable.** A dry place out of the sun is the basic choice.

## Misunderstanding 4｜No expiry date means it lasts indefinitely

**"Not printed" and "indefinite" are two different things.** The 3-year assumption applies to unopened products.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once opened, it still lasts as long as the label says

Once opened, the product is exposed to air and to your hands. **Use it up promptly regardless of the label.** As a general guide, the industry advises using products up within a few months to a year of opening.
