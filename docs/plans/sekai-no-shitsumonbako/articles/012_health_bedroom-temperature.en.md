# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about bedroom temperature?｜Japan Questions #12

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/bedroom-temperature` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Sleep with the air conditioner on, or off? Opinions divide around the world, and Japan's humid summers make the question pressing for anyone newly arrived. Japan's official guidance gives actual numbers.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "just get the temperature right." The national guidance gives a range for room temperature, and the Ministry of the Environment states plainly that "28°C is not the setting on the air conditioner."

## The numbers in the guidance

- **Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023** [健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023] (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, MHLW): a room temperature of **13–29°C** during sleep is described as desirable, and **26–28°C in summer** is reported to make sleep easier.
- **Ministry of the Environment heatstroke prevention information**: the often-quoted "28°C" — a figure you will hear everywhere in Japan in summer — is described as **a guide for room temperature, not the air conditioner's set temperature itself.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Air conditioning is bad for you even on hot days

Heatstroke happens indoors too. **The Ministry of the Environment and the MHLW have repeatedly stressed the importance of managing room temperature, including while you sleep.** Enduring the heat is the more dangerous choice.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Setting the air conditioner to 28°C is enough

The set temperature and the room temperature are different things. **Depending on insulation and sunlight, the room can pass 29°C even with the unit set to 28°C.** Check the room with a thermometer.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Lower the temperature and you will sleep

When humidity is high, sweat evaporates poorly, and the same temperature feels more oppressive. **Aim for around 60% humidity, and watch temperature and humidity together.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Air blowing directly on you is fine

A constant stream of air on the body can chill you. **Simply pointing the airflow away from your body changes how it feels.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜In winter, the warmer the better

The lower limit in the guidance is 13°C. **A bedroom that is too cold is not desirable**, but heating dries the air, which can affect throat and skin. Think about warmth and dryness at the same time.

## Misunderstanding 6｜One setting suits the whole family

Comfort varies with age and build. **Even in a shared room, adjusting individually with bedding is more realistic.**
