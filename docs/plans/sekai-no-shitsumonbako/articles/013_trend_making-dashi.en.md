# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about making dashi?｜Japan Questions #13

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/making-dashi` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Dashi — the light stock behind miso soup, noodle broth and much else — is the foundation of Japanese cooking. Yet a great deal of received wisdom about it is wrong. Once you understand the mechanism, you see why the two main ingredients are combined.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the longer you simmer, the stronger the dashi." The heart of dashi is not time but combining two kinds of umami.

**Glutamate** from kombu (昆布, dried kelp) and **inosinate** from katsuobushi (かつお節, dried, smoked bonito flakes — a fish product). Combine the two and the umami is perceived not as a sum but as **7–8 times** as strong as either alone. This is known as the **"umami synergy effect,"** and ichiban dashi (一番だし, "first dashi") makes use of it.

> **[Verify]** The "7–8 times" figure is based on material from the Umami Information Center and others. **Confirm against primary sources before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Every ingredient can be simmered the same way

Kombu kept at a rolling boil is generally described as releasing slime and off-flavours, and katsuobushi simmered for long loses its aroma. **Each ingredient is handled differently.** "Simmer for a long time" suits neither.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Making dashi is a lot of work

There is also a method that simply steeps kombu in water (mizudashi, 水出し — cold brewing). **It may take time, but that is not the same as taking effort.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Kombu alone, or katsuobushi alone, is enough

You can make dashi from one ingredient, but **the synergy arises only when the two are combined.** There is a rational reason for combining them.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Shop-bought dashi is cheating

Commercial dashi (the granules, sachets and concentrates sold in every supermarket) has its uses. **What matters is knowing what you are using and choosing accordingly.** Umami seasoning (うま味調味料, MSG) draws on the same glutamate.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The spent ingredients are rubbish

Ways of cooking with the spent ingredients (dashigara, だしがら) have been passed down in households across Japan. **They are also known as a way of reducing food waste.**

## Misunderstanding 6｜Dashi is only for Japanese food

Umami drawn from ingredients can be used beyond Japanese cooking. **The umami synergy effect has no nationality.**
