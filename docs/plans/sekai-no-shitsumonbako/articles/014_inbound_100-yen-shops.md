# Q. What do people get wrong about 100-yen shops in Japan?｜Japan Questions #14

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/100-yen-shops` ／ Status: **Draft — legal review pending**

These shops appear on almost every visitor's list, usually described as somewhere cheap.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the point is the price. The interesting part is how narrow the products are allowed to be.

Low prices are what you notice. Specificity is what makes them worth a visit.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Everything costs 100 yen

Many shops now carry 300-yen and 500-yen lines alongside the standard range. **Check the tag rather than assuming.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The price on the shelf is what you pay

Shelf prices are often shown before consumption tax. **The amount at the till may be slightly higher.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is all the same product everywhere

Ranges differ by chain and by branch, and lines change frequently. **An item you saw once may not be there next time.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Buy first, work out the use later

The most useful items solve one specific annoyance you already have. **Ask whether it solves something at home, not whether it is cheap.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Anything can go in your luggage

Scissors, knives and blades cannot travel in hand luggage, and liquids are restricted. **Check your airline's rules, and your own country's import rules, before buying.**
