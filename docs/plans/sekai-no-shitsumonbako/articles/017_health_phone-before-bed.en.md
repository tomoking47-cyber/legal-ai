# Q. What do people get wrong about using a phone before bed?｜Japan Questions #17

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/phone-before-bed` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"The light from the screen is bad for you" is one of the best-known pieces of sleep advice in the world. Far fewer people, in Japan or elsewhere, know what the Japanese government's own sleep guideline actually says. Its advice goes one step further than the light.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "as long as I keep the light down, it's fine". Japan's national guideline asks for something stronger than dimming the screen: do not bring the phone into the bedroom at all.

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) published the *Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023* (健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023). As conditions for good sleep, it lists **not looking at bright screens before bed, not bringing smartphones or tablets into the bedroom, and keeping the bedroom as dark as possible.**

## Being honest about the light

Several studies confirm that strong light at night affects the body clock and sleepiness. However, **studies disagree on how much difference a screen-level brightness makes.** Outdoor daylight reaches tens of thousands of lux; indoor lighting is a few hundred lux; a screen is weaker still.

We are not yet at the stage where "phone light disturbs sleep" can be stated as a hard number.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Night mode solves it

Changing the colour of the screen does not help if **the content is stimulating and pushes your bedtime later.** The guideline says "do not bring it in" because, as we read it, changing behaviour is more reliable than changing light.

## Misunderstanding 2｜"Keep it out of the bedroom" is unrealistic

Many people use their phone as an alarm clock. **A rule you cannot follow is worth nothing.** Simply placing the phone out of arm's reach reduces the number of times your hand drifts towards it without thinking.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Notifications don't matter

An interruption after you have fallen asleep cuts directly into your sleep. **Switching off notifications works, reliably.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Only screen time matters, not the content

A work email, a message that could turn into an argument, a video you want to see the end of. **If the content sets your mind working, falling asleep becomes harder whatever the light.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜The phone is why I can't sleep

Many factors affect sleep. **If you keep failing to sleep to the point where it disrupts your daily life, see a doctor** (in Japan, a general internal-medicine clinic, 内科 *naika*, is a normal first stop and is covered by health insurance).
