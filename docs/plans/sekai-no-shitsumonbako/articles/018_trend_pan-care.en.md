# Q. What do people get wrong about looking after frying pans?｜Japan Questions #18

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/pan-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Frying pans are made from very different materials, so care advice from one country rarely matches another. The habits you brought with you may be for a different kind of pan from the one in your Japanese kitchen. Knowing one number changes the decision.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "all frying pans can be handled the same way". Fluoropolymer (non-stick) coatings have a temperature limit of 260°C; iron and stainless steel do not.

## The number to know｜260°C

Fluoropolymer (so-called Teflon) coatings are generally described as having a **maximum continuous-use temperature of about 260°C**. Above this the coating begins to degrade, and **at about 350°C there is a risk of decomposition gases being released.**

**If you heat an empty pan (空焚き, *karadaki*, "dry heating"), the surface reaches this temperature within a few minutes.** If you realise you have dry-heated a pan, ventilate the room at once; if you feel unwell, see a doctor.

> **[Verify]** The temperatures are figures from manufacturer and industry documents. **Before publication, confirm them with the Japan Fluoropolymers Industry Association (一般社団法人 日本弗素樹脂工業会) or a manufacturer's official documents.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Stuck-on food comes off if you scrub hard enough

Coated pans can be damaged by steel wool, abrasive cleaners and **metal spatulas**. **Check the product's instructions first.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Burning the pan dry cleans it

This is advice for **iron pans** (鉄のフライパン). Non-stick coatings have the temperature problem described above. **Follow the instructions for your pan's material.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Detergent strips the oil film, so don't use it

This too is something sometimes said about iron pans. **Do not apply it to every material.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Running water over a hot pan cools it quickly, so it's a good idea

A sudden change in temperature can warp the pan and **cause the coating to peel**. **Let it cool before washing — that is the basic rule.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜A worn pan can be used indefinitely

Once the coating starts to peel, the tool has reached the end of its life. **Deciding when to replace it is part of looking after it.**
