# Q. What do people get wrong about cash and cards in Japan?｜Japan Questions #19

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/cash-and-cards` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Visitors arrive having read either "Japan is a cash country" or "cards work everywhere." Both are out of date, and the government's own figures show why.

## A. The common misunderstanding is treating it as one question. Cashless payment reached 58% in 2025, but acceptance still depends on the kind of place.

## The numbers

| Year | Cashless share of payments | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | **42.8%** | Government's 40% target hit a year early |
| 2025 | **58.0%** | New domestic indicator (46.3% on the older international-comparison basis) |
| 2030 | 65% | Interim target |
| — | 80% | Long-term target |

Source: Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, 31 March 2026.

**The direction is clear. The gaps are specific.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cards work everywhere now

Small restaurants, family-run shops, some temples and shrines, and rural areas may still be cash only. **Carry some cash as a fallback.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Nothing takes cards, so bring only cash

Chains, hotels, department stores and transport generally accept cards and IC cards. **Carrying only cash is no longer necessary either.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any ATM will take a foreign card

Not all ATMs accept cards issued abroad, and some have limited operating hours. **ATMs in convenience stores and post offices are the usual recommendation; check your card's network.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜IC transport cards are only for trains

They are also used at many shops, vending machines and lockers. **They function as small-value electronic money.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Tax-free is handled at any till

Tax-free purchases require a passport, a minimum spend and often a specific counter. **The system changes to a refund model on 1 November 2026 — confirm the current rules.**
