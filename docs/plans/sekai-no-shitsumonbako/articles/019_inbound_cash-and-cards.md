# Q. What do people get wrong about cash and cards in Japan?｜Japan Questions #19

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/cash-and-cards` ／ Status: **Draft — legal review pending**

Visitors arrive having read either "Japan is a cash country" or "cards work everywhere." Both are out of date.

## A. The common misunderstanding is treating it as one question. Acceptance depends on the kind of place, not on the country.

Large shops and chains differ from small, independent and rural ones.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cards work everywhere now

Acceptance has grown, but small restaurants, family-run shops, some temples and shrines, and rural areas may still be cash only. **Carry some cash as a fallback.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Nothing takes cards, so bring only cash

Chains, hotels, department stores and transport generally accept cards and IC cards. **Carrying only cash is no longer necessary either.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any ATM will take a foreign card

Not all ATMs accept cards issued abroad, and some have limited operating hours. **Check which networks your card works with before you rely on one.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜IC transport cards are only for trains

They are also used at many shops, vending machines and lockers. **They function as small-value electronic money.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Tax-free is handled at any till

Tax-free purchases require a passport, a minimum spend and, often, a specific counter. **Requirements change — confirm the current rules.**
