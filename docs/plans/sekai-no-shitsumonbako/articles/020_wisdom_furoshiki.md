# Q. What do people get wrong about furoshiki in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #20

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/furoshiki` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A furoshiki is a square cloth used to wrap and carry things. Abroad it is usually presented as a craft. Its history and its recent revival say otherwise.

## A. The common misunderstanding is treating it as decoration. It is a tool that has been re-adopted twice: once by a shogun's bathhouse, and once by a plastic-bag charge.

## Where the name comes from

The word *furoshiki* — literally "bath spread" — is generally traced to the **Muromachi period (1336–1573)**. Lords using the bathhouse built by the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu wrapped their clothes in cloths marked with family crests, so as not to confuse them with others', and stood on the cloth to dress afterwards. **It began as a practical solution to a sorting problem.**

> **[Verify]** The origin account is widely repeated by furoshiki makers and cultural sites; confirm against a reference source before publication.

## Why it came back

Japan introduced a **mandatory charge on plastic shopping bags on 1 July 2020**. The Ministry of the Environment had already promoted furoshiki as a bag alternative — including a "Mottainai Furoshiki" designed under environment minister Yuriko Koike, shown to delegates at a 3R ministerial meeting in **March 2006**.

**The cloth did not change. The context did.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is a traditional item no longer in use

It is sold in ordinary shops and used for gifts, lunch boxes, bottles and as a bag. **It did not stop being practical.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜There is one correct way to fold it

Different ties suit different shapes — flat, round, long, two bottles. **The fold follows the object, not a rulebook.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The cloth is the point

The point is that one flat cloth replaces several rigid containers, then folds away to nothing. **It stores as a square and works as a bag.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Any fabric works the same

Size and weave change what it can carry and how the knot holds. **Choose the cloth for the load.**
