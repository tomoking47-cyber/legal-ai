# Q. What do people get wrong about pilling (bobbles) on clothes?｜Japan Questions #21

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/pilling` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

When little balls of fibre appear on a jumper, the usual verdict is "cheap clothes". Look at how fibres actually behave and a different answer appears. Japan's textile testing bodies have written this up in some detail.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "pills form because the quality is poor". It is the other way round: pills stay on the surface precisely because the fibres are strong.

## How pills form

When friction acts on the surface of a fabric, the fibre ends lift and fuzz. The fuzz bunches together and tangles into a pill (毛玉, *kedama*, literally "hair ball"). The main causes are **friction while wearing (underarms, inside the sleeves, where a bag rubs) and friction during washing.**

Here comes the paradox. **Synthetic fibres such as acrylic and polyester are strong and hard to break, so the tangled pill does not fall off; it stays on the surface.** With weak fibres the pill tears away and drops off. Strong fibres hold it. **So "it pills because it was cheap" is not accurate.**

## Combinations that pill most

According to a textile testing body, **blends such as "acrylic × cotton" and "polyester × wool"** are generally described as needing particular care. When different fibres are mixed, the strong fibre catches the fuzz of the weak fibre and holds it in place.

> **[Verify]** The above is based on documents from a textile testing body (Menken Quality Inspection Association) and a dry-cleaning industry association. **Check the original text before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Expensive clothes don't pill

Natural fibres pill too. **Garments made from short fibres, or from several blended materials, pill more readily.** It is the composition, not the price.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Washing causes it

Most of the friction happens while you are wearing the garment. **Look at where the pills form and you can see the cause.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Just pull them off

Pulling drags the fibre out with the pill, and the fabric thins at that point. **Cut them off at the base, or use a purpose-made tool** (a fabric shaver, 毛玉取り器 *kedama-tori-ki*).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Fabric softener prevents it

Softener is expected to reduce friction, but **it does not stop pills from forming.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once it pills, the garment is finished

**Turn it inside out and put it in a mesh laundry bag (洗濯ネット, *sentaku netto*)** — that is the prevention method the industry association recommends. Reducing friction between garments is enough to keep pilling down.
