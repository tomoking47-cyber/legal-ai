# Q. What do people get wrong about morning light?｜Japan Questions #22

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/morning-light` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Get some morning sun" is advice you hear everywhere. Ask "isn't the light in my room enough?" and few people can answer with a number. Here are the numbers, as they appear in Japanese public health sources.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the light in my room does the same job". Outdoors is more than 20 times brighter than indoors, and it is outdoor light that moves the body clock.

## Orders of magnitude

| Place | Approximate illuminance |
|---|---|
| Outdoors, clear sky | **50,000–100,000 lux** |
| Outdoors, overcast | **about 10,000 lux** |
| Typical indoor lighting | **a few hundred lux (around 500 lux)** |

**2,500 lux or more** is often given as the guide figure for resetting the body clock; indoor lighting does not get there. **Even on a cloudy day, outdoors is more than 20 times brighter than indoors.** That is why "going outside" matters.

> **[Verify]** The illuminance figures and the "2,500 lux" guide are values widely quoted by sleep clinics and health information sites. **Before publication, confirm them against MHLW e-Health Net or an academic society's material.**

## Why light is needed

Under strictly controlled experimental conditions, the cycle of the human body clock has been measured at **about 24.2 hours**. Because that is longer than 24 hours, bedtime and waking time drift later day by day if nothing corrects them. **Morning light is what pulls that drift back every day.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜It works the same night

Adjusting the body clock only means something when you keep it up. **Do not judge the result after one day.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It's pointless unless it's sunny

An overcast sky still gives about 10,000 lux. **Bad-weather days count too.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜You have to look straight at the sun

There is no need to look directly at the sun, and **doing so can damage your eyes. Never do it.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Light at night doesn't matter

The release of melatonin (the hormone linked to sleep) is suppressed at levels far below 2,500 lux. Suppression has been reported at around 100 lux measured at face height, which means **ordinary indoor lighting is quite bright enough to matter at night**. **Get light in the morning, turn it down at night. The two go together as a pair.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Light alone decides your sleep

Regular waking and bedtimes, how active you are in the day, the environment before bed — many factors are involved. **If you keep failing to sleep, see a doctor.**
