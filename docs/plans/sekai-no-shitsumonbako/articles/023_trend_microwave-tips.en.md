# Q. What do people get wrong about using a microwave oven?｜Japan Questions #23

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/microwave-tips` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Microwave ovens are in kitchens all over the world. What is unusual in Japan is that **the number of accidents, and their causes, are recorded by public bodies.** Those records show where the risk really lies.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "it just heats things — it's a safe appliance". 157 accidents were reported in five years, and 31 of them (about 20%) came down to misuse or carelessness.

## Accidents in numbers

- **NITE (National Institute of Technology and Evaluation)**: in the five fiscal years 2014–2018, **157** microwave-oven accidents were notified. Compared with other products, **the share of accidents caused by user misuse or carelessness is described as high.**
- **National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan**: from April 2020 to December 2025 (five years and nine months), **521** consumer consultations about microwave ovens. It issued a fresh warning in March 2026.

> **[Verify]** The figures are based on a NITE press release (27 February 2020) and a National Consumer Affairs Center announcement (25 March 2026). **Check the original text before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜It heats evenly

A microwave heats by agitating the water in the food, so the wetter parts heat faster. **Stir part-way through, or change the position of the food** — that alone reduces uneven heating.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Eggs can go in as they are

**Both raw eggs and boiled eggs burst when heated, because the pressure inside rises.** NITE has published a warning poster about it. Moreover, **an egg that did not burst inside the oven can burst after you take it out**, so if you have heated one by mistake, leave it alone until it has cooled down.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The tighter the cling film, the better

Sealing tightly can raise the pressure inside. **Lay the film on loosely — that is the basic rule.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜For a small amount, just shorten the time

**Heating something with very little moisture can cause it to catch fire.** The smaller the amount, the more care is needed. NITE has also warned about accidents from "lazy cooking" (ずぼら調理, *zubora chōri* — careless shortcut cooking) (September 2024).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Reheating doesn't need to be thorough

**Under-heated food can cause food poisoning.** The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's guide figure for preventing food poisoning is a core temperature of 75°C for at least one minute.

## Misunderstanding 6｜Any container will do

Metal and some plastics cannot be used. **Always check the markings on the container** (in Japan, look for 電子レンジ対応 or 電子レンジ可, meaning "microwave-safe").
