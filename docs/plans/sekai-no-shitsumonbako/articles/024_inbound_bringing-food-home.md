# Q. What do people get wrong about bringing food home from Japan?｜Japan Questions #24

- Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Food is one of the most popular things to carry home, and one of the most commonly confiscated. Japan's own quarantine service has a page for exactly this question.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the question is about Japan. Two sets of rules apply: Japan's on the way out, and your own country's on the way in — and meat is the category where both bite.

## What Japan's Animal Quarantine Service says

The Animal Quarantine Service (Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries) advises that travellers **taking meat products abroad as souvenirs should contact the quarantine office at their departure airport or port to discuss export inspection.**

Export inspection is required where **the destination country demands a quarantine certificate**, and for products derived from **wild animals** (deer, boar, quail, pheasant, duck and similar). Requirements differ by destination and can take time to arrange — **early contact is recommended.**

> **[Verify]** At the time of writing, the service noted that **export quarantine certificates for pork and wild-boar products were suspended** following domestic cases of classical swine fever. Confirm the current status before publication — this changes.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If it is sold in a shop, it can be taken home

Being on sale here says nothing about another country's import rules. **Check with the customs and quarantine authority of your destination.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sealed packaging makes it acceptable

Many countries restrict meat, dairy, fresh produce, seeds and plants **regardless of packaging.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Small amounts do not need declaring

Some countries require every food item to be declared, sealed or not. **Declaring costs nothing; failing to declare can carry a penalty.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Anything can go in hand luggage

Liquids, pastes and gels are restricted, and that includes some sauces and sweets. **Check your airline's rules.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Heat and time do not matter

Chocolate, fresh sweets and chilled items may not survive a long journey. **Read the storage instructions before you buy.**
