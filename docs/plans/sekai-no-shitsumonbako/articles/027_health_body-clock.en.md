# Q. What do people get wrong about the body clock?｜Japan Questions #27

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/body-clock` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Body clock" is a familiar phrase in every language. The key fact — that it does not run on exactly 24 hours — is surprisingly little known. If you have recently moved to Japan and are still adjusting, that one fact explains a lot.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "you can fix it by willpower". The body clock runs on a cycle of about 24.2 hours, and it has to be re-set with light every day.

## The body clock in numbers

- Measured under strictly controlled experimental conditions, the human body clock is generally described as having a cycle of **about 24.2 hours**. It used to be quoted as "25 hours", but more precise measurement gives a value closer to 24.
- Longer than 24 hours means that **left alone, it drifts later by ten-odd minutes every day.**
- The main signal that resets that drift is **light**: morning exposure to light of **roughly 2,500 lux or more** is generally described as telling the body clock what time it is. Even on a cloudy day the outdoors exceeds 1,000 lux, so stepping outside is the practical cue.

> **[Verify]** "About 24.2 hours" is the value given in the Pharmaceutical Society of Japan's glossary and similar sources. **Confirm it against a primary source (academic society or research institute) before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜It runs on exactly 24 hours

It is 24.2. **That is why it needs adjusting every day.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Light is the only thing that sets it

Meal times and the timing of activity are generally described as playing a part as well. **Whether or not you skip breakfast is one of the factors.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜You can catch up by sleeping in at the weekend

If your waking time shifts a long way at weekends only, the clock shifts with it. **Keeping the gap with weekdays small makes it easier to manage.** The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's *Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023* also stresses a regular daily rhythm.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Night owls can be fixed, and should be

Morning types and evening types are generally described as individual differences. **The assumption that "everyone should become a morning person" is not accurate.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Drift has nothing to do with health

If a drift continues to the point where it disrupts your daily life, **do not try to deal with it on your own — see a doctor.**
