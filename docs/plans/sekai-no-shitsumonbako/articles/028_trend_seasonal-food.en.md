# Q. What do people get wrong about seasonal food (shun)?｜Japan Questions #28

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/seasonal-food` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

You will see the word 旬 (*shun*, "in season") on menus and supermarket signs all over Japan. It can sound like seasonal decoration. In fact shun is a phenomenon that **shows up as a price** in wholesale-market statistics.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "shun is a thing of the past". Shun is the period when arrivals at market rise and prices fall, and you can check it every month in public statistics.

## Shun is visible in the numbers

- The **Tokyo Metropolitan Central Wholesale Market** publishes **monthly arrival volumes and prices** for vegetables and fruit as official statistics.
- The **Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries' "Survey of Fruit and Vegetable Wholesale Markets"** (on e-Stat, the government statistics portal) also gives wholesale volumes, values and prices by market and by month.
- **"Vegetan" (ベジ探)**, run by the Agriculture & Livestock Industries Corporation (ALIC), shows graphs of arrival volumes and prices at the central wholesale markets of Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka and Fukuoka.

For the same vegetable, prices fall in the months when arrivals are high. **That is shun.** Not sentiment — supply and demand.

> **[Verify]** The above describes the existence and location of the statistics. **If price differences for a specific vegetable are written into the text, take the actual figures from Vegetan or the Tokyo market statistics and state the year and month.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Shun is a sentimental idea

When more of something reaches the market, its price falls. **It is a practical matter that goes straight to your household budget.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Shun is the same across Japan

Japan is long from north to south, so the same food comes into season at different times in different regions. **Look at the place of origin (産地, *sanchi*) on the label and you can see the timing.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Greenhouse growing has made it meaningless

Being available all year and being harvested in quantity at a certain time are two different things. **There are more options now; shun itself has not disappeared.** The peaks in the market statistics are still there.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Food in season is always more nutritious

Nutrient content also varies with variety, growing method and storage. **You cannot say across the board that "in season means more nutritious".** Even the Standard Tables of Food Composition published by MEXT (Japan's education and science ministry) give seasonal values for only some foods.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You need expert knowledge to know what's in season

What is cheap and piled high in the shop is your clue. **Looking at the shelves is the quickest way.**
