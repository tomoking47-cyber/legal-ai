# Q. What do people get wrong about caring for leather shoes?｜Japan Questions #31

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/leather-shoe-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Advice on leather shoes usually starts with tools and polishing routines. Before any of that, there is a number most people miss. In Japan's humid summers it is worth taking seriously.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "polishing is what care means". Your feet sweat about a glassful a day, and leather takes one to two days to dry. Resting the shoes is the heart of shoe care.

## The numbers behind "rest them"

- Sweat from the feet is generally described as **about one glass (コップ1杯) a day**.
- Moisture absorbed by a leather shoe is said to take **one to two days (around 48 hours)** to dry out, and longer in humid seasons.
- Hence the industry recommends **"wear for one day, rest for two or three days"**.
- Wearing shoes that are still damp day after day speeds up deterioration and shortens their life.

> **[Verify]** The figures above are guide values from shoe shops and repairers. **Before publication, confirm them with the Japan Leather and Leather Goods Industries Association or a manufacturer's official documents.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Care means applying cream

Cream applied on top of dirt seals the dirt in. **Clean first, then apply.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It's better to polish every day

Too much cream is generally described as making it harder for the leather to breathe. **The industry guide is roughly "once every 10 wears".** Match the frequency to how often you wear them.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It's fine to wear the same pair every day

As above, **wearing shoes before they have dried is the main cause of deterioration.** Rotating two or three pairs ends up extending the life of each pair.

## Misunderstanding 4｜If they get wet, dry them fast

Direct sunlight or the heat of a hairdryer can stiffen the leather. **Dry them slowly, in a well-ventilated place.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Shoe trees are only about keeping the shape

Besides stopping the shoe from losing its shape, **they draw out the moisture that has collected inside.** Wooden ones in particular are recommended (in Japanese shops, ask for シューキーパー, *shū kīpā*). Put them in as soon as you take the shoes off — that is the rule of thumb.
