# Q. What do people get wrong about drinking water (hydration)?｜Japan Questions #32

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/hydration` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Two litres a day" is a number that travels the world on its own. Material from Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) gives a more detailed breakdown, and it changes what the number means. In Japan's hot, humid summers the question is far from academic.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "two litres of drinks a day". In the MHLW's figures, an adult man takes in 2.5 litres of water a day, of which drinking water is 1.2 litres.

## The breakdown

According to material from the MHLW's "Drink Water for Your Health" campaign (「健康のため水を飲もう」推進運動), an adult man takes in **a total of about 2.5 litres** of water a day. The breakdown is as follows.

| Route | Amount |
|---|---|
| Water contained in food | **about 1.0 litre** |
| Drinking water | **about 1.2 litres** |
| Water produced inside the body | **about 0.3 litre** |

**"Drink two litres" may be counting the water in your food twice.**

> **[Verify]** The figures are based on the MHLW campaign material (PDF). **Check the original text before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Drink a fixed amount and you've got it right

How much you need varies with body size, activity, temperature and diet. **Japan's Dietary Reference Intakes set no reference value for water intake.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Always drink before you feel thirsty

Drinking little and often is recommended, but **that does not mean drinking to excess is recommended.** The campaign's purpose is to prevent heatstroke, stroke (cerebral infarction) and similar conditions.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more you drink, the healthier you are

Drinking a large amount in a short time can upset the balance of electrolytes in the body. **"More is better" is wrong.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Counting the volume is the reliable way

Watching the colour of your urine and how you feel is generally described as more practical. **Look at your condition rather than counting.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜The same guide applies to everyone

**If you have been given instructions on fluid intake because of kidney disease, heart disease or similar, always follow your doctor's instructions.** Your own doctor's instructions come before any general rule.
