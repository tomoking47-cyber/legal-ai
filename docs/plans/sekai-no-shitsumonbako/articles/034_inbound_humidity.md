Visitors check the forecast, see a familiar temperature, and pack accordingly. The number is only half the story — and the other half filled hospitals in 2025.

## A. The common misunderstanding is reading temperature alone. At high humidity sweat evaporates slowly, and the result in 2025 was a record 100,510 ambulance calls for heat illness.

## What the humidity did

The Fire and Disaster Management Agency's figures for **May–September 2025**:

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| People taken by ambulance for heat illness | **100,510** — first time over 100,000 since records began (2008) |
| June alone | **17,229** — a monthly record |
| Aged 65 or over | **57.1%** |
| Most common location | **The home — 38.1%** |

The last line matters most for visitors. **Heat illness here happens indoors as often as outdoors.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜It only matters in summer

The rainy season in early summer brings high humidity at moderate temperatures. **Laundry, shoes and bags all behave differently.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Thin clothing solves it

Fabric that moves air and dries quickly matters more than how little you wear. **Loose and breathable beats minimal.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Indoors is always comfortable

Air conditioning is often strong on trains and in shops. **Carry a light layer.** But note the figure above: many heat-illness cases happen in homes *without* cooling on.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Rain is the only reason to carry an umbrella

Umbrellas are also used against strong sun. **You will see plenty on clear days.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜The 28°C figure is a thermostat setting

The Ministry of the Environment states that the widely quoted 28°C is a **target room temperature, not an air-conditioner setting.** Rooms can exceed it with the unit set to 28.
