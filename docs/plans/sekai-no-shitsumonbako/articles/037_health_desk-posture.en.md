# Q. What do people get wrong about posture at a desk?｜Japan Questions #37

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/desk-posture` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

People all over the world complain that trying to hold "correct posture" leaves them more tired, not less. Japan has a national guideline on this, covering anyone who works on a PC, tablet or smartphone. Its answer is about time, not shape.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "just keep the correct posture". Japan's national guideline instead sets limits in time: do not work for more than one hour at a stretch, and rest for 10–15 minutes.

## The numbers in the guideline

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) guideline *Guidelines for Occupational Health Management in Work with Information Devices* (情報機器作業における労働衛生管理のためのガイドライン; 12 July 2019, Notice Kihatsu 0712 No. 3; partially revised 1 December 2021 by Kihatsu 1201 No. 7) sets out the following for work using PCs, tablets and smartphones.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| One continuous work period | **Not more than one hour** |
| Rest between work periods | **10–15 minutes** before the next continuous period |
| Short pauses | **About one or two** within each continuous work period |

It can be read as being concerned with **the length of time spent in the same posture, rather than the posture itself.**

> **[Verify]** The above is based on the work-management section of the guideline text. **Check the MHLW's original PDF before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Keeping your back straight the whole time is the right answer

Prolonged muscle tension leads to fatigue. **A posture that needs constant effort cannot be held, so it does not last.** This is thought to be why the guideline sets limits in time.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Buying a good chair solves it

A chair helps, but **the time you spend sitting does not change.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Take a break once you feel tired

The guideline does not say "when you are tired"; it says **"not more than one hour"**. **Break before you get tired.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Screen height is a matter of taste

If your gaze drops too far, the load on your neck increases. **Placing the top edge of the screen at about eye level** is generally described as one guide. The guideline also gives a guide for viewing distance.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Discomfort is caused by posture

Shoulder and neck complaints have many causes. **If they persist, do not try to solve them by correcting your posture; see a doctor** (clinics in Japan are covered by national health insurance).
