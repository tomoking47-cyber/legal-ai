Most visitors know to wash first. Fewer know that the rest of the "rules" are not folklore — several are written into a Ministry of the Environment notice that facilities are required to display.

## A. The common misunderstanding is thinking of it as a bath. It is shared water that everyone enters already clean, and the safety guidance is official.

## What the official notice says

Under the Hot Spring Act (Article 18), facilities post health cautions based on a **Ministry of the Environment notice of 1 July 2014**. Among the bathing precautions:

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Rinse first | Pour water over hands and feet, adjust to the temperature, and **wash before entering** |
| Time in the water | **3–10 minutes to begin with**; up to 15–20 minutes once accustomed |
| Do not bathe | **Immediately before or after meals, or after drinking alcohol** — especially when intoxicated |
| After exercise | Rest for about **30 minutes** first |
| Older people, children, those with disabilities | Advised **not to bathe alone** |

## Misunderstanding 1｜A quick rinse is enough

Wash and rinse thoroughly at the seated shower. **No soap should reach the shared water.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The small towel goes in the water

Keep it out — folded on your head or at the edge. **It is for drying, not for the water.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Swimwear is acceptable

Most facilities do not allow it in the bathing area. **Check the specific facility's rules.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Long soaking is better

The official guidance starts at **3–10 minutes**. Hot water is demanding on the body. **Take breaks, drink water, and leave if you feel unwell.**

## On tattoos

A 2015 Japan Tourism Agency survey (581 facilities responding) found **56% refused entry to tattooed guests, 31% did not, and 13% allowed entry on conditions** such as covering. **Policies vary by facility and have shifted since — check in advance.**
