# Q. What do people get wrong about the rainy season in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #40

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-rainy-season` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Early summer brings several weeks of high humidity. Households here treat it as a season with its own tasks — and one of those tasks has a five-hour deadline.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that it is simply a rainy period. Locally it is a moisture-management season, and the habits are aimed at humidity, not rain.

## Two things the season does

1. **It slows drying.** Laundry that stays damp beyond about **five hours** lets Moraxella bacteria multiply — the source of the "indoor-dried" smell identified in Kao's 2011 research. Fans, dehumidifiers and bathroom dryers exist to beat that clock.
2. **It grows rice.** The season is part of how the crop is watered and how reservoirs are refilled. **Inconvenient and necessary at the same time.**

> **[Verify]** Confirm the Moraxella finding with Kao's published material, and seasonal timing with the Japan Meteorological Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It rains constantly

It does not. **The constant is the humidity, not the rain.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It is the same everywhere in Japan

Timing and intensity differ by region, and Hokkaido is generally not counted as having one. **Check the region you are in.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Closing the windows keeps damp out

Sealed rooms trap the moisture already inside. **Ventilation in the drier hours, or a dehumidifier, is the usual approach.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜It only affects laundry

Cupboards, shoes, tatami and stored clothing all matter. **This is why airing things out is a seasonal habit.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is purely a nuisance

See point 2 above. **Rice does not grow without it.**
