# Q. What do people get wrong about drying laundry indoors?｜Japan Questions #41

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/indoor-drying` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan, drying laundry indoors (部屋干し, heya-boshi) is normal during the rainy season and in flats without a balcony. It is usually discussed as if a musty smell were inevitable. In fact the cause of the smell has been identified, and there is a time limit to work to.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the smell depends on the detergent". The cause is the metabolic by-products of bacteria that multiply on damp clothes, and they start to multiply after about five hours. Drying time matters more.

## What the smell is, and the five hours

In 2011, research by Kao (a major Japanese household-goods maker) identified the cause of indoor-drying smell as **the metabolic by-products of Moraxella bacteria**. Moraxella live naturally on human skin, prefer damp conditions, and feed on sebum and moisture.

If clothes stay damp, the bacteria are generally described as **starting to multiply after about five hours**. In other words, **whether the laundry is dry within five hours of washing** is the dividing line.

> **[Verify]** Kao's 2011 research and the "five hours" guide are cited on several Japanese lifestyle sites. **Check against Kao's official announcement before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Turn up the heat and it will dry

**Moving air across the clothes** does more than raising the temperature. The basic set-up is: lower the humidity with the air conditioner's dry mode (除湿, joshitsu), point a circulator fan (サーキュレーター, sold at any home-electronics store) at the laundry, and get it dry within five hours.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Hanging more at once is more efficient

Where garments touch, those parts do not dry. **Leaving space between items is the first priority.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜A closed room dries faster

The moisture leaving the clothes collects in the room. **Unless you ventilate or dehumidify, the water has only moved.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Thick items can be hung the same way

Thick parts dry slowly. **Turn items inside out, or arrange the hangers differently, to create a path for the air.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜If it smells, just wash it again

If long drying time is the cause, rewashing will repeat the problem. **Changing how you dry is the more reliable fix.**
