# Q. What do people get wrong about bath temperature in Japan?｜Japan Questions #42

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/bath-temperature` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japan likes its baths hot, and a deep soak is one of the first things visitors try. The government, however, warns that deaths during bathing run at about three times the number of road-traffic deaths. Here are the official guide figures.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the hotter the bath, the better it relieves fatigue". The Consumer Affairs Agency's guide is a water temperature of 41°C (106°F) or below, and no more than 10 minutes in the water.

## Bathing accidents in numbers

According to the Consumer Affairs Agency's safety warning —

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| People aged 65 and over who died by drowning (2022 / Reiwa 4) | **7,900** |
| Of these, in bathtubs at home or in residential facilities | **5,824** |
| Estimated sudden deaths during bathing (including causes other than drowning) | **About 19,000 a year** |

Government Public Relations Online describes this as **"about three times the number of road-traffic deaths"**.

The reasons given for the rise in accidents in winter are **sudden swings in blood pressure caused by the temperature difference between a warm living room and a cold changing area or bathroom** (Japanese bathrooms are often unheated), and **loss of consciousness caused by a rise in body temperature after a long soak in hot water**.

> **[Verify]** The figures are based on the Consumer Affairs Agency's warning (2022 data) and Government Public Relations Online. **Check the originals before publication and update to the latest year's data.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Hotter is better for you

**The Consumer Affairs Agency's guide is 41°C or below, for up to 10 minutes.** Long soaks at high temperature lie behind the accidents above.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The longer you soak, the more good it does

A long bath makes you lose fluid. **Drinking water before and after the bath** is recommended.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Just put up with a cold changing area in winter

**Warming the changing area and the bathroom** is one of the pillars of the Consumer Affairs Agency's warning. The temperature difference itself is described as a risk factor.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Bathing after drinking alcohol is fine

**Repeated warnings say to avoid bathing after drinking.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜One temperature suits the whole family

The deaths above are concentrated among older people. **If you live with family, it is also recommended to let someone know before you bathe, and to check on anyone who has not come out for a long time.**
