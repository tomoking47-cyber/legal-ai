# Q. What do people get wrong about tattoos and bathing in Japan?｜Japan Questions #44

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/tattoos-and-bathing` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

This is one of the most searched questions before a trip, and one of the most inconsistently answered — because the answer is genuinely facility by facility.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that there is a national rule. There is not. A 2015 government survey found facilities split roughly 56 / 31 / 13 between refusing, allowing, and allowing on conditions.

## The only nationwide data point

In 2015 the Japan Tourism Agency surveyed hot-spring and bathing facilities on their tattoo policies. Of **581 responding facilities** (from 3,768 surveyed):

| Policy | Share |
|---|---|
| Refuse entry to tattooed guests | **56%** |
| Do not refuse | **31%** |
| Allow on conditions (e.g. covering with a patch) | **13%** |

The agency followed up in 2016 with examples of accommodating practice, such as **accepting palm-sized tattoos when covered.** Reporting since then describes a gradual increase in facilities accepting tattooed guests — but **no national rule exists, and the survey is a decade old.**

> **[Verify]** Survey figures as reported by Nikkei (October 2015) and Travel Voice (March 2016). Confirm against Japan Tourism Agency material before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Tattoos are banned by law

There is no national law barring tattooed visitors from bathing facilities. **Individual operators set their own house rules.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Every facility refuses entry

See the table. **Around 44% did not refuse outright even in 2015.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Size does not matter

Some facilities accept small tattoos if covered, and refuse larger ones. **The distinction is theirs to make.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can check on arrival

Asking at the door after travelling is the worst moment to find out. **Check the facility's own website or contact them in advance.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜A private bath is the only option

Private or in-room baths are one route. **Some public facilities also set aside particular hours or areas.** Ask when you enquire.
