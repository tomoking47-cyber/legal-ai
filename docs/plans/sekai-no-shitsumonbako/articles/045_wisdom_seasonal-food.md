# Q. What do people get wrong about seasonal food in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #45

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/seasonal-food` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Seasonal eating is often presented abroad as a refined philosophy. In everyday life here it is closer to shopping sense — and it shows up in public wholesale-market statistics.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that it is about aesthetics. It is mostly about supply: when a food is abundant its wholesale price falls, and that is recorded month by month.

## Where the season is written down

- **Tokyo Metropolitan Central Wholesale Market** publishes monthly and annual statistics on arrivals and prices for fruit and vegetables.
- **MAFF's Fruit and Vegetable Wholesale Market Survey** (via e-Stat) gives quantity, value and price by market and month.
- **ALIC's "Vegetan" system** charts arrivals and prices at the Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka and Fukuoka central markets.

The same vegetable costs less in the months when more of it arrives. **That is the season, measured.**

> **[Verify]** If a specific price difference is quoted in the published article, take the figures from Vegetan or the Tokyo market statistics and state the month and year.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is a luxury approach

Buying what is plentiful is the cheaper way to shop. **The season is visible in the price, not only on the menu.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It is fixed by the calendar

Japan runs a long way north to south. **The same food peaks at different times depending on where it was grown.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Greenhouses ended it

Year-round availability added choices; it did not remove the peak. **The monthly statistics still show the mountain.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Seasonal always means more nutritious

Composition varies with variety, growing method and storage. **This cannot be stated as a rule.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜You need expertise to follow it

Look at what is stacked high and priced low. **The shop floor tells you.**
