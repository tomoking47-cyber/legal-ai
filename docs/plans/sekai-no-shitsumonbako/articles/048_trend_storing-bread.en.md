# Q. What do people get wrong about storing bread?｜Japan Questions #48

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-bread` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Whether bread belongs in the fridge divides opinion around the world, and Japan, with its soft sliced loaves (食パン, shokupan), is no exception. Once you know how starch behaves, the answer settles itself.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the fridge keeps it longer". The temperature range in which bread starch hardens fastest is around 0–5°C, and that is exactly the temperature of the fridge compartment.

## Starch "retrogradation"

In freshly baked bread the starch holds water and is soft (gelatinised). Over time the molecules realign into an orderly pattern, release their water, and the bread hardens. This is called **starch retrogradation (でんぷんの老化, "ageing" or β-conversion)**.

This retrogradation is generally described as **fastest at around 0–5°C (32–41°F)** (2–4°C according to some sources). **The fridge compartment (about 2–5°C) is the worst temperature range for bread.**

**Freezing**, on the other hand, passes quickly through the hardening range and stops the process. Bread frozen properly is generally described as **keeping close to fresh-baked condition for about one month**.

> **[Verify]** The temperature figures are based on explanations from a confectionery college and the baking industry. **Check against primary sources before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cold stops deterioration

As above, **the fridge's temperature range is precisely the range in which bread hardens.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Freezing spoils the taste

Because **it holds the bread close to its baked state**, freezing is the better option if you will keep it for more than a few days.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Freeze the whole loaf in one go

Frozen as one block, the whole thing has to be thawed every time you want some. **Divide it into single portions.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Thawing at room temperature is the safe way

**Toasting from frozen is generally described as losing less moisture.** If the product carries instructions, follow them.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cut off the mould you can see and the rest is fine

**The problem is not necessarily limited to the visible part.** If you see mould, do not eat it.
