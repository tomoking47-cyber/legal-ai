# Q. What do people get wrong about the concept of shun in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #50

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-concept-of-shun` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

*Shun* is usually translated as "in season." The translation is accurate and still loses something — and the something is measurable.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that shun means a whole season. It refers to a short window when a food is at its best, and that window shows up as a dip in wholesale prices.

## The window, and where to see it

A season lasts months. **A shun can last a couple of weeks.** During that window arrivals at wholesale markets peak and prices fall — which is why the Tokyo Metropolitan Central Wholesale Market's monthly statistics, and ALIC's "Vegetan" price-tracking system, are the closest thing to an official record of shun.

The word is also used in ordinary supermarket signage. **It is everyday vocabulary with a data trail.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Shun equals summer, autumn and so on

It marks a peak inside a season, not the season itself. **The word points at a narrow window.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜There is one shun per food

Some foods are described as having an early (*hashiri*), a peak (*sakari*) and a late (*nagori*) window, each valued differently. **The idea has gradations.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is only about taste

Abundance, price and the way a food is cooked all shift with it. **Cooking methods change with the window, not only the menu.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜It is a chef's concept

It is used in conversation and on shop signs. **Anyone shopping uses it.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Modern supply chains made it meaningless

Availability widened; the peak did not disappear. **What changed is that noticing it is now optional.**
