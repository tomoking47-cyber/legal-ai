# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about sunscreen?｜Japan Questions #51

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/sunscreen` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Sunscreen is used all over the world, but few people know the conditions under which the numbers on the label are measured. In Japan you will also see a second rating, "PA", next to the SPF. This article explains what both numbers assume, and why the amount you apply matters more than the number.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the higher the SPF, the safer you are". SPF is measured with 2 mg of product per cm² of skin, and most people apply less than half of that.

## The assumptions behind the numbers

- SPF and PA values are measured under internationally standardised test methods, with **2 mg (or 2 μL) per cm² of skin** applied.
- Actual use, however, has been reported at **about a quarter of the proper amount** in one study, and at **an average of 0.56 g, roughly half the theoretical amount** in another.
- The guide for the whole face is **0.7–0.9 g** (two pearl-sized drops of cream, or two 1-yen-coin-sized pools of liquid — a 1-yen coin is about 2 cm across).

**The performance on the label applies only when you apply the amount on the label.** Applying the labelled amount gets you closer to the labelled performance than choosing a higher number does.

> **[Verify]** 2 mg/cm² is widely presented as the international standard test condition. The usage-survey figures come from several dermatology and cosmetics information sites, so **check them against Japan Cosmetic Industry Association (JCIA) materials or similar before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜With a high SPF you do not need to reapply

Sweat and friction remove it. **Follow the product label and reapply.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜You can ignore PA

**SPF is a guide to protection against UVB; PA is a guide to protection against UVA.** They measure different things. PA is a rating used in Japan and has **four grades, from PA+ to PA++++**. UVA is known to pass easily through window glass, so check the PA rating for everyday situations too.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A small amount spread thinly is enough

As above, **at half the amount, the labelled performance is not delivered.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜You do not need it when it is cloudy or indoors

According to Ministry of the Environment materials, a considerable share of the ultraviolet light of a clear day still reaches the ground on a cloudy day. **Decide by whether you spend long periods by a window.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Any way of removing it is fine

How to remove it differs from product to product. **Check the label, and remove it without rubbing.**
