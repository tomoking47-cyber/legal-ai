# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how long to sleep?｜Japan Questions #52

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/sleep-length` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The figure "you should sleep eight hours" has taken on a life of its own around the world. Japan's official guidance does not take that form. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW, Japan's health ministry) issued a national sleep guide in 2024, and it is organised quite differently.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "everyone needs the same amount of sleep". The national guide is organised by age group.

In February 2024 the MHLW finalised the **"Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023" [健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023]**, which groups its recommendations **by age: "adults", "children" and "older adults"**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Eight hours is the right answer for every age

The guide is divided by age group. **It does not apply a single number to everyone.**

For adults, it indicates **getting the sleep you need, with 6 hours or more as a guide.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The longer you sleep, the better

Longer is not automatically better. **For older adults, the guide also mentions not spending too long in bed.**

> **[Verify]** This point also needs checking against the original text.

## Misunderstanding 3｜You can tell whether you get enough by counting the hours

One feature of the guide is its emphasis on **"sleep restfulness" (睡眠休養感, suimin kyūyō-kan) — the feeling that sleep has actually rested you.**
Besides the number of hours, **whether you feel strong sleepiness during the day** is described as a practical clue.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can catch up by sleeping in at the weekend

If your wake-up time shifts a lot, your body clock shifts with it. **Keeping the gap with weekdays small is easier to manage.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜If you cannot sleep, stay in bed and keep trying

Lying in bed for a long time without sleeping is not recommended.
**If it continues to the point of affecting your daytime life, see a doctor.**
