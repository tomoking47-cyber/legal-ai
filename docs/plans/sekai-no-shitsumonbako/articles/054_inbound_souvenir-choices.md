# Q. What do people get wrong about souvenir choices in Japan?｜Japan Questions #54

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/souvenir-choices` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Souvenir shopping is usually planned around what looks Japanese. Most of the regret afterwards is about weight, fragility and customs.

## A. The common misunderstanding is choosing by appearance. The items still in use a year later are light, flat, legal to import and bought where residents shop.

## Where residents shop

The domestic 100-yen shop market reached about **¥1.11 trillion in fiscal 2025** (Teikoku Databank), driven by the ¥150–¥500 tier of kitchen tools, stationery and functional household goods. **Supermarkets, stationery shops and household stores carry the items people actually use.** The tourist aisle is not where the range is.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bigger feels more generous

Weight and space are real costs on the way home. **Flat, light and useful travels better than large.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Food is a safe default

Japan's Animal Quarantine Service advises travellers **carrying meat products abroad to consult the quarantine office at the departure airport**, as many destinations require certificates. Dairy, fresh produce, seeds and plants are commonly restricted elsewhere regardless of packaging. **Check your destination's rules before buying food.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Anything can go in the cabin

Blades, liquids, pastes and gels are restricted in hand luggage. **Check your airline's rules.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Regional items are only available in that region

Many regional goods are sold at major stations and airports. **You do not have to carry them the whole trip.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Tax-free makes it cheaper at home

Tax-free removes Japanese consumption tax. **Your own country's duty and allowance limits still apply on arrival.**
