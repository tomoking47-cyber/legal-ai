# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing eggs?｜Japan Questions #58

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-eggs` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Whether eggs belong in the fridge is handled very differently from country to country. Japan is one of the few places where eggs are routinely eaten raw — over rice, in sukiyaki, as a topping — and its date labelling is designed on that assumption. If you have just arrived, the date on a Japanese egg carton does not mean what it means at home.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "there is one right answer worldwide". In Japan the best-before date (賞味期限, shōmi kigen) on eggs is set as "the period within which the egg is safe to eat raw when stored at 10°C or below" — which is itself unusual.

## How Japan's egg dates work

Following the 1998 (Heisei 10) revision of the enforcement regulations of the Food Sanitation Act (Japan's food-safety law), which took effect on 1 November 1999, the best-before date on shell eggs shows **the period in which the egg can be eaten raw without problems even if Salmonella happens to be present, assuming refrigerated storage at home (10°C or below)**.

The reasoning is that the iron Salmonella needs in order to multiply exists only in the yolk. The date is therefore calculated, using a formula relating days to temperature, from **the period until the yolk membrane weakens with age and iron passes into the white**. Materials from an industry body (the Japan Poultry Association's Committee on the Revision of Egg Date Labelling, 2010) set **an upper limit of 21 days for the best-before date, on the assumption of refrigerated storage**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The door pocket is where eggs go

The door changes temperature every time it is opened and closed. **A place that stays steadily at 10°C or below** is better suited.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wash eggs before storing them

Washing the shell can remove the film that protects it. **Store them unwashed.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Past the best-before date they are no good even cooked

The date is set on the basis of **safety for eating raw**. Even so, follow the label, and **if anything at all seems wrong, do not eat it.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Eggs anywhere can be eaten raw

The assumptions above are specific to how eggs are distributed in Japan. **Do not handle eggs the same way abroad.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Eggs can be stored after cracking

Once cracked, eggs spoil quickly. **Use them up the same day.**
