# Q. What do people get wrong about customs on the way home from Japan?｜Japan Questions #64

Most trip planning covers arriving. The return journey gets far less attention, and that is where things get taken away.

## A. The common misunderstanding is thinking customs is one process. Leaving Japan and entering your own country are two separate sets of rules — and Japan's quarantine service has a dedicated page for the first half.

## Japan's side｜what the Animal Quarantine Service says

Travellers taking **meat products** abroad are advised to **contact the Animal Quarantine Service at the departure airport** to discuss export inspection. Inspection is required where the destination demands a certificate, and for products from **wild animals** (deer, boar, quail, pheasant, duck).

Requirements differ by destination and take time to arrange. **Early contact is recommended.**

> **[Verify]** At the time of writing, export quarantine certificates for **pork and wild-boar products were suspended** following domestic classical swine fever cases. Confirm current status before publication.

## Your country's side｜the part visitors forget

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is all handled by the airline

Airlines enforce cabin and baggage safety rules. **Customs and quarantine are separate authorities.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Duty-free means no limits at home

Buying without Japanese consumption tax says nothing about your allowances. **Duty and allowance limits still apply on arrival.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Declaring things causes problems

Declaring is the process working normally. **Not declaring is what creates problems.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Only food is restricted

Plants, seeds, soil, animal products, some medicines and certain wildlife-derived goods are commonly restricted. **Check before you buy, not after.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Nobody checks

Detector dogs, X-ray screening and random inspection are routine at many borders. **Assume it will be checked.**
