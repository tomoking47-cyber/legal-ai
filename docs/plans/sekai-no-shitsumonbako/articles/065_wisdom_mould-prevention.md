# Q. What do people get wrong about preventing mould in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #65

In a humid climate, mould prevention is a routine household task. The most dangerous mistake in it has a mandatory warning label.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that it is a cleaning problem. It is mostly an airflow problem — and the one thing never to do is mix chlorine and acid products, which is why Japan requires a "Do Not Mix — Danger" label.

## The label with the law behind it

Household chlorine-based cleaners that can release **1.0 ppm or more of chlorine gas** in a standard test must carry the words **まぜるな危険** ("Do not mix — danger") — "do not mix" in yellow with black outline, "danger" in red, in a framed white box. This is a legal labelling requirement.

Inhalation incidents from cleaning products **ranked first among inhalation accidents every year from 2021 to 2024**. The National Consumer Affairs Center issued a fresh warning in March 2026 after incidents in bathrooms.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cleaning products solve it

They remove growth. **If the moisture stays, so does the cause.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Closing the room keeps damp out

A closed room holds the moisture already inside. **Ventilate, and run extraction after bathing.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Only bathrooms are at risk

Cupboards, behind furniture, under futons, inside wardrobes. **Anywhere air does not move.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Furniture flat against the wall is tidy

A small gap lets air pass behind. **That gap is doing a job.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Stronger means combining products

**Never.** See above. Ventilate while cleaning, use one product at a time, and rinse between products.
