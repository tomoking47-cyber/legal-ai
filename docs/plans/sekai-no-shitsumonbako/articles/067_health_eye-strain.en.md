# Q. What do people get wrong about eye strain?｜Japan Questions #67

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/eye-strain` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The longer we look at screens, the more eye strain comes up as a topic, in Japan as everywhere else. If you work in Japan, it helps to know that the national guideline from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) frames the countermeasure as **time**, not **light**. That changes what a sensible routine looks like.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "block blue light and the problem is solved". What Japan's national guideline actually sets out is a time structure: do not work at a screen for more than one hour without a break, and rest for 10 to 15 minutes between sessions.

## The numbers in the guideline

MHLW, *Guidelines for Occupational Health Management in Work with Information Equipment* (Notice Kihatsu 0712 No. 3, 12 July 2019):

- A single continuous session of screen work should **not exceed one hour**
- Allow a **10–15 minute pause** before the next continuous session
- Take **one or two short breaks** within each continuous session

**Blocking blue light is not positioned as a main countermeasure in this guideline.** Research into its effects is still ongoing, and no settled conclusion can be said to exist.

> **[Verify]** Check against the full text of the guideline. The same guideline also gives reference values for lighting levels and viewing distance.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Block blue light and the problem is solved

As above, **managing time is the core of the countermeasure.** Do not rely on blocking alone.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Rest when your eyes feel tired

The guideline does not say "when tired"; it says **"do not exceed one hour"**. Break off before you feel tired.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A dark room is kinder to the eyes

The greater the difference in brightness between the screen and its surroundings, the greater the load is generally described to be. **Do not make the room extremely dark.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Eye drops will restore your eyes

**Different products have different purposes.** Check the label and, if needed, ask the pharmacist at a drugstore (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Tired eyes recover on their own if you leave them

**If you have changes in vision, pain or headache, or if symptoms persist, do not judge for yourself. See an ophthalmology clinic (眼科, ganka; covered by Japanese health insurance).**
