# Q. What do people get wrong about rubbish bins in Japan?｜Japan Questions #69

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/rubbish-bins` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Government surveys of visitors have placed the shortage of public bins at the top of everyday frustrations for several years running.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that bins are missing by oversight. The shortage has a history, it is the number-one complaint in the official survey, and the system assumes waste goes home with the person who made it.

## The survey

The Japan Tourism Agency's survey of visitor experiences (fieldwork November 2025–January 2026) found **"too few rubbish bins" the most common difficulty, at 17.2%** — down from 21.9% in the previous round but still first. It was followed by communication with staff (15.4%) and crowding (12.9%).

## The history

Public bins were removed from stations and streets in the Tokyo area after the **1995 Tokyo subway sarin attack**, on security grounds. Whether that was the sole cause is debated — maintenance cost, crow damage and a carry-it-home norm also play a part — **but the removal was real, and it stuck.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜There must be a bin somewhere nearby

Often there is not. **Carrying a small bag is the practical solution most residents use.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Any bin will take any rubbish

Bins are usually sorted. **Look at the labels and the shape of the opening.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Shop bins are public bins

Bins outside a shop are for items bought there. **Using them for other rubbish is not intended.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Sorting rules are national

Categories are set by each of Japan's **1,741 municipalities** and differ between them. **The rules where you are staying may not match the next city.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is just inconvenient

The habit is tied to eating in one place rather than while walking. **Once you stop to eat, the packaging problem largely disappears.**
