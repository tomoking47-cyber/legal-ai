# Q. What do people get wrong about the order of cleaning in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #70

- Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Cleaning advice usually focuses on products. The sequence is what quietly decides how long the job takes — and one sequencing error has a legally mandated warning.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that order does not matter. Dust falls, so cleaning runs top-down and far-to-near; and products are used one at a time, never mixed.

## The one rule with a label

Chlorine-based household cleaners that can release **1.0 ppm or more of chlorine gas** must, by law, carry the words **まぜるな危険** — "Do not mix — danger." Inhalation incidents from cleaning products **ranked first among inhalation accidents every year from 2021 to 2024**, and the National Consumer Affairs Center issued a renewed warning in **March 2026** after bathroom incidents.

**If a product carries that label, it is used alone, with ventilation, and rinsed away before anything else.**

> **[Verify]** Confirm with the Consumer Affairs Agency labelling guidance and the NCAC release before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Start with the floor

Dust dislodged from shelves and surfaces lands on the floor. **Floors come last.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Start with the visible mess

Working from the far corner towards the exit means you never walk back across what you have finished. **The route matters.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Wet cleaning first

Dry removal before wet wiping avoids turning dust into smears. **Dry, then wet.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cleaning is a weekend job

Many households do small daily resets and keep the large clean for the year end. **Little and often, plus one big one.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Stronger means combining products

**Never.** See the label above.
