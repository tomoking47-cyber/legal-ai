# Q. What do people get wrong about caring for dry skin?｜Japan Questions #71

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/dry-skin` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Dry skin is a shared complaint the world over, but the usual response leans towards "adding" more moisture. Japanese skincare has a well-known layering habit, and one measure from dermatology explains why the order matters. Once you know it, the routine changes.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "add water and dryness is solved". The measure of dryness is how much water is escaping from the skin (transepidermal water loss), and what reduces the escape is oil, not water.

## Measuring by "how much escapes"

Quite apart from sweat, a small amount of water evaporates constantly through the stratum corneum (the outermost layer of skin). This amount is called **transepidermal water loss (TEWL)**, and **the lower the value, the stronger the barrier function of the stratum corneum** is generally described to be. It is used both to assess dry skin and in cosmetics testing.

Moisturisers work in two ways.

| Type | Action | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Water-based | **Holds on to** water | Glycerin and similar |
| Oil-based | Forms a film on the skin surface and **physically blocks evaporation** | Petroleum jelly (ワセリン, waserin) and similar |

**"Adding" alone does not change how much escapes.** The order is: add, then seal.

> **[Verify]** The explanation of TEWL is based on the SCCJ glossary and on pharmaceutical-company material for healthcare professionals. **Confirm before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Layer on more lotion and the skin stays hydrated

The water you apply evaporates. In Japan the watery first step is called *keshōsui* (化粧水, "lotion" or "toner"). **The step that follows it, sealing with oil, is what does the work.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wash thoroughly first, then moisturise

A cleanser that is too strong puts a load on the skin's surface function. **Review how you wash before you review what you apply.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dryness is a winter problem

Air-conditioned rooms, UV exposure and evaporating sweat mean **there are causes of dryness in summer too.** Japanese summers are humid but heavily air-conditioned indoors.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hot water gives a cleaner wash

Hot water is generally described as tending to strip away sebum the skin needs. **Lukewarm is the guide.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cosmetics alone can deal with dryness

Indoor humidity, bath temperature and friction from clothing are also involved. **If itching or eczema persists, see a board-certified dermatologist at a dermatology clinic (皮膚科, hifuka; covered by Japanese health insurance).**
