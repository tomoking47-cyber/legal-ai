# Q. What do people get wrong about "hie", always feeling cold?｜Japan Questions #72

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/feeling-cold` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Hie" (冷え), meaning cold hands, feet or body, is a word you will hear constantly in Japan, from pharmacies to office small talk. It is not a diagnosis in Western medicine. Even so, surveys show how many people in Japan say they experience it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "warm up and the problem is solved". "Hieshō" (冷え症, the condition of feeling cold) is the name of a self-reported symptom with no diagnostic criteria, and surveys report that more than half of women say they experience it.

## Where "hie" stands

- According to a website supervised by a research group of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW), **"hieshō" is not a diagnostic term in Western medicine but a socially accepted name for a condition based mainly on self-reported symptoms**, with no clear diagnostic criteria.
- A survey of the general public reported that **26.6% of men and 55.1% of women** said they experienced hie. In the national Comprehensive Survey of Living Conditions, the proportion reporting cold hands and feet is **more than twice as high in women as in men**.
- As for the mechanism, **several physiological factors are generally described as being involved**: peripheral circulation, the autonomic nervous system, hormones, and blood properties.

**No definition, and more than one cause. That is why there is no single answer such as "warm up and it is solved".**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Wear more layers and the problem is solved

Tight clothing can actually hinder circulation. **What matters is not the number of layers but avoiding constriction.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Hot drinks are enough

They warm you briefly, but that alone cannot be said to change the condition. **Do not rely on a single measure.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is a women's problem

More than one in four men report it too. **The assumption that it is "unique to women" is not accurate.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Exercise always improves it

Moving your body is recommended, but **there is no basis for asserting that it "always gets better".**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is just my constitution, nothing can be done

**If it comes with numbness, colour changes or pain, or if it suddenly gets much worse, do not put it down to your constitution. See a doctor.** Another cause may be hidden behind it.
