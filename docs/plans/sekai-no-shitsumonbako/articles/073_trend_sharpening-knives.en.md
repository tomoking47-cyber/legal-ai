# Q. What do people get wrong about sharpening kitchen knives?｜Japan Questions #73

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/sharpening-knives` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese kitchen knives are famous, and sharpening them is often assumed to be a job for experts. What it actually needs is regularity and a consistent angle. And sharpening is not only about performance: Japanese safety bodies regard it as a safety matter too.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a blunt knife is safer". A blunt blade needs force, and force is what makes the blade slip and cut your hand.

## The safety angle

NITE (the National Institute of Technology and Evaluation, Japan's product-safety body) was notified of **49 accidents involving children and kitchen goods** over the ten years from FY2010 to FY2019: 4 deaths, 8 serious injuries and 21 minor injuries. **Those 49 cases are mostly about cooking appliances — gas hobs (15 cases), electric kettles and the like — and the commonest accident type is burns.** The Consumer Affairs Agency has also published accident reports from medical institutions under the heading "Beware of injuries from knives and other blades in the kitchen".

**A sharp blade cuts where you aim it, with little force.** A blunt blade needs extra force, and when that force slips, it becomes an injury.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Sharpen only once the knife stops cutting

Sharpness fades gradually. **Touching up little and often is less work than restoring a badly dulled edge.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The angle can change each time

Keeping the same angle is generally described as the basic rule. **If the angle wobbles, the edge becomes rounded.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any knife can be sharpened with the same tool

The right method differs by steel and blade shape. **Check the product label and the maker's instructions.** Whetstones (砥石, toishi) are sold at home centres and 100-yen shops in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Knives can go in the dishwasher

**Some products are not dishwasher-safe.** Check the label.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If you cannot sharpen it yourself, give up

Shops offering a sharpening service (研ぎ, togi) exist all over Japan, including many knife shops and department stores. **Handing it over is a valid option.**

## Storage

**Keep blades out of children's reach.** Rethinking where you store them reduces the chances of a child reaching a blade in the first place.

Always follow the instructions for the tools you use, and handle blades with care.
