# Q. What do people get wrong about sorting rubbish in Japan?｜Japan Questions #74

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/sorting-rubbish` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Visitors often arrive expecting one national system and find that the rules changed when they changed city. That is not a mistake on their part. There are 1,741 systems.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that sorting rules are national. Each of Japan's 1,741 municipalities sets its own categories and collection days.

## The structure

The Ministry of the Environment's waste survey for fiscal 2023 covers **1,741 municipalities and 545 partial-affairs associations** — each responsible for its own household-waste system. Category counts vary from a handful to more than a dozen. **Tokyo's 23 wards alone are counted as separate systems in the survey.**

There is no single correct answer to "which bin does this go in." **It depends where you are.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜The categories are the same everywhere

They are not. **Check the rules for the place you are staying** — accommodation usually provides a leaflet.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Plastic is plastic

Bottles, caps, labels and other plastics are frequently separated from one another. **The bottle, its cap and its label may go three different ways.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Rinsing is optional

Containers are generally expected to be emptied and lightly rinsed. **This is part of the process.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can put it out any day

Collection days are fixed per category. **Putting rubbish out on the wrong day is one of the most common complaints about visitors in rented accommodation.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Hotels handle it, so it does not matter

In hotels, largely yes. **In rented accommodation, the rules apply to you.**
