# Q. What do people get wrong about caring for sensitive skin?｜Japan Questions #76

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/sensitive-skin` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Sensitive skin" (敏感肌, binkan-hada) is printed on a great many product labels in Japanese drugstores (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.), but it is not a medically defined category. That single fact changes how you should think about choosing products for it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "sensitive skin is a fixed skin type". There is no academic definition; surveys report that more than half of women say they have it, yet dermatology textbooks have no entry for it.

## What "no definition" means

- **"Sensitive skin" is not an academic term and has no medical definition.** It is generally described as a state in which the skin stings or tingles after cosmetics are applied but shows no visible symptoms.
- **53.4%** of women say they consider their own skin sensitive (private-sector survey); other reports put the figure at around 60%, and around 70% among women in their twenties.
- In a 2005 survey of doctors, **39.4% of doctors are reported to have answered that "sensitive skin is a matter of perception"**.

In other words, **a "for sensitive skin" label is a label aimed at a condition that has no definition.** Whether a product suits you cannot be known until you try it.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If it says "low irritation", it is fine for everyone

The label is a guide. **It does not mean the product suits every person.** The Japanese wording to look for is 低刺激 (tei-shigeki).

## Misunderstanding 2｜Naturally derived means no irritation

Origin and irritation are separate matters. **Naturally derived products can also disagree with your skin.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Use fewer products and the skin will always settle

Cutting steps helps in some cases, but **it cannot be said to work every time.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜A patch test is unnecessary

Before using a new product, **trying it on an inconspicuous patch of skin is recommended.** Precisely because the condition has no definition, testing is the only way to know.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Put up with redness and itching and the skin will get used to it

**Do not keep using a product on the assumption that your skin will adapt.** Stop using it and see a board-certified dermatologist at a dermatology clinic (皮膚科, hifuka; covered by Japanese health insurance). If there are visible symptoms, that is not "sensitive skin"; it is something for a dermatologist to look at.
