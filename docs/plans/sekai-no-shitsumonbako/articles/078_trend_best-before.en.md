# Q. What do people get wrong about Japan's "best before" dates?｜Japan Questions #78

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/best-before` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese food packaging carries one of two dates: 賞味期限 (shōmi kigen, "best before") or 消費期限 (shōhi kigen, "use by"). If you cannot read the kanji, they look identical. Government documents repeatedly point out that misreading these dates is one reason so much edible food is thrown away in Japan.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "once the date has passed, it cannot be eaten". The best-before date (賞味期限) is the date up to which quality is maintained; the date that marks safety is the use-by date (消費期限).

**Passing the date means something different in each case.** Throw food away without knowing which is which, and you throw away food that could have been eaten.

## Misunderstanding 1｜There is a rule that "five days or less means use-by"

This is **an old standard that has since been rejected.**

At one time the explanation "roughly five days or less is a use-by date, longer is a best-before date" was widely repeated. But **drawing the line mechanically at five days was judged inappropriate as a basis for setting dates under the definitions**, and the relevant ministries sorted the matter out in 2008. Today, each period is to be set on the basis of its own definition.

> **[Verify]** The above history is based on the Consumer Affairs Agency's *Guidelines for Setting Food Date Labels* (March 2025) and the agency's material on the date-labelling system. **Confirm against the originals before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The date still applies after opening

**The date assumes the package is unopened.** Once opened, use the food up promptly regardless of the label.

## Misunderstanding 3｜How you store it makes no difference

**The date applies only if the storage method on the label has been followed.** Carry a "keep refrigerated" item around at room temperature in a Japanese summer and the premise collapses, even within the date.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Dates are set by guesswork

Dates are to be set by the manufacturer on a scientific and rational basis. **At the same time, they build in a safety margin.** That is why passing a best-before date is not an instant "no".

## Misunderstanding 5｜Past the date is always still fine

How much margin there is depends on the food and how it has been stored. **Check the appearance and smell, and if anything seems wrong, do not eat it.**

## In numbers

According to estimates by the Ministry of the Environment and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (published 27 June 2025), **food loss in FY2023 was about 4.64 million tonnes**. Of that, **households accounted for about 2.33 million tonnes and businesses for about 2.31 million tonnes**, so **almost half comes from homes**. This was 80,000 tonnes less than the roughly 4.72 million tonnes of the previous year (FY2022), but still short of the reduction target for FY2030.

> **[Verify]** Figures are based on the Ministry of the Environment press release (FY2023 estimates). **Check for the latest year at the time of publication.**

For any actual product, always follow the label and the storage method printed on it. If anything seems wrong, do not eat it, even within the date. The rules may be revised.
