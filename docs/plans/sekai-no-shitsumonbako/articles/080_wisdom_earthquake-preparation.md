# Q. What do people get wrong about earthquake preparation in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #80

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/earthquake-preparation` ／ **Caution: safety**
- Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Preparation here is treated as ordinary household maintenance. Government surveys show which parts people actually do — and which they skip.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that preparation means an emergency bag. The official advice starts with securing furniture, and only 37.6% of people have done it.

## What the surveys show

| Measure | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Households that have secured furniture against toppling | **37.6%** | Cabinet Office survey, August 2025 |
| Households with 3+ days of drinking water stocked | **69.8%** | Cabinet Office survey, August 2025 |
| Households with 3+ days of food stocked | **59.7%** | Same |
| Households with 3+ days of hygiene supplies | **38.7%** | Same |

Official guidance calls for **at least 3 litres of drinking water per person per day**, and stocks for **at least 3 days, ideally a week.**

**The gap is clear: people stock water, but two-thirds have not secured the furniture that injures them before they reach it.**

> **[Verify]** Figures from Cabinet Office public-opinion surveys (2022 and 2025). Confirm before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A grab bag is the main thing

Securing tall furniture and appliances is the first item in official advice. **Injuries often happen before anyone reaches a bag.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Run outside immediately

Guidance from Japanese authorities generally advises protecting your head and staying put until the shaking stops. **Falling objects outside are a hazard.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Supplies means food only

Water, a torch, a radio, a power bank, medication, a portable toilet and cash are all commonly listed. **Water is the largest item by volume — 3 litres per person per day.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Stock it once

Stored food and water have dates. **Rotating stock through normal use is the standard advice.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Visitors do not need to think about it

Knowing your building's evacuation site and enabling your phone's emergency alerts takes minutes. **Municipal hazard maps are published online.**
