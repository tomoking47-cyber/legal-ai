# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about use-by dates (消費期限)?｜Japan Questions #83

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/use-by` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese food labels carry two different dates: 消費期限 (shōhi kigen, the use-by date) and 賞味期限 (shōmi kigen, the best-before date). Mix them up and your decision goes in the wrong direction. And the widely repeated explanation that "five days is the cut-off between them" has already been rejected.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "anything that keeps five days or less gets a use-by date, by rule". That mechanical split was ruled inappropriate in 2008. Today each date is set from the definition of the term.

## How the system defines the two dates

The Consumer Affairs Agency (消費者庁, Japan's consumer-protection agency) sets out the terms as follows:

| | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Use-by date (消費期限, shōhi kigen)** | The date up to which the food, stored in the way stated, **can be eaten safely, without spoilage or other deterioration** |
| **Best-before date (賞味期限, shōmi kigen)** | The date up to which the food, stored in the way stated, **keeps its full quality** |

An explanation once spread that "roughly five days or less means a use-by date; anything longer means a best-before date". In 2008 the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries clarified that **a mechanical five-day split is not an appropriate way to set a date from the definitions**. The Consumer Affairs Agency's current guidelines (March 2025) also require dates to be set from the definitions.

> **[Verify]** Check against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Guidelines for Setting Food Date Labels" and its material on the history of the date-labelling system.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Both dates have some leeway

**The one with leeway is the best-before date.** The use-by date is about safety. Do not eat food that is past it.

## Misunderstanding 2｜If it looks fine, it is fine

Food can be unsafe even when its look and smell have not changed. **Put date management first.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Heating makes it safe

**Heating does not necessarily solve the problem.** Do not decide to heat and eat food that is past its use-by date.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The date stretches if the pack is unopened

The date applies **only to an unopened pack stored in the way shown on the label**. It is not something that gets extended.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Freezing extends the date

Freezing at home does not extend the date printed on the label. **Follow the label.**
