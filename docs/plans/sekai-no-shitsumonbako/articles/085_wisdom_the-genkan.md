# Q. What do people get wrong about the genkan in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #85

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-genkan` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The entrance area of a Japanese home is usually described as a hallway. It works more like a threshold — and in a home of 90.86 m², a threshold is worth the floor space it takes.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that it is wasted floor space. It is the boundary that keeps outdoor dirt out of the rest of the home, and it has been there since at least the Heian period.

## Why a small home gives up floor to it

The 2023 Housing and Land Survey puts the **average dwelling at 90.86 m² with 4.26 rooms.** In that footprint, every square metre matters — and yet almost every home keeps a genkan. **The reason is what happens beyond it:** floors people sit and sleep on must stay clean, so dirt has to stop somewhere.

Picture scrolls from the **Heian period (794–1185)** already show footwear being removed before entering. **The step predates the architecture around it.**

> **[Verify]** Housing figures from the Statistics Bureau (2023 survey). Historical note from Web Japan (Ministry of Foreign Affairs). Confirm before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is just an entryway

Shoes come off below the step and stay there. **Everything above the step is treated as clean floor.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The height difference is stylistic

The drop separates the two zones physically, so dirt has somewhere to stay. **Remove the step and the boundary stops working.** Modern flats keep the distinction even when the step is slight.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Shoes can point any way

Turning shoes to face the door is a common courtesy, especially when visiting. **It also makes leaving easier.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜It is only found in homes

Clinics, schools, some restaurants, temples and traditional inns use the same arrangement. **Watch the entrance before you walk in.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is a small space by accident

It also holds umbrellas, coats and deliveries — the things that should not travel further in. **It is a buffer, sized for that job.**
