# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about "natsubate" (summer fatigue)?｜Japan Questions #87

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/summer-fatigue` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Natsubate" (夏バテ) is a word you will hear everywhere in a Japanese summer. It describes feeling drained by the heat, but it is not a medical term. Its neighbour, heatstroke (熱中症, netchūshō), does have a clear clinical classification — and in 2025 Japan recorded its highest ever number of ambulance call-outs for it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "I feel sluggish, but it's only natsubate, so there's nothing to worry about". Natsubate has no definition; heatstroke has a four-grade classification. Symptoms are what tell them apart.

## The numbers and the classification

**Between May and September 2025, 100,510 people were taken to hospital by ambulance for heatstroke** — the first time the figure has passed 100,000 since the statistics began in 2008 (Fire and Disaster Management Agency). **57.1% were aged 65 or over, and the most common place of onset was the home (38.1%).**

The 2024 clinical guideline classifies heatstroke into **four grades**.

| Grade | Symptoms | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Grade I (mild)** | Dizziness, light-headedness on standing, heavy sweating, muscle pain or cramps. Consciousness normal | Cool the body in a cool place; take in fluids and salt |
| **Grade II (moderate)** | **Headache, nausea, fatigue, reduced concentration and judgement** | **See a doctor** |
| **Grade III (severe)** | Impaired consciousness, convulsions, liver or kidney dysfunction | Hospital admission |
| **Grade IV (most severe)** | Core body temperature of 40.0°C (104°F) or above with a reduced level of consciousness | Intensive care |

**Many of the symptoms people are tempted to call "natsubate" overlap with Grade II.**

> **[Verify]** The ambulance figures are from the Fire and Disaster Management Agency; the classification is from the Japanese Association for Acute Medicine's "Heatstroke Clinical Practice Guideline 2024". **Check the original documents before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Natsubate is caused by heat alone

The gap between air-conditioned rooms and the outdoors, disrupted sleep and an unbalanced diet are also generally described as factors. **And "natsubate" itself has no definition.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Cold food and drink will put you right

It may feel better for a moment, **but that alone cannot be said to change your condition.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜If you have no appetite, you need not eat

Finding a form of food you can manage is recommended. **Going on without eating is not desirable.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Fluids alone are enough

When you have sweated heavily, public advice calls for attention to **both fluids and salt.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Sluggishness is just natsubate, so there is nothing to worry about

**If you have any symptom at Grade II or above in the table — headache, nausea, poor judgement, no sweating, a rising body temperature — move to a cool place at once and see a doctor.** Heatstroke can kill.
