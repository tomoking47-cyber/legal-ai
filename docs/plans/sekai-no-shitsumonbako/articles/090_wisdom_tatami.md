# Q. What do people get wrong about tatami in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #90

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/tatami` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Tatami is usually described as flooring. It behaves more like a large, replaceable furnishing — and its size is not even fixed.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a tatami mat is a standard unit. There are at least four regional sizes, and the largest is 26% bigger than the smallest.

## The sizes

| Name | Dimensions | Area | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kyōma** | 955 × 1910 mm | 1.824 m² | Kansai, Shikoku, Chūgoku, Kyūshū |
| **Chūkyōma** | 910 × 1820 mm | 1.656 m² | Nagoya region |
| **Edoma** | 880 × 1760 mm | 1.549 m² | Kantō and north |
| **Danchima** | 850 × 1700 mm | 1.445 m² | Apartment blocks (post-1950s public housing) |

Room sizes are still commonly quoted in mats. **The same "six-mat room" can differ by up to 26% in floor area depending on the region** — which is why square-metre figures are recommended alongside.

> **[Verify]** Dimensions from tatami industry references; the standard JIS A 5901 recognises multiple sizes. Confirm before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is installed like a floor

Mats are laid in, not fixed down. **They can be lifted, turned and replaced individually.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It cannot be cleaned

It is swept and wiped along the grain, and kept dry. **Water is the thing to avoid, not cleaning.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Heavy furniture is fine on it

Point loads leave marks. **Pads under legs are the usual answer.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜A mat is a mat

See the table. **Moving from Osaka to Tokyo, your furniture may not fit the same "six-mat" room.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is disappearing

Fewer homes are entirely tatami, but single tatami rooms remain common, and thin mat products are sold for other floors. **It changed form rather than vanished.**
