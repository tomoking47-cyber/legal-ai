# Q. In what order should I take off my makeup?｜Japan Questions #91

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/makeup-removal-order` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The order in which you take off makeup is one of the steps that decides how much strain your skin takes. In Japan, makeup remover (クレンジング, kurenjingu) and face wash (洗顔料, senganryō) are sold as separate products, and the sequence below assumes that.

## A. The basic pattern is "hard-to-remove areas first, with a product made for that area; then the whole face". Splitting the face into parts causes less friction than washing the whole face hard.

## The basic sequence

1. **Eyes and lips** and other hard-to-remove areas, with a product made for them (look for ポイントメイクリムーバー, "point makeup remover")
2. Take off the **whole face**
3. Use a **face wash** (if the product says "W洗顔不要", no double cleansing needed, follow that label)

The Japanese Dermatological Association guideline gives twice a day as the guide for face washing and describes over-washing as a strain on the skin. **The take-off step counts as the evening one of those two washes.**

## Why split it up

Makeup removers are designed to lift oil-based dirt (makeup, sunscreen). If you raise the strength for the whole face to match the hardest areas, **the easy areas take more force than they need**. Separate the parts from the whole and each gets the minimum it needs.

> **[Verify]** The face-washing guide is from the Japanese Dermatological Association guideline. **Check the primary source before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Taking it all off in one go is quicker

As above, **splitting it up ends up being less of a strain.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The longer you work it, the more comes off

The longer you move a product over the skin, the more friction. **Keep to the time and amount on the product label.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The same product is fine for the eyes

**The skin around the eyes is generally described as thin.** Use a product labelled for the eye area and do not rub.

## Misunderstanding 4｜As long as it comes off, the method does not matter

**No rubbing, and no residue left after rinsing** — those are the practical points. The hairline, the chin and the sides of the nose are where residue tends to stay.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Wipe-off sheets are easy and just as good

Wiping is a movement that involves friction. **Whether to use them every day is a call to make on the state of your skin.**
