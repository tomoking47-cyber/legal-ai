# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about choosing supplements?｜Japan Questions #92

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/choosing-supplements` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Supplements are used all over the world, but in Japan the rules changed sharply in 2024. If you buy supplements at a Japanese drugstore (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.) or online, knowing why they changed is the starting point for choosing well.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "they can stand in for meals". Supplements are foods; after the health-damage cases of 2024, Japan moved to make manufacturing controls and the reporting of harm compulsory.

## What changed in 2024

In March 2024, health damage linked to products made from red yeast rice (紅麹, beni-kōji) came to light. In response:

- **For Foods with Function Claims (機能性表示食品, a Japanese category of food whose maker notifies a health claim to the authorities) sold in supplement form, manufacturing control based on GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) became a requirement the notifying business must meet.**
- **Reporting information on health damage to the authorities became compulsory.**
- The amendments under the Food Labelling Act and the Food Sanitation Act **came into force on 1 September 2024.**

In other words, **the assumption "it's a food, so it's safe" was revisited by the regulators themselves.**

> **[Verify]** The content of the reforms and the date they came into force are from Consumer Affairs Agency Food Labelling Division material (August–September 2024). **Check the original before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜They work like medicines

**Supplements are foods.** They are not meant to heal or prevent any disease.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The more you take, the better

**There are reports of health damage from taking too much.** Do not exceed the amount on the label.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is natural, so it is safe

**The 2024 health damage came from a naturally derived ingredient.** Origin and safety are two different things.

## Misunderstanding 4｜It is fine to take them alongside my medication

**Some are known to interact with medicines.** If you are on medication, always ask your doctor or pharmacist.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The label means the government vouches for that product

Foods with Function Claims are notified on the business's own responsibility. **The government does not confirm the effectiveness of each individual product.**

## People who need particular care

**If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have a chronic condition, take medication, or are buying for a child, always ask a doctor or pharmacist first.**
