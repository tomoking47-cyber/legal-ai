# Q. In what order should I do my food prep (下ごしらえ)?｜Japan Questions #93

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/prep-order` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

This looks like a question about kitchen efficiency, but it is also one about hygiene. The order itself is written into the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's food-poisoning prevention points. In a small Japanese kitchen, often with one chopping board, the order matters all the more.

## A. The basic rule is "food eaten raw first, raw meat and fish last". This is not about efficiency; it is the order that stops bacteria being passed on.

## "Preparation" in the Six Points

The preparation section of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Six Points for Preventing Food Poisoning at Home" says: **do not cut vegetables or fruit to be eaten raw with a knife or board that has cut raw meat or fish**, and **wash the tools straight after use and pour boiling water over them.** Of the three principles of food-poisoning prevention — **"don't pass it on, don't let it multiply, kill it"** (つけない・増やさない・やっつける) — preparation is the step that covers **"don't pass it on"**.

## The basic sequence

1. **Wash your hands**
2. **Vegetables and fruit to be eaten raw**
3. **Vegetables to be cooked**
4. **Raw meat and fish (last)**
5. **Wash the tools you used, and your hands**

> **[Verify]** Check against the original Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare leaflet.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The order is for efficiency

**It is for hygiene.** Do not cut raw vegetables with tools that have just handled raw meat or fish.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wash the board and one is enough

You can use it once washed, but **keeping to the order is the surer way.** Use separate boards if you can.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Cutting everything first, then cooking, is more efficient

Some ingredients dry out or discolour from the cut surface. **Some are better cut just before use.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The longer you marinate, the better

**The right time depends on the ingredient and the seasoning.** Longer is not automatically better.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You can skip hand-washing part-way through

**Public advice calls for washing your hands each time you change ingredient.**
