# Q. What do people get wrong about expiry dates on cosmetics in Japan?｜Japan Questions #94

Visitors turn a Japanese product over looking for a date and often find only a code. The rule behind that has been in place since 1980.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a missing date means an oversight. Under Japan's pharmaceutical law, only cosmetics that may deteriorate within three years unopened must carry an expiry date.

## The rule

Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act requires an expiry date only on cosmetics that **may change in quality within three years of manufacture under appropriate storage.** A 1980 ministerial notice specifies three categories that must be dated:

1. Products containing **ascorbic acid** (vitamin C) or its esters or salts
2. Products containing **enzymes**
3. Any product **likely to change in character or quality within three years**

Everything else is exempt — on the basis that it will **keep its quality for at least three years unopened.** The batch (lot) number on the base is how the manufacturer traces it.

> **[Verify]** Rule as described by the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association FAQ and legal commentaries. Confirm before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜No date means it lasts forever

It means the product met a stability condition **unopened**. Industry guidance suggests using opened products within **several months to a year**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The code on the base is meaningless

That is the lot number. **It identifies when and where the batch was made.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜You can tell by looking

Separation, colour change and odour are useful signs — **but change is not always visible.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Refrigerating extends the life

Cooling a product not designed for it can change texture, and repeated temperature swings are worse than steady storage. **Follow the storage instructions on the pack.** Bathrooms are humid and unsuitable.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The rule is the same as at home

Other jurisdictions use period-after-opening symbols or mandatory dates. **This is a different system, not a missing one.**
