# Q. What do people get wrong about airing futons in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #95

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/futon-airing` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

On a clear day you will see bedding hanging from balconies across a whole street. What it does — and does not do — is measurable.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that sunlight kills dust mites. Bedding surfaces reach about 43°C in August sun; mites need 50°C for 20–30 minutes. Airing is about moisture, not sterilising.

## The numbers

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Temperature needed to kill house-dust mites | **50°C for 20–30 minutes** (faster at 60°C+) |
| Futon surface temperature in August sun | **about 43°C** (top), 36°C underneath |
| Humidity above which mites thrive | **60%** |
| Recommended airing window | **10:00–15:00**, when humidity is lowest |
| Airing time by material | Cotton 2–4 h; synthetic 1.5–3 h; down about 1 h |

**Airing lowers humidity below the level mites like. It does not cook them.** A futon dryer that reaches 50°C+ does the latter.

> **[Verify]** Figures from bedding-industry and weather-service sources. Confirm before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is about disinfecting in sunlight

See above. **The practical purpose is drying.** Bedding absorbs moisture every night; airing releases it.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Longer in the sun is better

Extended strong sun can degrade fabric and colour. **A few hours in the middle of the day is the guidance.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Beating it hard is part of the process

Vigorous beating damages the filling and **breaks up mite debris into finer airborne particles.** Light brushing of the surface is now the advice.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Any day will do

Airing on a humid day adds moisture instead of removing it. **The weather decides the day.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜It only applies to traditional futons

Mattresses, duvets and pillows all take on moisture. **Where balconies are unavailable, indoor racks and dryers do the same job.**
