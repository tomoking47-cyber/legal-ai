# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about what time to go to bed?｜Japan Questions #97

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/bedtime` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"What time should I be in bed by?" is a question asked the world over. Japan's national guidance puts the weight somewhere other than the clock time. Here is what the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's sleep guide actually says.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "going to bed at a particular time is what matters". The national guide gives sleep durations by age group and stresses regularity; given that the body clock runs on a cycle of about 24.2 hours, the time you get up is the real anchor.

## The numbers in the guide

"Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023" [健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023] (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, February 2024):

| Age group | Guide |
|---|---|
| Primary-school children | **9–12 hours** |
| Junior and senior high-school students | **8–10 hours** |
| Adults | **6 hours or more** |
| Older people | Avoid **spending 8 hours or more in bed** |

**It does not say what time to go to bed.** What it gives is the length of sleep and a regular daily rhythm.

The body clock runs on a cycle of about 24.2 hours and is reset by light each morning. **So what you should keep constant is the time you get up.**

> **[Verify]** The figures are from several documents presenting the 2023 Guide. **Check the original before publication.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Sleep is worthless unless you sleep during a particular window

The old explanation that "one particular window is special" **is not supported in that form.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The earlier you go to bed, the better

Going to bed early when you are not sleepy **can simply add to the time you lie awake.** For older people, not spending too long in bed is what is stressed.

## Misunderstanding 3｜As long as you keep your bedtime, getting-up time is free

**Getting-up time is what relates to the body clock.** The time you take in morning light is the time the clock is set.

## Misunderstanding 4｜At the weekend you can sleep in as late as you like

The bigger the gap from weekdays, the further the body clock drifts. **Keeping the gap small makes Monday easier.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜On a sleepless night you should stay in bed and try harder

Lying in bed for a long time unable to sleep is not recommended. **If it continues, see a doctor.**
