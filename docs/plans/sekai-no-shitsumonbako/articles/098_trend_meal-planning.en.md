# Q. In what order should I plan my meals (献立)?｜Japan Questions #98

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/meal-planning` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Planning a kondate (献立, the day's menu) looks like a matter of cooking skill. But Japan has had a national guide to "what, and how much" for twenty years. This article puts that guide beside a practical order for deciding what to cook.

## A. The basic order is "check what you have → main dish → staple and soup → adjust with side dishes". The national "Japanese Food Guide Spinning Top" sorts dishes into five groups and gives a daily guide for each.

## The Japanese Food Guide Spinning Top (食事バランスガイド)

Drawn up in 2005 (Heisei 17) **jointly by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries**. It sorts daily meals into **five groups — staple foods (主食: rice, bread, noodles), side dishes (副菜: vegetable dishes), main dishes (主菜: meat, fish, eggs, soy), milk and dairy, and fruit —** and gives a daily guide for each group in a unit called **"tsu (SV, serving)"**.

It is drawn as a spinning top (koma), carrying the message that **the top stays steady while it spins — that is, while you exercise**. The basic model assumes **2,200 ± 200 kcal**, a day's guide for adult men with low physical activity and adult women with ordinary or higher activity.

> **[Verify]** Check against the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries' Food Guide pages.

## The basic sequence

1. **Check the fridge and the store cupboard** (things you want to use up come first)
2. **Decide the main dish** (meat, fish, eggs, soy products)
3. **Decide the staple and the soup**
4. **Top up the vegetables with side dishes**
5. Batch any prep you can reuse across dishes

## Misunderstanding 1｜Decide the menu first, then shop

Decide first, and what is already at home gets left over. **Starting from your stock wastes less.** Household food waste in Japan runs at about 2.24 million tonnes a year (FY2024 estimate, see #88).

## Misunderstanding 2｜Every meal should be a different dish

Reusing the same prep is a common piece of household know-how. **It is not cutting corners.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more dishes, the better

It is not the number of dishes but **whether the five groups are covered.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Bulk buying works out cheaper

Not if you cannot use it all up. **Buying only what you can finish is the surer route.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜You cannot plan a menu without counting nutrients

**Putting together a staple, a main and a side** is enough as a starting shape. No calculation needed.
