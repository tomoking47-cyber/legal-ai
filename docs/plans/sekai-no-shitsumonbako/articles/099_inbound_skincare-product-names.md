# Q. What do people get wrong about skincare product names in Japan?｜Japan Questions #99

The English words on Japanese skincare are real English words. They are just not being used the way you expect — and the category behind them is regulated.

## A. The common misunderstanding is trusting the English on the front. "Lotion" is a watery liquid, "milk" is a light moisturiser, and neither may claim more than the law's list of 56 permitted effects.

## The names, decoded

| Japanese | English on label | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 化粧水 | **lotion** | Thin, watery liquid applied after cleansing | Adds water to the outer skin layer — **which then evaporates** |
| 乳液 | **milk** | Light moisturiser | Oil content forms a film that **slows water loss** (transepidermal water loss) |
| 美容液 | **serum / essence** | Concentrated step | Varies by product — **read the instructions** |

The sequence follows from the chemistry: water first, then oil to hold it. Neither may claim effects outside the **56 permitted claims** set by a 2011 Ministry of Health notice.

> **[Verify]** Notice reference 薬食発0721第1号. Confirm before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜"Lotion" means a creamy lotion

**It is not the thick lotion the word suggests in English.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜"Milk" is a cleanser

乳液 is a moisturiser. **A separate cleansing milk also exists — read the rest of the pack.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜"Essence" and "serum" are interchangeable

美容液 is labelled either way, and usage differs. **The instructions, not the word, tell you where it goes.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The order is obvious from the names

The names do not encode the sequence. **The pack states where the product belongs.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜These products "lock in" moisture

Water-based products add moisture; **oil-based products slow its loss.** Neither locks anything. The measurable quantity is transepidermal water loss, and it is the oil step that lowers it.
