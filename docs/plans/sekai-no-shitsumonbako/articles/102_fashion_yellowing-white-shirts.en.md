# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about yellowed white shirts?｜Japan Questions #102

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/yellowing-white-shirts` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Yellowed collars and cuffs on white shirts are a standard question on Q&A sites everywhere. In Japan, most households keep both "oxygen" and "chlorine" bleach on the shelf, and confusing the two is where many problems start. If you have just arrived and are staring at the bleach aisle in a drugstore, this article is for you.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "any bleach is the same". Chlorine and oxygen bleach are different chemicals: some fabrics cannot take chlorine bleach even when they are white, and mixing the two is dangerous.

## What the yellowing is, and the types of bleach

The main cause of yellowing is **oxidised skin oil**. Sebum that was not fully washed out turns yellow over time.

| Type | Active ingredient | Suitable fabrics | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine bleach (塩素系, enso-kei) | Sodium hypochlorite | White cotton, linen, polyester, etc. | **Not for wool, silk, nylon or coloured/patterned items. Mixing with anything acidic produces toxic gas** |
| Oxygen bleach, liquid (酸素系・液体) | Hydrogen peroxide | Most fibres, including coloured/patterned | Check the product label for wool and silk |
| Oxygen bleach, powder (酸素系・粉末) | Sodium percarbonate | Cotton, linen, synthetics | **Not for wool or silk** (alkaline) |

The **triangle symbol** on the care label shows whether bleach is allowed. There are three: a plain triangle (any bleach), a triangle with two diagonal lines (oxygen bleach only), and a crossed-out triangle (no bleach).

> **[Verify]** Check the bleach-symbol categories on the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page, and the "Do not mix — danger" (まぜるな危険) labelling under the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (miscellaneous industrial goods) at the Consumer Affairs Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The yellow is the colour of sweat

Sweat itself is almost colourless. **The colour is oxidised skin oil**, which is why it shows up more after a shirt has been stored than right after wearing. The basic rule is to wash before putting seasonal clothes away.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Chlorine is stronger, so it is the right choice

Stronger does not mean all-purpose. Chlorine bleach damages **wool, silk, nylon and polyurethane**, and strips colour from patterned items. Even on a white cotton shirt, **buttons and embroidery thread** can discolour.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The longer you soak, the better it works

Many oxygen-bleach products state a maximum soaking time on the label (for example 30 minutes to 2 hours). **Leaving items for hours damages the fabric and the colour.** Always follow the time on the product.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Any hot water will do

Powdered oxygen bleach is generally described as working better at **around 40–50°C (104–122°F)**, but **you cannot go above the number in the wash-tub symbol on the care label.** If the label says "30", the limit is 30°C.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Mixing chlorine and oxygen bleach makes them stronger

**As the "Do not mix — danger" label says, they must not be mixed.** Chlorine bleach releases toxic chlorine gas when it meets acidic cleaners, citric acid or vinegar. Never use both in the same container or the same soaking water.
