# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing bags?｜Japan Questions #104

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/storing-bags` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

How to store a leather bag is a question people ask in every country. In Japan there is an extra factor: homes here are humid, and the conditions inside your closet can decide how long a bag lasts. If you have moved here from a drier climate, the habits that worked at home may not work here.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "seal it in its box and a bag and it is safe". In a Japanese home, moisture builds up inside and speeds up mould and the breakdown of synthetic leather. Storage is about "keeping the shape and letting air through", not "sealing".

## Japanese homes and the conditions for mould

- Tokyo's **annual mean relative humidity is around 60–70%**, and in the rainy season (June–July) the **monthly mean reaches roughly 75%** (Japan Meteorological Agency, historical data).
- Mould is generally described as growing readily at **70% humidity or above and 20–30°C**, and the back of a closet tends to be more humid than the room.
- Synthetic leather (polyurethane) undergoes **hydrolysis**, a chemical change driven by moisture that makes the surface sticky and then peel. It is generally described as **starting within a few years of manufacture**, whether or not the bag is used.

| Storage point | Reason |
|---|---|
| Stuff the bag (e.g. paper wrapped in non-woven cloth) | Keeps the shape |
| Keep it in a non-woven (不織布, fushokufu) dust bag, never plastic | Lets air through |
| Store on a middle or upper shelf, not the bottom of the closet | Moisture collects low down |
| Take it out of the bag and air it once a month | Prevents damp and mould |
| Keep dehumidifier packs (calcium chloride, etc.) from touching the bag directly | Leaking liquid causes discolouration |

> **[Verify]** Check the humidity figures in the Japan Meteorological Agency's "Historical weather data search" (Tokyo, monthly normals). The timescale of hydrolysis varies by maker and material, so we have avoided a definite number of years.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The original box is the best place

Cardboard absorbs moisture and then releases it inside the closed box. **If you use the box, add a desiccant and open it regularly.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜A plastic bag keeps dust off

**Plastic traps moisture.** A breathable non-woven dust bag is the standard choice.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Synthetic leather is tougher than real leather

Synthetic leather (polyurethane) resists water, but **hydrolysis caused by humidity gives it a shorter life than real leather**. Dry storage does not stop the process, but it can slow it down.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Leather needs plenty of cream

Too much cream leaves the surface sticky and feeds mould. The basic method is **a thin layer, after a dry wipe, tested first on a hidden spot**. Cream cannot be used on suede or nubuck.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Add a moth repellent and you are covered

Some clothing moth repellents (pyrethroid types, for example) can **discolour synthetic leather, metal fittings and glitter**. Check the "do not use with" section on the product label.
