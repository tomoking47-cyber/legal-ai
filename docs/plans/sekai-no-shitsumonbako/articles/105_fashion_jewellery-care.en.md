# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about jewellery care?｜Japan Questions #105

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/jewellery-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"My silver has gone black" and "the gold plating has worn off" are questions asked the world over. In Japan, fineness marks (hallmarks from the Japan Mint) are common on precious-metal items, so you can usually check what you are holding before choosing how to care for it. This article explains how to read those marks and what each metal can take.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "all metal is polished the same way". Purity, plating and the presence of stones change which methods are safe. In particular, polishing cloths containing abrasive must not be used on plated pieces.

## First, check the material

Some precious-metal items sold in Japan carry a voluntary **fineness hallmark from the Japan Mint (造幣局)**. Examples of the stamps you may find:

| Stamp | Meaning |
|---|---|
| K18 / 750 | 75.0% gold |
| K10 / 417 | 41.7% gold |
| SV925 / 925 | 92.5% silver (sterling silver) |
| Pt900 / Pt950 | 90.0% / 95.0% platinum |
| GP / GF | Gold-plated / gold-filled (**a different metal underneath**) |

> **[Verify]** Check the fineness categories and stamps on the Japan Mint's "Hallmarking of precious-metal products" page.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Black silver is rust

The blackening is **sulphurisation** (a reaction with sulphur in the air or in sweat), not rust (oxidation). **It is a surface change only, so a silver-polishing cloth or a dedicated dip brings it back.** It happens fast in hot-spring (onsen) towns.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Toothpaste is fine for polishing

The abrasive is coarse: **plating wears off, and soft stones such as pearl, opal and turquoise get scratched.** Only solid silver (SV925) should be polished this way, and then with a dedicated silver cloth rather than an abrasive one.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Gold does not tarnish

Pure gold (K24) hardly changes colour, but **K18 and K10 contain alloying metals (silver, copper) and can discolour from sweat and cosmetics.** Wiping with a soft cloth after wear is the basic habit.

## Misunderstanding 4｜An ultrasonic cleaner can clean anything

Ultrasonic cleaning is **not suitable for pearls, emeralds, opals or glued-in stones**. Stones can loosen or crack. Use it only for hard stones such as diamond and sapphire that are securely held in prong settings.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is fine to wear jewellery in the bath or an onsen

**The sulphur in hot-spring water blackens silver quickly, and chlorine (pools, bleach) damages gold alloys and plating.** Taking jewellery off before bathing, swimming and housework is a habit widely shared among people living in Japan.
