# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about looking after a watch?｜Japan Questions #106

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/watch-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

How to read the water-resistance marking on a wristwatch is one of the most misunderstood topics on Q&A sites in every country. Japan has its own industrial standard (JIS) that defines the water-resistance classes, and what "◯-bar water resistant" means is set out there. If you bought a watch in Japan, this is the system the label follows.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "if it says water resistant, I can take it into the water". "Water resistant for daily life (3 bar)" is meant for hand-washing and rain, not for swimming or bathing.

## Water-resistance classes (a general summary based on JIS B7021)

| Marking | Intended use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Water resistant for daily life (3 bar / 30 m) | Hand-washing, rain | Swimming, bathing, washing the car |
| Enhanced water resistance for daily life (5 bar) | Wet work, shallow swimming | Diving in, underwater swimming |
| Enhanced water resistance for daily life (10–20 bar) | Swimming, skin-diving | Scuba diving |
| Diver's watch | Scuba diving | Diving deeper than the marked depth |

"30 m water resistant" does **not** mean you can dive to 30 m. **It is the value from a static pressure test**; when you move your arm, the local pressure exceeds it.

> **[Verify]** Check the classes and their intended uses against the Japan Clock & Watch Association's "About water resistance in watches" and the text of JIS B7021.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A water-resistant watch is fine in the bath or an onsen

**Changes in temperature make the gaskets expand and contract, and steam or soap can get inside.** Many makers advise against wearing a watch while bathing or in a sauna.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Water resistance lasts forever

What keeps water out is a **rubber gasket**, and rubber degrades with age. The usual advice is to **have it replaced at battery changes or at a check-up every 2–3 years**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜If it gets wet, dry it with a hairdryer

Heat harms the inside of the watch. The basic rule: **wipe with a dry, soft cloth, and if you see moisture inside the glass, take it to a watch shop promptly**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Magnetism is an old-fashioned worry

**Magnets in everyday objects are on the increase**: smartphone and tablet covers, magnetic bag clasps, headphones. Both mechanical and quartz watches are affected, and magnetism can make them gain, lose or stop. The Japan Clock & Watch Association advises **keeping watches at least 5 cm from magnetic products**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜No need to wipe off sweat

The salt in sweat **causes rust and can cause metal allergy**. **Wiping the case back and strap with a dry cloth when you get home** is a habit shared among people living in Japan.
