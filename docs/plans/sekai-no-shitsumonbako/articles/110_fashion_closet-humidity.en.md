# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about closet humidity?｜Japan Questions #110

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/closet-humidity` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Mould and musty smells in the closet are a shared problem in every humid region. In Japan, some summer months have a mean humidity above 75%, and generations of households have built up practical know-how for keeping stored clothes dry. If you have come from a drier climate, the products in the drugstore (dehumidifier packs, slatted boards) may be unfamiliar. Here is how people here use them.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "put in a dehumidifier pack and the problem is solved". A pack only absorbs moisture from a limited space; unless you stop the supply of moisture (damp laundry, outside air, the floor), it cannot keep up. The foundations are "dry before storing", "leave gaps" and "open the doors regularly".

## The humidity numbers

- Tokyo's monthly mean relative humidity is **around 75% in June, July and September**, and **in the 50s** in winter (Japan Meteorological Agency normals).
- Mould is generally described as growing readily when three conditions coincide: **humidity of 70% or more, a temperature of 20–30°C, and food (dust, skin oil)**. A closet easily meets all three.
- Calcium-chloride dehumidifier packs (除湿剤, joshitsuzai) state on the label **how much moisture each unit absorbs (for example, roughly 400–800 mL)** and how long it lasts.

| Measure | Reason |
|---|---|
| Store laundry only once completely dry | Stops moisture being carried in |
| Leave a palm's width between garments | Lets air move |
| Once a week, open the doors to air the closet (not on rainy days) | Lets moisture escape |
| Dehumidifier packs low down, moth repellent high up | Moisture sinks; repellent vapour spreads from top to bottom |
| Do not put clothes straight back after wearing; hang them overnight first | Lets sweat moisture evaporate |

> **[Verify]** Check the humidity normals in the Japan Meteorological Agency's "Historical weather data search", and the conditions for mould growth in Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Public Health material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜More dehumidifier packs means more effect

Packs **work only in a space that is close to sealed.** If there is a wide gap around the doors, they keep absorbing moisture from the room air and fill up quickly.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A fan or air circulator is pointless

**Moving air alone reduces the amount of mould that settles.** Opening the closet doors and running a fan during the rainy season is a method widely shared among people living in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A slatted board does nothing for floor moisture

Moisture collects under storage boxes that sit directly on the floor. **Raising them a few centimetres on a slatted board (すのこ, sunoko) lets air pass underneath.** It works in rented flats too.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Wiping removes mould

Wiping the surface **leaves the roots (hyphae) behind.** For mould on clothing, wash according to the care label and put the garment in sunlight. For mould on walls, **use rubbing alcohol (消毒用エタノール) or a chlorine mould remover — one or the other, never both — and keep the room ventilated.** **Never mix chlorine products with acidic cleaners.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Only the rainy season needs attention

**Winter condensation** is also a source of moisture. A closet on an outside wall gets condensation on the inside, because heating keeps the room warm while the wall stays cold. Ventilation is needed in winter too.

### About this article
