# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about caring for a down duvet?｜Japan Questions #112

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/duvet-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Can you wash a down duvet (羽毛布団, umō futon) at home, and how should you air it? People ask this everywhere. In Japan, hanging bedding outdoors in the sun (布団を干す) is a deep-rooted habit, and the arrival of down duvets has muddled the old rules. If you live here you will see futons draped over balconies; this article explains why a down duvet needs different handling.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "handle a down duvet like a cotton-wadded futon: put it in the sun and beat it". Beating breaks the down, and ultraviolet light degrades the shell fabric. The basics are "let air through" and "follow the label".

## What the label tells you

- Down duvets fall under the **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (Textile Goods Quality Labelling Regulations)**, which requires the label to state the **fibre composition of the face fabric, backing fabric and filling**, the **home laundering and care instructions (care symbols)**, and the **name and contact details of the labelling party**. Fill weight is not a statutory labelling item; it is shown under industry voluntary standards such as the Japan Down Products Cooperative Association's Gold Label.
- A figure such as "Down 90%" is the **ratio of down (the soft breast plumage) to feathers**; **the higher the down ratio, the lighter and warmer** the duvet tends to be.
- If the care label (Japan uses the international JIS L0001 symbols) shows a **wash-tub symbol, it can be washed at home**, but because of its size you need a machine with a **large enough capacity (e.g. 7–8 kg or more)** as a rough guide.

| Care | Guide | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Airing | 1–2 times a month, in shade or briefly (about 1 hour per side), with the cover still on | Prevents UV damage to the shell fabric |
| Beating | Don't. At most, smooth the surface by hand | Breaks the down; damages the shell |
| Washing | Follow the label; about once a year or when soiled | Avoids stripping too much of the down's natural oils |
| Drying | Completely. Check the label before using a tumble dryer | Moisture inside causes odour and mould |
| Storage | In a breathable bag, not tightly compressed | Keeps the down clusters' ability to recover their loft |

> **[Verify]** Check the mandatory label items against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Textile Goods Quality Labelling Regulations". Airing times and frequency are based on material from bodies such as the Japan Down Products Cooperative Association and are not definitive figures.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The longer it sits in full sun, the better

The down only needs **enough time to release its moisture**; **long exposure to direct sunlight weakens the shell fabric.** The Japan Down Products Cooperative Association advises **a well-ventilated shady spot**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Beating it with a futon beater makes it fluffier

Beating **breaks the down and can make it leak through the shell**. Refresh the air inside by **shaking it lightly with both hands or smoothing it**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dry cleaning is the safe option

Dry-cleaning solvents can **strip too much of the down's natural oils**; down is generally described as suited to **washing in water**. Even when the label carries a dry-clean symbol, many people ask a cleaner that offers wet cleaning.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Vacuum storage bags save space with no downside

Strong compression over a long period **damages the down's ability to recover its loft.** Keep vacuum bags for short moves; for storage, the basic rule is **fold loosely in a breathable bag**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A smell means poor quality

Down is an animal product, and **it can give off a distinctive smell when it absorbs moisture.** Airing and drying usually reduces it; if it stays strong, consider washing or asking a specialist cleaner.
