# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about washing a down jacket?｜Japan Questions #113

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-down-jackets` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Whether you can wash a down jacket at home comes up every winter on Q&A sites in cold countries. In Japan too, changes to care labels and larger washing machines mean more people now wash theirs at home. This article sets out what to check first and where the process usually goes wrong.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "down can never be washed". If the care label shows a wash-tub symbol, you can wash it at home. Most failures happen not in the wash but because "the jacket was not dried thoroughly and the down clumped".

## Three things to check before washing

1. **The care label** (Japan uses the international JIS L0001 symbols: is there a wash-tub symbol, what temperature, how many bars underneath)
2. **The shell and outer fabric** (nylon and polyester wash easily in water; **if the outer is real leather or a wool blend, home washing is often ruled out**)
3. **Attachments** (remove any fur trim, close the zips, empty the pockets)

| Step | Guide |
|---|---|
| Detergent | Neutral detergent for delicates (おしゃれ着用洗剤, oshareki-yō senzai). No bleach, no fabric softener |
| Water temperature | At or below the number on the label (usually 30°C / 86°F) |
| Washing | Press-wash by hand, or a gentle machine cycle in a mesh laundry bag |
| Spin | Short. A long spin shifts the down into lumps |
| Drying | **Lay flat → reshape and dry in the shade, and break up the clumps by hand as it dries.** Drying completely can take **2–3 days** |

> **[Verify]** Check the meaning of the symbols against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page. The drying time is a guide only and depends on conditions.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Once washed, the loft never comes back

Clumped down is the result of **incomplete drying**. **Dry it completely and break up the clumps repeatedly while it dries**, and in most cases the loft returns. If the label shows a **tumble-dry (low heat)** symbol, a tumble dryer works well.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Fabric softener makes it fluffy

Fabric softener can **coat the surface of the down and make it clump more.** Do not use it on down.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dry cleaning is the safe option

Down is generally described as suited to **washing in water**; dry-cleaning solvents can strip too much of its natural oils. Even when the label shows a P or F in a circle, many people ask a cleaner that offers wet cleaning.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Spot-clean a stain by dabbing hard at it

The shell fabric of a down jacket is thin; **rubbing damages it and the down leaks out.** The basic method is to dilute neutral detergent and **press** it on with a sponge.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Straight into the wardrobe after washing

**Moisture left inside causes odour and mould.** Until the jacket **no longer feels cold to the touch** it is not dry, and many people leave it a further day after that.
