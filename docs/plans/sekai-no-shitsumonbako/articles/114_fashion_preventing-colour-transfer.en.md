# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about colour running in the wash?｜Japan Questions #114

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/preventing-colour-transfer` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"My white shirt came out pink" is the world's most common laundry story. In Japan, the change to the care-label system has put "machine washable" on more garments, and attention to colour transfer has slipped as a result. This article covers how dye moves between clothes and the everyday habits Japanese households use to stop it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "colour only runs when a garment is new". Transfer happens whenever a wet garment lies against something coloured, however many times it has been washed. The basics of prevention are "separate", "hang up straight away" and "check for colourfastness".

## When colour transfers

- **Dye dissolves into the water** (in the wash liquor) → and settles on other garments
- **Wet garments touch** (left in the drum after the cycle, stacked after spinning) → dye moves directly
- **The hotter the water**, the more readily dye dissolves

| Prevention | How |
|---|---|
| Colourfastness test | Wet an inconspicuous spot, press a white cloth on it and see whether any colour comes off |
| Wash separately | Whites apart from darks. **Wash new dark garments alone for the first few washes** |
| Lower the temperature | As low as the label allows |
| Don't leave it in the machine | Hang up as soon as the cycle ends. **Leaving wet clothes stacked together is the most dangerous moment** |
| Colour-catcher sheets | A shop-bought sheet (色移り防止シート, iroutsuri bōshi shīto) that absorbs dye in the wash water. Use as a back-up |

**If the wash-tub symbol on the care label (Japan uses the international JIS L0001 symbols) has bars underneath, a gentler machine setting is called for.** Respecting the wash intensity also helps limit dye loss.

> **[Verify]** Check the meaning of the symbols against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page.

## Misunderstanding 1｜One wash and a new garment is safe

Dark colours (black, navy, red, denim) **keep releasing a little dye however many times they are washed.** The basic rule is never to wash them with whites.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Bleach will restore a garment that has picked up colour

**Chlorine bleach cannot be used on coloured or patterned garments.** Oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) sometimes brings them back, but check the fabric and the label (triangle symbol); acting **before the garment dries** is generally described as giving a better chance.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Extra rinses will prevent it

Rinsing only reduces dye in the wash water; it does nothing against **transfer by contact after spinning**. **Hanging up straight away** matters more.

## Misunderstanding 4｜With a colour-catcher sheet you can mix everything

The sheet is a **back-up**. There is no assurance it will catch everything, and it does not replace the basic rule of separating whites and darks.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Hand-washing never transfers colour

Hand-washing transfers colour too if you **soak** the garment in the same water as others. The basics are **alone** and **briefly**.
