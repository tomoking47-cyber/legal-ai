# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how often to shampoo?｜Japan Questions #115

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/how-often-to-shampoo` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Should I shampoo every day?" is argued over more in Western communities than almost any other hair question. In Japan a daily bath is the norm, so daily shampooing tends to be taken for granted — but the basis for that is habit, not medicine. If you have moved here, you may be wondering whether to follow the local pattern.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "there is a correct number of times". The right frequency depends on how oily your scalp is, your hair type, how much you exercise and the season. Daily washing is common in Japan because of the humidity and the bathing habit, not because it is a global standard.

## What to weigh up

- The scalp is generally described as producing **more sebum than the T-zone of the face**; leftover sebum and sweat can cause **odour and blocked pores**.
- On the other hand, **over-washing can lead to a dry, itchy scalp**.
- People in Japan bathe **almost daily** (according to various time-use surveys), and shampooing follows suit.

| Situation | Common guide |
|---|---|
| Oily scalp, sweated, used styling product | Wash the same day |
| Prone to dryness, winter, older age | Some people cut back to every other day |
| Persistent redness, itching or dandruff | Not a question of frequency — see a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance) |

Shampoo is sold either as a **cosmetic** or as a **quasi-drug (医薬部外品, a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines; sold as "medicated")**. The claims a cosmetic may make are limited to the **56 items set by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare** (e.g. "cleanses the scalp and hair", "controls dandruff and itching").

> **[Verify]** Check the 56 items against "Revision of the scope of cosmetic efficacy claims" (MHLW notice Yakushoku-hatsu 0721 No. 1, 21 July 2011).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Daily washing thins your hair

**We can find no official evidence that shampooing increases the number of hairs shed.** Hairs that come out while washing were already at the shedding stage of their cycle.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Not washing is better for your hair ("no-poo")

Leftover sebum causes **odour and blocked pores**. Adjusting the amount — for instance **rinsing with warm water only on some days** — is one thing, but "never washing" calls for caution on hygiene grounds.

## Misunderstanding 3｜More lather means a better wash

Lather is there **to reduce friction**; volume and cleaning power are separate things. The basic technique is to **massage the scalp with the pads of your fingers and not scrub the ends**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hot water strips sebum better

Hot water **removes the sebum you need and leads to dryness**. **Around 38–40°C (100–104°F)** is the usual guide.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Rinsing is done when the foam is gone

Residue **causes itching and dandruff**. A guide shared among people in Japan is to **spend about twice as long rinsing as washing**.
