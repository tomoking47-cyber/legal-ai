# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how to wear perfume?｜Japan Questions #116

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/how-to-wear-perfume` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Where to apply perfume, and how much, is a frequent question in communities everywhere. In Japan there is a cultural leaning towards "keep fragrance subtle", and the effect of scent on the people around you is the subject of official awareness campaigns. This article covers the basics and the local etiquette a newcomer should know.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the more you put on, the longer it lasts". How long a scent lasts is decided by its concentration (the fragrance-oil percentage); adding more only increases the effect on the people around you. In 2021, five Japanese ministries and agencies jointly called for consideration over "fragrance".

## Concentration categories (a general guide)

| Name | Typical fragrance-oil % | Typical lasting time |
|---|---|---|
| Parfum | 15–30% | 5–7 hours |
| Eau de parfum | 10–15% | around 5 hours |
| Eau de toilette | 5–10% | 3–4 hours |
| Eau de cologne | 1–5% | 1–2 hours |

The categories are not legal definitions and **vary between makers and countries.** In Japan perfume is sold as a **cosmetic**, and full ingredient labelling is compulsory.

> **[Verify]** The concentration bands are a guide based on industry material such as the Japan Fragrance Association's. They are not official definitions, so do not present them as firm figures.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Spray on the wrists and rub them together

Rubbing **changes how the scent develops through heat and friction, and the top notes evaporate faster.** Once applied, leave it alone and let it dry naturally.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It lasts longer on clothes

Perfume **can stain silk and pale fabrics.** The basic rule is **apply to skin**; if you want it on clothing, test the lining or a hidden spot first.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The neck and behind the ears are the best spots

Warm areas project scent well, but **it reaches anyone talking to you at close range strongly.** In Japan a widely shared method is to apply to the lower body — **waist, backs of the knees, ankles** — so the scent rises gently.

## Misunderstanding 4｜How much you wear is a matter of taste

It is officially recognised that **some people become unwell from fragrance**; in 2021 five bodies — the Consumer Affairs Agency, the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and the Ministry of the Environment — issued a joint poster. Be **especially restrained in shared spaces** such as workplaces, schools, medical facilities and trains.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Perfume is fine in sunlight

**The combination of fragrance ingredients and ultraviolet light can cause dark spots or irritation** on the skin (phototoxicity). If you apply it to skin during the day, the cautious choice is **somewhere covered by clothing**, or to avoid direct sun. **Once opened, store it away from direct sunlight.**
