# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about chapped hands and hand cream?｜Japan Questions #117

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/hand-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Rough, chapped hands are a shared winter complaint, and "which cream works?" comes up again and again on Q&A sites in every country. In Japan, what a hand cream sold as a cosmetic may claim is fixed by law, and words that promise to make a condition disappear are not among the permitted words. This article explains what cream can and cannot do, and the habits Japanese households rely on.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "pick a good cream and chapped hands are solved". The main cause is water, detergent and alcohol stripping the skin's oil film; cream is there to "protect". Cracked or bleeding skin is beyond what cream is for — it is a matter for a dermatology clinic.

## Background

- Chapped hands include the condition called **hand eczema**, and the Japanese Dermatological Association has published **"Clinical Guidelines for Hand Eczema" (2018)**. **Wet work, detergents and alcohol sanitiser** are listed as the main irritants.
- Several academic-society and government documents note that **hand-sanitiser use rose sharply during the COVID-19 pandemic**, and with it enquiries about chapped hands.
- Hand cream is a **cosmetic** (some are quasi-drugs — 医薬部外品, a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines). As a cosmetic it may only claim what falls within the **56 items**, such as **"moisturises the skin", "protects the skin" and "prevents dry skin"**.

| Situation | Everyday practice in Japan |
|---|---|
| Wet work | Rubber gloves over cotton gloves. **Lukewarm** water rather than hot |
| After washing or sanitising | **Dry your hands, then apply cream straight away** |
| At bedtime | Apply cream generously and cover with cotton gloves |
| Washing up | Dilute the detergent, or use a dishwasher |

> **[Verify]** Check the guideline's year and content on the Japanese Dermatological Association website, and the 56 items against MHLW notice Yakushoku-hatsu 0721 No. 1.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Apply cream once your hands are already chapped

Cream **stands in for the skin's oil film and slows the loss of moisture**. The basic habit is to apply it **before the skin roughens, every time you wash your hands**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The more expensive the cream, the better it works

A hand cream sold as a cosmetic **may differ in ingredients, but it cannot claim to heal, and you should not expect it to.** Choose one you can afford to keep using and whose feel you like; **if you have symptoms, a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance)** is the sensible route.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Alcohol sanitiser replaces hand-washing

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare regards **washing with soap under running water as the basic method**, with alcohol as the fallback when you cannot wash. **Doing both every time doubles the irritation**, so choose according to the situation.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Rubber gloves mean no chapping

**Sweat and humidity inside** rubber gloves are irritants too. **Wearing cotton gloves underneath and avoiding long stretches of wear** are the everyday Japanese practices. Anyone with a rubber (latex) allergy chooses nitrile.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Wait and see with cracks, using shop-bought cream

**Bleeding, severe itching or blisters** are beyond the scope of a cosmetic. **See a dermatology clinic.**
