# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about antiperspirants?｜Japan Questions #118

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/antiperspirants` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The difference between an antiperspirant and a deodorant is one of the most-asked questions on overseas Q&A sites. In Japan the two fall into separate legal categories — quasi-drug and cosmetic — and you can tell them apart from the packaging. This article explains how to read the label at the drugstore (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.) and what the common myths get wrong.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "antiperspirant and deodorant are the same thing". In Japan, a product containing a sweat-reducing (antiperspirant) ingredient is a quasi-drug (医薬部外品, a category between cosmetics and medicines); one that only controls odour is a cosmetic. Look for the words 医薬部外品 (iyakubugaihin) on the package.

## The categories in Japan

| Category | Label | What it may claim | Typical ingredients |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quasi-drug** (医薬部外品, "medicated") | "医薬部外品" printed on the package | May claim to **reduce sweat and prevent odour** | Antiperspirant agents (e.g. aluminium chlorohydrate), antibacterial agents |
| **Cosmetic** | No category shown, or "化粧品" | Within the 56 items, e.g. **cleanses the skin, masks odour with fragrance** | Fragrance, absorbent powders, etc. |

A quasi-drug contains an active ingredient **approved by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare**, and **"prevention of underarm odour (wakiga)" and "antiperspirant"** are recognised claims. A cosmetic may not make these claims.

> **[Verify]** Check the categories and the scope of claims against the MHLW's "Scope of efficacy claims for quasi-drugs" and notice Yakushoku-hatsu 0721 No. 1.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Stopping sweat is bad for you

Antiperspirant agents **temporarily block the openings of the sweat glands**; used on limited areas, the effect on temperature regulation is generally described as small. They are, however, **not meant for use over the whole body.** If you are concerned, ask a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).

## Misunderstanding 2｜Aluminium causes health damage

On the link between antiperspirant aluminium compounds and breast cancer or dementia, **several bodies including the American Cancer Society (ACS) state that "no clear evidence has been established"**. The ingredients are approved actives in Japan too. Anyone still uneasy has the option of choosing a product in the cosmetic category.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Apply in the morning before you go out

Antiperspirant agents **act on the sweat glands when you are sweating little**. Applying **at night, to dry skin after a bath**, is generally described as working better, and "night application" is shared practice among people in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 4｜More is more effective

**Over-spraying can cause cold injury (frostbite) to the skin** through cooling, and the Consumer Affairs Agency has issued warnings about it. **Do not spray the same spot continuously for more than a few seconds.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Fine to use straight after hair removal

**Skin just shaved or depilated is sensitive to irritation**, and the product may sting or cause redness. **Leave a gap** to be safe.
