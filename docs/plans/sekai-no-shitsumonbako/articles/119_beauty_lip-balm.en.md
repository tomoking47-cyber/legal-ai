# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about lip balm?｜Japan Questions #119

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/lip-balm` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The idea that "lip balm is addictive" has circulated on overseas forums for years. In Japan, lip balm (リップクリーム, rippu kurīmu) comes in three legal categories — cosmetic, quasi-drug and medicine — and the label tells you what each is for. This article explains how to read them and what the "addiction" story gets wrong.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "lip balm is addictive". No evidence of dependence has been confirmed. The usual reasons lips feel "drier the more you apply" are a licking habit, irritating ingredients, and rubbing every time you reapply. The practical approach is to choose by category (cosmetic / quasi-drug / medicine).

## The three categories

| Category | Label | Purpose | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic | No category shown | **Moisturises, prevents dryness, protects the lips** | Everyday protection |
| Quasi-drug (医薬部外品, a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines) | "医薬部外品" | An active ingredient **helps prevent chapping** | Seasons when your lips chap easily |
| Medicine (Class 3 OTC, etc.) | "第3類医薬品" etc. | **For cracked, peeling lips** | **When you have symptoms.** Follow the dosage and directions |

In Japan, an **SPF figure** on lip balm is permitted for a cosmetic. Lips have no sebaceous glands and are **easily affected by ultraviolet light**.

> **[Verify]** Check the scope of claims for each category against the MHLW's 56 cosmetic items and the scope of efficacy claims for quasi-drugs.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The more you apply, the drier your lips get

Lips **have no sebaceous glands and a thin outer layer**, so they dry out easily. If they feel dry after applying balm, the cause is usually **irritating ingredients such as menthol**, **a licking habit**, or **friction while applying**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Press hard and go side to side for better coverage

Applying **lightly, up and down**, along the vertical lines of the lips causes less friction; this is widely shared among people in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Medicine-class lip balm is best used all the time

A medicine is for **when you have symptoms, within the stated dosage and directions**. The usual approach is to **switch back to the cosmetic category once the symptoms have settled**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Lips don't need sunscreen

Lips are prone to **drying and peeling from UV exposure**. Using a **lip balm with an SPF figure** in the daytime is sensible.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once opened, it keeps for years

Lip balm **touches the lips directly and picks up the bacteria that live in the mouth**. Many people **use it up within one season to a year of opening**, and stop using it if there is **discolouration, a change in smell, or separation**.
