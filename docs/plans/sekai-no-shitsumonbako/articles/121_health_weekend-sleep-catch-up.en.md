# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about catching up on sleep at the weekend?｜Japan Questions #121

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/weekend-sleep-catch-up` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Sleeping in at the weekend to make up for a short working week is a recurring topic on Q&A sites in every country. Japan is one of the shortest-sleeping countries in international comparisons, so interest in "stocking up" on sleep is high. If you live here you will hear the word for it: *nedame* (寝だめ). Here is what Japan's official sleep guidance actually says.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is that a long sleep at the weekend wipes out the weekday deficit. The sleep guide of Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) explains that shifting your weekend wake-up time far from your weekday time disrupts the body clock. This is called social jet lag. The rule of thumb is to keep the shift within 2 hours.

## In numbers

- The **MHLW "Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023" (健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023)** gives adults a benchmark of **6 hours or more** and recommends **not shifting your weekend wake-up time far from weekdays**. A large gap between the mid-point of weekday sleep and weekend sleep is called **social jet lag**.
- In the OECD time-use data (2021), Japan's average sleep time was **7 hours 22 minutes**, the **shortest** among the countries covered.
- In the National Health and Nutrition Survey (国民健康・栄養調査, Japan's annual government health survey), the share of adults **averaging under 6 hours of sleep a day** has stayed at **around 40%**.

| Weekday sleep | What to do at the weekend (spirit of the guide) |
|---|---|
| Under 6 hours, night after night | Look for ways to lengthen weekday sleep. Catching up at the weekend only goes so far |
| Getting up 2 hours or more later than on weekdays | Social jet lag. Leads to Monday sleepiness and sluggishness |
| Topping up with a nap | Early afternoon, **30 minutes or less** as a guide |

> **[Verify]** Check the wording of the Sleep Guide 2023 and the latest year's percentage in the National Health and Nutrition Survey. The OECD figure is the time-use data in the "Gender Data Portal 2021".

## Misunderstanding 1｜You can bank sleep

You can partly "repay" a deficit afterwards, but **sleeping extra in advance is generally described as almost impossible.** The basis is to get the **right amount every day**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sleeping until noon at the weekend is rest

If you get up late, **the time you first see morning light is also late, and the body clock drifts later.** That is a common reason for not being able to sleep on Sunday night and struggling on Monday.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Going to bed earlier is the same thing

The body clock is generally described as being set mainly by **wake-up time and morning light**. **Fix your wake-up time, and go to bed earlier if you are sleepy.** That follows the guide's approach more closely.

## Misunderstanding 4｜If you cannot catch up at the weekend, there is nothing you can do

The guide mentions **a nap of 30 minutes or less in the early afternoon** as a way to deal with sleepiness. Long naps, or naps after late afternoon, interfere with night-time sleep.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Japanese people sleep less because of their constitution

International differences in sleep time are generally attributed largely to **working hours, commuting time and daily habits**. Seeing it as **a matter of environment, not constitution**, makes the solutions easier to see.
