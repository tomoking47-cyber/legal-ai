# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about eating before bed?｜Japan Questions #122

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/eating-before-bed` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Does eating before bed make you fat?" and "How late is too late for a snack?" are questions asked everywhere. In Japan, the sleep guide of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) touches on eating before bed, and frames it in terms of sleep quality. Here is how the Japanese guidance and everyday practice fit together.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is that the clock time itself is what makes you gain weight. The main problems with a late meal are that total intake tends to rise, and that sleep quality drops. The rule of thumb is to finish eating 2–3 hours before bed. On days when dinner is late, adjust the amount and the content.

## What to base the decision on

- The **MHLW "Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023" (健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023)** states that **a meal just before bed may lower sleep quality**, and recommends regular meal times.
- The **"Japanese Food Guide Spinning Top" (食事バランスガイド, issued by MHLW and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries)** gives **daily benchmark amounts for staples, main dishes and side dishes**. A late-night snack counts within those amounts.
- The National Health and Nutrition Survey (国民健康・栄養調査, Japan's annual government health survey) shows that the share of people who **start dinner at 21:00 or later** tends to be high among men in their 20s to 40s.

| Time until bed | Guide |
|---|---|
| 3 hours or more | A normal dinner is fine |
| 2–3 hours | Avoid fatty fried food and large portions |
| 1–2 hours | A small amount of something easy to digest (soup, tofu, rice porridge etc.) |
| Under 1 hour | Avoid solid food; one option is to leave it until the next morning |

## Misunderstanding 1｜Eating late always makes you fat

Body weight is decided by **the difference between what you take in and what you use over the whole day**. The problems with a late meal are that **you are very hungry, so the amount tends to grow**, and that **you burn little before bed**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Eating something before bed helps you fall asleep

With food in the stomach, **the body keeps working on digestion, cannot fully rest, and sleep may be lighter.** If hunger keeps you awake, limit yourself to **a small warm drink or something easy to digest**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Skipping dinner is the healthiest option

Skipping dinner is generally described as leading to **overeating the next morning or hunger in the middle of the night**. A practice among people living in Japan is to **split the meal**: something light in the early evening, and something light again after getting home.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Alcohol helps you fall asleep, so it can stand in for a late snack

The sleep guide explains that **alcohol may help you fall asleep faster, but makes the second half of the night lighter**. Drinking before bed is not recommended on sleep-quality grounds.

## Misunderstanding 5｜For a late snack, fruit is always better than sweets

Fruit contains sugar. **A large amount is no different.** The point is **"how much"**, not "what".
