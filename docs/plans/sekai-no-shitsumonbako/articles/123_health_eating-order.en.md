# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about the order in which you eat?｜Japan Questions #123

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/eating-order` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Vegetables first" is a way of eating that spread in Japan, and expat communities often ask whether it really means anything. If you eat with Japanese colleagues you may notice them starting with the salad or the soup. Here is how the practice compares with Japan's official dietary guidance.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is that as long as you keep to the order, you can eat anything. Eating order is known as a supporting technique that helps prevent overeating and softens the spike in blood sugar after a meal. If the amount and the content are not right, it loses most of its point.

## Where the official guidance stands

- The **"Japanese Food Guide Spinning Top" (食事バランスガイド)**, issued by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, shows **the balance of amounts between staples, side dishes, main dishes, milk and dairy, and fruit**. It does not prescribe an eating order.
- **Health Japan 21 (third term)** (健康日本21（第三次）, the national health-promotion plan) sets a target of **350 g of vegetables a day** and **7 g of salt a day**.
- "Vegetables first" appears in many municipal health-promotion leaflets as a technique that leads to **chewing well and eating slowly**, and **makes it easier to feel full**.

| Order technique | Expected benefit | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Start with vegetables and soup | More chewing, less overeating | Little effect if the vegetable portion is small |
| Main dish (meat or fish) next | Protein gives a sense of satisfaction | Watch the total if the main dish is fatty |
| Staple (rice or bread) last | The staple portion shrinks naturally | Cutting too far does not match your activity level |

> **[Verify]** Check the Health Japan 21 (third term) target values, and the latest year's vegetable intake in the National Health and Nutrition Survey (the adult average is **about 260–280 g**, below target).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Eating in the right order solves blood-sugar problems

Eating order is a **supporting** technique. Blood sugar after meals varies greatly between individuals. **If you are concerned, this is a matter for a doctor**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Any vegetable will do

**Potatoes and other tubers, pumpkin and sweetcorn** are high in carbohydrate, and the guidance sometimes excludes them from the vegetable count. The usual practice is to start with **leafy greens, mushrooms and seaweed**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜If rice comes last, you need not think about how much

The staple should be **an amount that matches your activity level**. Cutting it too far can lead to more snacking.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Eating order is a Japanese superstition

"Fibre first" has also been studied overseas. **However, almost no country writes an eating order into its official dietary guidelines. It is positioned as a supporting technique.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Eating fast is fine as long as the order is right

Most of the benefit of "vegetables first" comes from **chewing more and taking longer over the meal**. Many municipal leaflets give a benchmark of **chewing each mouthful 20–30 times**.
