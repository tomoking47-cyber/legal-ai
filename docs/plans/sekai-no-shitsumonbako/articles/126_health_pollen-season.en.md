# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about getting through pollen season?｜Japan Questions #126

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/pollen-season` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Hay fever exists all over the world, but Japanese cedar (sugi) pollen is unusual in both volume and duration. Many foreign residents develop symptoms for the first time after a few springs here. In 2023 the government adopted an overall pollen-allergy strategy, covering pollen forecasts and the cedar forests that produce it. Here is what the public guidance says and what people in Japan actually do.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is that pollen is only an outdoor problem. Pollen rides indoors on clothes and hair and settles on the floor. The approach of people living in Japan centres on "don't bring it in, don't stir it up indoors", and this matches the thinking in the official material.

## Numbers and policy

- A Ministry of the Environment survey puts the **prevalence of cedar pollen allergy at about 40%** (38.8% in the 2019 nationwide survey for the Japanese Guidelines for Allergic Rhinitis).
- **In May 2023 the government adopted the "Overall Picture of Pollen Allergy Countermeasures"** (花粉症対策の全体像). Its three pillars are **faster felling and replanting of planted cedar forests (a 20% cut in pollen sources within 10 years)**, **more accurate pollen forecasts**, and **wider uptake of therapies**.
- Cedar pollen in the Kanto region (Tokyo area) flies from **early February to April**; cypress (hinoki) from **March to May**.

| Situation | What people in Japan do | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Going out | Mask and glasses. Avoid the heaviest periods, **around midday and around sunset** | Ministry of the Environment, "Pollen Allergy Environmental Health Manual" |
| Coming home | **Brush off clothes at the entrance; do not bring your coat into the living space** | Same |
| Laundry and bedding | On high-pollen days, **dry laundry indoors**; vacuum futons and bedding | Same |
| Cleaning | **Wipe surfaces first** (so as not to stir pollen up); ventilate only briefly | Same |

## Misunderstanding 1｜Pollen does not fly on rainy days

Less pollen flies in the rain, but **the next sunny day tends to bring a heavy count, as the pollen knocked down by the rain is added to the new release**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Pollen is heaviest first thing in the morning

In urban Kanto the peaks are generally described as coming **around midday and around sunset** (when pollen higher in the air comes down to ground level).

## Misunderstanding 3｜Stop ventilating and the room is safe

Without ventilation, **house dust and carbon dioxide** become a different problem. The usual practice is to **ventilate briefly with the net curtains closed**, at **times of low pollen such as early morning or night**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Over-the-counter medicine means no need for daily precautions

Medicine is a means of keeping symptoms down. **Reducing your contact with pollen** helps to reduce the amount of medicine you need. **Ask a pharmacist or doctor about which medicine to choose.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once you have hay fever, nothing can be done

Therapies that address the underlying allergy, such as **sublingual immunotherapy** (舌下免疫療法), are available under Japanese health insurance. The government strategy also aims to spread them more widely. **Whether you are a suitable candidate is for a doctor to decide.**
