# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about dealing with dry air in winter?｜Japan Questions #127

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/winter-dryness` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Dry indoor air in winter is a shared complaint wherever people heat their homes. In Japan, "40–60% humidity" is a widely known official benchmark, and as humidifiers became common a set of home-grown techniques grew up around them. If you have just spent your first winter in a Japanese flat with a heat-pump air conditioner, this will be familiar.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is that more humidification is always better. Above 60% humidity you get condensation and mould. The benchmark is 40–60%, and the basic tool is a combined thermometer-hygrometer to check it.

## In numbers

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Relative humidity under the Building Environmental Hygiene Management Standards | **40–70%** | MHLW (Building Management Act) |
| Indoor humidity generally described as comfortable | **40–60%** | Various public sources |
| Tokyo, January monthly mean relative humidity | **About 52%** (outdoors) | Japan Meteorological Agency, climatological normals |
| Heated room without humidification | Can fall to **the 20s–30s%** | Typical measurements |

Air **holds more water vapour as it gets warmer**. So the same amount of moisture gives a lower relative humidity once the heating warms the room. That is the mechanism behind "dry rooms in winter".

> **[Verify]** Check the standard against the MHLW Building Environmental Hygiene Management Standards, and the humidity normals in the Japan Meteorological Agency "Past weather data search".

## Misunderstanding 1｜Without a humidifier you cannot humidify

**Drying laundry indoors, leaving the bathroom door open, cooking a hot-pot (nabe), keeping houseplants, hanging up a wet towel** — many methods are shared among people living in Japan. The effect is gentler than a humidifier, but they cost nothing in electricity.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A humidifier only needs water, not maintenance

**Bacteria multiply easily in the water tank**, and ultrasonic models in particular spray the water out as fine droplets. **Change the water daily and clean the unit regularly as the product label instructs.** Cases of **"humidifier lung"** (加湿器肺, an inflammatory lung reaction) from neglected humidifiers have been reported.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The higher the humidity, the better for skin and throat

**Above 60%, condensation forms on windows and walls and causes mould and dust mites.** Keep within **40–60%**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Air-conditioner heating dries the air, so better not to use it

An air conditioner **does not remove moisture; relative humidity falls simply because the air is warmer**. Kerosene and gas heaters give off water vapour as they burn, but **ventilation is essential** with them. **Whatever the heating, the key is combining humidification with ventilation.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜A mask cannot stop a dry throat

Wearing a mask in bed is widely used among people living in Japan because **the moisture in your own breath eases dryness in the mouth and nose**. The precondition is **not to force it if you find it hard to breathe**.
