# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about daily step counts?｜Japan Questions #128

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/daily-steps` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"10,000 steps a day" is probably the world's best-known health target, and more and more people in every country are asking where it came from. The answer is Japan. Japan's national physical-activity guidance was revised in 2024 and now gives concrete step-count benchmarks. Here is what it says.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is that 10,000 steps is a scientific standard. It is a rule of thumb that spread in Japan in the 1960s. The current guide from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW, 2023 edition) gives adults "about 8,000 steps or more a day" and older people "about 6,000 steps or more".

## In numbers

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark for adults | **60 minutes or more of physical activity a day (about 8,000 steps or more)** | Physical Activity and Exercise Guide for Health Promotion 2023 |
| Benchmark for older people | **40 minutes or more a day (about 6,000 steps or more)** | Same |
| Average steps in Japan (men aged 20–64) | **About 6,600** | National Health and Nutrition Survey (2023) |
| Average steps in Japan (women aged 20–64) | **About 5,700** | Same |
| Health Japan 21 (third term) target | **Aged 20–64: 8,000 steps; aged 65 and over: 6,000 steps** | MHLW |

"10,000 steps" is generally traced to **the product name of a pedometer launched in 1965** (the *Manpo-kei*, literally "10,000-step meter"). It was never set as a medical threshold.

> **[Verify]** Check the Guide 2023 benchmarks and the latest year's average step counts in the National Health and Nutrition Survey. The origin of "10,000 steps" is the received account given in several explanatory articles.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If you cannot reach 10,000, there is no point

The basic principle of the Guide 2023 is **"move a little more than you do now"**. It cites research suggesting that **even 2,000 extra steps** brings health benefits.

## Misunderstanding 2｜More steps is all you need

The guide also recommends **strength training 2–3 times a week**. Step count is one indicator of physical activity, not the whole of it.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Walking only counts if you do it in one go

The current thinking is that **short bursts of under 10 minutes count towards the total**. Steps from commuting, shopping and housework are included.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Sitting time can be offset with steps

The guide lists **reducing sitting time** as a separate item in its own right. Habits such as **standing up once every 30 minutes** have a value of their own, apart from steps.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Older people are safer not walking, because of falls

The Guide 2023 recommends that older people do **balance exercises in addition to walking**. **Loss of muscle from not moving** is generally described as raising the risk of falls more. **If you have an existing condition, ask your doctor.**
