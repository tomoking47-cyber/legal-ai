# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about measuring blood pressure at home?｜Japan Questions #129

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/home-blood-pressure` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Home blood-pressure monitors are common in every country, but the questions about when and how to measure never stop. Japan has guidelines from the Japanese Society of Hypertension (JSH) that set out home measurement in concrete detail. If a Japanese doctor has asked you to keep a blood-pressure diary, this is the method they have in mind.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is that the reading at the clinic is the correct one and home readings are only a rough guide. The JSH guidelines state clearly that where clinic and home readings differ, the home reading takes priority. The precondition is that you measure under the right conditions.

## The guideline method (JSH2019)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| When to measure | **Morning** (within 1 hour of waking, after urinating, before breakfast and before any medication) and **evening** (before bed) |
| Posture | Seated in a chair, after **1–2 minutes of rest** |
| Arm | An upper-arm monitor is recommended. Cuff at **heart level** |
| Number of readings | **2** readings per occasion, averaged, or 1 reading |
| Record | Every day, **for as long as possible** |

| Category (home blood pressure) | Systolic | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | **Under 115** | **Under 75** |
| Hypertension | **135 or over** | **85 or over** |

At the clinic the threshold for hypertension is **140/90**; for home readings **a value 5 lower** is used.

> **[Verify]** Check the figures and the method against the Japanese Society of Hypertension "Guidelines for the Management of Hypertension 2019 (JSH2019)". If a revised edition has been published in 2025, follow the latest version.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A wrist monitor is easier, so it is just as good

The guidelines recommend **upper-arm monitors**. Wrist readings **change easily with the position of the arm**, and are treated as reference values.

## Misunderstanding 2｜One high reading means hypertension

Blood pressure **changes with every reading**. Judge by **the average of morning and evening readings over several days**. Before worrying about a single figure, keep recording.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Deep breaths before measuring give an accurate reading

Trying too hard to breathe deeply or relax **moves the reading away from your everyday value.** "Sit quietly for 1–2 minutes" is generally described as enough.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Measuring over thick clothing makes no difference

**You cannot measure accurately over a thick sleeve.** Wrap the cuff over a single thin shirt or on bare skin. Also avoid rolling up a sleeve so that it squeezes the arm.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A high reading means the home monitor is faulty

Home monitors sold in Japan carry **JIS certification or medical-device approval**. For interpreting the numbers, **take your record to a doctor and ask** (a local clinic, covered by Japanese health insurance). Do not adjust any medication on your own judgement.
