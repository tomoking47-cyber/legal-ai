# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about looking after a reusable water bottle?｜Japan Questions #132

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/water-bottle-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Reusable bottles are everywhere now, but "what can I put in it?" and "how do I wash it?" are common questions in every country. In Japan, cases of metal leaching into acidic drinks kept in metal bottles have been reported, and public bodies have issued warnings. Here is what Japanese makers and consumer authorities say.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a bottle is a bottle, whatever you put in it". If acidic drinks — fizzy drinks, sports drinks, fruit juice — sit for hours in a metal bottle, metal can leach out through scratches on the inside. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government have issued warnings. The basic rule is to check the instruction leaflet for the list of drinks you must not put in.

## Incidents and warnings

- **The Tokyo Metropolitan Government has published a case (2008) in which six people showed symptoms of copper poisoning after drinking a sports drink kept in a metal bottle whose inside was damaged.** The cause is described as **the acidic drink seeping through the damaged part into the double-wall cavity and dissolving the copper used in the insulating structure**. Separately, in 2020 the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare warned about copper poisoning from a sports drink left in a kettle.
- Bottle manufacturers forbid it in their instruction leaflets: **putting fizzy drinks or dry ice in raises the internal pressure, so the inner stopper or lid may refuse to open, or break and fly off** (other than products sold as carbonation-safe).
- Drinks that most makers list as **"do not fill with"** in their instruction leaflets: **carbonated drinks, dairy, drinks containing fruit juice or pulp, salty liquids such as miso soup, and dry ice**.

| Care | Guide |
|---|---|
| Every day | Straight after use, with neutral washing-up liquid and a soft sponge. **Take off the lid and the gasket (パッキン, pakkin)** and wash them separately |
| About once a week | Check the gasket and the grooves of the lid for grime. For mould, use oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) — check the product label first |
| Do not use | Metal scourers, abrasive cleaners, **chlorine bleach** (they scratch and corrode the inside) |
| Drying | Dry completely and store with the lid off |

> **[Verify]** Check the year and details of the Tokyo case in the published material of the Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Public Health, and the fizzy-drink incidents in the reports of the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Stainless steel never rusts or leaches

Stainless steel **can corrode where it has been scratched**. **If you can see scratches or peeling inside, stop using the bottle** — that is the safe side.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A sports drink can be handled like water

Sports drinks are **acidic and contain salt**, which makes them one of the drinks most likely to dissolve metal. **If you fill up with one, keep it short and finish it**, or use a **bottle sold as suitable for sports drinks**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Hot tea in the bottle means bacteria cannot grow

As the drink cools, bacteria grow more easily. **Take special care with drinks containing sugar or starch, such as barley tea (mugicha) and sports drinks.** The basic rule: **finish the drink the same day and wash the bottle every day.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can wash it without removing the gasket

**The grooves of the lid and the underside of the gasket are where mould appears first.** Most makers say to **remove and wash them every time**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Just put it in the dishwasher

**Most vacuum-insulated bottles are not dishwasher-safe.** High temperatures can peel the coating and reduce the insulation. **Check the label.**
