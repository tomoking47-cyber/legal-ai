# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about reusing and disposing of frying oil?｜Japan Questions #133

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/reusing-frying-oil` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

How many times can you reuse frying oil, and how do you get rid of it? Households everywhere ask the same thing. Japan has its own habits — setting the oil with a solidifying powder, or soaking it up with newspaper — and never pouring it down the sink is taken for granted. If you have just moved here, the disposal rules may be the unfamiliar part.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "oil can go down the drain". Almost every Japanese municipality forbids it. Whether to reuse oil is judged by its condition, not by counting uses, and disposal follows your municipality's waste-sorting rules.

## What to look for

| Condition of the oil | Judgement |
|---|---|
| Darker colour, thicker or sticky | Time to replace |
| **Fine bubbles that do not disappear** when heated | Deterioration is advanced |
| **Smoke at around 180°C** (fresh oil: 230°C or above) | Replace |
| Fried food tastes flat, oil smells rancid | Replace |

- Guides often quote **2–4 uses**, but **how fast oil deteriorates depends on what you fried (batter, fish, meat)**, so judge by condition.
- For household waste oil, the usual method in Tokyo's 23 wards is to **soak it into newspaper or cloth, or set it with a shop-bought solidifier (凝固剤, gyōkozai), and put it out as "burnable waste" (燃やすごみ, moyasu gomi)**. A growing number of municipalities also run **collection points (supermarkets, ward offices)**.
- An industry body, the National Federation of Oil and Fat Business Cooperatives, estimates **household waste cooking oil at around 100,000 tonnes a year**; only about 30% of municipalities collect it separately.

> **[Verify]** Smoke points differ by type of oil. Check the waste-oil estimate against Ministry of the Environment and MAFF material. Always follow your own municipality's guidance on sorting.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A small amount down the sink is fine

**It cools and hardens inside the drain pipes, causing blockages and bad smells.** **Even the oil left in a frying pan should be wiped out with kitchen paper before washing.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜If I keep it in an oil strainer pot, I can reuse it as often as I like

Straining only **removes the crumbs**; it does not stop oxidation. **Store it away from light and air with the lid on**, and judge by the number of uses and the condition.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dispose of it while it is still hot — it is quicker

There are **solidifiers that are added to hot oil**, but **soaking into newspaper or pouring into a container is done once the oil has cooled**. **Hot oil meeting water is dangerous.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Pour it into a plastic container and throw it out

The Japanese standard is to **stuff a milk carton (牛乳パック, gyūnyū pakku) with newspaper, pour in the oil, and tape the top shut**. Some municipalities do not accept PET bottles. **Check your municipality's guidance.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Reusing oil is bad for you

Reusing oil an appropriate number of times, while it is in good condition, is ordinary household practice. The point is **not to keep using deteriorated oil (colour, bubbles, smoke)**; the number of uses in itself is not the problem.
