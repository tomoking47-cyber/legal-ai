# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing miso?｜Japan Questions #134

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-miso` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Miso is Japan's basic seasoning, but even Japanese people disagree about how to store it once the pack is open. As more people abroad buy miso, the questions "room temperature or fridge?" and "what is the white stuff on the surface?" keep coming up. Here is the practical answer.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "miso is fermented, so it cannot go off". It is true that it rarely spoils, but after opening it darkens and loses its aroma. Once opened, keep it in the fridge, or in the freezer for long-term storage. The white crystals on the surface (tyrosine) are harmless.

## What to look for

| State | Storage | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unopened | Follow the label (many products are fine at room temperature) | Salt and fermentation keep microbes from growing |
| Opened | **Fridge**. Cover the exposed surface with plastic wrap | Slows browning and loss of aroma |
| Long term (months) | **Freezer** | High salt content means it does not freeze solid, and the flavour changes little |

- Miso's **salt content is about 6–13% depending on the product**. White miso (shiro miso) tends to be low in salt, red miso (aka miso) and soybean miso (mame miso) higher, and **the lower the salt, the faster it deteriorates**.
- "Dashi-iri" miso (with stock added) **contains fish or kelp extracts that can spoil**, so the label usually asks for **refrigeration after opening**.
- Miso labelling follows the **Food Labelling Act** and uses a **"best-before" date (賞味期限, shōmi kigen — the date up to which it tastes best)**. Passing that date does not mean it is inedible.

> **[Verify]** Check the salt range against the Japan Federation of Miso Manufacturers Cooperatives (Zenmiso) or the Standard Tables of Food Composition in Japan, and the approach to date labelling against Consumer Affairs Agency material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The white stuff on the surface is mould

Usually it is crystals of the amino acid **tyrosine**, and **there is no need to remove it.** But **blue, black or green fluffy growth** is mould. Cut that part out generously, and stop using the miso if it is widespread.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Once it darkens you cannot eat it

The colour change is the **Maillard reaction** (sugars reacting with amino acids). **The aroma and taste change, but in itself this is not a safety problem.** Fridge or freezer storage slows it down.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Frozen miso goes solid and unusable

**Because of the high salt content, miso does not freeze hard in a household freezer.** It stays soft enough to scoop with a spoon and can be used without thawing.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Make miso soup in a batch and reheat it

The **ingredients in miso soup (tofu, potato) spoil easily**. **Do not leave it at room temperature; finish it the same day.** If you want to prepare ahead, many people **refrigerate the stock with the cooked ingredients and stir in the miso just before eating**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Miso is high in salt, so it is bad for you

A bowl of miso soup (1 tablespoon of miso) contains **about 2 g of salt**. Japanese households share the habit of **more ingredients, less liquid and a stronger dashi** to keep within the daily target (under 7 g).
