# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing soy sauce?｜Japan Questions #135

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-soy-sauce` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Soy sauce has reached kitchens all over the world, but what people do with an opened bottle varies from country to country. In Japan, "refrigerate after opening" is now printed on the label, and double-walled "fresh-keeping" squeeze bottles (密封ボトル, mippū botoru) have become common. Here is how the Japanese approach works.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "soy sauce is salty, so it keeps for years at room temperature". It rarely spoils, but after opening it oxidises: it turns black and loses its aroma. Refrigerate after opening and aim to finish the bottle in about a month.

## What to look for

| State | Storage | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Unopened | Room temperature, out of direct sunlight | Best-before date (usually **18 months to 2 years**) |
| Opened (ordinary bottle) | **Fridge** | Finish within **about 1 month** |
| Opened (double-walled sealed bottle) | Many products allow room temperature | **About 90 days** (per the label) |

- Soy sauce contains **about 14–16% salt equivalent** (koikuchi, the standard dark type). Reduced-salt soy sauce is **about 8%** and is generally described as deteriorating faster after opening.
- The main change in quality is **oxidation**. **Darker (blacker) colour, less aroma and a heavier taste** are the signs of deterioration — **this is not spoilage**.
- The Japanese Agricultural Standards (**JAS**) define the **types (koikuchi dark, usukuchi light, tamari, saishikomi twice-brewed, shiro white) and grades** of soy sauce.

> **[Verify]** The after-opening periods are approximate figures based on individual makers' labels. Check the JAS categories against MAFF's "Japanese Agricultural Standard for Soy Sauce".

## Misunderstanding 1｜Turning black means it has gone off

It is **colour change from oxidation**, not a safety problem. But **the flavour has declined.** Many people move such a bottle over to cooking rather than table use.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The fridge spoils the flavour

The opposite. **Low temperature slows oxidation.** An opened bottle kept at room temperature is generally described as losing flavour faster.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A sealed bottle keeps the quality unchanged once opened

A sealed bottle is built to **limit contact with air**. It slows deterioration but does not stop it. Use the **period on the label (usually 90 days)** as your guide.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Anything floating on the surface is mould

A white film on soy sauce may be **film-forming yeast (産膜酵母, sanmaku kōbo)**, which is **generally described as low-risk but spoils the flavour.** Remove it and use up the bottle quickly, or discard the bottle if the film is widespread. **If in doubt, throw it out** — that is the safe side.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The big bottle is better value

The guide is an amount you can finish **within a month of opening**. For a household that uses little, a **small bottle or a sealed bottle** wastes less in the end.
