# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about eating natto?｜Japan Questions #136

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/natto` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Natto — fermented soybeans — is known abroad as "the hardest Japanese food to try", and at the same time gets attention as a health food. This article sorts out how to eat it, how to store it and how to read the label, based on Japanese public information.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "natto is fermented, so the date does not matter". Natto carries a best-before date and must be kept refrigerated. Past the date, fermentation continues: the flavour and smell get stronger and the white specks (tyrosine) increase. That in itself is not spoilage.

## What to look for

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Storage | **Refrigerated (10°C or below)**. The pack is marked 要冷蔵 (keep refrigerated) |
| Best-before date | **About 1–2 weeks** from manufacture (varies by product) |
| Freezing | Possible. **Thaw at room temperature or in the fridge**; the texture changes slightly |
| White specks on the surface | **Tyrosine** (amino-acid crystals). Harmless |
| Past the date | Fermentation advances; **ammonia smell and bitterness** get stronger. **If in doubt, throw it out** |

- One pack of natto (about 40–50 g) contains **about 7–8 g of protein and about 3 g of dietary fibre** (Standard Tables of Food Composition in Japan, 8th edition).
- **People taking warfarin (an anticoagulant) are usually told by their doctor or pharmacist to avoid natto, because its vitamin K weakens the drug's effect.**
- Natto labelling follows the **Food Labelling Act**: the ingredients list shows **the origin of the soybeans and their genetically-modified status**.

> **[Verify]** Check the nutrition figures against the Standard Tables of Food Composition in Japan (8th edition, 2023 supplement), and the GM labelling rules against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Genetically Modified Food Labelling System" (revised April 2023).

## Misunderstanding 1｜The number of times you stir changes the nutrition

Stirring changes the **stickiness and mouthfeel**, but **there is no confirmed evidence that it adds nutrients.** It is a matter of taste.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Putting it on hot rice destroys the nutrients

Some enzymes from the natto bacteria are described as heat-sensitive, but **we could find no official evidence that the temperature of the rice changes the overall nutritional value much.** In practice, eat it the way you like.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Eat a lot every day and you will be healthy

Natto is **a soy product that belongs within a balanced diet**. **Under Japan's Health Promotion Act and the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, no one may claim that it prevents any specific disease.** About one pack a day is the commonly quoted guide.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Use the whole sauce sachet

One sachet of sauce (tare) contains **about 0.5–0.8 g of salt equivalent**. People watching their salt **use half, and top up with dashi or ginger**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You cannot take natto abroad

Natto is a processed food and **can be brought into many countries**, but some countries restrict the **sauce sachets if they contain meat extract**. **Check the quarantine rules of your destination.**
