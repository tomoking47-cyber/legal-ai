# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about brewing Japanese green tea?｜Japan Questions #139

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/brewing-green-tea` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Green tea has spread around the world, but "bitter" and "astringent" are common reactions, and questions about brewing never stop. Japanese teas differ sharply in the water temperature they need, and that temperature decides the taste. Here is the guide, type by type.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "tea is made with boiling water". The astringency of green tea (catechins) comes out at high temperatures; the savoury umami (theanine) comes out even at low temperatures. The basic rule is gyokuro at 50–60°C (122–140°F), sencha at 70–80°C (158–176°F), and boiling water for hōjicha and genmaicha.

## Guide by type

| Type | Water temperature | Leaves (per person) | Steeping time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gyokuro (shade-grown, premium) | **50–60°C** | 3 g (20–30 mL water) | 2–2.5 min |
| Premium sencha | **Around 70°C** | 2–3 g (60–80 mL water) | 1.5–2 min |
| Standard sencha | **80–90°C** | 2–3 g (90–100 mL water) | 1 min |
| Fukamushi (deep-steamed) sencha | **70–80°C** | 2–3 g | 30 sec – 1 min |
| Hōjicha (roasted), genmaicha (with roasted rice), bancha | **Boiling (95–100°C)** | 3 g | 30 sec |

- The temperatures are based on guidance from the **Japan Tea Central Public Interest Incorporated Association (Nihon Cha-gyō Chūōkai)** and others. The rule of thumb shared in Japanese households: **each time you pour boiled water into another vessel — a cooling bowl (yuzamashi) or a cup — it drops by about 10°C**.
- **Caffeine in brewed sencha is about 20 mg per 100 mL** (about a third of coffee); gyokuro is higher, at **about 160 mg** (Standard Tables of Food Composition in Japan).
- Japan produces **about 75,000 tonnes** of tea a year (2023), with **Shizuoka and Kagoshima accounting for over 70%** (MAFF).

## Misunderstanding 1｜The second steeping is spent leaves, not worth drinking

After the **umami compounds** come out in the first steeping, use **slightly hotter water for a shorter time** for the second, and the balance of astringency and aroma shifts. Enjoying the leaves **up to the third steeping** is normal.

## Misunderstanding 2｜More leaves means stronger and better

Too many leaves and **the astringency comes out first.** The guide is **2–3 g (one teaspoon) per person**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Leave the leaves in the pot (kyūsu) for next time

**Damp leaves left sitting grow bacteria and lose flavour.** Once you have finished pouring, discard the leaves and wash the pot.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The fridge is the best place for tea leaves

**Unopened, the fridge or freezer is fine**, but **once opened, taking the pack in and out causes condensation and damp.** After opening, keep it **in an airtight container in a dark place at room temperature, and use it within about a month**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cold-brewed green tea is just weak tea

Cold brewing at **low temperature draws out little astringency (catechins) or caffeine, and brings out the umami (theanine).** The character is different, not weaker. For hygiene, **brew it in the fridge and finish it the same day.**
