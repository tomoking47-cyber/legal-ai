# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about keeping barley tea and other cold-brewed drinks safe?｜Japan Questions #140

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/cold-brew-hygiene` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

A jug of cold tea in the fridge is part of every Japanese summer. Barley tea (mugicha) is the classic, and as cold brewing spreads abroad, "how long does it keep?" is asked more and more often. The answer surprises many people: mugicha spoils faster than you would expect.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "in the fridge it keeps for a week". Mugicha contains starch, so bacteria grow in it easily. Even refrigerated, finish it within 1–2 days, and wash and dry the jug every time.

## What to look for

| Drink | Guide (refrigerated) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mugicha (cold-brewed or boiled) | **1–2 days** | Contains **starch and sugars** that feed bacteria |
| Green tea (cold-brewed) | **1 day** | Catechins are antibacterial, but it must not be left at room temperature |
| Black tea, rooibos (cold-brewed) | **1–2 days** | As above |
| Tap water (stored) | **2–3 days refrigerated** (deteriorates once the chlorine is gone) | Tokyo Metropolitan Waterworks Bureau guidance |

- A **Tokyo Metropolitan Government study** reported that mugicha **left at room temperature** showed **a large increase in bacterial counts within a few hours**.
- The Tokyo Metropolitan Waterworks Bureau gives **2–3 days refrigerated** for stored tap water, and **the same day** at room temperature (the chlorine dissipates and the disinfecting effect is lost).
- The **spout and gasket (パッキン, pakkin)** of a bottle or jug are where grime lingers most.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Boiling sterilises it, so it keeps longer

Just after boiling the bacterial count is low, but **bacteria get in as it cools and every time you pour.** Boiled mugicha is also **1–2 days in the fridge**. **Let it cool down first, rather than putting it in the fridge hot.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Leaving the tea bag in makes it stronger

**Left in, it produces off-flavours and cloudiness, and gives bacteria a foothold.** **Take the bag out** once the brewing time is up.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Rinsing the jug with water is enough

**The starch in mugicha stays on the inside as a slimy film.** Wash it every time with detergent and a sponge, **taking off the lid, gasket and spout**, then dry it. **For narrow-necked bottles, a long-handled sponge** is the standard tool.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Drinking from the bottle is fine as long as it goes back in the fridge

**Once you drink from the bottle, bacteria from your mouth get in, and if there is sugar they multiply fast.** Anything you have drunk from should be **finished the same day**; better still, **pour it into a glass**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The cloudiness or sour taste is my imagination

**Cloudiness, sourness, thickening or a change in smell** are signs of spoilage. **If in doubt, throw it out** — that is the safe side.
