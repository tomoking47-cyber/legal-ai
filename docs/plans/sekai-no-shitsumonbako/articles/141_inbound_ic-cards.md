# Q. What do people get wrong about IC cards (Suica, PASMO) in Japan?｜Japan Questions #141

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/ic-cards` ／ **Caution: services changed in 2023 and 2025**
- Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Transport IC cards are the single most asked-about practical item before a trip. Much of what circulates online describes a system that has changed twice since 2023.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that an IC card is only a train ticket. It is a prepaid e-money card accepted at most convenience stores, vending machines and many shops nationwide — and the version you should get depends on your phone and the length of your stay.

## The current options

| Option | Who it suits | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Welcome Suica** (physical, 28-day validity) | Short visits, Android or no phone | No ¥500 deposit; **unused balance is not refunded** |
| **Welcome Suica Mobile** (app, launched 6 March 2025) | iPhone / Apple Watch users | Top up by credit card via Apple Pay; 180-day validity; since October 2025 links with JR-EAST Train Reservation and Touch de Go! Shinkansen |
| **Suica / PASMO in Apple Wallet** | iPhone users staying longer | Can be topped up with foreign cards in Wallet |
| Regular Suica / PASMO (physical) | Longer stays | ¥500 deposit, refundable at the issuing operator's station (a ¥220 handling fee is deducted from any remaining balance) |
| Regional cards (ICOCA, manaca etc.) | Any | **Interoperable for transport nationwide** under the 10-card alliance |

- Sales of unregistered regular Suica and PASMO cards were **suspended from 8 June 2023 to 1 March 2025** due to a chip shortage (registered cards returned in autumn 2023); unrestricted sales have since resumed.
- IC cards work for fares in **all major urban areas**, but **not on every rural line**, and a card cannot be used across some regional boundaries in a single journey.
- Maximum balance: **¥20,000**.

> **[Verify]** Launch dates, validity periods and refund rules with JR East (Welcome Suica page) and PASMO before publication. Service details changed in 2023 and 2025 and may change again.

## Misunderstanding 1｜You need one card per city

The ten major IC cards are **mutually accepted for transport across Japan.** A Suica bought in Tokyo works on the Osaka subway and Fukuoka buses.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Cash is easier

Fares between two stations often differ by a few yen for IC users, and **buying paper tickets requires reading the fare map for every trip.** The card removes that step.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is only for trains

**Convenience stores, many supermarkets, vending machines, coin lockers and some taxis** accept it. Tap the reader marked with the IC logo.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can top up with a foreign credit card at the machine

At station machines, **top-up is cash only.** Card top-up is possible **inside Apple Wallet or the Welcome Suica Mobile app.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜The card works on the Shinkansen

Ordinary IC cards **do not cover Shinkansen fares** by default. Separate services are needed: Smart EX (Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu), Touch de Go! Shinkansen for non-reserved seats on JR East lines (now linkable from Welcome Suica Mobile), or paper tickets. Note Hayabusa/Hayate are all-reserved.
