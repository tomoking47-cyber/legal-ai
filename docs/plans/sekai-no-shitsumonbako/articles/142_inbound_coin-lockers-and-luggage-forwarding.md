# Q. What do people get wrong about coin lockers and luggage forwarding in Japan?｜Japan Questions #142

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/coin-lockers-and-luggage-forwarding` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Travelling light between cities is something Japan makes unusually easy — but the systems are unfamiliar to most visitors, and the rules (especially size limits and same-day delivery) are often misreported.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a big suitcase can simply go in a locker. Most lockers are for small and medium bags, and large ones are scarce at busy stations. For a suitcase, the realistic tool is door-to-door luggage forwarding, which usually takes one day, not same-day.

## Coin lockers

| Size (approx.) | Typical price per day | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Small: 35 × 34 × 57 cm | **¥300–400** | Daypack, shopping bags |
| Medium: 57 × 34 × 57 cm | **¥500–600** | Carry-on case |
| Large: 84–117 × 34 × 57 cm | **¥700–1,000** | Check-in suitcase (limited number) |

- Payment: coins, and at many stations **IC card** (which also serves as the key).
- Time limit: typically **3 days**; after that the contents are moved to a storage office and a fee is charged.
- Peak times at major stations (e.g. Tokyo, Shinjuku, Kyoto), **large lockers are often full by mid-morning.**

## Luggage forwarding (takuhaibin)

- Services such as Yamato Transport's **TA-Q-BIN** carry a suitcase from a hotel or convenience store to your next hotel or the airport.
- Typical cost for a suitcase within the main islands: **roughly ¥2,000–3,000**, depending on size and distance.
- **Standard delivery is the next day**; same-day is generally not available for ordinary bookings. Airport delivery usually needs **two days' lead time**.

> **[Verify]** Locker prices and sizes vary by operator and station; forwarding prices change annually. Check the JR East locker page and Yamato Transport's TA-Q-BIN English page before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Lockers are everywhere

They cluster at **stations and some department stores**. Tourist sites, shrines and small towns rarely have them. Plan storage at the station you arrive at.

## Misunderstanding 2｜You can leave a bag for a week

Most lockers allow **up to 3 days**. Longer storage means a **manned luggage office** (at major stations) or your hotel.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Forwarding is same-day

**Next-day is the norm.** Send your suitcase the evening before you travel and take a daypack for the first night.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You must find a courier office

**Most hotels and many convenience stores** accept forwarding parcels at the counter. Ask at the front desk.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Anything can be forwarded

Cash, valuables, **lithium batteries loose in a bag**, perishables and fragile items have restrictions. Keep passports, medicines and electronics with you.
