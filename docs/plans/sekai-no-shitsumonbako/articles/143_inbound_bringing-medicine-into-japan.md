# Q. What do people get wrong about bringing medicine into Japan?｜Japan Questions #143

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/bringing-medicine-into-japan` ／ **Caution: prohibited ingredients; check before you travel**
- Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

This is the practical question with the highest stakes: getting it wrong can mean confiscation at the airport or, for certain ingredients, a criminal matter. Much online advice is outdated or simply guesses.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that "prescription in hand" means anything is allowed. Japan lets you bring in a limited personal supply without paperwork (generally one month of prescription medicine, two months of over-the-counter), requires a prior import confirmation for more, and bans some ingredients that are legal elsewhere — notably pseudoephedrine above a threshold and most stimulants used for ADHD.

## The rules in outline (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare)

| Category | Allowed without paperwork | Above that |
|---|---|---|
| Prescription medicine | **Up to 1 month's supply** | **Yakkan Shoumei / Import Confirmation** required in advance |
| Over-the-counter medicine | **Up to 2 months' supply** | Import Confirmation |
| External-use products (creams, drops) | **Up to 24 units** per product | Import Confirmation |
| Injectables (e.g. insulin pens, EpiPen) | **Up to 1 month**, with prescription | Import Confirmation |

- **Narcotics** (e.g. codeine above certain strength, morphine, some sleep medicines): a **separate permit** from a Regional Bureau of Health and Welfare is needed **before travel**.
- **Stimulants**: medicines containing **amphetamine, methamphetamine, or dextroamphetamine (e.g. Adderall)** are **prohibited** and cannot be brought in with any paperwork. **Methylphenidate (Ritalin, Concerta)** requires a narcotics-type permit.
- **Pseudoephedrine**: products containing **more than 10% pseudoephedrine** are treated as stimulant raw materials and are prohibited. Many Western decongestants fall on the wrong side.

> **[Verify]** Confirm quantities and the current procedure name ("Import Confirmation", formerly Yakkan Shoumei) with the MHLW English page before publication. This area changes and the consequences of an error are severe.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A doctor's letter covers everything

A letter is **useful evidence** but does not replace the **Import Confirmation** for quantities above the limit, or the **narcotics permit** for controlled substances.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Anything legal at home is legal here

Several ADHD medicines and some decongestants are **prohibited or controlled** in Japan regardless of your prescription. **Check the ingredient, not the brand.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Medicines can be posted ahead

Mailing medicine to yourself in Japan is **import** and subject to the same rules — often stricter, because customs handles it without you present.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Keep pills in a pill organiser to save space

Customs officers need to **identify the medicine**. Keep the **original packaging and prescription label**; carry the doctor's letter for anything unusual.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You can buy the same thing in Japan

Many common medicines are sold in **different strengths or not at all** (for example, some hormonal contraceptives require a prescription and consultation). Bring what you need within the limits.
