# Q. What do people get wrong about tipping and restaurant etiquette in Japan?｜Japan Questions #146

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/tipping-and-restaurant-etiquette` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

"Do I tip?" is the first question most visitors ask, and the answer is short. The longer, more useful answers are about the things that replace tipping: the table charge, the wet towel, the call button, and how you pay.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that leaving a tip is a kind gesture. In Japan it is generally not expected and can cause confusion; service is included in the price, and some restaurants add a seat charge (otoshi) instead. Paying happens at the till, not the table, in most places.

## What replaces tipping

| Item | What it is | Typical amount |
|---|---|---|
| **Otoshi / tsukidashi** | A small starter served automatically at izakaya, functioning as a table charge | **¥300–800 per person** |
| **Service charge** | Added at some hotels and high-end restaurants, stated on the menu | **10%** |
| **Late-night charge** | Some izakaya and karaoke after 22:00–23:00 | **10%** or a fixed fee |
| Oshibori | Hot or cold wet towel for hands | Free |

- Consumption tax is **10%** on eating in and **8%** on takeaway. Menu prices must be shown **tax-inclusive** (since April 2021).
- Payment is usually at the **cash register near the door**; take the bill slip from your table. In some izakaya you pay at the table.
- **Cash is still widely used**; card acceptance is common in cities but not universal in small restaurants (see the article on cash and cards).

> **[Verify]** Tax-inclusive pricing rules with the National Tax Agency; otoshi price ranges are observational.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Leaving coins on the table is polite

Staff may **chase you to return it.** If you want to express thanks, say "gochisōsama deshita" (thank you for the meal) as you leave.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The otoshi is a scam

It is a **customary table charge** in the form of food, usually listed on the menu in Japanese. You can ask to decline it at some places, but many treat it as non-optional. Budget for it at izakaya.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Waiting to be asked how the food is

Staff generally **do not check in mid-meal.** Use the **call button** on the table or raise a hand and say "sumimasen".

## Misunderstanding 4｜Splitting the bill is awkward

Splitting equally ("betsu-betsu") at the till is common. Splitting by item is less common and slower; many places will still do it if asked politely.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Noise is rude everywhere

**Slurping noodles is normal.** Talking on the phone at the table, and loud groups in quiet restaurants, are what draw looks. Read the room: izakaya are loud, sushi counters are not.
