# Q. What do people get wrong about power plugs and voltage in Japan?｜Japan Questions #148

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/power-plugs-and-voltage` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A small but daily question. Japan's electricity is unusual on two counts — the voltage is 100 V, lower than almost anywhere else, and the country runs on two different frequencies depending on region.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a plug adapter alone solves everything. Japan uses type A plugs (two flat pins, like the US) and 100 V. Most phone and laptop chargers accept 100–240 V and only need an adapter; hair dryers, straighteners and some kettles may not, and Japan's grounded type B sockets are rare.

## The facts

| Item | Japan | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Voltage | **100 V** | US/Canada 120 V; UK 230 V; EU 230 V |
| Frequency | **50 Hz east** (Tokyo, Tohoku, Hokkaido) / **60 Hz west** (Nagoya, Osaka, Kyushu) | Boundary near the Fuji River, Shizuoka |
| Plug | **Type A** (two flat parallel pins) | Same shape as US ungrounded |
| Grounded socket (Type B) | **Uncommon** in homes and older hotels | Kitchens and newer buildings have them |

- Japan is **one of only two countries** (with parts of Mexico) using 100 V.
- Type A pins in Japan are **the same size**, unlike US plugs where one pin is wider — so some US polarised plugs **do not fit** Japanese sockets without an adapter.
- The 50/60 Hz split dates to the 1890s, when Tokyo imported German generators and Osaka imported American ones.

> **[Verify]** Frequency boundary and voltage with the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy; plug details with the Japan Electrical Safety & Environment Technology Laboratories (JET).

## Misunderstanding 1｜UK/EU devices just need an adapter

Only if the device is rated **100–240 V** (check the label). A 230 V-only hair dryer on 100 V will **run weakly**; that is not dangerous but it is useless. A 100 V-only Japanese device taken home to 230 V **can be destroyed** — relevant for souvenir electronics.

## Misunderstanding 2｜US plugs always fit

**Polarised US plugs** (one wide pin) may not fit older Japanese sockets. Grounded (three-pin) US plugs need an adapter almost everywhere except newer hotels.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The frequency does not matter

Electronics with switch-mode chargers do not care. **Motors and clocks designed for a single frequency** (some older appliances, certain microwave ovens) do. Modern devices sold in Japan are dual-frequency.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hotels have plenty of sockets

Older business hotels often have **one or two accessible sockets**. A small multi-port USB charger solves most of it.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Buying a Japanese hair dryer is a good souvenir

It is designed for 100 V. **At home it may burn out unless it is a dual-voltage travel model.** Check the label before buying.
