# Q. What do people get wrong about coin laundries in Japan?｜Japan Questions #150

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/coin-laundries` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Travelling for two weeks with carry-on luggage depends on doing laundry. Japan has a dense network of self-service laundries, and they have modernised faster than most visitors expect — but a few conventions trip people up.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a coin laundry is a grim, cash-only, wait-around experience. Japan has roughly 27,000 self-service laundries, many open 24 hours, with app payment, combined washer-dryers, and machines that dispense detergent automatically. The main pitfalls are hot dryers that shrink clothes and washers that use cold water by default.

## Numbers and how it works

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Self-service laundries in Japan | **about 27,000** (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare survey) |
| Wash (up to ~9 kg) | **¥300–600** |
| Dry (gas dryer) | **¥100 per 8–10 minutes**; a full load needs **30–40 min** |
| Washer-dryer combined cycle | **¥1,000–1,500** for a full load, ~60 min |
| Hours | Many **24 hours**, unstaffed |

- **Detergent is usually dispensed automatically** by the machine; you do not need to bring any. Check for the sign 洗剤自動投入.
- **Wash water is cold** by default; warm-wash options are rare. Detergents are formulated for cold water.
- Dryers are **gas-powered and hot**; wool and delicate synthetics can shrink. Use the low-heat setting if offered, or air-dry.
- Many hotels have a **guest laundry corner** with one or two machines, often cheaper than commercial laundries.

> **[Verify]** Laundry counts with the MHLW "コインオペレーションクリーニング営業施設" survey; prices are observed 2025 ranges.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bring detergent from home

Most machines add it automatically, and **adding your own creates too many suds.** Look for the sign before you pour anything in.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Coins only

Coins remain standard at older shops, but chains increasingly take **IC cards, QR payments and their own apps** (which also tell you when the cycle ends).

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dryers are gentle

Japanese laundry dryers are typically **gas dryers**, faster and hotter than home electric ones. **Wool, elastane and printed T-shirts** suffer. Check the care label — see the article on reading care labels.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You must wait by the machine

Unstaffed shops expect you to **leave and come back**; taking someone else's finished laundry out and putting it on the folding table is considered acceptable if a machine is needed. Do not leave clothes long after the cycle ends.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Shoes go in the washer

Many laundries have a **dedicated sneaker washer and dryer** (スニーカーランドリー). Using the regular washer for shoes is frowned upon and may be prohibited by the shop.
