# Q. What do people get wrong about the kotatsu?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #151

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-kotatsu` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The heated low table with a quilt is one of the most photographed objects in Japanese homes. Outside Japan it reads as cosy furniture; inside Japan it is a heating strategy born of houses that were, until recently, barely insulated.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a kotatsu is a quaint alternative to central heating. It is a rational response to a specific housing stock: heat the person, not the room. Used with the room at a modest temperature, it cuts energy use sharply — and it has its own safety rules, chiefly about falling asleep under it and about what touches the heater.

## Why it exists

- Japan's housing stock is **poorly insulated by rich-country standards**: only about **13% of existing homes** met the 1999 energy-saving insulation standard as of 2018 (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism). New homes have been required to meet insulation standards only since **April 2025**.
- Heating the whole house was therefore expensive and leaky. The kotatsu — a **300–600 W** heater under a table, with a quilt to trap the warmth — heats **the people**, allowing the room to stay around **18–20°C**.
- The Ministry of the Environment's winter guidance ("Warm Biz") recommends a room temperature of **20°C** as a reference.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It heats the room

It heats **what is under the quilt**. This is the point: **head cool, feet warm** is comfortable at a lower room temperature, and that is where the saving comes from.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sleeping under it is the ultimate comfort

It is the **main cause of kotatsu-related harm**: dehydration, low-temperature burns from prolonged contact, and in older units overheating. Manufacturers and the National Institute of Technology and Evaluation (NITE) advise **not sleeping under it.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any table with a heater is a kotatsu

The modern electric type has a **flat heater unit with a guard**; old charcoal or "hori-gotatsu" (sunken pit) types still exist in rural houses and have carbon monoxide risk. Do not improvise one.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The quilt can be any blanket

The **kotatsu-gake** is a quilt sized to reach the floor on all sides and made of **flame-retardant** or at least non-synthetic-fluff fabric. Laundry drying **on or against the heater unit** is a known fire cause (NITE case reports).

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is only for old-fashioned homes

Sales remain steady, and **compact one-person kotatsu** for small flats and remote work are a growth segment. It pairs well with a **sensor-controlled thermostat** and a cheaper room temperature.
