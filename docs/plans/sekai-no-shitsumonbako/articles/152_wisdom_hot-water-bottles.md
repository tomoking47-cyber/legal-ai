# Q. What do people get wrong about the yutampo (hot-water bottle)?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #152

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/hot-water-bottles` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The yutampo — a metal, plastic or rubber hot-water bottle — never went away in Japan and has come back strongly as energy prices rose. It is cheap, silent and needs no electricity overnight. It also causes a specific injury every winter.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a hot-water bottle is harmless because the water is "only" hot. Low-temperature burns from prolonged skin contact at 44–50°C are the main risk, and they are worse than they look. The rule that Japanese households follow: wrap it, put it in the bed before you get in, and take it out or move it away from the skin when you sleep.

## The numbers behind the rule

| Contact temperature | Time to a low-temperature burn (guide) |
|---|---|
| **44°C** | about **3–4 hours** |
| **46°C** | about **30 minutes–1 hour** |
| **50°C** | about **2–3 minutes** |

These figures are cited by the Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE in their annual winter warnings. A wrapped yutampo surface sits in exactly this range for hours.

- Fill temperature: **hot but not boiling** for plastic and rubber types (typically **≤ 70–80°C**, per product); metal types take boiling water but **must be filled to the brim** so the metal does not deform as it cools.
- Cost: a **2 L** yutampo filled from a kettle uses roughly **0.2 kWh**, or about **¥6** at Tokyo rates — versus an electric blanket at ¥1–2 per hour.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If it does not feel hot, it is safe

Low-temperature burns happen **without pain at the time**, especially in sleep, in older people, and in people with reduced sensation (diabetes). **Warm the bed, then move the bottle away from the body.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜A thick cover makes it safe indefinitely

The cover slows heat transfer but the surface **still sits at 40–50°C for hours**. Covers are essential; they do not remove the time rule.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Microwave types are the same

Gel or grain types heated in a microwave have **their own heating time limits**; overheating has caused ruptures and burns. Follow the printed seconds exactly.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Metal ones can be filled loosely

A partly filled metal yutampo **can dent inward as it cools** and, if reheated on a stove with the cap on, **can burst**. Fill fully; never heat with the cap closed.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is for children and the elderly

Those are precisely the groups **most at risk of low-temperature burns**, because they may not notice or cannot move away. For children, warm the bed first and remove the bottle.
