# Q. What do people get wrong about the year-end clean (ōsōji)?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #153

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-year-end-clean` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Every December, Japanese households, schools and offices do a deep clean before the new year. It looks like a national chore; it is closer to a maintenance schedule with a fixed date, and it has a specific order that makes it work.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that ōsōji is a bigger version of weekly cleaning. It is a once-a-year pass over the things weekly cleaning skips — ventilation fans, window tracks, light fittings, the space behind appliances — done top to bottom and back to front, so that dust falls onto surfaces you have not yet cleaned.

## The order

| Step | Where | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Declutter and discard first (check **municipal collection dates** — many areas have final collections before the 28th–30th) | You cannot clean around things |
| 2 | **High places**: ceiling, light fittings, tops of shelves, curtain rails | Dust falls down |
| 3 | Walls, windows (inside and out), screens, tracks | Before floors |
| 4 | Kitchen: range hood/fan, stove, oven, fridge exterior and gasket | Grease needs time to soak — start early in the day |
| 5 | Bathroom, toilet, drains | Mould and scale need dwell time |
| 6 | **Floors last**, back to front toward the door | You do not walk on what you cleaned |
| 7 | Genkan (entrance) last of all | It is the boundary; finish there |

- The tradition traces to **susu-harai** ("soot sweeping"), historically held on **13 December** in the Edo period; many temples still do it on that day.
- Practical timing: most households clean between **26 and 30 December**; **31 December is traditionally avoided** as too rushed to welcome the new year.
- Municipal waste collection **pauses over the new year** (typically **31 December to 3 January**), so the discard step must come first.

> **[Verify]** Susu-harai date and collection schedules are general; check your own municipality's year-end calendar.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Start with the floor

Floors are **last**. Everything you clean above them drops dust and drips; doing them first means doing them twice.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Stronger chemicals mean faster

Grease and scale respond to **time and the right pH** more than to strength: alkaline (baking soda, sesquicarbonate) for grease; acidic (citric acid) for scale. **Never combine chlorine bleach with acids** — see the baking soda and citric acid article.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It has to be done in one day

Spreading it over **a week of short sessions** is how most households actually do it. A list on the fridge, one zone a day.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Outdoor windows in December are pointless

Cold, dry December air means **less pollen and fewer insects** than spring, and rain is rare in eastern Japan. Windows and screens cleaned now stay clean until pollen season.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is only about cleanliness

The functional payoff is **inspection**: you find the leaking hose, the dead battery in the smoke alarm, the gasket that no longer seals. Households often pair ōsōji with **replacing smoke-alarm batteries and checking the emergency kit.**
