# Q. What do people get wrong about cleaning with baking soda and citric acid?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #154

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/baking-soda-and-citric-acid` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Two cheap powders do most of the cleaning in many Japanese homes: sodium bicarbonate (baking soda, "jūsō") and citric acid ("kuen-san"). The approach is simple chemistry — match the cleaner's pH to the dirt — and it comes with one absolute rule about chlorine bleach.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that "natural" means interchangeable and harmless. Baking soda is a weak alkali for grease and odours; citric acid is a weak acid for limescale and soap scum. They do different jobs, they neutralise each other if combined, and citric acid or vinegar mixed with chlorine bleach releases toxic chlorine gas.

## Which powder for which dirt

| Dirt | pH of the dirt | Use | Do not use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grease, food splatter, body oils, sour smells | Acidic | **Baking soda** (pH ~8) or sesquicarbonate (pH ~9.8) | Citric acid (does nothing) |
| Limescale, kettle furring, soap scum, urine scale, ammonia smell | Alkaline | **Citric acid** (pH ~2) | Baking soda |
| Mould | — | Neither reliably; **chlorine-based mould remover, used alone, with ventilation** | Any acid in the same room at the same time |

- Baking soda is sold as a **food additive or a cleaning grade** — the cleaning grade is cheaper and not for eating.
- Citric acid **corrodes marble, some natural stone, iron and aluminium**; do not leave it on metal fittings.
- Baking soda's mild abrasiveness scratches **acrylic, lacquer and some coated pans**.

> **[Verify]** The chlorine-gas hazard is documented in the Consumer Affairs Agency and Tokyo Metropolitan Government safety advisories on "mazeru na kiken" (do not mix) labelling.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Mix baking soda and citric acid for a "power foam"

The fizz is carbon dioxide from **the two neutralising each other**. What remains is close to salt water. The bubbles can help lift loose debris in a drain, but as a cleaner the mixture is weaker than either powder alone.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Citric acid is a mild alternative to bleach

They are unrelated. **Citric acid or vinegar added to chlorine bleach or a chlorine mould remover releases chlorine gas.** Japanese chlorine products carry the legally required warning **まぜるな危険** ("Danger — do not mix"). Use on separate days, rinse well between.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Baking soda dissolves in cold water

Poorly. For a spray, use **warm water (about 1 teaspoon per 100 mL)**; for stubborn grease, sesquicarbonate dissolves better and is more alkaline. Wear gloves for prolonged use of either.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Citric acid is safe on everything in the bathroom

It etches **marble and travertine**, dulls **brass**, and can damage **iron fittings** if left on. Test, apply, wait 10–30 minutes, rinse thoroughly.

## Misunderstanding 5｜These replace all commercial cleaners

They handle most daily dirt. **Mould, blocked drains and disinfection** are where commercial products still do the job — used **one at a time**, following the label.
