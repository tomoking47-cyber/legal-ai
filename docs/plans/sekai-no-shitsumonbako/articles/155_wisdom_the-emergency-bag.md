# Q. What do people get wrong about the household emergency bag?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #155

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-emergency-bag` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Most Japanese households keep a packed bag by the door or in a cupboard, and municipalities publish checklists for it. The idea has spread abroad — often as a survival kit for wilderness scenarios, which is not what the Japanese bag is for.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the emergency bag is for surviving in the wild. It is a "grab bag" for the first day of evacuating to a shelter — light enough to carry while walking, packed with what a shelter does not provide (your medicines, glasses, documents, phone power, hygiene items). Bulk supplies stay at home as a separate stockpile.

## Two different things

| | Emergency bag (hijō-mochidashi-bukuro) | Home stockpile (bichiku) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Leave the house **within minutes** | Survive **at home** when supplies are cut |
| Weight | **Adult: about 10–15 kg maximum**; less for elderly and children | No limit |
| Duration | **First 1–3 days** | **At least 3 days; 7 days recommended** (Cabinet Office) |
| Location | **By the exit** or in the bedroom | Kitchen, cupboard, car |

### What the bag needs that shelters often lack (Cabinet Office / Tokyo Bōsai checklists)

- **Prescription medicines and a copy of the prescription**, spare glasses or contact lenses, hearing-aid batteries
- **Copies of ID and insurance cards**, a list of contacts on paper, some **cash in coins and small notes** (¥10 coins for payphones)
- **Phone charger and a power bank**, a small torch, a radio
- Water **500 mL × 2**, a few ready-to-eat items
- **Hygiene**: mask, wet wipes, hand sanitiser, portable toilet bags, sanitary items, a towel
- Season items: rain poncho, emergency blanket, hand warmers or a fan
- For infants, elderly, pets: their specific supplies

> **[Verify]** Weight guidance and stockpile durations with the Cabinet Office disaster-preparedness site and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's "Tokyo Bōsai" guide.

## Misunderstanding 1｜More is better

If you cannot carry it down stairs and walk **2 km**, it fails at its one job. Households do a **walk test** and strip it down.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Food and water are the priority

Shelters usually receive food and water within a day. **Medicines, glasses and documents** are what nobody else can supply for you.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Pack it once

Batteries drain, medicines expire, children grow, seasons change. Japanese households check it **twice a year**, often on **1 September (Disaster Prevention Day)** and **1 March**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜One bag for the family

**One bag per adult**, and a small bag for each child who can carry one. If one bag is lost or one person is away, the rest still have theirs.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Keep it in the deepest cupboard

**Route to the exit** decides the location: by the door, or beside the bed (earthquakes at night). Not in a room that may become inaccessible.
