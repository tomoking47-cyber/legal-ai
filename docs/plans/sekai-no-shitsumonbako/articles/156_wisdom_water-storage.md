# Q. What do people get wrong about storing water for emergencies?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #156

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/water-storage` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Water is the first supply to run short after a major earthquake, and in Japan the guidance on how much to keep is specific and widely known. The details — how long bottled water keeps, what the bath is for, how to rotate — are where households go wrong.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a few bottles in the cupboard is enough. The Cabinet Office guideline is 3 litres per person per day for drinking and cooking, for at least 3 days and ideally 7 — that is 21 litres per person for a week, before any water for hygiene. Bottled water past its date is usually still drinkable; the date is about volume loss, not safety.

## How much

| Household | 3 days (minimum) | 7 days (recommended) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 person | **9 L** | **21 L** |
| 2 people | 18 L | 42 L |
| 4 people | 36 L | **84 L** (that is 42 × 2 L bottles) |

- Basis: **3 L per person per day** for drinking and cooking (Cabinet Office, Tokyo Metropolitan Government).
- **Hygiene water** (toilet flushing, washing) is separate: households keep **bath water** in the tub overnight and use it for flushing, and store water in the **water heater tank** where fitted.
- After the 2011 Great East Japan Earthquake, water supply outages lasted **more than a week** in many affected municipalities; after the 2024 Noto earthquake, some areas waited **months**.

> **[Verify]** Per-person figures with the Cabinet Office; outage durations with MHLW water-supply damage reports for 2011 and 2024.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bottled water expires

The "best before" date on Japanese bottled water reflects **slow evaporation through the bottle**, which would make the labelled volume inaccurate under the Measurement Act. Sealed water stored away from heat and sunlight is generally considered drinkable after the date, though taste may change. **Long-term storage water (5–10-year) is simply thicker-walled bottles.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Tap water in bottles keeps as long

Filled tap water keeps its chlorine for about **3 days at room temperature** (Tokyo Waterworks). After that, use it for **hygiene, not drinking**, unless boiled.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The bath water is for drinking

It is for **flushing and washing**. A toilet flush uses **6–13 L** depending on the model; one bathtub (about **200 L**) covers a family for several days of flushing.

> **After a major earthquake, do not flush at all until the drainage pipes and the public sewer have been confirmed undamaged.** Water poured into a broken pipe can back up into the room. Tokyo's Bureau of Sewerage tells households to use a **portable toilet** (携帯トイレ) until the all-clear, and to keep at least a week's supply of them.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Store it all in one place

Split between the **kitchen, a cupboard on another floor, and the car**. A collapsed shelf or a room you cannot enter should not take all of it.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Buy it and forget it

Use **rolling stock**: drink from the stockpile, replace what you use, keep the total. This keeps the water fresh without a separate "emergency" purchase — see the rolling-stock article.
