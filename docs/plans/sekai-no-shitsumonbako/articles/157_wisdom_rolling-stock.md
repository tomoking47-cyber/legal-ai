# Q. What do people get wrong about "rolling stock" food storage?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #157

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/rolling-stock` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

"Rolling stock" (rōringu sutokku) is the Japanese government's name for a simple habit: keep a little more of the food you already eat, use the oldest first, and replace what you use. It turned emergency stockpiling from a separate shelf of unappetising rations into part of the weekly shop.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that emergency food means special survival rations. The Cabinet Office and Ministry of Agriculture recommend a rolling stock of ordinary shelf-stable foods you eat anyway — rice, retort curry, instant noodles, canned fish, dried foods — enough for 3 to 7 days, cycled through normal meals so nothing expires.

## How much, and of what

| Category | Per person for 7 days (Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries guide) |
|---|---|
| Water | **21 L** |
| Staple (rice, noodles, crackers) | about **21 meals' worth** (e.g. 2 kg rice + packets) |
| Main dishes (retort, canned) | about **21 items** |
| Extras (fruit, sweets, drinks) | as preferred — **morale matters** |

- The method: **stock a little more than usual → eat the oldest → replace on the next shop.** Aim to hold **3–7 days**.
- Include foods that need **no heating or water**: canned goods, crackers, jelly drinks. Then foods that need only hot water: cup noodles, alpha rice, instant miso soup.
- Have a way to heat water without electricity: a **cassette gas stove with 6–9 canisters** (MAFF suggests roughly **1 canister per day** for a family).

> **[Verify]** Quantities with MAFF's "Disaster Stockpile Guide for Households" and the Cabinet Office site; adjust for infants, elderly, allergies and medical diets.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Special emergency food is better

It is expensive, often unfamiliar, and forgotten until it expires. **Food you already like** gets eaten in a disaster — appetite drops under stress, and children refuse the unfamiliar.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Instant noodles are enough

Salt-heavy, low in protein and fibre, and needing hot water. Balance with **canned fish, beans, retort dishes, dried vegetables, and vitamin sources** (canned fruit, juice).

## Misunderstanding 3｜Keep it separate from the kitchen

The point is integration: it lives **in the pantry**, eaten in rotation. A separate box gets ignored.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Check it once a year

**Every shop** is a check: when you take one, you buy one. Many households also do a full **"stockpile dinner"** once a month to keep things moving.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Rolling stock is only for earthquakes

The same shelf covered households during **the 2020 supply disruptions and typhoon-related shop closures.** Illness and heavy snow are more frequent than earthquakes.
