# Q. What do people get wrong about opening windows and ventilating a home in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #158

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/ventilation-habits` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Opening windows morning and evening is a household reflex in Japan, reinforced during 2020 by public guidance that gave it numbers. Visitors often assume it is about fresh air; it is at least as much about moisture and mould.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that ventilation is a nice-to-have in a country with air conditioning. In a humid climate with tightly built modern flats, moving air is how households keep relative humidity below the mould threshold and clear the moisture of cooking, bathing and drying laundry indoors. The guidance is concrete: two openings, twice an hour for a few minutes, or the mechanical vent left running.

## The guidance

| Method | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Window ventilation | **Open two windows on opposite sides for 5–10 minutes, twice an hour** | MHLW 2020 ventilation guidance |
| Mechanical (24-hour) ventilation | **Required in all new homes since 2003**; 0.5 air changes per hour | Building Standards Act amendment |
| Target humidity | **40–60%**; mould accelerates above **70%** | Various public health guidance |
| Bathroom | Run the fan **during and for 1–2 hours after** bathing, door closed | Tokyo Metropolitan Government mould guidance |

- The 2003 requirement for **24-hour mechanical ventilation** followed cases of "sick house syndrome" from formaldehyde in tightly built homes. The small vents in walls and the bathroom fan that never turns off are that system — **do not switch it off or tape the vents**.
- Ventilating in the **morning** clears overnight moisture (breathing, futon); in the **evening** it clears cooking moisture.

> **[Verify]** Guidance figures with the MHLW 2020 documents and the Building Standards Act provisions on mechanical ventilation.

## Misunderstanding 1｜One window is enough

Air needs an **inlet and an outlet**. One open window mainly lets air stand. **Diagonal pairs** — windows on opposite walls — move it through.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Air conditioning ventilates

Most home air conditioners **recirculate** indoor air and do not exchange it with outside. They control temperature and (when dehumidifying) moisture, not freshness.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Do not ventilate in the rainy season — it lets humidity in

On a rainy day, outdoor humidity is high, but **indoor humidity from laundry and bathing is often higher still**. Short ventilation plus a dehumidifier or air-conditioner dry mode is the usual combination. Long open windows on rainy days do more harm.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The little wall vents are draughts to be blocked

They are the **intake for the mandatory 24-hour ventilation**. Blocking them in a tightly built flat pushes humidity up and condensation onto north-facing walls — which is where the mould starts.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Winter ventilation just wastes heat

Ten minutes with two windows costs little heat compared with the moisture problem it solves: **condensation on windows and in wardrobes**. Households ventilate in the **warmest part of the day** in winter.
