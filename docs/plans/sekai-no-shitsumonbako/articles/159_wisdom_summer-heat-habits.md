# Q. What do people get wrong about Japanese summer heat habits?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #159

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/summer-heat-habits` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese summers combine 35°C days with 70–80% humidity, and the country records tens of thousands of heatstroke ambulance calls a year. The everyday responses — from the salt sweets in every office to the wet towels around the neck — look like folklore. Most map directly onto public-health guidance.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese summer customs are quaint. Uchimizu (sprinkling water), sudare blinds, the neck towel and the hand fan are low-cost interventions aimed at the same things the Ministry of the Environment's heat guidance targets: radiant heat, evaporation, and keeping the body's cooling working. The one custom worth dropping is "enduring without air conditioning".

## Habits and what they do

| Habit | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Uchimizu** (sprinkling water on pavements at dusk) | Evaporative cooling of the surface; works when the sun is off the ground, not at noon |
| **Sudare / yoshizu** (reed blinds outside the window) | Blocks solar gain **before** it reaches the glass — more effective than curtains inside |
| **Neck towel, cooling towel** | Cools blood near the surface; wet and re-wet |
| **Salt sweets, barley tea** | Replaces sodium and fluid lost in sweat — barley tea is caffeine-free and the standard summer drink |
| **Hand fan (sensu/uchiwa)** | Moves air over sweating skin; no battery |
| **Yukata, linen, loose cuts** | Airflow and evaporation |

## Misunderstanding 1｜Air conditioning is unhealthy; endure it

**About 40% of heatstroke transports are from homes**, and older people are the majority. The Ministry of the Environment says to **use air conditioning without hesitation**, aiming for a room around **28°C**, and to check on elderly relatives who "do not feel hot".

## Misunderstanding 2｜Drink only when thirsty

Thirst lags need, especially in older people. Guidance: **drink regularly, about 1.2 L a day beyond meals in ordinary conditions**, and add **salt (0.1–0.2%) when sweating heavily.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Uchimizu at midday cools the street

At midday the water evaporates instantly and the humidity rises. Households sprinkle **in the early morning and at dusk**, on shaded ground.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Curtains block the heat

Once sunlight is through the glass, much of the heat is inside. **Exterior shading** — sudare, awnings, a green curtain of climbing plants — is why old houses were survivable.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Children in strollers are in the shade

A stroller seat sits **close to the hot pavement**, often several degrees hotter than at adult head height, and the canopy traps heat. Use a **sunshade with airflow, a wet towel, and check the child's back**.
