# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about exfoliation (scrubs and peels)?｜Japan Questions #162

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/exfoliation` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Removing" dead skin is one of the most divisive topics in overseas skincare communities. In Japan, the most a cosmetic exfoliant may claim is that it "removes old dead skin cells" — nothing beyond that is allowed. This article explains what that means for the products on drugstore shelves (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.) and how people here use them.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "dead skin is bad, so the more you remove, the better your skin looks." The stratum corneum (角質層, the outermost layer) holds water in the skin and shields it from outside irritants. The basic rules of exfoliation are: keep to the stated frequency, and stop if you get redness or stinging.

## What to base your decision on

| Type | How it works | Frequency guide (follow the product label) | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scrub (physical) | Particles rub the surface off | **Once or twice a week** | Rubbing too hard; coarse particles |
| Wipe-off toner (拭き取り化粧水, fukitori keshōsui) | Alcohol or acid wipes the surface | Daily-use to a few times a week | Irritation from alcohol |
| AHA / BHA products (chemical) | Acids loosen dead cells | From **once or twice a week** | **Sunscreen is essential**; do not layer with other irritating actives |
| Enzyme face wash (酵素洗顔, kōso sengan) | Protein-digesting enzymes | Once or twice a week | Go easy if your skin dries out easily |

- The stratum corneum is generally described as **about 0.02 mm** thick, with cells renewing on a cycle of **roughly 28 days** (longer with age). **Removing too much means outrunning that cycle.**
- The effects a cosmetic may claim are within the 56 items, such as **"removes old dead skin cells" and "smooths the skin"**. **"Normalises skin turnover" cannot be said of a cosmetic under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law).**
- **Chemical peels at a medical clinic** are a medical procedure; the concentrations and purpose differ from cosmetic products.

> **[Verify]** The thickness and renewal cycle of the stratum corneum are general dermatology descriptions; check against Japanese Dermatological Association materials. The 56 items are in MHLW notice Yakushoku-hatsu 0721 No. 1.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Scrub every day and your skin stays smooth

**Over-scrubbing thins the stratum corneum and leaves skin dry, red and easily irritated.** Follow the frequency on the label and **cut back according to how your skin feels.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Stinging means it is working

It is an **irritation reaction**. The rule is to **rinse it off at once and stop using it.** If it persists, see a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).

## Misunderstanding 3｜Acid peels are fine without sunscreen

Skin whose dead cells have been loosened by acid is generally described as **more vulnerable to UV.** The usual practice is to **wear sunscreen over it in the daytime**, or to **use the product at night.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Layering several exfoliants works faster

Stacking irritants — scrub plus acid plus a retinol-type product — is **the classic route to skin trouble.** Try **one type at a time, with a gap between them.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Dark spots and roughness are dead skin, so remove it and they go

**Dark pores are often oxidised sebum**, and **roughness is often dryness**; exfoliating may change neither. **Work out the cause first** (see our article on pore care).
