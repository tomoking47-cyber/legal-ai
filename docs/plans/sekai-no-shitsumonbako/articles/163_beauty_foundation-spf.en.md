# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about SPF in foundation?｜Japan Questions #163

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/foundation-spf` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"If my foundation has SPF, do I still need sunscreen?" is one of the most common make-up questions in every country. The answer depends on what an SPF number actually means and how much product you really apply. Japanese labels also carry a "PA" rating you may not have seen at home; this article explains both.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "SPF30 foundation gives the same protection as SPF30 sunscreen." SPF is measured with a fixed amount of product (2 mg per cm² of skin), and foundation is applied at a fraction of that. The practical approach is to wear sunscreen underneath and regard the foundation as a back-up.

## In numbers

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| SPF test conditions | **2 mg per cm² of skin** (Japan Cosmetic Industry Association SPF test method / ISO 24444) |
| Amount needed for the whole face | About **0.8 g** (the size of a 500-yen coin, or two pearls) |
| Typical amount of foundation applied | **About 0.2–0.5 g** (**a quarter to a half** of the test amount) |
| Highest SPF that may be shown | **SPF50+** (JCIA voluntary standard) |
| PA grades | **PA+ to PA++++** (Japan's four-grade rating for UVA protection) |

When you apply half the amount, protection is generally described as **falling by more than half, not simply to half the SPF value** (protection changes exponentially with the amount applied).

> **[Verify]** Check the test method and labelling rules against the JCIA "SPF Test Method Standard" and "Voluntary Standard on PA Labelling". Application amounts vary by product and face size.

## Misunderstanding 1｜SPF foundation means no sunscreen is needed

**You apply too little for the labelled protection.** Even for an ordinary commute, the basic routine is **sunscreen as a base, foundation on top.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜SPF30 sunscreen + SPF20 foundation = SPF50

**They do not add up.** The ceiling is the higher of the two values, and in practice it is lower because too little is applied.

## Misunderstanding 3｜You cannot reapply over make-up, so give up

People in Japan share ways of reapplying over make-up: **a powder foundation or face powder with SPF on top**, or **a sunscreen spray or stick.** The usual guide is **every 2–3 hours.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜No SPF needed on cloudy days

**Taking a clear sky as the baseline, thin cloud still lets through about 80–90% of the UV, overcast cloud about 60%, and rain about 30%**, according to the Japan Meteorological Agency. UVA also passes through window glass.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Higher SPF is worse for the skin

There is **no direct link** between the SPF value and how irritating a product is. People who worry about irritation often choose products based on **physical UV blockers (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide)**, and **whether one suits your skin varies from person to person.**
