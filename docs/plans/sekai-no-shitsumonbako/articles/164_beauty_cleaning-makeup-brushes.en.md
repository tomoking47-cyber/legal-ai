# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about washing make-up brushes and sponges?｜Japan Questions #164

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/cleaning-makeup-brushes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Overseas communities mostly ask "how often should I wash my tools?"; in Japan the usual question is "how do I wash them without ruining them?". This article covers both, using products you can find in a Japanese drugstore (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.) or 100-yen shop.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "if it does not look dirty, it does not need washing." Sponges and brushes collect sebum, foundation and the bacteria that normally live on the skin, and can be a cause of breakouts. As a guide: wash liquid-foundation sponges every use to every few days, brushes once a week, and dry them completely before use.

## Guidelines

| Tool | How often (guide) | How to wash | How to dry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponge for liquid or cream | **Every use to every 2–3 uses** | Press-wash in neutral detergent (中性洗剤, chūsei senzai — mild washing-up liquid) or a dedicated brush cleaner | In a well-ventilated place, **completely** |
| Powder brush | **About once a week** | Lukewarm water plus brush cleaner or diluted neutral detergent. **Keep the base dry** | Bristles pointing down, laid flat |
| Liquid brush | **Every few uses** | As above. Remove oil with a make-up cleanser | As above |
| Powder puff | **Once a week** | Press-wash in neutral detergent | Dry completely |
| Replacement | Sponge: **1–3 months** ／ Brush: when bristles shed, splay or smell | | |

- A damp sponge kept in a closed pouch **can grow mould.** Put it away **only when dry.**
- Switching to disposable sponges or cotton pads is another hygiene habit shared among people in Japan.

> **[Verify]** Frequencies and replacement intervals are guides based on manufacturers' instructions. Check cases of skin trouble against the consultation records of the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Boiling water disinfects them

Boiling water **dissolves the adhesive and damages natural bristles.** **Lukewarm water** is the rule. Some people use **disinfecting ethanol (消毒用エタノール, sold at drugstores) after checking the label allows it**, but it makes sponges deteriorate faster.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wash a brush thoroughly right down to the base

**If water gets into the base (the metal ferrule), the adhesive loosens and bristles fall out.** Dip only the tips into the cleaning solution.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A hair dryer speeds up drying

Heat **frizzes bristles and warps sponges.** **Air-dry**, with at most the breeze from a fan.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A washed sponge lasts for years

Even when washed, **a sponge loses its bounce and dirt stays in tiny tears.** 1–3 months is the guide. **If it still smells after washing, or crumbles, replace it.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Breakouts are only ever caused by the cosmetics

**Dirty tools** can be the cause. Many people report that **when changing products did not help, washing or replacing the tools did.** **If symptoms continue, see a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).**
