# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about using a hair dryer?｜Japan Questions #165

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/using-a-hair-dryer` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The idea that "air-drying is kinder to your hair" is deeply rooted in many countries. In Japan most people bathe daily and wash their hair in the evening, so drying it before bed is a fixed habit, and a good deal of practical know-how about hair dryers has built up around it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "hair dryers damage hair, so air-drying is better." Wet hair has an open surface and is weak against friction; sleeping with wet hair means rubbing against the pillow and a sweaty scalp. The guide is: towel-dry thoroughly, hold the dryer 15–20 cm away and keep it moving, dry from the roots, and finish with cool air.

## In numbers

| Item | Guide |
|---|---|
| Air temperature at the nozzle | **Around 100–120°C** on many models (at close range the hair surface heats up very fast) |
| Distance from the hair | **15–20 cm** |
| Hair surface temperature | Keep the dryer moving so it **does not exceed 60–70°C** (the approach taken in product manuals) |
| Order | **Roots → mid-lengths → ends**, then **cool air** to settle the surface |
| Saving time | **Towel-dry carefully** (press with a highly absorbent towel); **choose a model with strong airflow** |

- Hair dryers fall under the Electrical Appliance and Material Safety Act (the PSE mark). **Dust clogging the air intake** can cause overheating and fire. The National Institute of Technology and Evaluation (NITE) warns people to **clean the intake** and to watch for **twisted or frayed cords.**
- Wet hair is generally described as having **open cuticles and being weak against rubbing.** Sleeping with wet hair is linked to **breakage from friction and a poorer scalp environment from a damp pillow.**

> **[Verify]** Temperatures and distances are guides from major manufacturers' instruction manuals. Check accident cases against NITE's product accident database.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Air-drying does the least damage

**The longer hair stays wet, the longer it stays weak against friction.** The scalp also stays damp. **Drying quickly** is generally described as easier on both hair and scalp.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Blasting with hot air is the efficient way

Holding hot air on one spot **raises the surface temperature and leads to dryness and coarseness.** **Keep moving and spread the heat** across the whole head.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Start with the ends

The ends dry easily; **the roots are the hardest part to dry.** Starting **from the roots** avoids over-drying the ends.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The cool setting is pointless

A final blast of cool air **settles the opened surface, adding shine and hold.** It also **cools a scalp that has built up heat.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Leaving it plugged in by the bathroom sink is convenient

**Electric shock near water, frayed cords and dust in the intake** are causes of accidents. NITE's advice is to **unplug after use and not wrap the cord around the body** (a cause of breaks at the base of the cord).
