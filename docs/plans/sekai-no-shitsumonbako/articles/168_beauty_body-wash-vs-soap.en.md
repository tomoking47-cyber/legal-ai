# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about body wash versus bar soap?｜Japan Questions #168

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/body-wash-vs-soap` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Which is better for the skin, soap or body wash?" comes up regularly in every country. Japan has a strong bar-soap tradition that survived the arrival of body wash, so both are in daily use and there is a lot of practical experience to compare. This article also explains the "medicated" label you will see on some Japanese body washes.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "one of them is definitely better for the skin." The difference lies mainly in the cleansing agent (soap is alkaline; most body washes are neutral to mildly acidic) and in convenience, and which suits your skin varies from person to person. What they share is: wash with the foam, rinse thoroughly, and avoid hot water.

## Comparison

| Item | Bar soap | Body wash |
|---|---|---|
| Main cleansing agent | **Soap (sodium salts of fatty acids etc.)**, **mildly alkaline (pH 9–10)** | Synthetic surfactants or soap-based. Most products are **neutral to mildly acidic** |
| Cleansing power | Removes sebum well | Varies widely between products |
| Convenience | Needs lathering; many people use a foaming net | A fixed dose from a pump; foam types also exist |
| Storage | **Must drain** (left wet it dissolves and harbours bacteria) | Keep the nozzle clean |
| Legal category | Cosmetic or general goods (**"toilet soap" (化粧石けん) is a cosmetic**) | Cosmetic or quasi-drug (medicated) |

- **A body wash labelled "medicated" (薬用, yakuyō) is a quasi-drug (a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines)**; it contains **antibacterial ingredients** and may state approved effects such as **"prevents body odour and sweat odour" and "prevents acne".** In the cosmetic category, claims stay within the range of **"cleanses the skin".**
- The skin surface is generally described as **mildly acidic (pH 4.5–6)**. Straight after washing with soap it shifts towards alkaline, but **healthy skin is described as returning within a few hours.**

> **[Verify]** The pH figures are general dermatology descriptions. Check the range of quasi-drug claims against MHLW materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Body wash is too weak to get you clean

It varies by product. **"Thorough" and "refreshing" (さっぱり) types strip sebum strongly; "moisturising" (しっとり) types are gentler.** **Most dirt is generally described as coming off well enough with foam and warm water.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Soap is bad for the skin because it is alkaline

Healthy skin **restores its pH within a few hours.** People **prone to dryness and the elderly** often choose products with moisturising ingredients or lower alkalinity; **what suits you varies from person to person.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Scrubbing hard with a nylon towel is the Japanese way

**Friction from a nylon wash towel is known in dermatology as a cause of pigmentation (friction melanosis).** The current general advice is to **roll the foam over the skin with your hands or a soft towel.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜You must wash the whole body with soap every day

Dermatology materials present the view that the sweatier, dirtier areas (**armpits, feet, neck, groin, back**) need daily washing, while **areas with little sebum such as the arms and legs can have water-only days.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Leftover foam will rinse away by itself

**Unrinsed foam collects on the back, the nape and behind the ears** and can cause rashes. **Spend as long rinsing as you spent washing.**
