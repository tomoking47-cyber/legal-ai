# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about caring for oily skin?｜Japan Questions #170

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/oily-skin-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Shine and excess sebum are a particular worry in humid East Asia, and in overseas communities the question "is more washing better?" comes up again and again. In Japan there is a clear line between what a cosmetic may claim and what belongs to a dermatologist, and this article follows that line. It also introduces a few everyday items, such as oil-blotting paper, that you will find in any Japanese drugstore.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "sebum is the enemy, so strip it all away." Sebum also protects the skin. Strip too much and secretion is generally described as increasing to make up for the dryness — a state often called "inner dry" (インナードライ, a Japanese term for skin that is oily on the surface but dehydrated underneath). The basic approach is: wash no more than twice a day, and never skip moisturising.

## What to base your decision on

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| How often to wash | **Twice a day, morning and evening**, is the usual guide. More than that tends to cause dryness |
| Face wash | **Wash with foam and rinse with lukewarm water (about 32–34°C / 90–93°F).** Hot water strips too much sebum |
| Moisturising | **Do not skip it.** Many people choose low-oil gel or milky-lotion types |
| Sebum during the day | **Press with oil-blotting paper (あぶらとり紙, aburatori-gami) or a tissue.** Do not rub |
| Sebum and the seasons | **Sebum secretion is generally described as rising with temperature**, so the problem worsens in summer |

- What a cosmetic may claim is within the 56 items, such as **"cleanses the skin (by removing dirt)", "tightens the skin" and "supplies and retains moisture and oil in the skin".** **"Suppresses sebum secretion" cannot be said of a cosmetic** (it is in the territory of approved quasi-drug claims; a quasi-drug is a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines).
- **Recurring or inflamed acne** is beyond the scope of cosmetics and is **a matter for a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).** The Japanese Dermatological Association publishes **clinical guidelines for acne vulgaris.**

> **[Verify]** Check the 56 items against MHLW notice Yakushoku-hatsu 0721 No. 1, and the guidelines against the Japanese Dermatological Association "Guidelines for the Management of Acne Vulgaris and Rosacea 2023".

## Misunderstanding 1｜Wash more often and the shine goes

Many people report that **over-washing causes dryness and makes sebum more noticeable, not less.** **How you wash, and moisturising, matter more than how often.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Oily skin does not need moisturiser

**Water and oil are separate things.** When water is lacking, the surface is described as shining with sebum while the skin underneath is dry. **Moisturising with a light, "refreshing" (さっぱり) type** is the standard approach.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Blotting paper should take off all the oil

**Taking too much leads to dryness.** Most people **press lightly on the T-zone only.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜An alcohol toner to tighten the skin solves it

It may feel fresh for a moment, but **alcohol can cause dryness and irritation.** Whether it suits you **varies from person to person.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Oily skin is just your constitution, so nothing helps

It changes with **sleep, diet, season and hormones.** If acne is involved, a dermatology clinic can prescribe **medicines covered by health insurance.** There is no need to keep struggling within the limits of cosmetics.

### About this article

What we share here are **ideas and everyday practices passed down among people living in Japan,** together with general information drawn from public sources. They are not official recommendations, and they are not professional instruction. Practices vary between households and regions.
