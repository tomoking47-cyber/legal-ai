# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing shoes?｜Japan Questions #172

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/storing-shoes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan you take your shoes off at the door, so they gather in the genkan (entrance hall) and storing them is an everyday problem. Add the humid climate, and it is no surprise that people here share a great many tips for making shoes last.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "put them in a box in the closet and they are safe". In Japanese housing, damp breeds mould, and synthetic-leather soles crumble through hydrolysis. The basics are "one day worn, one day rested", "let the damp out before putting them away", and "air them regularly".

## What to go by

| Item | Guide | Why |
|---|---|---|
| After wearing | **Dry overnight somewhere well ventilated** (do not put them straight into the shoe cabinet) | Feet are generally described as giving off **about a cup of sweat (roughly 200 mL) a day** |
| Rotation | **Do not wear the same pair on consecutive days** | The damp never fully leaves |
| Shoe cabinet | **Dehumidifier packs (silica gel, etc.)**; **open the doors regularly** | Kept shut, humidity climbs above 70% |
| Long-term storage | **Clean them, dry them, wrap in newspaper or non-woven fabric**. Shoe trees keep the shape | Mould, loss of shape |
| Synthetic leather and trainer soles | **Hydrolysis within a few years** (it progresses even when unworn) | The nature of polyurethane |

- **Mould thrives at humidity of 70% or more and 20–30°C.** A shoe cabinet by the front door comes close to those conditions once rainy-day shoes go in.
- **If leather gets wet, stuff it with newspaper and dry in the shade**; apply cream once dry. **Hairdryers and direct sun stiffen leather.**

> **[Verify]** The sweat figure is a general physiology statement. Check the mould conditions in the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Bureau of Public Health material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The box they came in is the best place to keep them

**Cardboard absorbs moisture and releases it again.** If you use the box, **add a desiccant and open it regularly**, or use **a case with ventilation holes**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sealed in a plastic bag, they cannot go mouldy

**The opposite is true.** The damp is trapped inside. **A non-woven bag** or **a breathable cloth** is the basic choice.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Trainers do not deteriorate if you never wear them

**The polyurethane in the sole hydrolyses even when unworn.** That is why trainers **crumble a few years after being put away**. **Wearing them now and then, and storing them somewhere dry**, can slow this down but cannot stop it.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cream leather shoes as soon as they are dry

**Applying cream before wet leather has dried right through traps the moisture and causes mould.** **Only once fully dry**, and thinly.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A shoe-cabinet deodoriser also removes damp

**Deodorisers and dehumidifiers are different things.** For damp, use **silica gel or calcium-chloride types** (除湿剤, joshitsuzai); for smells, **activated charcoal and the like**. **Once the damp goes, the smell often lessens too.**
