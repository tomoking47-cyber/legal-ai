# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about shrunken knitwear?｜Japan Questions #174

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/shrunken-knitwear` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"I washed my jumper and now it fits a child" is a failure story told the world over. Here we explain, from the way the fibre works, why knitwear shrinks, whether it can be brought back, and in which cases it cannot.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding sits at both extremes: "shrunken knitwear can never be restored" and "it is easy to restore". Wool shrinkage comes in two kinds. "Felting", where the fibres lock together, does not reverse. Shrinkage from drying or from the yarn relaxing can sometimes be stretched back with lukewarm water and a conditioner-type soak. Prevention is the surest route.

## The two kinds of shrinkage

| Type | What is happening | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|
| **Felting (shrink-milling)** | The scales on the surface of the wool tangle together through **water, heat and friction**; the fabric becomes stiff and thick | **No** |
| **Relaxation shrinkage** | Yarn that was stretched during knitting and finishing returns to its original length in water. The fabric stays soft | **Can recover to some extent** |

- **The bar under the tub on the care label** (gentle process) and **the temperature number** are precisely there to prevent felting.
- A "restoration" people try at home: **dissolve a little hair conditioner or fabric softener in lukewarm water at about 30°C (86°F), soak for 30 minutes → spin briefly → lay flat and stretch a little at a time → reshape and dry flat.** It can work **if the fabric has not gone stiff**.

> **[Verify]** The restoration method is a general one described by textile bodies and the dry-cleaning trade; results cannot be assured. Check the meaning of the symbols on the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Only high temperature causes shrinkage

It is the combination of **temperature, water and friction (rubbing, a hard spin, the turning of a tumble dryer)**. **Even in cold water, hard rubbing shrinks it.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Stretching it with steam always brings it back

**Felted fabric does not stretch.** For a piece that has gone stiff and felt-like, people in Japan have the idea of **remaking it into small accessories**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The dry cleaner can restore it

**Sometimes yes, sometimes no.** Restoration at the cleaner is also limited to relaxation shrinkage. **Ask "will it come back?" when you hand it in.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Fabric softener prevents shrinkage

Softener **reduces friction and static**, but **cannot prevent shrinkage caused by heat and strong mechanical action.** Prevention is **the labelled temperature, press-washing and drying flat**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Acrylic and polyester knits do not shrink

Synthetic fibres **do not felt the way wool does**, but **at high temperature (tumble dryer, boiling water) they can shrink or stiffen**. The temperature on the label matters for synthetics too.
