# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about when to replace socks and underwear?｜Japan Questions #175

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/when-to-replace-socks-and-underwear` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"How many years do socks last?" and "When should I replace underwear?" are surprisingly common questions on forums worldwide. Japan has a habit of talking about a sock's "lifespan", and makers' and retailers' guides and everyday judgement are shared openly. Here is what people go by.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "you can use them until they get a hole". Socks and underwear lose their function even without a visible hole: the elastic stretches, the fabric thins, and smells linger. The guides are six months to a year for socks (depending on how often they are worn), six months to a year for underwear, and around 100 wears for a bra.

## Guides

| Item | When to replace | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| Socks (a daily rotation of about 10 pairs) | **6 months to 1 year** | Heel thin enough to see through, loose elastic, pilling, a smell that survives washing |
| Undershirts (T-shirts, vests) | **6 months to 1 year** | Stretched neckline, yellowing, thin fabric |
| Pants and knickers | **6 months to 1 year** | Stretched elastic, fabric going sheer |
| Bras | **Around 100 wears** (about a year at twice a week) | Bent wire, creased cups, loose even on the innermost hook |
| Sock elastic | — | Marks stay on the skin for a long time; the socks slide down |

- **Polyester blends and nylon reinforcement** last longer than all-cotton, and **reinforcing yarn at heel and toe** changes the lifespan.
- Wash **in a mesh bag, inside out, at low temperature**; **a tumble dryer speeds up shrinkage and the deterioration of the elastic** (check the care label).
- Socks and underwear fall under the **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act** and carry **fibre composition and care labels**.

> **[Verify]** The guides are general figures based on guidance from textile and apparel trade bodies and major makers; they vary greatly with the product and how it is used.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If there is no hole, it works just the same

**Socks with thin heels slip inside the shoe and wear the shoe's lining too.** Undershirts with slack elastic stop absorbing sweat.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Expensive ones last for years

Price and durability do not match. **Reinforcing yarn, knit density and how you wash** decide the lifespan far more.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Bleach brings yellowed items back

Yellowing is **oxidised skin oil**. Oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) can lighten it, but **repeated bleaching weakens the fabric.** In practice, **by the time it yellows, the fabric is at its limit too**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A bra just goes in the machine and on the line

**The wire and cups deform under the machine's force.** The basics are **hand-washing, or a mesh bag on a gentle cycle, hooks fastened, cups reshaped before drying**; **resting bras in rotation** makes them last longer.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Replacing them is wasteful

People in Japan cut waste by **"buying several identical pairs, so an odd sock can be re-paired when its partner wears out"**, and by **turning old underwear into cleaning rags**.
