# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about caring for glasses?｜Japan Questions #178

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/glasses-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Wiping your lenses on your shirt is a habit the world over, and it is the one Japanese opticians warn about most. Japan has a large eyewear industry (Sabae city in Fukui prefecture makes over 90% of the country's frames), and here are the care basics shared within it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "any soft cloth will do for lenses". Wiping them dry scratches the lens with the dust sitting on it. The basics are "rinse with water → neutral detergent → rinse → blot with a tissue → lens cloth". Hot water and alcohol damage the coatings.

## The steps, and what to avoid

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Rinse under the tap (cool water, not warm)** to wash the dust off |
| 2 | **One drop of neutral (washing-up) detergent**, worked gently with the fingertips |
| 3 | Rinse the detergent off completely |
| 4 | **Blot the water off with a tissue** (do not rub) |
| 5 | Finish with a **lens cloth** |

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| **Hot water and heat** (lenses risk heat cracks from around **60°C / 140°F**) | The base material expands and the coating cracks |
| **Alcohol, sanitising wipes, acetone** | Can damage the coating |
| **Dry wiping** | The dust on the lens scratches it |
| **Soap, alkaline or acidic cleaners** | Discolour the coating and frame |
| Saunas, leaving them in a car | Heat deforms the lens and frame |

- Lens **coatings (anti-reflective, scratch-resistant)** are several thin layers, and their common weakness is **heat and chemicals**.
- **The nose pads and the parts behind the ears** collect skin oil, sweat and make-up, which cause **verdigris (green copper corrosion)** and discolouration. Have them replaced at an optician regularly (often free, or a few hundred yen).
- **About 95% of the frames made in Japan come from Fukui prefecture** (the production area centred on Sabae and Fukui cities) (Fukui prefecture material).

> **[Verify]** Check the hot-water temperature guide and the detergent advice with lens makers and the Japan Optical Industry Federation (日本眼鏡関連団体連合会). Check the Sabae production share against Fukui prefecture's published material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Dry-wiping with a lens cloth is the right way

**The lens cloth is for finishing.** Wiping with dust still on the lens causes **scratches**. **Rinse first.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Hot water removes skin oil better

**Heat cracks the coating.** The usual guide is **60°C / 140°F or above**, though it can happen lower depending on how long and in what conditions. Wash with **cold water.** Wearing glasses in the bath or a sauna is avoided for the same reason.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sanitising wipes keep them clean

**The alcohol can damage the coating.** **Neutral detergent and water** are enough.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Straighten a bent frame yourself

**Plastic snaps, and metal keeps the distortion.** Leave it to the optician's adjustment (often free of charge).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Lenses can be used until they are scratched

**Deterioration of the coating and micro-scratches cloud your vision and tire your eyes.** Opticians generally advise **a check every 2–3 years**, including a review of the prescription.
