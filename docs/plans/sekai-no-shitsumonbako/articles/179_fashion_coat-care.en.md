# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about caring for coats?｜Japan Questions #179

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/coat-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Many people dry-clean a coat only once a season, so "how do I look after it in between?" is asked everywhere. In Japan there is a habit of brushing a coat and airing it in the shade after coming home, shared as a way of cutting down on dry-cleaning. Here is how it works.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "if the coat does not look dirty, nothing needs doing". A wool coat takes in dust and moisture every time it is worn; left alone, that leads to moth damage and shine. The basics are "brush after coming home → air overnight in the shade → rotate", and dry-cleaning is usually once, at the end of the season.

## Everyday care

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| After coming home | **Clothes brush, top to bottom**, to remove dust. Concentrate on shoulders, collar, cuffs and hem | Dust feeds moths and abrades the fibres |
| Overnight | **Air in the shade on a thick hanger** (do not put it straight into the wardrobe) | Lets sweat and damp out |
| Rotation | **Do not wear the same coat on consecutive days** | Lets the fibres recover |
| Rain | **Blot with a towel and air in the shade.** No hairdryer | Shrinkage, shine |
| End of season | **Dry-clean → remove the plastic, use a non-woven cover**, moth repellent on top | Leaves no sweat or skin oil behind |

- **Shine on wool** is fibres flattened by friction reflecting the light. **Holding steam just above the surface and lifting the nap with a brush** can make it less visible.
- The **circle symbols (P, F, W)** on the care label show the type of professional cleaning. **W is wet cleaning, which cannot be reproduced at home.**

> **[Verify]** Check the meaning of the symbols on the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page. The care frequency is general guidance from textile bodies and the dry-cleaning trade.

## Misunderstanding 1｜More dry-cleaning means cleaner

**Repeated dry-cleaning strips the natural oils and the handle of the fabric deteriorates.** The general guide is **once a season, before putting the coat away**. Sweaty areas are managed with brushing and airing.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Brushing is only for looks

**Dust and food crumbs are the main cause of moth damage.** Brushing also **settles the nap and drives damp out from between the fibres**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Keep it in the dry-cleaner's plastic

**The plastic traps damp and causes mould and discolouration.** **Remove it and switch to a non-woven cover.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Removing pills does more harm than good

**Left alone, pills spread and pull in the surrounding fibres.** Use a pill remover or small scissors and take **only the surface** (see our article on pilling).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Down coats do not get dirty, so they need no care

**Skin-oil marks at collar and cuffs are just the same on down.** Combine **spot-washing** with **a full wash following the label** (see our article on washing down).
