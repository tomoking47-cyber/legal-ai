# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about caring for rainwear and waterproof clothing?｜Japan Questions #180

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/rainwear-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

With a rainy season (tsuyu, roughly June to July) and autumn typhoons, rainwear gets a lot of use in Japan, and "my waterproof has stopped working" comes up every year. Most of the time it is a care problem, not deterioration, and understanding how waterproof fabric works solves it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "rainwear must not be washed". Waterproof-breathable fabrics (Gore-Tex and the like) lose breathability and water repellency through dirt and skin oil, so washing is recommended. The key points are "no softener or bleach", "rinse the detergent out completely", and "restore the repellency with low heat".

## Waterproof versus water-repellent

| Term | Meaning | Why it fails | How to restore it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Waterproof** | The fabric (the membrane) does not let water through | **Seam tape peeling, membrane deterioration** | Repair, or re-taping the seams |
| **Water-repellent** | A surface finish makes water bead and roll off | **Dirt, skin oil and friction** stop the finish working | **Wash → low-heat tumble dry or iron with a pressing cloth → repellent product** |
| **Breathable** | Lets water vapour out from the inside | Clogged by dirt and skin oil | Wash |

- When repellency fails, water spreads across the surface (**"wetting out"**); **breathability stops working, the inside gets clammy, and it often feels as if the jacket has "leaked"**.
- How to wash: **zips closed, neutral detergent (or an outdoor-gear wash), gentle machine cycle or hand-wash, rinse at least twice, short spin**. **No softener, bleach or powder detergent** (they damage the surface finish and the breathability).
- Restoring repellency: **a low-heat tumble dryer (if the label allows) or a low iron with a pressing cloth** re-aligns the surface finish. If that does not work, **repellent spray, outdoors**.

> **[Verify]** Check the washing details against the fabric maker's official care guidance (e.g. Gore-Tex) and the care label.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Washing removes the waterproofing

**The opposite is true.** Dirt blocks both repellency and breathability. **Makers recommend regular washing.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Softener keeps it supple

**Softener coats the surface and ruins repellency and breathability.** Do not use it.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Repellent spray brings anything back

**Wash first → restore with heat.** Spraying over dirt achieves nothing. **Spray outdoors and do not breathe it in** (the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan has issued warnings about inhalation accidents with fluorine-based sprays).

## Misunderstanding 4｜A wet inside proves the waterproofing has failed

Usually it is **sweat condensation** (reduced breathability) or **water getting in at the neck, cuffs or zip**. **Seeping through the seams** means the seam tape has peeled, and **that is a repair job**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Roll it up in a bag when not in use

**Long compression and damp speed up hydrolysis and delamination of the membrane.** **Dry it completely and store it on a hanger.** **A sticky inside, or powder coming off**, are signs of deterioration.
