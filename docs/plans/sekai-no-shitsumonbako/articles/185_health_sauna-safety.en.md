# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how to take a sauna?｜Japan Questions #185

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/sauna-safety` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Sauna's popularity in Japan has grown fast since around 2019, and the ritual "sauna → cold plunge → rest in the open air" is widely shared. At the same time, the Consumer Affairs Agency and local governments keep warning about bathing accidents, so the safety line needs setting out.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the longer you tough it out, the more it works." A sauna room is 80–100°C and the cold plunge around 15–20°C, and the sudden change swings blood pressure sharply. The guide is "sauna 6–12 minutes, cold plunge about 1 minute, rest 5–10 minutes," adjusted to how you feel — and skip it after alcohol, when unwell, or if you have an existing condition.

## In numbers

| Item | Guide |
|---|---|
| Sauna room temperature | **80–100°C (176–212°F)** (dry sauna) / **60–70°C (140–158°F)** (high-humidity type) |
| Cold plunge (水風呂, *mizuburo*) | **around 15–20°C (59–68°F)** (varies by facility) |
| One set | Sauna **6–12 min** → cold plunge **30 s–1 min** → rest **5–10 min**. **2–3 sets** |
| Fluids | **At least one glass per set.** One sauna session is generally described as producing **around 500 mL** of sweat |
| Avoid | **After alcohol, right after a meal, short of sleep, with a fever, dehydrated**; **uncontrolled high blood pressure or heart disease** |

- **The Consumer Affairs Agency reports that most sudden deaths while bathing are among older people**, and that they **peak in winter** (an estimated **about 19,000** deaths while bathing a year, from research by the Tokyo Metropolitan Institute for Geriatrics and Gerontology). The temperature gap between sauna and cold plunge involves the same mechanism (**a sudden swing in blood pressure**).
- The Japan Sauna & Spa Association's **"How to take a sauna"** guidance includes **rinsing off sweat with a ladle of water (*kakeyu*) before the cold plunge** and **not pushing yourself**.

> **[Verify]** Check the estimate of deaths while bathing against the Consumer Affairs Agency's published material, and the sauna etiquette against the Japan Sauna & Spa Association's guidance. Temperatures are typical figures for ordinary facilities.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The "totonou" feeling proves it is good for you

*Totonou* (ととのう, roughly "getting in tune") is generally described as a sensation produced by **sudden changes in the autonomic nervous system and blood flow** caused by the temperature gap. **Feeling good and being safe are different things.** **Dizziness, palpitations or nausea** are the body's warning — stop right there.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The proper way into the cold plunge is head first, all at once

**The sudden cold shock sends blood pressure sharply up.** The usual facility guidance is **rinse off the sweat, then go in slowly, hands and feet first**. **If you have heart concerns, skipping the cold plunge** is an option.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sauna makes you lose weight

What you lose is **water**, and it comes back when you drink. **The dehydration risk is the bigger issue**, so avoid long sessions for weight loss.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A sauna after drinking sobers you up

**After alcohol, dehydration and blood-pressure swings combine — the most dangerous combination.** Facilities ban it too.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Sauna is training, so you have to endure

**Endurance is the doorway to accidents.** Rather than the clock: **"get out when it feels hot."** **If you have an existing condition, are pregnant or take medication, ask your doctor** first.
