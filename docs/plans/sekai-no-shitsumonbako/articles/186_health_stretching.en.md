# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about stretching?｜Japan Questions #186

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/stretching` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Should you stretch before exercise?" is one of the topics on which advice has shifted most in exercise communities everywhere. In Japan, static stretching is a fixture of school PE lessons, and that received wisdom is out of step with current guidance.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "before exercise, the basic thing is static stretching — long holds, no bouncing." Current general guidance separates the two: before exercise, dynamic stretching that warms you up while moving; after exercise, and for everyday flexibility, static stretching. Common to both: never stretch to the point of pain.

## Which to use when

| Type | What | When | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dynamic stretching** | Arm circles, leg swings, light jumping on the spot — moving the joints **while in motion** | **Before exercise** | 5–10 min, until you are warm |
| **Static stretching** | No bouncing; **hold at the point where it feels like a pleasant stretch** | **After exercise, after a bath, before bed** | **20–30 seconds per spot**, 2–3 times |
| Radio calisthenics (*rajio taisō*) | Includes dynamic and static elements | Morning, work breaks | About 3 min for No. 1 |

- The MHLW **Physical Activity and Exercise Guide for Health Promotion 2023** [健康づくりのための身体活動・運動ガイド2023] recommends **varied physical activity including flexibility exercises**, and **balance exercises for older people**.
- Many studies find that **long static stretching before exercise can temporarily reduce strength and explosive power straight afterwards**. Guidance from the Japan Sports Agency and the Japan Sport Association also makes **a (dynamic) warm-up** the basic step before exercise.
- The usual guide for **holding a static stretch is 20–30 seconds**; **longer is not better**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The more it hurts, the better it works

**Pain is a sign the muscle is tensing to defend itself** — it is not lengthening. **Stop at the point of a pleasant stretch.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Bouncing stretches you further

Bouncing (ballistic stretching) **makes the muscle contract by reflex and causes injury**. **Dynamic stretching is "moving," not "swinging."**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Stretching before exercise prevents injury

Many studies find that **static stretching alone does little to prevent injury**; **a dynamic warm-up** has more support.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hold your breath while you stretch

**Holding your breath tenses the muscles and raises blood pressure.** Breathe out slowly as you stretch.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Flexibility does not come back with age

Several studies find that **with continued practice, range of motion improves at any age**. Doing it **after a bath, when the body is warm**, is generally described among people living in Japan as the easiest way to keep it up. **If you have a condition or an injury, ask a doctor or physiotherapist.**
