# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about radio calisthenics (rajio taisō)?｜Japan Questions #187

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/radio-calisthenics` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Radio calisthenics (*rajio taisō*, ラジオ体操) is the most widely known exercise in Japan, and the sight of children doing it on summer-holiday mornings is often shown abroad. It tends to be seen as "light exercise for children and older people," but there is a basis to its design and to how it is used.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "it is too light to count as exercise." Radio Calisthenics No. 1 takes about 3 minutes, contains 13 movements and is designed to move all the body's main joints. Its purpose is not strength but keeping everyday range of motion and giving the day a rhythm; done every day, it covers part of the recommended amount of physical activity.

## In numbers

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Origin | **1928**, created by the Postal Life Insurance Bureau of the Ministry of Communications; broadcasting began on NHK radio |
| Current form | **No. 1 (1951)**: 13 exercises, **about 3 min 13 s** / **No. 2 (1952)**: 13 exercises, about 3 min |
| Broadcast | **NHK AM** (Radio 2 was discontinued in March 2026 and the AM services were consolidated into a single channel), **every morning at 6:30**; also on television |
| Intensity | **4.0 METs for No. 1 / 4.5 METs for No. 2** (equivalent to brisk walking) |
| Who does it | Surveys put **20–30% of adults at "sometimes to every day"** |

- The MHLW **Physical Activity and Exercise Guide 2023** [身体活動・運動ガイド2023] recommends that adults do **60 minutes or more of physical activity a day (3 METs or more)**; No. 1 and No. 2 back to back take **about 6 minutes and count towards that**.
- **Care homes for older people and workplace morning assemblies** adopt it because it **needs no equipment, needs little space, and moves every major joint in turn**.

> **[Verify]** Check the dates and durations against Japan Post Insurance's "History of radio calisthenics" and NHK's published material. MET values are from the National Institute of Health and Nutrition's "METs table of physical activities."

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is exercise for children and older people

No. 1 is designed to be **easy to do whatever your age**; No. 2 is **somewhat harder and was made for the working-age population**. **Done with correct form, many people feel muscle soreness the next day.** **If you have a long-term condition or joint pain, there will be movements you cannot do. Work only within a comfortable range, and talk to a doctor if you have any concerns.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Any vague waving of the arms is the same

**Each of the 13 movements targets particular joints and muscles.** Fully extending the arms, lifting the heels, twisting the trunk — **making the movements big** changes the intensity.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It does not count unless you do it at 6:30 every morning

**Any time of day is fine.** NHK videos and apps let you do it whenever you like. Many people do just No. 1 **to break up long sitting during the working day**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜It is enough on its own to make up for a lack of exercise

Radio calisthenics is **one part of the recommended physical activity**. The 2023 Guide also recommends **aerobic exercise such as walking, and strength training 2–3 times a week**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is an old routine, so it is out of date

In the 70-plus years since No. 1 was set, **the movements have barely changed**. But its design — **moving every major joint in about 3 minutes with no equipment** — is consistent with current thinking on flexibility and range of motion.
