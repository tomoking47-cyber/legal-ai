# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about sitting too long?｜Japan Questions #188

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/sitting-too-long` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Sitting is the new smoking" has spread around the world. Japan is generally described as a country where adults' sitting time is long by international comparison, and the 2023 national guide mentioned "sedentary behaviour" for the first time. Here is what that means in practice.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "if you exercise, the time you spend sitting does not matter." The MHLW Guide 2023 lists "reducing time spent sitting" as an item independent of how much you exercise. The guide figure is to stand up and move once every 30 minutes to an hour.

## In numbers

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Japanese adults' sitting time on weekdays | **About 7 hours** (among the longest of 20 countries compared) | Bauman et al. (2011), University of Sydney international comparison |
| The 2023 Guide's approach | **Reduce sedentary behaviour (sitting or lying, energy expenditure 1.5 METs or less) and move often** | MHLW |
| How often to stand up | **Once every 30 minutes to 1 hour** | Various public sources |
| Guidelines for screen (VDT) work | **No more than 1 hour of continuous work, with a 10–15 minute break between work periods** | MHLW (2019 revision) |

- Many studies link long sitting time with health risks **independently of how much exercise you do**, which is the background to its mention in the 2023 Guide.
- Several surveys show that **the spread of working from home** removed the commute walk and pushed sitting time up further.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Exercise at the weekend, and sitting all week is fine

**The effect of exercise and the effect of sitting are assessed as separate things.** You need **both**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A standing desk solves it

**Standing all day is its own strain** (swollen legs, lower back). The point is **"do not stay in one posture"** — **switch between sitting and standing**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Standing counts as exercise

Standing burns a little more than sitting, but **it is no substitute for exercise**. While you are up, **walk, take the stairs, or march on the spot** — that is the practical version.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A break is time to "rest"

In the VDT work guidelines, **the break is time to move your eyes and your body**. **Look away from the screen, stand up and walk** — that is what the break is really for.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Japanese people sit too much because of work, so nothing can be done

Workplace tricks widely shared in Japan include **a timer to stand up every hour, putting the copier and the kitchenette further away, and taking phone calls standing**. The trick to keeping it up is **a system, not willpower**.
