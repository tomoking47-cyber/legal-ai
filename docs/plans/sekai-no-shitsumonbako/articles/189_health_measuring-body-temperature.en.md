# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about taking your temperature?｜Japan Questions #189

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/measuring-body-temperature` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Since 2020, people all over the world have taken their temperature far more often, and questions like "how many minutes under the arm?" and "are no-touch forehead thermometers accurate?" have surged. In Japan, thermometers are regulated as medical devices with their own standards, and there is a set way of measuring.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "you get the same temperature wherever you measure." Underarm, mouth, ear, forehead and rectum all give different readings, and so do the device types (direct-reading, predictive and non-contact). The standard Japanese underarm method is: place the tip in the centre of the armpit from below at an angle, hold the arm tight against the body, and wait 10 minutes for a direct-reading thermometer, or until a predictive one displays.

## Sites and device types

| Site / type | Features | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Underarm (axillary), direct-reading** | Standard in Japanese homes. **About 10 minutes for the armpit temperature to stabilise** | Often around 36.0–37.0°C (96.8–98.6°F) |
| Underarm, predictive | Predicts from the rise curve. **About 15–30 seconds.** Many models switch to direct reading if you keep it in place after the display | As above |
| Mouth (under the tongue) | **About 0.2–0.5°C higher** than underarm. Standard in Europe and North America | — |
| Ear (tympanic) | Fast, but **error from the angle of the ear canal and from earwax** | — |
| **Forehead, non-contact** | Infrared from the skin surface. **Varies with outside air, sweat and make-up**; for screening | Large individual variation |
| Rectal | Closest to core temperature. Used for infants | About 0.5°C higher than underarm |

- Household thermometers are **"controlled medical devices" under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's drugs and devices law)**, certified to standards such as **JIS T 1140 (electronic clinical thermometers)**.
- Body temperature has a **daily rhythm (about 0.5–1°C)**: **lower in the morning, higher in the evening**. It reads high **right after a meal, exercise or a bath**. The basic rule is to compare **at the same time, at the same site, with the same thermometer**.

> **[Verify]** Check the JIS standard number and the medical-device classification against MHLW and Japan Federation of Medical Devices Associations material, and the site differences against standard physiology references.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Normal is 36.5°C, and anything else is abnormal

**Normal temperature ranges from the 35s to around 37°C depending on the person.** **Know your own baseline** first (measure morning, noon and evening for a few days). The usual guide figure for "fever" under Japan's Infectious Diseases Control Act is **37.5°C (99.5°F) or above**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The no-touch forehead type is the most accurate

It is **the fastest and the least accurate**. Outside air, sweat or a bath just before can shift it by several degrees. It suits **screening**, but **to judge how you are, measure again under the arm or similar**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜With a predictive thermometer, the beep means you are done

The beep gives a **predicted value**. **To know precisely, leave it in place for 10 minutes and read the direct measurement** (on compatible models).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Anywhere in the armpit will do

**Place the tip in the centre (the hollow) of the armpit at about 30 degrees from below, and press the arm against the body.** **Wipe away sweat.** Off-centre, the reading comes out low.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Right after exercise or a bath is the same

**It reads high for about 30 minutes.** Rest first, then measure. **Your temperature straight after waking** is the most stable baseline for comparison.
