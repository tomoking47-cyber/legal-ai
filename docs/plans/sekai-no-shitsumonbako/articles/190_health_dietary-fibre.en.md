# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about getting enough dietary fibre?｜Japan Questions #190

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/dietary-fibre` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Dietary fibre is the classic "nutrient people do not get enough of" in every country, and Japan is no exception. Here we set out the gap between the national target and actual intake, and the easiest ways to add fibre at a Japanese table.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a green salad every day is enough." Leafy salads contain surprisingly little fibre, and Japanese intake falls short of the target (by about 3 g for men and about 1 g for women). The efficient route is to switch your staple to brown rice, barley or wholemeal, and to eat more beans, mushrooms, seaweed and root vegetables and potatoes.

## Misunderstanding 1｜I eat salad every day, so I am fine

**A plate of lettuce gives under 1 g.** **Root vegetables, beans, mushrooms, seaweed and potatoes** are the main sources, and **changing your staple** makes the biggest difference.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Vegetable juice does the job

In most vegetable juices, **the fibre is largely lost in processing**. Look at the "dietary fibre" (食物繊維) figure on the label: many products give **about 1–2 g per bottle**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more fibre, the better

**Increasing it suddenly brings gas and bloating.** **Large amounts from supplements** are generally described as possibly affecting the absorption of minerals. **From food, a little at a time** is the rule.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Switching white rice for brown is hard on the digestion

**Chew well, soak the rice longer, or mix in partly milled rice (分づき米, *buzuki-mai*) or barley.** **If your digestion is weak, or for older people, starting with barley rice (麦飯, *mugi-meshi*) or partly milled rice** is the realistic route.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Eat fibre and your bowels will always improve

Fibre comes in **soluble (seaweed, fruit, barley)** and **insoluble (grain husks, beans, vegetables)** forms, and **without enough fluid, stools can become harder**. **Take it together with water.** If symptoms continue, see a doctor.
