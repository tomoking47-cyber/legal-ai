# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about when to use cling film and when to use aluminium foil?｜Japan Questions #192

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/plastic-wrap-and-foil` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Cling film (called "wrap", ラップ, in Japan) and aluminium foil are in every kitchen in the world, yet people hesitate over "can this go in the microwave?" and "which do I wrap it in?" Japanese cling film comes in two material families, and the answer depends on reading the label.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "all cling film is the same." Japanese household cling film is mainly either polyvinylidene chloride (PVDC) — clingy and heat-tolerant — or polyethylene (PE) — cheap, soft and less heat-tolerant. Their heat tolerances differ: around 110°C versus around 140°C. Aluminium foil cannot go in the microwave; it is for the oven and grill.

## Comparison

| Item | PVDC cling film | PE cling film | Aluminium foil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heat tolerance | **About 140°C** | **About 110°C** | Takes high heat (**not for the microwave**) |
| Cold tolerance | About −60°C | About −70°C | Freezer-safe |
| Cling and barrier | **High** (blocks smells and oxygen well) | Lower | Blocks light and oxygen |
| Microwave | **Yes** (some makers advise keeping it off direct contact with the food) | Yes (**may melt on oily foods**) | **No** (sparks, fire) |
| Best for | Fridge and freezer storage, blocking smell transfer | Temporary covering, low cost | Oven and grill, as a drop-lid (otoshibuta), foil-wrapped baking |

- Cling film falls under the **positive-list system of the Food Sanitation Act**, and carries labels for **heat tolerance, cold tolerance and precautions for microwave use**.
- In the microwave, **oily foods (fried food, curry)** can **exceed 140°C**. Even with PVDC film, the makers' advice is to **drape it loosely without touching the food**.

> **[Verify]** Heat tolerances are guide figures based on the labels of major products. Check the microwave precautions with each maker and with Consumer Affairs Agency material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Aluminium foil can be used to reheat food in the microwave

**Metal reflects microwaves and causes sparks and fire.** Foil is for the **oven function**. In an **oven toaster**, place it where it **does not touch the heating element**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It is fine for cling film to touch the food while it heats

PE film **can melt at around 110°C**, and even PVDC film gets hot on the surface of **oily foods**. The rule is **drape it loosely over the dish and leave a gap above the food**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sour or salty foods can be wrapped in aluminium foil

**Umeboshi (pickled plums), pickles, tomatoes, lemons and miso** **corrode aluminium, causing holes and black marks**. **Wrap in cling film first**, then foil — or use a container.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Using up cling film is bad for the environment, so avoid it

The main job of cling film is **to cut food waste by preventing drying, oxidation and smell transfer**. More people are combining it with **reusable lidded containers, silicone lids and beeswax wraps**, choosing whichever fits.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The cutter on the box is paper, so it is safe

**A paper cutter can still cut you.** Keep the box out of children's reach. **PVDC film gives off chlorine-based gas when burned, so follow your municipality's sorting rules** when disposing of it (usually "burnable waste" or "plastics").
