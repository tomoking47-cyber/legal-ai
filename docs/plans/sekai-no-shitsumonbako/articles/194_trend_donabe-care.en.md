# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about looking after a donabe (clay pot)?｜Japan Questions #194

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/donabe-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The donabe (土鍋, earthenware pot) comes into its own in hot-pot (nabe) season, and is now known abroad by its Japanese name. Here we answer "what do I do before first use?" and "why does it crack?" from the nature of the ceramic itself.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a donabe is ready to use as soon as you buy it." The clay body is full of tiny pores. Without "medome" (目止め) before first use — simmering rice-rinsing water or rice porridge in it — the pot can leak, absorb smells and crack. In use, the basics are: never put a wet base over the flame, never heat it empty, never cool it suddenly.

## What to go on

| Situation | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First use | **Medome**: fill to 80% with water, add **rice-rinsing water or leftover rice**, simmer over a low flame to a porridge, let it cool, then wash | Starch fills the pores in the clay |
| Before heating | **Wipe the base dry** | Heating a wet base quickly causes cracks |
| Heating | **Start on a low flame**; **never heat it empty** | Sudden temperature change, overheating |
| Washing | **After it has cooled**, a little detergent, **no soaking** | Water on a hot pot cracks it; long soaking soaks in |
| Drying | **Dry completely, base upwards** | The base is bare clay; if damp, it grows mould |
| Storage | Only when dry. **Put newspaper between pots** | Humidity |

- Most donabe are **not compatible with induction (IH) hobs** (only those **labelled IH-compatible**); check the product label for **microwave and oven use** too.
- A **crack** can sometimes be stopped for a time with medome, but **if it keeps leaking, stop using it** — the safe side.
- NITE (National Institute of Technology and Evaluation, Japan's product-safety body) has issued alerts about **gas canisters bursting because a pan too large for the cassette-gas stove was used**, among other accidents.

> **[Verify]** The medome method is a general procedure based on guidance from pottery regions and makers. Check accident cases in NITE's product-accident information.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Medome is done once only

**The effect fades with use.** Repeat it when leaks or smells start to bother you.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Once smells have soaked in, it is time for a new pot

Methods shared among people living in Japan include **simmering tea leaves, or diluted vinegar**, to reduce the smell. **Doing medome again afterwards** usually settles it.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dunking the hot pot in water gets the washing done quickly

**Sudden cooling is the biggest cause of cracking.** Wash **after it has cooled**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A donabe is fine on any gas burner

**A large donabe on a tabletop cassette-gas stove (kasetto konro) can overheat the gas canister, which is dangerous.** Check the **size limit in the stove's instructions** (the Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE have issued alerts).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Donabe crack so easily that a plastic-handled metal pot is better value

A donabe has cooking advantages — **gentle, even heat and high heat retention** — and **with care lasts more than ten years.** Most cracks come from three avoidable causes: **sudden cooling, heating empty and a wet base**.
