# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about looking after an iron frying pan?｜Japan Questions #195

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/cast-iron-pan-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Iron frying pans are enjoying a worldwide revival, but many people give up because "it rusts" or "everything sticks." In Japan, two routines — "abura-narashi" (油ならし, seasoning) and "abura-gaeshi" (油返し, pre-oiling before each use) — are well established. Once you know them, the pan lasts for years.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "never wash an iron pan with detergent." The oil film built up by the initial seasoning does not come off to any great degree with a light wash in detergent. The key points are: heat the pan and swirl oil round it before cooking (abura-gaeshi); wash straight after use, put it back on the heat to drive off the water, and rub in a thin coat of oil.

## The routine

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| First use (abura-narashi, seasoning) | Wash and dry → **heat empty over a medium flame to burn off the anti-rust coating** (depends on the product) → add a generous amount of oil and **heat on low for 5 minutes** → pour off the oil and wipe. **Frying vegetable scraps** reduces the iron smell |
| Every time, before cooking (abura-gaeshi) | **Heat over a medium flame, add a generous amount of oil and swirl it over the whole surface** → pour the excess back into a pot → cook |
| After cooking | **Wash while still hot with hot water and a scrubbing brush (tawashi)** (a little detergent is fine) → **put it on the heat to drive off every trace of water** → **rub in a thin coat of oil** |
| If it rusts | **Scrub off with a metal scourer or scouring powder** → season again |
| If food burns on | **Fill with hot water, bring to the boil, let it soften and scrape off** |

- An iron pan **holds a stable high surface temperature, so it suits searing and stir-frying**. But **simmering strongly acidic dishes (tomato, vinegar) for a long time** leaches iron, affecting both the flavour and the surface.
- Some people hope for **extra dietary iron**, but **the amount of iron released in cooking varies greatly with the dish and the time, and it cannot be counted on as a nutrient source.**

> **[Verify]** The routine is a general method based on the instruction manuals of major makers; it varies by product (whether there is an anti-rust coating, nitriding treatment, etc.).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Detergent strips the whole oil film

**A baked-on oil film (polymerised oil) does not come off to any great degree with detergent.** If you are worried, **hot water and a tawashi alone** are enough. **Re-oil after washing with detergent** and there is no problem.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Food sticks because the pan is iron

The main cause is **not enough heat**. **Heat the pan properly, then swirl the oil (abura-gaeshi)**, and food sticks far less. **Cold food into a cold pan** sticks worst of all.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Once it rusts, it is finished

**Rust can be removed.** Scrub it off with a metal scourer, season again and the pan comes back. Unless **the rust has eaten holes in the surface**, it is usable.

## Misunderstanding 4｜No need to dry it after washing — oil stops rust

**Oil over remaining water means rust forms under the oil.** The order is **put it on the heat and drive off every trace of water first**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Iron pans are too heavy to handle

**A thin pan of around 1.6 mm plate** weighs little more than a fluoropolymer-coated aluminium pan of the same size. **Thick ones (2.3 mm and up)** store more heat and suit steak and gyoza — a way of choosing shared among people living in Japan.
