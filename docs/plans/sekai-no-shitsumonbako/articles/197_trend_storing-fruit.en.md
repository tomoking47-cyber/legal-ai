# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing fruit?｜Japan Questions #197

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-fruit` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Can bananas go in the fridge?" and "Do apples spoil the fruit next to them?" are questions asked the world over. Fruit divides into kinds that **ripen after picking** and kinds that do not, and that difference decides how to store it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "all fruit keeps longer in the fridge." Bananas, avocados, kiwis, peaches and pears are "climacteric" — they ripen after harvest — and chilling them before they are ripe can halt ripening or damage them. The ethylene given off by apples speeds ripening, so keep apples away from other fruit, or deliberately put them with fruit you want to ripen.

## Fruit that ripens after picking, and fruit that does not

| Ripens after picking (ripen at room temperature, then chill) | Does not ripen after picking (chill from purchase) |
|---|---|
| Bananas, avocados, kiwis, peaches, pears, mangoes, melons, persimmons (some) | Strawberries, grapes, cherries, citrus, apples (already ripe), pineapples, watermelon (room temperature is fine until cut) |

| Fruit | Storage guide | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bananas | **Room temperature**; once ripe, the fridge is fine (the skin turns black, the inside is still edible) | Chilling injury below 13°C (55°F) |
| Apples | **Fridge** (in a plastic bag) | **Give off a lot of ethylene.** Keep apart from other fruit |
| Strawberries | **Fridge, 2–3 days.** Wash just before eating | Spoil easily |
| Citrus | Cool room temperature to fridge | Prevent drying out |
| Avocados | Ripen at room temperature → fridge | Once cut, lemon juice slows browning |

- **Ethylene** is a plant hormone given off by fruit; **apples, bananas, melons and peaches** produce a lot. **Keep it away from fruit you do not want to over-ripen; put fruit you want to ripen faster (avocado, kiwi) in a bag with an apple** — the approach described by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) and others.
- **Chilling injury**: tropical fruit such as bananas, pineapples and mangoes can **blacken below 10–13°C (50–55°F)**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bananas rot in the fridge

**The skin turning black** is chilling injury; **the flesh stays as ripe as it was.** Chilled once ripe, bananas keep **several days longer** than at room temperature.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Apples make other fruit rot

Apple ethylene **speeds ripening**, so keep apples away from **fruit that must not over-ripen** (strawberries, grapes). **Put them with avocados or kiwis you want to ripen quickly** and they become useful.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Wash fruit before storing it

**Washing removes the protective surface layer, and the moisture makes fruit spoil faster.** Strawberries and grapes in particular should be **washed just before eating**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cut fruit is fine at room temperature for a few hours

**Bacteria multiply readily on a cut surface.** Under the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's approach to food-poisoning prevention, **refrigerate fruit as soon as it is cut and do not leave it out at room temperature for long**. **Eat cut fruit the same day.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Freezing destroys the nutrients

**Freezing does not greatly reduce vitamin C and the like; for long storage, nutrients are generally described as better kept frozen than chilled.** Bananas, berries and mangoes are frozen for smoothies and so on, which also **cuts food waste**.
