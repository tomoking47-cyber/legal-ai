# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing meat?｜Japan Questions #198

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-meat` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Storing meat is a topic that leads straight to food poisoning, and "how many days in the fridge?" and "can I refreeze after thawing?" come up again and again in every country. Japanese household fridges have a "chilled" compartment and a "partial-freezing" compartment, and how you use them changes how long meat keeps.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "until the use-by date, it does not matter where in the fridge it goes." The colder meat is kept, the slower bacteria multiply. 10°C or below is the standard for refrigeration; the chilled compartment (about 0°C) and the partial compartment (about −3°C) extend storage further. Minced meat spoils fastest: the same day or the next is the guide.

## Storage guide

| Type of meat | Main fridge compartment (about 3–5°C) | Chilled compartment (about 0°C) | Freezer (−18°C or below) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole joint or block | **3–4 days** | About 5 days | 1 month |
| Slices and cuts | **2–3 days** | 3–4 days | 2–3 weeks |
| **Minced meat** | **Same day to next day** | About 2 days | 2 weeks |
| Chicken (thigh, breast) | **1–2 days** | 2–3 days | 2–3 weeks |
| Processed meat (ham, sausages, once opened) | Follow the label (a few days) | — | — |

- The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare gives **10°C or below for the fridge and −15°C or below for the freezer** as a guide, and advises **refrigerating straight after purchase, and bagging meat so the juices cannot leak and keeping it apart from other food**.
- In Japanese fridges the **chilled compartment (チルド室) is about 0°C and the partial compartment (パーシャル室) about −3°C (lightly frozen)**; both suit **short-term storage of meat and fish**.
- **Drip (meat juice) is a source of Campylobacter and Salmonella contamination.** Keep meat **on the bottom shelf so it cannot drip on to other food**.

> **[Verify]** Storage days are general guide figures based on MAFF, Consumer Affairs Agency and major-maker guidance; they vary with freshness at purchase and temperature control. The use-by date on the label takes priority.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Within the use-by date, anywhere in the fridge is the same

**The date is a guide that assumes the storage method on the label (e.g. 4°C or below) was followed.** **The area near the door pocket warms up easily**; the rule is to keep meat **at the back of the bottom shelf or in the chilled compartment**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Frozen, it keeps for months

**Bacteria do not multiply in the freezer, but fat oxidises and the meat dries out (freezer burn), so the flavour declines.** A household freezer warms and cools as the door is opened, so **2 weeks to 1 month** is the guide for flavour.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Thawed meat can be refrozen

**Bacteria multiply during thawing, and refreezing lowers the quality**, so the principle is **avoid refreezing**. The standard answer is **divide into single-use portions before freezing**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Meat that has changed colour has gone off

**The surface exposed to air turns red while overlapping parts go dark**; this is a reaction with oxygen, not spoilage. The signs of spoilage are **a sour smell, sliminess or stickiness**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Washing removes the bacteria

**Rinsing raw meat under the tap spreads bacteria round the sink in the splashes.** MHLW and MAFF advise **do not wash meat; cook it thoroughly (centre at 75°C for at least 1 minute)**.
