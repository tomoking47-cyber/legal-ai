# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing fish?｜Japan Questions #199

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-fish` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japan eats a great deal of fish, and households have built up know-how on handling it. Yet on Q&A sites abroad, "how many days will the fish I bought keep?" and "can I eat sashimi the next day?" are perennial questions. We set out the answers, taking into account the rise in food poisoning from Anisakis (a parasitic worm found in fish).

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "fish keeps longer than meat." Fish spoils faster than meat: 1–2 days for fillets, and sashimi the same day. Anisakis is killed by heating (70°C or above, or 60°C for 1 minute) or by freezing (−20°C for at least 24 hours), but a household freezer needs extra time.

## Storage guide and the figures

| State | Fridge (chilled compartment recommended) | Freezer |
|---|---|---|
| Whole fish (with guts) | **Same day.** Remove guts and gills, rinse and pat dry, and it keeps until the next day | 2–3 weeks |
| Fillets and cuts | **1–2 days** | 2–3 weeks |
| Sashimi (sold for raw eating) | **Same day** | Not suitable (texture suffers on thawing) |
| Dried fish (himono) | 2–3 days | 1 month |
| Shellfish (asari clams, etc.) | Same day to next day (in salt water) | After purging the sand, can be frozen in the shell |

- **Food poisoning from Anisakis reached 432 cases in 2023 (Reiwa 5)**, the **highest count by causative agent** (MHLW food-poisoning statistics). Eating **mackerel, saury, bonito, sardine and squid** raw is the main cause.
- MHLW's guidance against Anisakis is **freezing at −20°C for at least 24 hours, or heating to 70°C or above (or 60°C for 1 minute)**. **Vinegar, salt, soy sauce and wasabi do not kill it.**
- **The guts are the part of a fish that spoils fastest**; if you buy a whole fish, the rule is to **remove them early**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Supermarket sashimi can be eaten the next day

**The "for raw consumption" label assumes "eat today."** The safe side is to **cook it the next day** before eating.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Fresh fish has no Anisakis

Anisakis is **a parasite living in the guts of the live fish**; it has nothing to do with freshness. **After death, it moves into the flesh over time**, so **gutting early** helps, but the real measures are **checking by eye and removing it, freezing, or cooking**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜One night in the home freezer makes it safe

A household freezer **warms and cools as the door is opened, and takes time to reach −20°C at the centre.** The common advice is to **allow a margin, such as 48 hours or more**. **Conditions differ from commercial blast freezing.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜If it does not smell, it is fine

**Histamine food poisoning** from fish happens when **red-fleshed fish (tuna, bonito, mackerel, yellowtail) is left at room temperature** and histamine forms. **Cooking does not break it down, and you cannot smell it.** **Refrigerate straight after purchase and never leave it out** — the only prevention.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Wash fish before freezing it

The basic method is **a quick rinse in salt water, pat completely dry, wrap each piece in cling film, then into a bag**; **any remaining water causes freezer burn and frost**. **Freezing with a seasoning (salt, miso or soy sauce)** is shared among people living in Japan as a trick that "resists freezer burn and can go straight into the pan after thawing."
