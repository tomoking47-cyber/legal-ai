# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing spices and condiments after opening?｜Japan Questions #200

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-spices-and-condiments` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Spices and condiments are often assumed to "never go off, so they last for years." Once opened, though, the aroma fades, humidity makes them clump, and oils oxidise. Here is a guide to shelf life after opening, written for the hot, humid Japanese kitchen.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "dry seasonings have no expiry." Spices rarely go off, but their aroma compounds fall sharply within months of opening, and humidity can bring mould. As a guide: ground spices 6 months to 1 year, whole spices 1–2 years, and mayonnaise, ketchup and dressings about 1 month after opening.

## Guide after opening

| Condiment | Where to keep it | Guide after opening | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground spices (curry powder, paprika, etc.) | **Dark place at room temperature**, sealed | **6 months to 1 year** | Humidity makes them clump; heat beside the hob drives off the aroma |
| Whole spices (black peppercorns, etc.) | Same | **1–2 years** | Crush just before use to keep the aroma |
| Dried herbs | Same | **6 months to 1 year** | If the colour has faded, so has the aroma |
| **Mayonnaise** | **Fridge** (1–10°C; keep it away from direct cold air — around the door pocket is recommended) | **About 1 month** | Separates at 0°C or below |
| **Ketchup** | Fridge | **About 1 month** | Darkens if left at room temperature |
| Dressing (emulsified type) | Fridge | **About 1 month** | Discard if it separates or the smell changes |
| Mentsuyu (concentrated noodle-soup base) | **Fridge** | **A few days to a few weeks** (see the label) | **Mould after being left out at room temperature once opened** is a common complaint |
| Shichimi and ichimi (chilli blends) | Fridge or a dark place | A few months | Can attract insects |

- **Shelf life after opening is separate from the best-before date (賞味期限)**; the label's **"use promptly after opening" or "refrigerate after opening"** takes priority. **Mould in mentsuyu left at room temperature after opening** is a case that also reaches the customer help desks of noodle-soup-base makers.
- Under the **Food Labelling Act**, processed foods carry a **storage method**. **"Store at room temperature away from direct sunlight" is the condition for the unopened product.**

> **[Verify]** The figures after opening are general numbers based on the labels and Q&A of major makers. The product label takes priority.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Spices do not go off, so ignore the date

**Even if they rarely go off, the aroma fades, they clump when they absorb moisture, and mould can appear.** **Writing the purchase date on the jar** is standard practice among people living in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Next to the hob is the handiest place

**Heat and steam are the biggest causes of lost aroma and of moisture getting in.** Keep spices **in a drawer away from the hob**. **Do not shake the jar over the pan** (steam gets in).

## Misunderstanding 3｜Mayonnaise is oil and vinegar, so room temperature is fine

**The label says refrigerate after opening.** **Unopened, room temperature is fine**, but **once opened, room temperature speeds up separation and changes in smell**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Mentsuyu is salty, so it is fine at room temperature

**Most labels say refrigerate after opening, even for the concentrated type.** Left at room temperature, **mould, or swelling and spoilage from yeast**, can occur. **Use it up soon after opening**, or choose a small bottle.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Everything lasts longer in the fridge

**Ground spices can pick up condensation each time they leave the fridge, and clump.** The basic split is **dry goods, sugar and salt in a dark place at room temperature; liquids, emulsions and fermented products in the fridge**.
