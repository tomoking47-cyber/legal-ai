# Q. What do people get wrong about Japanese supermarkets?｜Japan Questions #201

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/supermarkets` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Visitors photograph convenience stores and depachika food halls, but the ordinary neighbourhood supermarket is where residents shop, where prices are lowest, and where a few unwritten rules catch newcomers out.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that convenience stores are where Japan buys its food. Supermarkets sell most of it, at roughly 20–40% lower prices, with evening discounts on fresh food, a bagging table you pack at yourself, and a rice section that repays a minute's attention.

## How it works

| Feature | What to expect |
|---|---|
| **Evening markdowns** | Stickers of **20–50% off** (割引 / 半額) on sushi, bento and fresh food from roughly **18:00–20:00** |
| **Bagging** | You pay at the till, then **pack your own bags at the sakkudai (bagging table)**. Plastic bags cost **¥3–5** since the July 2020 charge |
| **Self-checkout** | Common in chains; many have **"semi-self" tills** where staff scan and you pay at a machine |
| **Point cards** | Offered at most tills; declining ("kekkō desu") is normal |
| **Baskets** | Take a basket, not a trolley, for a small shop; trolleys hold baskets |
| **Opening hours** | Typically **9:00 or 10:00 to 21:00–23:00**; some 24 hours |

- Japan has about **23,000 supermarkets** (Supermarket White Paper 2024, based on the associations' annual census), against roughly **55,700 convenience stores** (Japan Franchise Association, Dec 2024).
- The **plastic bag charge** became mandatory on **1 July 2020**; most residents carry a folding bag.
- **Egg cartons** are not refrigerated in many stores; eggs are dated for **raw consumption** (see the eggs article).

> **[Verify]** Store counts with the Japan Supermarket Association and Japan Franchise Association; bag-charge date with the Ministry of the Environment.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Convenience stores are cheaper because they are everywhere

They are **more expensive** — you pay for the location and hours. For a week's food, the supermarket is the budget choice.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Staff will bag your shopping

At most supermarkets the cashier puts items back in a basket and **you bag them yourself** at the table behind the tills. Standing at the till to pack holds up the queue.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Prices shown are what you pay

Prices must be shown **tax-inclusive** (since April 2021), so the shelf price is the till price. Some stores show both; the larger number includes tax.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You cannot try before you buy

**Tasting counters (shishoku)** are common at weekends, and fruit is often sold by the piece so you can buy one. **Squeezing fruit** is frowned upon.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is all raw fish and rice

The **deli section (sōzai)** sells ready meals — fried chicken, croquettes, salads, grilled fish — that are what many households actually eat on weeknights, and they are what gets discounted after 18:00.
