# Q. What do people get wrong about department stores and depachika?｜Japan Questions #202

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/department-stores-and-depachika` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The basement food hall — depachika — is one of the most recommended experiences in Japanese cities, and department stores remain the destination for gifts and tax-free luxury. The etiquette and the economics are not obvious.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a depachika is a food court. It is a market of counters from famous shops, where you buy to take away (there is often nowhere to sit), and where samples are offered but grazing is not the point. Department stores themselves are gift institutions: the wrapping and the paper bag carry meaning.

## The layout

| Floor | What is there | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **B1–B2 (depachika)** | Food counters: bento, sweets, deli, bakery, sake, fresh produce | **Take-away**; a few counters have eat-in bars |
| 1F | Cosmetics, accessories, tax-free counter | Tax-free processing usually here or on a designated floor |
| Middle floors | Fashion by brand; kimono on an upper floor | Sizes run small (see the sizing article) |
| Top floor | Restaurants, event hall (**seasonal food fairs**), sometimes a rooftop garden | Restaurants have queues at 12:00 |

- Department-store sales were about **¥5.9 trillion in 2023**; **duty-free sales to visitors** accounted for a record share, exceeding **¥600 billion** in FY2024 (Japan Department Stores Association).
- **Wrapping** is free and expected for gifts; the store's paper signals the gift's provenance. Say "purezento de" (as a gift).
- **Depachika bento** are discounted **30–50% from about 19:00**; the best-known counters sell out earlier.

> **[Verify]** Sales figures with the Japan Department Stores Association's monthly and annual reports; the tax-free rules change on 1 November 2026 (see the tax-free article).

## Misunderstanding 1｜You can eat as you browse

Depachika are **shops, not food courts**. Eating while walking is frowned on; look for the small eat-in corner or take your purchases to a park or your hotel.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Samples mean free lunch

Tasting is offered to help you choose. **Taking several without buying** is noticed. One taste, then a decision, is the local rhythm.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Everything is expensive

Individual items — one piece of wagashi, one croquette, a single bento — are priced like a good lunch (**¥300–1,500**). The whole cakes and gift boxes are where the prices climb.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The clerk is upselling when they ask about ice

They are asking whether you want a **cooling pack (horeizai)** and for **how many hours** you will carry the food. Answer with a number of hours; the pack is free.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Department stores are dying

Regional stores have closed, but flagship stores in Tokyo, Osaka and Fukuoka posted **record sales in 2023–24**, driven by inbound luxury spending. The depachika is the anchor that keeps residents coming.
