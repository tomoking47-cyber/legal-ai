# Q. What do people get wrong about Japanese home centres (DIY stores)?｜Japan Questions #203

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/home-centres` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Home centres are where many of the ideas foreigners admire — the storage solutions, the gadgets, the garden tools — actually come from. Visitors rarely enter one, because they are suburban and assumed to be for tradespeople.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese household ingenuity is a 100-yen-shop phenomenon. The deeper source is the home centre, a suburban big-box format with 5,000-plus stores nationwide selling tools, storage, gardening, pet and camping goods — and increasingly a place visitors seek out for practical souvenirs.

## The sector

| Typical location | Suburban, by road; some urban "compact" formats | — |

- Sections that visitors find useful: **storage and organisation**, **kitchen tools**, **cleaning goods**, **stationery**, **gardening (bonsai tools, seeds — note export restrictions on plants)**, **outdoor and disaster-preparedness goods**.
- Many stores offer **timber cutting to size (¥50–100 per cut)** and **tool rental**; some have a **workshop space** you can use after buying materials.
- Compared with 100-yen shops, home centres carry **higher-grade versions of the same idea** — the same drawer divider in thicker plastic, the same hook rated for more weight.

> **[Verify]** Store count and sales with the Japan DIY Home Centre Association's annual data.

## Misunderstanding 1｜They are for professionals

Layout, signage and pricing are aimed at **households**. Weekend mornings are families and retirees.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Everything is the same as a 100-yen shop but pricier

Overlap exists at the low end. The difference is **durability and load ratings**, and categories 100-yen shops cannot stock (**timber, paint, plumbing, seeds, large storage**).

## Misunderstanding 3｜You cannot bring the goods home

Small goods are fine. **Seeds, plants and soil** face **plant quarantine** on entry to most countries; **knives** must go in checked luggage; **aerosols** are limited. Check your home country's rules.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Prices are fixed and high

Home centres run **weekly leaflet discounts** and clear seasonal stock (heaters in March, fans in September) at deep cuts.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is only about buying

The **idea** is the product. Walking the storage and kitchen aisles shows how a small home is organised in Japan — the "Everyday Wisdom" articles draw on exactly this.
