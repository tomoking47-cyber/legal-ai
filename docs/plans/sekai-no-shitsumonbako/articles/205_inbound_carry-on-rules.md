# Q. What do people get wrong about carry-on rules on Japanese flights and trains?｜Japan Questions #205

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/carry-on-rules` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Domestic flights, the Shinkansen and even city trains each have luggage rules that differ from what many visitors expect — including a reservation requirement for large suitcases on some bullet trains that catches people at the platform.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that "carry-on" means the same thing everywhere. Japanese domestic flights allow 10 kg (7 kg on some smaller aircraft) with size limits by seat count, and the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen require a free reservation for any bag over 160 cm in total dimensions — with a ¥1,000 charge if you board without one.

## The rules

| Mode | Limit | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Domestic flights (JAL, ANA)** | **10 kg**, 1 bag + 1 personal item | Size **55 × 40 × 25 cm** (aircraft with 100+ seats); **45 × 35 × 20 cm** on smaller aircraft |
| Low-cost carriers (Peach, Jetstar) | **7 kg** total | Strictly weighed at the gate |
| **Tokaido / Sanyo / Kyushu Shinkansen** | Bags **160–250 cm** (sum of three sides) **need a free "oversized baggage" seat reservation** | No reservation: **¥1,000 fee** and staff stow it where possible. **Over 250 cm: not allowed** |
| Other Shinkansen (Tohoku, Hokuriku etc.) | No reservation system; overhead rack or behind last row | Space is limited |
| City trains and subways | No formal limit for suitcases; **bicycles must be bagged** | Avoid rush hours (7:30–9:00) with large bags |
| Highway buses | 1 suitcase in the hold, usually under **30 kg / 160 cm** | Varies by operator |

- The Shinkansen oversized-baggage rule started **May 2020**. A typical **check-in suitcase (about 70 cm tall) is roughly 150–160 cm** in total — measure before you travel.
- Liquids on **domestic** flights are **not** restricted to 100 mL containers (unlike international departures); but sharp items and lithium batteries follow standard rules.

> **[Verify]** Airline size/weight limits with JAL and ANA; Shinkansen rule details with JR Central's oversized-baggage page. Airlines change limits.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Shinkansen luggage is first-come, first-served

On the Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu lines, **large bags need the reservation** — free when you book a seat in the last row of the car (the space behind it is yours). Without it, you pay ¥1,000 on board.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Domestic flights have the same allowance as international

**10 kg** is the usual carry-on limit — lower than many international economy allowances. Checked baggage on JAL/ANA domestic is **20 kg free** in economy.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Liquids are limited to 100 mL on all flights

**Domestic flights in Japan do not apply the 100 mL rule.** International departures do.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can take a suitcase on a rush-hour train

You can, but **a large case on a packed Yamanote Line at 8:15 is a hardship for everyone**. Use the airport express lines (Narita Express, Skyliner, Haruka) which have luggage racks, or travel after 9:30.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Luggage forwarding is the only alternative

It is the best one for suitcases (see the lockers article), but for the Shinkansen the reservation route is free and simple if done at booking.
