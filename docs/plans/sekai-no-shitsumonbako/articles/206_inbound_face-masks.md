# Q. What do people get wrong about face masks in Japan?｜Japan Questions #206

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/face-masks` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Masks in Japan predate the pandemic by a century and outlasted the guidance that ended it. Visitors ask whether they are still required, whether wearing one is expected, and why so many people still do.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that mask-wearing in Japan is a pandemic rule that has not been lifted. Masks have been a personal choice since 13 March 2023. People still wear them for pollen (spring), colds (winter), cosmetic reasons and on crowded trains. Nobody will require one of you, but clinics and some care facilities still ask.

## The facts

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Government position | **"Individual judgement" since 13 March 2023**; recommended only in medical facilities, care homes, and crowded trains during peak infection periods |
| Pre-pandemic use | Widespread since the **1918 influenza**; annual **hay-fever season (Feb–Apr)** drives sales |
| Domestic mask market | About **¥200–300 billion** a year at pandemic peak, now lower but well above 2019 |
| Still common where | Trains at rush hour, hospitals, pharmacies, kitchens and food counters, winter |
| Type sold | Mostly **non-woven disposable**; sizes are **small for Western faces** — look for "large" (大きめ) |

- Surveys in 2024–25 still found **around 30–50% of people masking on urban trains** in winter, lower in summer.
- Reasons people give beyond infection: **pollen**, **dry throat on planes**, **no makeup day**, **not wanting to be spoken to**.

> **[Verify]** The 13 March 2023 change with the MHLW mask page; market and survey figures with industry associations and press surveys.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Masks are still required

**No.** They are a personal choice. **Clinics, hospitals and some nursing homes** ask for them; follow signage.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Everyone still wears one

Rates vary by season and setting. **Summer streets are mostly unmasked**; winter trains and pharmacies are not.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Wearing a mask when you have a cold is odd

The opposite: **going out with a cough and no mask draws looks.** If you catch a cold on holiday, buy a pack — every convenience store has them.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Any mask fits

Japanese masks are sized for Japanese faces. **Look for 大きめ or ふつう+** if you have a larger face; otherwise ear loops pull.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Japanese people mask because they are told to

Before 2020, mask sales peaked every **February–April with pollen**, not with flu. The habit is **self-protection and courtesy**, and it is not going away.
