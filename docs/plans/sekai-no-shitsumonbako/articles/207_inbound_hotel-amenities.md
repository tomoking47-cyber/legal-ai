# Q. What do people get wrong about Japanese hotel rooms and amenities?｜Japan Questions #207

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/hotel-amenities` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese hotel rooms surprise on both sides: smaller than expected, and stocked with things most hotels abroad do not provide — pyjamas, a full toiletry kit, a kettle, a bidet toilet. Since 2022, the amenities themselves have been changing.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a Japanese hotel room is a smaller version of a Western one. Business hotels run at 12–18 m² with a semi-double bed, and compensate with amenities: sleepwear, toothbrush and razor, slippers, a bath, a heated toilet seat, and often a free laundry corner. The disposable amenities are moving to the lobby "amenity bar" under a 2022 plastics law.

## What to expect

| Item | Business hotel (¥8,000–15,000) | City hotel / ryokan |
|---|---|---|
| Room size | **10–18 m²** (some as small as 10 m²) | 25 m² and up; ryokan rooms sized in tatami mats (**8–10 jo ≈ 13–16 m²**) |
| Bed | Semi-double (**120 cm**) for one; twins are two 100–110 cm beds | Double 140–160 cm; ryokan futon on tatami |
| Sleepwear | **Yes** (pyjamas or a gown) | Yukata at ryokan — worn to dinner and the bath |
| Toiletries | Toothbrush, razor, comb, cotton, shampoo/conditioner/body wash | Same, often a brand |
| Bath | **Unit bath**: deep short tub, shower over it, wet-room floor | Larger; ryokan have communal baths |
| Toilet | **Heated seat with washlet** almost universal | Same |
| Extras | Kettle, tea, fridge (empty), free coin laundry or ¥300–500 machines, trouser press | Kaiseki dinner, in-room dining |

- The **Act on Promotion of Resource Circulation for Plastics (April 2022)** requires large hotels to cut single-use plastics. Result: many have **moved toothbrushes, razors and combs to a lobby amenity bar** — take what you need at check-in.
- **Check-in is often 15:00, check-out 10:00–11:00**; luggage can be left before and after.
- **Smoking rooms still exist**; book "non-smoking" (禁煙) explicitly.

> **[Verify]** The plastics law provisions with the Ministry of the Environment; room sizes are typical ranges.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The room is a mistake — it is too small

**It is the room.** Business hotels are designed for one person and a suitcase. If you need space, look for "superior" or "deluxe" grades, or a city hotel.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Nothing is provided, bring everything

The opposite. **Sleepwear, full toiletries, slippers and a kettle** are standard. Bring your own **face wash and deodorant** (rarely provided), and **a larger towel** if you want one.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The amenities have disappeared

They have **moved to the lobby** under the plastics law. If you did not see the amenity bar at check-in, ask the front desk.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Wear the pyjamas only in the room

At **ryokan**, the yukata is worn to dinner, in the corridors and to the bath — that is what it is for. At business hotels, keep the pyjamas to your room and the laundry corner.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The bathroom floor has a leak

**Unit baths are wet rooms**: the whole floor drains. Shower with the curtain inside the tub, and use the bath mat provided.
