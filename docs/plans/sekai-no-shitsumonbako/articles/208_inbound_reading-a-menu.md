# Q. What do people get wrong about reading a Japanese menu?｜Japan Questions #208

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/reading-a-menu` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A menu is the moment most visitors feel most lost — and the moment Japan is most accommodating. Picture menus, plastic food models, ticket machines and set-meal conventions are all there to help, once you know how they work.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that you need Japanese to order. Most restaurants use one of four systems that need almost none: a photo menu, a window display of food models, a ticket machine, or a tablet. The vocabulary that actually matters is about 15 words — set meal, large portion, without, and the allergens.

## The systems

| System | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Ticket machine (shokkenki)** | Ramen, curry, soba, gyudon chains | Insert cash or IC card, press the button, hand the ticket to staff. **Top-left button is usually the standard dish** |
| **Photo menu / tablet** | Chains, izakaya, family restaurants | Tap or point; tablets often have an **English toggle** |
| **Plastic food models (shokuhin sampuru)** | Department-store restaurant floors, station buildings | Point at the window display; staff know the item |
| **Written menu only** | Small local places, sushi counters | Ask for "osusume" (recommendation) or "omakase" (leave it to you); state a budget |

## The 15 words

| Japanese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 定食 teishoku | **Set meal**: main, rice, miso soup, pickles |
| セット setto | Set with drink or side |
| 大盛り ōmori / 特盛 tokumori | Large / extra-large (often free or ¥100) |
| 小盛り komori | Small |
| おかわり okawari | Refill (rice at some teishoku places is free) |
| 〜抜き -nuki | **Without** (e.g. 玉ねぎ抜き, no onion) |
| 辛さ karasa | Spice level |
| 麺硬め men katame | Firm noodles (ramen) |
| ランチ ranchi | Lunch set, usually cheaper than dinner |
| お会計 okaikei | The bill |
| 別々 betsu-betsu | Separate bills |
| 持ち帰り mochikaeri | Take away |
| 店内 tennai | Eat in (tax rate question at convenience stores) |
| アレルギー arerugī | Allergy |
| 苦手 nigate | Cannot eat / dislike |

> **[Verify]** Vocabulary is standard; allergen labelling on restaurant menus is voluntary (see the allergy labelling article).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Restaurants without English menus do not want foreigners

They are **small and busy**. Pointing at a neighbour's dish or the window model is normal, and "kore o kudasai" (this one, please) works everywhere.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The ticket machine is an obstacle

It is the **easiest system in the world**: no ordering conversation, no bill, no tipping. Cash is the default; many now take IC cards.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Set meals are tourist menus

**Teishoku is what workers eat at lunch**, and the best-value meal in Japan: main, rice, soup and pickles for **¥800–1,200**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can customise freely

Removing an ingredient (**-nuki**) is usually fine; **substituting** is not part of the culture and may be refused, politely. Ramen shops often let you choose noodle firmness, oil and salt level — those are the customisations offered.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The camera translation app is enough

It reads the words but misses the **system** — that the machine wants money first, that the paper on the wall is today's specials, that the smaller number is the lunch price. Watch what the person before you does.
