# Q. What do people get wrong about queueing and reservations in Japan?｜Japan Questions #209

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/queueing-and-reservations` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan's queues are orderly and its most famous restaurants are booked out months ahead — both true, and both misread. Where you stand, how you hold a place, and whether you need a booking at all follow rules that are not written down.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that queueing is optional courtesy. Queues are the system: marked lines at train doors, name lists at restaurants, and a strict "the whole party must be present" rule at popular shops. Reservations are needed for a narrow band of famous places; most restaurants, including excellent ones, are walk-in.

## How queues work

| Setting | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Train platforms** | Stand in the **painted lines**; two lines per door; **let passengers off first**, then board. Ladies-only cars (marked pink) on some lines during morning rush |
| **Restaurants** | **Write your name on the list** at the door or take a ticket from a machine; **the whole party must be present** to be seated |
| **Popular shops / releases** | Staff issue **numbered tickets (seiriken)** — often early morning; **one per person, present in person** |
| **Escalators** | Stand on the **left** in Tokyo, **right** in Osaka; walking up is discouraged by rail operators, and **Saitama (2021) and Nagoya (2023) have ordinances requiring riders to stand still** (no fines) |
| **Taxis** | Queue at the marked stand; doors open automatically |
| **Convenience-store tills** | A single "fork" queue feeds several tills — **wait at the head of the one line** |

## Reservations

- **Need a booking**: high-end sushi and kaiseki, restaurants with under 10 seats, famous ramen with online ticketing, teamLab-type attractions, Ghibli Museum/Park (**advance sale only** — Museum tickets for the next month go on sale on the 10th via Lawson Ticket; no tickets at the door), popular hotels in cherry-blossom season.
- **Walk-in is normal**: izakaya, teishoku, most ramen, department-store restaurant floors, chains.
- **No-show culture**: cancellations without notice are a serious issue; some restaurants now take **credit-card guarantees or deposits**, and hotels may charge **100% for same-day cancellation**.

> **[Verify]** Specific attraction booking systems change yearly; check each official site.

## Misunderstanding 1｜You can save a place for friends

At restaurants, **the party must be complete** to be seated, and joining a queue late is noticed. At shops with tickets, **one ticket per person present**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A queue means the place is a tourist trap

Residents queue too, and a **queue of locals at 11:45** is the most reliable restaurant signal in the country.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Everything good needs a reservation

The famous handful does. **Most excellent food in Japan is walk-in** — go before 12:00 or after 13:30 for lunch, before 18:30 for dinner.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cancelling is no big deal

A no-show at a small restaurant can wipe out its evening. **Cancel by phone or the booking platform as early as possible**; many now charge for late cancellations, and hotels enforce cancellation policies strictly.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Standing on the wrong side of the escalator is a faux pas

The left/right convention varies by city, and rail operators now **ask everyone to stand still on both sides** for safety. Follow the people in front of you and hold the rail.
