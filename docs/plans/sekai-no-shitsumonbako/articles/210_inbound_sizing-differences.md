# Q. What do people get wrong about clothing and shoe sizes in Japan?｜Japan Questions #210

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/sizing-differences` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Buying clothes and shoes is on many visitors' lists, and it is where disappointment is most common: the letter on the label means something different, and the shoe size uses a different unit entirely.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a Japanese M is an international M. Japanese sizes run one to two sizes smaller than US and European equivalents, shoe sizes are in centimetres of foot length, and widths in Japan are generally wider (especially E–3E). Try on, and know your foot length in centimetres.

## Conversions (approximate)

### Shoes (JIS, in cm of foot length)

| Japan (cm) | US men | US women | UK | EU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23.0 | — | 6 | 4 | 36–37 |
| 24.0 | 6 | 7 | 5 | 38 |
| 25.0 | 7 | 8 | 6 | 39–40 |
| 26.0 | 8 | 9 | 7 | 41 |
| 27.0 | 9 | 10 | 8 | 42–43 |
| 28.0 | 10 | 11 | 9 | 44 |

Sizes above **28.0 cm (men) and 25.5 cm (women)** are limited in ordinary shops; look for "large size" (大きいサイズ) sections or specialist stores.

### Clothing (women's tops, guide)

| Japan | US | UK | EU |
|---|---|---|---|
| S (7) | XS (0–2) | 6 | 34 |
| M (9) | S (4) | 8 | 36 |
| L (11) | M (6–8) | 10 | 38 |
| LL / XL (13) | L (10) | 12 | 40 |

- Japanese shoe sizing follows **JIS S 5037**: the number is **foot length in cm**, with width grades **A–F (E, 2E, 3E, 4E common)**. **3E is roughly a US wide.**
- Clothing follows **JIS L 4004/4005** with numeric "号" sizes (7, 9, 11, 13) for women and chest-based sizes for men; **sleeve and inseam lengths run short**.
- Many chains publish the **actual garment measurements (寸法) in cm** on the tag or website — use those, not the letter.

> **[Verify]** Conversion tables are approximations; brands vary. JIS references with the Japanese Standards Association.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Buy your usual letter size

**Go up one or two.** Or ignore letters and read the **cm measurements** on the tag — Japanese labels are unusually precise.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Shoe sizes are just a different number system

They are **centimetres of your foot**. Measure heel to longest toe standing; that number is your Japanese size. Add 0.5 cm for thick socks.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Japanese shoes are narrow

**The opposite**: standard Japanese widths (E–2E) are wider than many Western standard lasts, because Japanese feet are on average wider. If you have narrow feet, look for D or C widths.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Large sizes do not exist

They exist in **specialist shops and online**; department stores and big fashion chains carry limited ranges beyond L. Men's tall sizes (身長 180 cm+) are the hardest.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Returns are easy if it does not fit

**Return policies are stricter than in the US**; many shops refuse returns of worn or tagged-removed items, and some allow exchange only. **Try on before paying.**
