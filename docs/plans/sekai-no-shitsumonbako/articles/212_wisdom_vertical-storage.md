# Q. What do people get wrong about vertical storage in small Japanese homes?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #212

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/vertical-storage` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Foreign visitors to a Japanese flat notice how much is stored without the room looking full. The technique is mostly one idea, applied everywhere: use the height, and keep the floor clear.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese homes are tidy because people own less. Average living space per person is about 40 m² and urban flats are smaller, so storage goes up — to the ceiling, over doors, on the backs of doors, in the gap beside the fridge — and objects are stored standing so any one can be taken without moving the rest.

## The methods

| Method | Where | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Tension poles and shelves (tsuppari)** | Any gap between two walls or under a counter — **no screws** | Curtain, shelf, hanging rail, bathroom shelf |
| **Over-door / top-of-closet shelf** | The 30–40 cm of dead space above a closet rail or door | Seasonal bedding, suitcases |
| **Slim wheeled trolleys** | The **10–15 cm gap** beside the fridge or washing machine | Spices, cleaning goods, bottles |
| **Standing files and dividers** | Drawers, cupboards | Plates on edge, pans on edge, clothes folded upright |
| **Hooks on the inside of doors** | Cupboard doors, the genkan | Lids, brushes, bags, keys |
| **Stackable drawer units sized to the closet (oshiire)** | The standard closet depth (**about 80–90 cm**) | Clothes, in units that roll out |

- The 2023 Housing and Land Survey puts the average dwelling at **90.86 m²** of floor area, which works out at roughly **40 m² per person**; in Tokyo's 23 wards the average rental flat for one person is **about 25–30 m²**.
- The **oshiire** (built-in closet with a middle shelf, sized to bedding) is the origin of Japan's modular storage market: drawer units are sold in **widths of 39, 44 and 54 cm** to fit half or third of a standard closet.
- The principle common to the popular Japanese organising methods: **store vertically, one category per container, everything visible at once.**

> **[Verify]** Housing figures with the 2023 Housing and Land Survey (MIC); product dimensions are standard ranges.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The secret is owning less

Owning less helps, but **the structural reason is height**. A 2.4 m room has three times the volume of its floor area suggests; most homes abroad use the bottom third.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Renters cannot install storage

**Tension poles and adhesive hooks** are designed for rentals where drilling is forbidden. Load ratings are printed on the packaging — respect them.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Stacking is storing

Stacking hides everything but the top item. **Standing** — plates on edge in a rack, clothes folded to stand in a drawer — lets you see and take any one.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Buy storage first

Japanese organisers say the opposite: **measure the space, sort what stays, then buy containers that fit exactly**. Storage bought first becomes clutter.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Small homes are a hardship to be pitied

Small homes cost less to heat, clean and furnish, and they force decisions. The Everyday Wisdom articles on refill packs, single-purpose tools and seasonal rotation all exist because of the small home.
