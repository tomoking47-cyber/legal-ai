# Q. What do people get wrong about drying laundry on the balcony in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #215

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/balcony-laundry` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Every Japanese balcony has a pair of hooks or arms for a laundry pole, and on a sunny morning a city looks like it has hung itself out to dry. Dryers exist but are not the default; the reasons are cost, space and preference.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japan air-dries laundry for lack of dryers. Households choose it: electricity is expensive, flats are small, and sun-dried sheets are valued. The system has its own equipment — the pole, the pinch hangers, the "peg octopus" — and rules about privacy, wind and pollen that a newcomer needs to learn.

## The equipment and the rules

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| **Laundry pole (monohoshi-zao)**, 2–4 m, on balcony arms | Hangers hook onto it; fixed to the arms with clips or ties against wind |
| **Pinch hanger (the "octopus")**, 20–50 pegs on a frame | Socks, underwear, small items; one hook to the pole |
| **Wide hangers** with sliding arms | Shirts dry without shoulder marks |
| **Bath-towel hangers** (wide, three bars) | Towels dry flat |
| **Laundry cover / net** | Pollen season, privacy, sudden rain |
| Balcony rules | Do not hang **over the rail** (falling objects); many condominium rules **ban futon over the rail** |

- Electricity in Japan costs around **¥31 per kWh** (the nationwide reference unit price set by the Electric Appliance Fair Trade Council in 2022; Tokyo's 2026 metered bands run ¥29.8–40.5 per kWh); a heat-pump dryer cycle uses **about 1 kWh**, a heater-type **2–3 kWh** — so a family drying daily saves roughly **¥1,000–3,500 a month** by hanging.
- Drying time: **3–5 hours** on a dry sunny day; humidity in June and September can leave clothes damp all day, hence the **indoor-drying industry** (see that article).
- **Pollen season (February–April)** and **yellow dust (spring)** make many households dry indoors or cover the laundry.

> **[Verify]** Electricity rates change; recalculate. Condominium rules vary — check your building's regulations.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Japan lacks dryers

Washer-dryers are common; they are just not used daily. **Cost and the belief that sun-dried laundry is better** decide it.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Hang it anywhere on the balcony

**Over the rail is discouraged or banned** (things fall; it looks untidy to neighbours). Everything hangs from the pole on the inside of the rail.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The sun is always good

Sun bleaches **dark and coloured clothes**; many households hang **colours inside-out or in shade** and reserve full sun for whites, towels and sheets.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Leaving laundry out overnight is fine

**Dew and morning humidity re-wet it**, and it signals an empty flat. Laundry comes in before dusk.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The clothes will smell of nothing

Wet laundry that dries slowly — a humid day, too tightly packed — develops the **"room-drying smell"** (bacteria). The fixes: **space between items, a fan, and never leave wet laundry in the machine**.
