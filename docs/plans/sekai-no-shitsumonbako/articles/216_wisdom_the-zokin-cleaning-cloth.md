# Q. What do people get wrong about the zōkin (Japanese cleaning cloth)?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #216

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-zokin-cleaning-cloth` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese children wipe their classroom floors with a folded cloth every day. That cloth — the zōkin — is a hand-sewn rag, and it carries a whole approach to cleaning: use what you have, wipe rather than spray, and clean as part of the day rather than as an event.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese cleanliness is about products. The basic tool is a rag made from an old towel, folded and stitched into a thick pad, used damp and wrung out hard, then washed and hung to dry. Wiping with water removes most household dirt; chemicals are the exception, not the rule.

## The method

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| The cloth | **Old bath towel or two hand towels, folded and stitched** (about 20 × 30 cm); school children are asked to bring one each term |
| Wringing | **Wrung hard** — a damp cloth cleans, a wet one smears and leaves water to dry into marks |
| Folding | Folded in **quarters or eighths**; turn to a clean face as one gets dirty, so a single cloth gives **8–16 clean surfaces** |
| Order | **Top to bottom, far to near**; floors last (see the year-end clean article) |
| Water vs chemicals | Water for dust and most grime; **baking soda** for grease, **citric acid** for scale (see that article); commercial cleaners for mould |
| After | **Rinse, wring, hang to dry** — a wet cloth left balled up smells within a day |

- Japanese schools include **cleaning time (sōji no jikan)** in the day — typically **10–20 minutes** — in which pupils clean their own classrooms and corridors. It is regarded as part of education, not janitorial work.
- Microfibre cloths have joined the zōkin; for glass and screens they are better. For floors, the towel zōkin is preferred because it is heavier and can be wrung harder.
- **Colour coding** — one cloth for floors, one for tables, one for the toilet — is the household norm.

> **[Verify]** School cleaning practices with MEXT guidance on school activities; the rest are widely shared conventions.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Spray cleaners are the standard

For daily cleaning the standard is **water and a well-wrung cloth**. Sprays leave residue and cost money; the cloth costs nothing.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A wetter cloth cleans better

**Damp, not wet.** A dripping cloth spreads dirt and leaves streaks; the wringing is the skill children are taught first.

## Misunderstanding 3｜One cloth for everything

**Separate cloths by area**, usually by colour, and never let the toilet cloth meet the kitchen. Wash cloths separately from clothes.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cleaning is a weekend job

The zōkin approach is **a few minutes daily**: wipe the table after meals, the sink after washing up, the floor spots as they appear. The big clean is once a year.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Children cleaning school is a cost-saving trick

It is a **stated educational aim** — responsibility for shared space. Most schools also employ cleaning staff for what children cannot do.
