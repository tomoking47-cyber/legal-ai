# Q. What do people get wrong about oshibori (the wet towel)?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #217

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/oshibori` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A rolled towel — hot in winter, cold in summer — arrives before the menu at almost every Japanese restaurant. Visitors use it for their faces, their necks, the table, or not at all, and wonder if there is a rule. There is, loosely.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the oshibori is a face towel. It is for the hands, offered before eating in a culture that eats with chopsticks and often with fingers (sushi, onigiri). Wiping the face is tolerated for older men and small children, frowned on for everyone else; wiping the table is not what it is for. It is also an industry: rental towels laundered under a hygiene standard.

## What it is

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | **Clean hands before eating** |
| Temperature | **Hot (about 50–60°C) in cold months, chilled in summer** |
| Types | **Cloth rental** (laundered, returned to the supplier) or **disposable paper/non-woven** in a sealed pack |
| After use | **Fold or roll loosely and set it beside your plate** — staff will take it; do not leave it crumpled or use it as a napkin |
| Industry standard | Rental oshibori are laundered under **hygiene standards set by MHLW guidance and the industry association**, with bacterial count limits |
| Convenience stores | A disposable one is offered with bento — ask if not (**"oshibori arimasu ka?"**) |

- The habit dates to the **Edo period**, when inns left a bucket of water and cloths for arriving guests; rental oshibori appeared in the **1950s**.
- The **Japan Oshibori Association** estimates rental oshibori use in the **hundreds of millions per month**, with each towel laundered and reused **dozens of times**.

> **[Verify]** Usage figures with the Japan Oshibori Association (全国おしぼり協同組合連合会); hygiene standards with MHLW's rental-oshibori guidance (貸おしぼりの衛生等管理要領).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Wipe your face with it

**Hands only.** Face-wiping (and, worse, neck- and armpit-wiping) is the caricature of the middle-aged salaryman; it is not polite.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Use it as a napkin during the meal

It is offered **before** the meal for hands. Keeping it to wipe fingers between sushi pieces is acceptable; using it to wipe your mouth repeatedly or mop spills is not. Ask for paper napkins.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Wipe the table with it

The table is the restaurant's job. If the table is dirty, ask staff.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Take it home

Cloth oshibori are **rental property** returned to the laundry. Disposable ones you may keep.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is just a towel

It is a **greeting**. Being handed a hot towel on a cold day, or a cold one in August, is the restaurant's first act of hospitality, and it sets the tone.
