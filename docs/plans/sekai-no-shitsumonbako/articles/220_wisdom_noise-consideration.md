# Q. What do people get wrong about noise and neighbour etiquette in Japanese flats?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #220

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/noise-consideration` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese apartment buildings are quiet, and newcomers assume the walls are thick. They are not. The quiet comes from a shared set of habits about what you do after 21:00, how you close a door, and what you say when you move in.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese buildings are soundproof. Many are wooden or light-steel with thin partitions; the quiet is behavioural. The habits: no washing machine or vacuum late at night, no shoes indoors, soft closing of doors and windows, and a small gift and greeting to the neighbours when you move in. Noise is the number-one neighbour complaint in Japan.

## The unwritten timetable

| Time | What is avoided in flats |
|---|---|
| **Before 8:00 and after 21:00–22:00** | Washing machine, vacuum cleaner, hair dryer near a shared wall, musical instruments, loud TV |
| **After 22:00–23:00** | Bathing in older buildings with noisy plumbing (varies), moving furniture, phone calls on the balcony |
| Any time | **Walking in shoes indoors**, slamming doors, running children (rugs and play mats are used), balcony parties |

- **Noise is the most common complaint** to building managers and to police "life safety" consultations; the Ministry of the Environment's household noise pages list **footsteps, doors, washing machines and TV** as typical sources.
- Local government **noise ordinances** do reach households: Tokyo's Environmental Protection Ordinance (環境確保条例 Art. 136, Table 13) caps **everyday domestic noise** at the property boundary — at night (23:00–06:00) **40 dB in low-rise residential zones and 45 dB in other residential zones** — though enforcement against a neighbour is rare, so in practice it is treated as a civil matter, handled by the **building management association (kanri kumiai) or landlord**.
- **Moving-in greeting (hikkoshi no aisatsu)**: a small gift (**¥500–1,000**; towel, sweets, detergent) to the neighbours **on both sides, above and below**, with "I've just moved in, please excuse any noise" — it pre-empts most future friction.

> **[Verify]** Tokyo ordinance limits with the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's environmental noise standards; complaint statistics with the Ministry of the Environment.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The walls are thick

**Light construction is normal**; you will hear your neighbour's alarm. The quiet is because everyone acts as if the walls were paper.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Daytime noise is fine

Daytime is more tolerant, but **shift workers and infants sleep in the day**. A vacuum at 10:00 is fine; drums are not.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Nobody talks to neighbours anyway

They do not chat, but **the move-in greeting is expected**, and it is the moment that determines whether a later noise is forgiven or reported.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Complain directly to the neighbour

Direct confrontation is avoided. **Tell the management company or landlord**, who deliver an anonymous notice to the whole floor. Doing this yourself, politely, is acceptable only for a one-off.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Children's noise is exempt

It is the **most disputed category**. Households with children use **thick play mats, rugs and slippers**, and greet the downstairs neighbour specifically.
