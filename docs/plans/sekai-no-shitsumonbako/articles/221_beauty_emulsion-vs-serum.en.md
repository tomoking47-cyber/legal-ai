# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about emulsions and serums?｜Japan Questions #221

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/emulsion-vs-serum` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese skincare has many steps: lotion (化粧水, keshōsui — a watery toner), serum (美容液, biyōeki), emulsion (乳液, nyūeki — a light, milky moisturiser) and cream. Visitors often ask why there are so many stages, and what an "emulsion" actually is. Here we sort the products by their legal category and their role.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "an emulsion and a serum both 'put active ingredients in', so one or the other will do." In Japanese usage they have different jobs. An emulsion mixes water and oil and acts as a "lid" that slows the evaporation of water from the skin. A serum "adds" a higher level of a particular ingredient. Legally, however, both are "cosmetics", and the claims they may make are the same 56 items.

## Roles (the customary classification)

| Product | Main role | Texture | Legal category |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lotion (化粧水, keshōsui) | Gives water; helps the next product spread | Watery | Cosmetic |
| **Serum (美容液, biyōeki)** | **Adds a higher level of particular ingredients** (moisturising or skin-conditioning ingredients, etc.) | Gel to slightly thick | Cosmetic (some are **quasi-drugs**) |
| **Emulsion (乳液, nyūeki)** | **Adds water and oil, and slows evaporation** | Milky (oil-in-water) | Cosmetic |
| Cream | More oil; a firmer **lid** | Thick | Cosmetic |

- These classes are **industry custom** and **have no legal definition.** The Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law) has only three categories: "cosmetics", "quasi-drugs" and "medicines".
- A cosmetic may claim only **56 listed effects** ("moisturises the skin", "protects the skin", "keeps the skin healthy", and so on). The **same range** applies to a serum and to an emulsion. "Erases wrinkles" or "heals dark spots" cannot be claimed.
- A **quasi-drug ("medicated", 薬用) serum** — a quasi-drug is a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines — may state **approved claims** for its approved active ingredient (e.g. niacinamide, tranexamic acid, retinol): "suppresses melanin production and prevents dark spots and freckles", or "makes fine lines caused by dryness less visible (efficacy evaluation test completed)".

> **[Verify]** Check the 56 items against MHLW notice Yakushoku-hatsu 0721 No. 1, and the scope of quasi-drug claims against Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) materials. "Makes fine lines caused by dryness less visible" is wording based on the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association's efficacy evaluation test guideline.

## Misunderstanding 1｜An emulsion is oil, so oily skin does not need one

The oil content of emulsions **varies widely between products**, and light, fresh types exist. The basic idea in Japanese skincare is that **if you add water and put no lid on it, the water evaporates and the skin dries** (see our article on oily skin).

## Misunderstanding 2｜The more expensive the serum, the better it works

Cosmetic serums all share **the same range of permitted claims**; the differences are in ingredients and formulation. **The quasi-drug label and its active ingredient** tell you more than the price does.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more serums you layer, the better

**Layering several serums can stack up irritating ingredients, or leave product sitting unabsorbed on the skin.** The usual advice is to **pick one or two goals**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Lotion after emulsion penetrates better

The customary order is **from the most watery product to the most oily**. Lotion after emulsion means **putting water on top of a film of oil**. **Products the maker has designed as a "pre-emulsion" (先行乳液, senkō nyūeki)** are the exception — follow the label.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A Western "moisturiser" is the same as an emulsion

A "moisturiser" abroad is closer to **emulsion and cream combined**; it is not split as finely as in Japan. **Replacing "lotion → serum → emulsion" with a single moisturiser** can work perfectly well, depending on your skin type.
