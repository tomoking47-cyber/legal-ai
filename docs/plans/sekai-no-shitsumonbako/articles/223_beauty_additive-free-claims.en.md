# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about "additive-free" (無添加) claims?｜Japan Questions #223

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/additive-free-claims` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Mutenka" (無添加, "additive-free") is one of the most visible words in a Japanese cosmetics aisle, but what has been left out differs from product to product. Like "clean beauty" in the West, the word is used without any legal definition.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "additive-free means nothing has been added, or that the product is safe." "Mutenka" has no legal definition; the product is only stating what it does not contain. Since full ingredient labelling became compulsory for cosmetics in 2001, "free of former designated ingredients" has little meaning today. Under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations (Japan's advertising law), an unsupported "additive-free, therefore safe" is a problem.

## Background of the system

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1980 onwards | **Designated-ingredient labelling system**: **102 ingredients plus fragrance (103 in total)** with a possible link to allergy etc. had to be listed. The claim "free of designated ingredients" spread |
| **April 2001** | **Switch to full ingredient labelling**. The designated-ingredient system was abolished. **As every ingredient is now listed, "additive-free" has nothing left to compare against** |
| Today | "Mutenka" is governed by **industry self-regulation** (the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association's advertising guidelines), which require makers to **state clearly what has been left out** |

- Under the **Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations**, unsupported superiority claims such as **"additive-free, so safe for skin" or "additive-free, so non-irritating"** risk counting as **misleading representations of quality** (優良誤認, yūryō gonin). The Consumer Affairs Agency issued a **guideline in 2022** on "additive-free" claims for food, and the same thinking is referred to for cosmetics.
- Ingredients most often "left out": **parabens (preservatives), fragrance, colourants, mineral oil, alcohol (ethanol) and UV absorbers**. **Preservative-free products often deteriorate faster after opening**, and are given a shorter use-by period.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Additive-free means no skin trouble

**Naturally derived ingredients (essential oils, plant extracts) can also cause allergies.** "Mutenka" only means certain ingredients are absent; **it does not mean non-irritating.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Parabens are dangerous

Parabens are **preservatives with a long record of use, and Japan's Standards for Cosmetics set a maximum concentration for them**. **We can find no official basis for calling them "dangerous".** Some people do not tolerate them, but the same is true of any ingredient.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Preservative-free means higher quality

Without a preservative, **bacteria grow more easily and the product's life after opening is shorter.** "Preservative-free" products usually assume **a small container, refrigeration and quick use**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜If it says "mutenka", there is no need to read the ingredients

**Reading the full ingredient list** is more reliable. Ingredients are listed **in descending order of quantity (those at 1% or below may be in any order)**. **Remembering which ingredients do not suit you** is the practical approach.

## Misunderstanding 5｜"Organic" and "natural" have set standards

In Japan **there is no public certification scheme for "organic" cosmetics.** Products with a private certification (such as the international COSMOS scheme) carry its mark. **"Organic" without a mark is self-declared.**
