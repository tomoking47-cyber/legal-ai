# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about "quasi-drugs" and "cosmetics"?｜Japan Questions #224

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/quasi-drugs-vs-cosmetics` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese cosmetics shelves are full of products marked "薬用" (yakuyō, "medicated") or "医薬部外品" (iyakubugaihin, "quasi-drug"). Visitors often ask whether these are medicines or cosmetics. The category is unique to Japan, and it draws a clear line around what a product may claim.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a quasi-drug is a premium cosmetic, and is bound to work better." A quasi-drug is a category for products that contain an approved active ingredient at an approved concentration and may state approved claims (for example "prevents dark spots and freckles", "prevents acne"). It is true that a quasi-drug "may say more" than a cosmetic. It does not mean the effect is stronger.

## The three categories (Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act, Japan's cosmetics and drugs law)

| Category | Purpose | Approval | Label | Claims allowed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Medicines (医薬品)** | Medical use and prevention of disease | Each product approved | "Class 1–3 OTC drug" etc. | Approved indications |
| **Quasi-drugs (医薬部外品)** | **Mild action on the body**, for specific purposes (**prevention, hygiene**) | Each product approved (active ingredient and concentration) | **"医薬部外品"** ("薬用", medicated, is the customary word) | **Approved claims**: prevents dark spots and freckles, prevents acne, prevents rough skin, prevents body odour, prevents dandruff and itching, etc. |
| **Cosmetics (化粧品)** | **Cleansing, beautifying, adding attractiveness, keeping healthy** | Notification only (no approval) | No label, or "化粧品" | Within the **56 items** (moisturises, protects, etc.) |

- **"Yakuyō" (medicated) is not a legal term**; it is the customary word for a quasi-drug. Look for the words "医薬部外品" on the package to be sure.
- **Typical quasi-drug active ingredients**: brightening (**tranexamic acid, arbutin, vitamin C derivatives, niacinamide**), anti-inflammatory (**dipotassium glycyrrhizinate**), antibacterial (**isopropyl methylphenol**). **The Japanese term "bihaku" (美白, "brightening") is limited to the meaning "suppresses melanin production and prevents dark spots and freckles"**; **"makes skin whiter" or "erases spots" cannot be claimed.**
- **The 2013 case of leukoderma (white patches) caused by the brightening ingredient Rhododenol** showed that quasi-drugs can also cause skin trouble. On 4 July 2013 the marketing companies began a **voluntary recall**, and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) published the status of that recall.

> **[Verify]** Check the category definitions against Article 2 of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act, the scope of quasi-drug claims and active ingredients against MHLW materials, and the Rhododenol history against MHLW's published documents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Quasi-drugs are stronger than cosmetics

**"Mild action" is part of the definition of a quasi-drug.** Containing an active ingredient and being strong are different things. **Cosmetics sometimes contain the same ingredient as a "skin-conditioning ingredient"**; the difference is **the approved concentration and the claims allowed**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜If it says "medicated", it is a medicine

**It is not a medicine.** It is a quasi-drug. **It cannot be used for medical purposes.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Brightening cosmetics make dark spots disappear

The approved "brightening" claim for quasi-drugs is **"suppresses melanin production and prevents dark spots and freckles" — that is, prevention**. **No cosmetic or quasi-drug is approved to remove spots that already exist.** That is the domain of a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).

## Misunderstanding 4｜A quasi-drug cannot cause skin trouble

**As the Rhododenol case shows, quasi-drugs can also cause serious skin trouble.** If your skin reacts, stop and see a dermatologist.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Other countries have the same category

**The quasi-drug is a category unique to Japan.** "Cosmeceutical" abroad is not a legal term; the US has two categories (cosmetics and drugs), and the EU has one (the Cosmetics Regulation). **When a Japanese quasi-drug is sold abroad, it is handled locally as either a "cosmetic" or a "drug".**
