# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about in-store testers and free samples?｜Japan Questions #225

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/tester-hygiene` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Testers are a fixture of Japanese cosmetics counters, in department stores and drugstores (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.) alike. "Who knows who has used this tester — is it safe?" is a question asked abroad too. Since 2020, most companies have changed how they run their testers.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the shop looks after the testers, so they are clean." A tester is touched by an unknown number of people, and lipsticks, mascaras and creams in particular are formats where bacteria grow easily. The basics: test on the back of your hand, use disposable applicators and spatulas, and never apply a tester directly to your eyes or lips.

## What to consider

| Type of tester | Risk | How to test |
|---|---|---|
| **Lipstick (stick)** | Traces of contact with mouths; bacteria | **Ask staff to wipe the surface, then take some with a finger or cotton bud onto the back of your hand.** Do not apply to your lips |
| **Mascara, liquid liner** | Shared brush | Only where **disposable brushes or applicators** are provided. Never directly on the eyes |
| Cream in a jar | Touched with fingers | **Take it with a spatula** (ask staff) |
| Powders | Relatively low | Disposable applicator, or the back of your hand |
| Lotion, pump bottles | Relatively low | Dispense onto the back of your hand |
| **Free samples (sachets)** | Low if unopened | Check any date on the label; **once opened, use it up in one go** |

- Cosmetics that **stay stable for 3 years or more unopened are not required to carry an expiry date**, so in practice **you cannot tell when a tester was opened**. The Japan Cosmetic Industry Association advises shops to **replace and wipe testers regularly**.
- **Since 2020**, many shops have **stopped "touch-ups" (applying product directly to the customer's face) or made them appointment-only**, and **providing disposable tools** has become standard.
- A "sample" is **the same product as the one sold**, but **the labelling (ingredients, manufacturer) is sometimes abbreviated**. If you cannot see the full ingredient list, check the full-size product on the shelf.

> **[Verify]** Check the scope of the expiry-labelling requirement against Article 61 of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law), and tester hygiene against the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association's in-store hygiene guide.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A tester tells you whether a product suits your skin

**The back of the hand and the face are different skin, and one trial tells you only colour and texture.** **Whether a product suits you takes several days to judge**, so **taking a sample home** is the reliable way.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A wiped lipstick is clean

Wiping removes the surface, but **not bacteria that have got inside.** **Do not apply it directly to your lips.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The age of a tester does not matter

**A tester opened long ago may differ from the real product in scent, colour and texture.** If it has **separated, discoloured or changed smell**, it tells you nothing useful.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Samples keep for years

**Sachet samples are thin and deteriorate easily in light and heat**, so **use within about a year, and in one go once opened**. Discard any whose date you cannot tell.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Having staff apply it to your face is the surest way

With touch-ups, **hygiene of brushes and sponges varies from shop to shop.** Choose a shop that takes appointments and **uses disposable tools**, or **test on the back of your own hand** — the safer option.
