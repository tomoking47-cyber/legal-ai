# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about patch-testing hair dye?｜Japan Questions #226

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/hair-dye-patch-test` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Home hair colour is convenient, but every year in Japan people report rashes from it, and the Consumer Safety Investigation Commission published a report in 2015. Allergy to the same ingredients (diamines) is an issue abroad as well.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "I used it before with no problem, so I do not need to test." Hair-dye allergy can appear suddenly after many uses, and once it has developed you can no longer use any product containing the same ingredient. The product label asks for a patch test 48 hours before every use.

## What to consider

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Main ingredients responsible | **Diamines such as para-phenylenediamine (PPD) in oxidative (permanent) hair dyes** |
| Symptoms | **A few hours to 48 hours** after use: **redness, itching and swelling** of the scalp, face and neck. **Swollen eyelids** are characteristic |
| Regulation | Oxidative hair dyes are **quasi-drugs** (a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines). **A patch test 48 hours before use** is stated in the product's precautions |
| Consumer Safety Investigation Commission | **October 2015** report on "skin disorders caused by hair dye". Strongly recommended that **anyone who has once reacted should stop using it** |
| Accident reports | **Around 200 skin-injury cases from hair dye are registered each fiscal year** in the Consumer Affairs Agency's Accident Information Database (as of the 2015 report) |

### Patch test procedure (example from product labels)

1. **48 hours before use**, mix a small amount of solution 1 and solution 2
2. Apply a patch the size of a 10-yen coin (roughly 2 cm across) to the **inside of your arm** and **let it dry naturally**
3. **Check after 30 minutes and after 48 hours.** If there is **redness, itching or swelling, do not use the product**
4. **Do not wash the area** during the 48 hours

> **[Verify]** Check the report's date and content against the published documents of the Consumer Affairs Agency's Consumer Safety Investigation Commission, and the enquiry numbers against National Consumer Affairs Center materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It was fine before, so it will be fine this time

**Diamine allergy appears suddenly, once "sensitisation" has built up.** Many cases involve people who had used dye for years, so **testing every time** is what the label asks for.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A mild rash just means being a bit more careful next time

**Once an allergy has developed, later reactions can be more severe.** The Commission recommends that **"anyone who has reacted even once should not use diamine hair dyes again".**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Hair manicure and colour conditioners are the same thing

**Acid hair colours ("hair manicure", ヘアマニキュア) and colour conditioners mostly contain no diamines and are classed as cosmetics.** However, **some products marketed as "botanical" or "henna" do contain oxidative (diamine-type) dyes**: in **May 2026** the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan reported **anaphylaxis** caused by henna products for wigs that contained oxidative dye. **Always check the listed ingredients** — and note that **they colour differently, and some people react to other ingredients in them.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜It is safe at a salon

**The ingredients are the same.** At a salon too, **always tell the stylist about any past reaction**. **Anyone who has ever reacted is told not to use oxidative hair dye at all — and not to patch-test either**, because the test itself can trigger a strong reaction. If you still want to colour your hair, ask a dermatologist or your stylist about products that contain no oxidative dye.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Redness at the hairline is just the dye stinging

**Irritation (stinging on the spot) and allergy (swelling hours to two days later) are different things.** **Symptoms that appear later may be an allergy** and need **a visit to a dermatology clinic** (covered by Japanese health insurance). **Swelling of the eyelids or face** especially.
