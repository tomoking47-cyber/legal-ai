# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about conditioner and hair "treatment"?｜Japan Questions #229

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/conditioner-vs-treatment` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

A Japanese hair-care aisle carries "conditioner", "treatment" (トリートメント, torītomento — a richer conditioner), "hair mask" and "rinse" (リンス, rinsu — the older word for conditioner). Visitors often ask whether they are all the same thing. In short: the legal category is the same, and the differences are industry custom.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a treatment restores hair from the inside." Hair is made of dead cells, and damaged parts do not return to their original state. In customary usage, conditioner (rinse) smooths the surface to improve the feel, while a treatment contains more oil and moisturising ingredients and coats the surface more heavily. Both are cosmetics, and the claims they may make are the same range.

## The customary classification

| Name | Customary role | Typical use | Legal category |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rinse / conditioner | **Smooths the surface for slip and shine**; reduces friction and static | **Every wash**, mid-lengths to ends, **can be rinsed off straight away** | Cosmetic |
| Treatment (トリートメント) | **More oil and moisturising ingredients** than conditioner. Heavier | Once to a few times a week, or every wash. Many products say **leave for a few minutes** | Cosmetic (some are **quasi-drugs**, a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines) |
| Hair mask / hair pack | A concentrated treatment | About once a week | Cosmetic |
| Leave-in treatment (洗い流さないトリートメント) | Oil, milk or mist. **Protects against dryer heat and friction** | After towel-drying | Cosmetic |

- A cosmetic may claim only such things as **"supplies and keeps the hair's water and oil", "makes hair supple", "gives hair body and resilience", "prevents split ends and breakage"**. **Wording like "repairs hair" cannot be claimed** (the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association's advertising guidelines require a footnote even for the word "補修" [hoshū, mend]).
- **"Penetrates inside"** also requires, for hair, **a footnote explaining that it means only as far as the inside of the cuticle**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A treatment brings damaged hair back

**Hair does not regenerate.** A treatment **coats the surface, improves feel and shine, and helps prevent further damage.** **Breakage and split ends can only be cut off.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Conditioner plus treatment doubles the effect

It is **layering two products with the same role**, and mostly just **weighs hair down and makes it greasy**. The usual advice is to **choose one**, or **skip conditioner on treatment days**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Put it on the scalp to moisturise the scalp too

**On the scalp it causes blocked pores and greasiness.** Apply from **mid-lengths to ends**. Scalp products are a separate thing (see our article on scalp care).

## Misunderstanding 4｜The longer you leave it, the deeper it penetrates

Most products state that **a few minutes is enough**. **Leaving it longer adds nothing and leads to residue left after rinsing.** Keep to **the time on the label**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Rinse lightly to leave some product in

**Residue causes greasiness, itching and dandruff.** Rinse **until the slippery feel is gone**. **"Leaving product in" is the job of the leave-in type.**
