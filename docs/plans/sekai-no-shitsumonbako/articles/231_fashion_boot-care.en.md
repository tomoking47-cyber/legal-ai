# Q. What do people get wrong about caring for and storing boots?｜Japan Questions #231

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/boot-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Autumn and winter boots come in smooth leather, suede and synthetic leather, and how you put them away decides what state they are in next year. In Japan they sit in storage right through the humid rainy season and summer, so this article is organised by material with that in mind.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "when the season ends, just put them back in the box". Boots absorb more sweat and moisture than any other shoe, and stored with dirt and damp still inside they grow mould and lose their shape. The basic routine is: remove the dirt → dry completely → hold the shape → store upright somewhere with airflow.

## Care by material

| Material | Everyday care | End of season | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Smooth leather** | Brush off dust after wearing, cream once a month | Clean → cream → **waterproofing spray outdoors only** | If wet, stuff with newspaper and dry in the shade. No hairdryer |
| **Suede and nubuck** | **Lift the nap with a suede brush**, rubber-style suede cleaner | Brush → waterproofing spray | **Cream cannot be used** (it flattens and stiffens the nap) |
| **Synthetic leather** | Wipe with a damp cloth | Wipe dry, **store with a desiccant** | **Hydrolysis** advances even when unworn (a few years) |
| **Rubber (rain boots)** | Rinse with water, dry in the shade | Dry completely, **keep out of direct sunlight** | UV and heat harden and crack the rubber |
| **Shearling and fleece-lined** | Dry the inside, deodorise | Draw **all moisture out of the lining** | Many cannot be washed (check the label) |

- The inside of a boot absorbs **foot sweat (about 200 mL a day)**. **Wearing them again the next day before they have dried** is the cause of smell and mould. **Wear for one day, rest for one or two** is the basic rule.
- For storage, **stand the shaft upright with a boot keeper (or rolled newspaper or magazines)** and **never stack them lying down** (creases, lost shape). **Put a desiccant inside.**
- **Inhalation accidents with fluorine-based waterproofing sprays** have been the subject of warnings from the Consumer Affairs Agency, the Japan Poison Information Center and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government. **Spray outdoors, from upwind, and do not breathe it in. Never use one in a poorly ventilated space such as an entrance hall, indoors or inside a car.**

> **[Verify]** The sweat figure is a general physiology statement. Confirm the waterproofing-spray accidents against the published material of the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Suede cannot be cared for at home

**A suede brush, a suede eraser and waterproofing spray** are enough for routine care. **Water leaves marks**, so the basics are to avoid rainy days and to apply waterproofing spray in advance.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Dry wet boots in front of the heater

**Sudden heat stiffens leather and causes cracking, shrinkage and glue coming apart.** **Stuff with newspaper, changing it as it dampens, and leave in a well-ventilated shady spot for one or two days.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more cream you rub in, the longer the leather lasts

**Too much cream leaves a sticky surface that attracts dust and feeds mould.** **Spread a rice-grain amount thinly, and wipe off the excess once it has dried.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The back of the closet, in the box, is the safest place

**No airflow is exactly the condition mould needs.** **If you use the box, put a desiccant in it and open it once a month.** **Never a plastic bag.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Synthetic leather boots need no care and last for years

**Synthetic leather (polyurethane) hydrolyses in humid air; within a few years the surface goes sticky and peels even if the boots are never worn.** Dry storage only slows it down. **Buy them and wear them** is how synthetic leather is meant to be used.
