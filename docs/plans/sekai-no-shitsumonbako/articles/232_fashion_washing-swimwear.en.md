# Q. What do people get wrong about washing swimwear?｜Japan Questions #232

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-swimwear` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"It stretched after one season" and "the colour faded" are complaints heard everywhere in the world, and most of the time the cause is how the swimsuit was washed and dried. In Japan the three enemies of swimwear are well known: pool chlorine, sea salt and sunscreen.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "swimwear is made for water, so it can go through a normal machine wash". The stretch fibre in swimwear (polyurethane, also sold as elastane or spandex) is damaged by chlorine, salt, the oils in sunscreen, heat and hard rubbing. The basic routine is: rinse in fresh water straight after use → hand-wash gently in a neutral detergent → press out the water in a towel → dry in the shade.

## What to go on

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Straight after use | **Rinse in fresh water** (at the pool or beach if you can) | Removes chlorine, salt and sand. **Do not leave it sitting wet** |
| Washing | **Press-wash in lukewarm water (30°C / 86°F or below) with a neutral detergent (the "delicates" type, おしゃれ着用)**. In a machine: **laundry net + gentle cycle + short programme** | Polyurethane is degraded by **alkaline detergents, bleach and fabric softener** |
| Removing water | **Press between towels**. **Do not wring; keep the machine spin short** | Fibres stretch |
| Drying | **Reshape and dry in the shade**. **No direct sun, no tumble dryer** | UV and heat fade the colour and weaken the stretch |
| Storage | Dry completely and **store without sharp folds**. Hangers stretch the shoulder straps, so lay it flat | — |

- Most swimwear is **nylon or polyester plus 10–20% polyurethane**, and **polyurethane degrades with chlorine, UV, heat and time** (a useful life of **one to three years** is the usual guide, depending on how often it is used).
- **Sunscreen, especially oil-based sunscreen, causes yellowing and discolouration where it touches the fabric.** **Wait a while after applying before you put the swimsuit on**, and **keep it off the fabric when you reapply**.
- Care labels in Japan use the international **JIS L0001** symbols. Swimwear usually carries **the tub-with-hand (hand wash), do-not-bleach, do-not-tumble-dry and dry-in-shade** symbols.

> **[Verify]** The polyurethane degradation and lifespan figures are general guides based on textile-industry bodies and makers' information.

## Misunderstanding 1｜After the pool, just let it dry

**Chlorine stays in the fibres after drying and keeps degrading them.** **Rinsing in fresh water the same day** comes first.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Get rid of the chlorine smell with bleach

**Bleach damages polyurethane.** Wash in a neutral detergent and **rinse thoroughly**; that reduces the smell.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A tumble dryer is quicker and more hygienic

**Heat destroys the stretch in polyurethane.** Drying in the shade is quite enough; swimwear is thin and dries in a few hours.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hanging it on a hanger dries it faster

**A wet swimsuit on a hanger stretches the straps and the fabric.** **Dry it flat**, or **lay it on a towel**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Just carry the wet swimsuit home in a plastic bag

**Sealed-in moisture starts mould and smell within a few hours.** **Rinse it, wrap it in a towel, and wash it as soon as you get home.** If you use a plastic bag at all, keep it to **the short trip home**.
