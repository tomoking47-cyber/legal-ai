# Q. What do people get wrong about caring for scarves and gloves?｜Japan Questions #233

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/scarves-and-gloves` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Scarves and gloves touch your skin every day, yet they are among the items most often never washed all winter. They pick up sweat, skin oil and make-up, and they come in wool, cashmere, leather and more. Here is how to handle each.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "accessories don't need washing". A scarf soaks up neck oil and foundation every day; gloves soak up hand sweat and oil. The basic rule is to wash (or wipe) them a few times during the season and always at the end of it. Whether to hand-wash or wipe depends on the material.

## By material

| Material | Washable? | Method | Drying |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wool or cashmere scarf** | **Yes, if the label carries the hand-wash symbol** | Neutral detergent, **30°C (86°F) or below**, press-wash. **Do not rub or knead** | **Dry flat** (hanging stretches it) |
| Acrylic scarf | Yes (check the label) | As above, or machine in a laundry net on a gentle cycle | Dry flat |
| **Knitted gloves** | Yes (check the label) | Wearing them while you wash with foam keeps the shape | Fingertips up, laid flat |
| **Leather gloves** | **No** (no water washing) | Wipe with a dry cloth; leather cleaner for marks; **turn inside out and air the lining in the shade** | Reshape and dry in the shade |
| Synthetic-leather gloves | Surface only | Wipe with a damp cloth | — |
| Touchscreen gloves | Check the label | **Do not rub: the conductive thread is easily damaged** | — |

- **Cashmere has very fine fibres and pills and shrinks easily.** **Washing it correctly, rather than washing it less,** is generally described as the cleaner and longer-lasting approach (**two or three washes a season** is the usual guide).
- **Yellowing around the neck** is foundation and skin oil. **Spot-wash it (dab with diluted neutral detergent)** during the season.
- **Store** wrapped in non-woven fabric **with a moth repellent**. Wool and cashmere are **the fibres moths eat most readily** (see our article on moth repellents).

> **[Verify]** Check the meaning of the symbols in the Consumer Affairs Agency's care-labelling guide. The cashmere washing frequency is a guide based on textile-industry bodies and makers' information.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cashmere must always go to the dry cleaner

**If the label carries the hand-wash symbol, you can wash it at home.** Failures come from **three things: temperature, rubbing and drying on a hanger**, and all three can be avoided.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A scarf doesn't touch the skin much, so it stays clean

**The back of the neck and under the chin concentrate skin oil and foundation.** **Wash it a few times a season**, and always at the end.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Leather gloves caught in the rain just need a wipe

**Wet leather stains and stiffens.** **Press with a towel straight away, reshape, and dry in the shade.** Once dry, **apply a thin layer of leather cream**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Wash one glove at a time

**Unless the pair is washed together, the colour and feel will differ.** Always both at once.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Washing before storage is a chore; wash them at the start of next season

**Skin oil left over the summer leads to moth holes and yellowing.** **Wash before you put them away**: that is the iron rule of the seasonal wardrobe change (衣替え, koromogae).
