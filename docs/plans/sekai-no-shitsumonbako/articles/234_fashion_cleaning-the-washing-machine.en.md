# Q. What do people get wrong about cleaning the washing-machine drum?｜Japan Questions #234

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/cleaning-the-washing-machine` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Black flakes on laundry that is supposed to be clean" is a worry shared around the world, and the cause is mould on the back of the drum. In Japan's hot, humid climate the hidden side of the drum is an ideal breeding ground, so a body of know-how and a range of dedicated drum cleaners (洗濯槽クリーナー, sentakusō kurīnā, sold at drugstores and 100-yen shops) have grown up around it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "water and detergent run through the machine every day, so it must be clean". On the outside of the drum, the side you cannot see, detergent residue, skin oil and damp let mould grow. The usual guide is a drum clean once a month (with a dedicated chlorine-type or oxygen-type cleaner), leaving the lid open to dry after use, and keeping to the stated detergent dose.

## What to go on

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Where mould grows | **Between the inner drum and the outer tub, the lint filter, the detergent drawer, the rubber door seal (front-loaders)** |
| How often to clean | **A drum clean about once a month** (makers' guidance). Machines with a **drum-dry function**: run it about once a week |
| Types of cleaner | **Chlorine type (sodium hypochlorite)**: breaks down mould, quick／**Oxygen type (sodium percarbonate)**: lifts mould off so it floats, **suits top-loaders**, works best with **water at around 40°C (104°F)** |
| How to use | **Follow both the product label and the machine's manual.** Many front-loaders **cannot take oxygen-type cleaners because of the foam** |
| Daily prevention | **Leave the lid open after use**, **do not leave laundry sitting in the drum**, **detergent and softener at the stated dose**, **empty the lint filter every load** |

- **Too much detergent** is one of the biggest causes: **undissolved residue sticks to the back of the drum and feeds mould**.
- **The combination that releases toxic chlorine gas is chlorine-type plus an acidic product.** **Never use a chlorine cleaner together with — or on the same day as — citric acid, vinegar or any acidic cleaner.** This is what the "まぜるな危険" (do not mix — danger) label on the bottle means.
- Chlorine-type and oxygen-type cleaners should also not be combined: **use one or the other on its own** (they work differently, and mixing them does not clean better). **Always ventilate the room while using them.**
- **Behind the rubber seal of a front-loader** water collects easily; **wiping it once a week** is what the makers advise.

> **[Verify]** Check the cleaning frequency and which cleaners are allowed in the manual for your own machine. Check the chlorine safety notes against the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Bureau of Public Health material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The black flakes are undissolved detergent

More often they are **mould peeling off the back of the drum**. Flakes are **the signal to run a drum clean**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Pour in a shop-bought drum cleaner, run a cycle, done

Most **oxygen-type products need several hours of soaking**, and you have to **scoop out the floating mould repeatedly**. **Chlorine types are quick, but watch the smell and the effect on clothes.** Use the **drum-clean course in the manual**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Pour in very hot water to sterilise it

Going above **the machine's heat tolerance (many are rated to 50°C / 122°F or below)** damages the parts. **Around 40°C** is the guide for an oxygen-type cleaner to work well.

## Misunderstanding 4｜More detergent means cleaner

**Undissolved residue feeds mould**, and **rinse residue irritates the skin**. Keep to **the stated dose** and deal with heavy soiling by **pre-washing**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Keeping the lid closed keeps dust out and is cleaner

**A closed lid traps moisture and grows mould.** **Open the lid to dry after use** is the basic rule. Because **children have climbed in and been trapped** (reported by NITE, Japan's product-safety agency), one reasonable choice is **to use the child lock where there is one and, in homes with small children, to keep the lid closed and use the drum-dry function instead**.
