# Q. What do people get wrong about how much detergent to use?｜Japan Questions #235

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/how-much-detergent` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"More detergent gets things cleaner" is one of the most stubborn laundry myths in the world. In Japan detergents have become highly concentrated, so "one cap" means something different for every product, and the measuring itself has become a source of confusion, especially if you cannot read the Japanese label.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "more detergent, more cleaning". Detergent is designed around a stated dose for a given volume of water; going over it does not add cleaning power, but does add rinse residue, mould in the drum and skin irritation. The basic rule is to check the water volume (or the weight of the load) and measure out the amount printed on the product.

## What to go on

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Basis of the dose | Printed per product against **water volume (L) or load weight (kg)**: **"○ mL per 30 L of water", "○ g per 3 kg of laundry"** |
| Concentrated types | **One cap is anywhere from 10 to 25 mL**, varying widely by product. **Never measure with another product's cap** |
| Too much | **Rinse residue (skin irritation, yellowing, smell), food for mould in the drum, foam that lowers cleaning power** |
| Too little | Dirt not removed, **redeposition** (loosened dirt settles back on the clothes) |
| Heavy soiling | Rather than more detergent: **pre-wash, soak or spot-wash** |
| Fabric softener | **Do not add it at the same time as detergent** (use the automatic dispenser). Keep to the stated dose |

- Detergent falls under Japan's **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act**, which makes it compulsory to state **ingredients, pH type (acid/neutral/alkaline), the guide dose and precautions for use** on the pack.
- Washing machines with **automatic dosing** give the wrong amount unless you **set the detergent type (concentrated or standard)**.
- **Overloading the machine** means **the water cannot circulate and the dirt stays**, even with the right amount of detergent. **Seven to eight tenths of the machine's rated capacity** is the guide.

> **[Verify]** Check the compulsory labelling items against the Consumer Affairs Agency's Quality Labelling Regulations for Miscellaneous Manufactured Goods. The dose on the product label always takes priority.

## Misunderstanding 1｜More foam means it is washing better

**Foam is not a measure of cleaning power.** Excess foam **stops the clothes rubbing against each other, lowers cleaning power and hinders rinsing.** In a front-loader the sensor detects the foam and **adds extra rinses, wasting water and time**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜One cap is the same for every detergent

**One capful is anywhere from about 10 to 25 mL depending on the product.** **Look at the lines on that bottle's own cap** and match them to the water volume on the label.

## Misunderstanding 3｜No lingering scent means not enough detergent

**Lingering scent is about fragrance and fabric softener, not detergent.** **More detergent does not make the wash any cleaner** (see our article on fabric softener).

## Misunderstanding 4｜For soaking, the more detergent you dissolve the better

Soaking also works at **the stated concentration**; what does the work is **time (30 minutes to several hours)**. A stronger solution adds nothing and **causes colours to run on patterned items**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Using less detergent is being stingy

**The right dose is the cleanest and the gentlest on skin and machine alike**: that is the makers' own guidance. **Keeping a measuring cup next to the washing machine** is a habit shared among people living in Japan.
