# Q. What do people get wrong about problems with dry cleaners?｜Japan Questions #237

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/dry-cleaning-troubles` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"It came back from the cleaner shrunk, discoloured, or missing a button." The National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan receives several thousand such complaints a year. The same problems exist abroad, but Japan has something unusual: an industry-wide "accident compensation standard" that sets out how a cleaner should pay you.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "hand it to the cleaner and anything will come back as good as new". Cleaners work within the limits of the care label, and even inside those limits the fabric can change because of ageing or the nature of the dye. When something goes wrong, compensation follows the industry's Dry-Cleaning Accident Compensation Standard: it is calculated not on the purchase price but on a depreciated value based on when the item was bought.

## Numbers and how the system works

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Number of complaints | **Dry-cleaning complaints to the National Consumer Affairs Center and local consumer centres run at 4,000–6,000 a year** (2010s onward) |
| Main complaints | **Discolouration, shrinkage, damage, loss, stains not removed, late return** |
| **Dry-Cleaning Accident Compensation Standard** | **A voluntary industry standard** set by Zenkuren, the national federation of dry-cleaning trade associations. Calculated as **purchase price × compensation ratio (depreciated by time since purchase)** |
| Example ratios | **Under 1 year from purchase: about 70–100% of the purchase price**; **at 3 years about 30–50%**; **5 years or more: about 10–20%** (varies with the average service life of the item type) |
| Time limits for a claim | Under the standard, **within 6 months of collecting the item**; the shop cannot be held liable once **1 year has passed since the item was due for collection** |

- Cleaners are registered under the **Dry Cleaning Business Act (クリーニング業法)** and **are obliged to process items according to the care label**. **Anything not covered by the label (wet cleaning, for example) requires the shop's own judgement and an explanation to you.**
- **Checking the item on the spot when you collect it** is the single biggest key to getting a problem resolved. **Take off the plastic cover and look at the colour, shape and trimmings.**

> **[Verify]** Check the complaint numbers and the compensation ratios against the latest material from the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan and Zenkuren.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A dry cleaner can get any stain out

**Old stains and stains that have already discoloured sometimes cannot be removed.** **Tell the counter what the stain is and when it happened**; that speeds up the decision on whether they can deal with it.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Compensation is the full purchase price

It is **depreciated according to when you bought the item**. **A receipt or anything showing the purchase date** makes the calculation clear. **Declare expensive items at the counter and ask whether insurance or a special rate is available.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Open the plastic cover when you get home

**After 6 months a claim becomes difficult**, but in practice **checking on the spot is the surest way**. **The plastic cover traps moisture, so take it off at home as well.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Dry cleaning is safe for anything

Dry cleaning (petroleum or fluorinated solvents) **shrinks less than washing in water**, but it **removes water-soluble dirt such as sweat poorly**, and **solvents can damage glue, prints and some buttons**. The **circle symbol (P/F)** on the label says whether it is allowed.

## Misunderstanding 5｜There is nothing you can do about it

**Raise it with the shop first; if it is not resolved, contact your local consumer affairs centre (hotline 188).** **The industry compensation standard** exists, and most shops handle claims on that basis.
