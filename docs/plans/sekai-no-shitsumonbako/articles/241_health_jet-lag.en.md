# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about jet lag?｜Japan Questions #241

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/jet-lag` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Jet lag is a shared complaint among international travellers, and the time difference between Japan and Europe or North America is 8–14 hours. The sleep guide published by Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) takes the view that **the body clock is moved by light and by meal times**. Here we turn that idea into practical steps for a trip to or from Japan.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "if I sleep on the plane, I will avoid jet lag." Jet lag is the gap between your body clock and local time, and the main tools for closing it are morning light and meal times at your destination. As a rule of thumb, the body clock shifts only about 1–1.5 hours a day, so depending on the size of the time difference, adjusting can take several days.

## The numbers

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Body clock cycle | **Slightly longer than 24 hours** (on average around 24 hours 10 minutes). **Reset every day by morning light** |
| Speed of adjustment | **About 1–1.5 hours a day**. Tokyo to London (8–9 hours' difference) takes **several days to a week** |
| Eastward (advancing the clock) vs westward (delaying it) | **Eastward is harder** (the body clock is better at delaying than advancing) |
| Using light | **Get strong light in the local morning** and **avoid light in the local evening** (sunglasses, dimmer screens) |
| Meals | **Eating at the local breakfast time** moves the peripheral body clocks |
| Naps | **Early afternoon, 30 minutes or less**. Long naps slow the adjustment |

- The MHLW *Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023* [健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023] describes **morning light and regular meal and waking times** as the basis for adjusting the body clock.
- **Melatonin** is **classed as a medicine in Japan** (only a prescription product for children is approved) and **is not sold as a supplement in Japan.** If you bring melatonin bought abroad, personal-import rules apply, such as a limit of **up to two months' supply** (see our article on bringing medicines into Japan).

> **[Verify]** The speed of body-clock adjustment is a general statement from sleep medicine. Check the status of melatonin in Japan against MHLW documents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Sleeping a lot on the plane prevents it

What matters is **matching your sleep to the destination's time**; **simply sleeping longer does not move the body clock.** As a guide: if you land in the morning, sleep on the plane; if you land in the evening, stay awake on the plane.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Recover with a nap on arrival

**A long nap, or a nap in the late afternoon or later, stops you sleeping in the local night and slows the adjustment.** If you really must, **early afternoon, 30 minutes or less**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A drink to fall asleep makes jet lag milder

**Alcohol makes the second half of your sleep shallower and adds to dehydration.** Combined with dry cabin air, it increases fatigue after arrival.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You cannot shift to local time before you leave

**Starting 2–3 days before departure, moving bedtime and waking time one hour a day towards destination time** works with the way the body clock behaves. It is generally described as most useful for **eastward travel**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Young people do not get jet lag

**Adjustment tends to slow with age**, but younger people are affected too. **Do not schedule anything important on the first day of the trip** — a practice shared among Japanese business travellers.
