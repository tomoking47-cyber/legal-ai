# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about alcohol and sleep?｜Japan Questions #242

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/alcohol-and-sleep` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The "nightcap" (寝酒, nezake) survives as a habit in many countries, Japan included. In February 2024 Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) published the country's first drinking guideline, which sits alongside its sleep guide. Here is what the two documents say about a drink before bed.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a drink helps you sleep well." Alcohol makes you fall asleep faster, but it makes the second half of the night shallower and increases waking during the night and in the early morning. The MHLW drinking guideline (2024) states plainly that a nightcap lowers sleep quality, and gives 20 g of pure alcohol a day (for men) as the level at which the risk of lifestyle-related disease rises.

## The numbers

| Item | Details | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Drinking level that raises the risk of lifestyle-related disease | **40 g or more of pure alcohol a day for men, 20 g or more for women** | Health Japan 21 (third term) [健康日本21（第三次）] |
| Approach of the drinking guideline (February 2024) | **Count in grams of pure alcohol**. **A nightcap lowers sleep quality**; **do not use drink to deal with anxiety or insomnia** | MHLW |
| What 20 g of pure alcohol looks like | **500 mL beer (5%)**, **one gō of sake (180 mL, 15%)**, **200 mL wine (12%)**, **60 mL whisky (40%)** | Same |
| Effect on sleep | Falling asleep is faster, but **sleep in the second half is shallower, with more waking in the night and early morning, snoring and breathing pauses (apnoea)** | Sleep Guide 2023 |
| Time to break down | For a person weighing 60 kg, **about 4–5 hours for 20 g of pure alcohol** (varies widely) | e-Health Net |

- **Pure alcohol (g) = volume drunk (mL) × strength (%) ÷ 100 × 0.8**. More Japanese labels now show the figure.
- **With habitual use the sleep-inducing effect fades and the amount rises** (tolerance), so the guideline explains that **drinking as a way of managing insomnia is counterproductive**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A nightcap gives you a good night's sleep

**You fall asleep faster, but sleep quality drops.** Waking in the night, waking early and getting up for the toilet are typical.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A small amount has no effect

**Even a small amount is generally described as affecting the second half of the night**, and the usual guide is to finish drinking **3–4 hours before bed**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Once the alcohol is gone you sleep deeply again

**As alcohol is broken down, the sympathetic nervous system becomes more active and you wake.** Since 20 g takes **4–5 hours**, **a drink just before bed kicks in during the middle of the night**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Beer is lighter than sake

**Compare by grams of pure alcohol.** **500 mL beer ≒ one gō of sake ≒ 20 g**. It is the amount, not the type.

## Misunderstanding 5｜"I can't sleep, so I drink" is unavoidable

The drinking guideline states **not to use alcohol to deal with insomnia**. **If trouble falling asleep continues, see a doctor.** **Measures other than alcohol** (a bath, light, a fixed waking time) are the basics of the sleep guide.
