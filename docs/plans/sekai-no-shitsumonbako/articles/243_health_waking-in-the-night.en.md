# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about waking in the night?｜Japan Questions #243

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/waking-in-the-night` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"I wake in the middle of the night and cannot get back to sleep" is a complaint that grows with age, and one of the most frequent sleep questions on forums in every country. Here we draw on the sleep guide published by Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and on the general view in sleep medicine.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "waking in the night is abnormal." Sleep runs in cycles of about 90 minutes, passing through light sleep each time, and brief waking happens to everyone. It becomes a problem when you cannot get back to sleep, or when your days suffer. The basic measures are: do not look at the clock, do not spend long hours in bed, and avoid alcohol and caffeine before bed.

## What to consider

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Sleep cycle | Deep sleep and light sleep (REM) alternate in cycles of **about 90 minutes**. **Waking is most likely at the join between cycles** |
| Effect of age | **Less deep sleep and more waking** is a natural change. But **how much sleep you need varies from person to person**: the Sleep Guide 2023 notes that **older people may still need as much sleep as other adults**, and uses **time in bed (not more than 8 hours)** rather than sleep length as the guide |
| Main causes of waking in the night | **Alcohol before bed, caffeine, needing to urinate at night, snoring or breathing pauses, bedroom temperature, light and noise, stress, spending too long in bed** |
| If you wake | **Do not look at the clock**; **if you are not asleep within 15–20 minutes, get out of bed** (sit quietly in a dark room and return when sleepy) |
| When to see a doctor | **Three or more nights a week, for three months or more**, with **daytime sleepiness or other problems** (the usual definition of insomnia) |

- The MHLW *Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023* [健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023] recommends **not spending longer in bed than you need**, and that **older people do not spend more than 8 hours in bed**. **The longer you are in bed, the more light sleep and waking you get.**
- **Needing to urinate at night** is one of the main causes of waking, and can improve if you review **fluids, alcohol and caffeine before bed**. **If it happens often, see a urologist** (泌尿器科, hinyōkika).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Normal is eight hours straight through

**Brief waking is within the normal range.** **If you can fall asleep again, there is no problem.** Worrying about the "break" itself makes it harder to sleep.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Go to bed early to sleep longer

**When time in bed exceeds the sleep you need, sleep gets shallower and waking increases.** Go to bed once you are sleepy.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Check the time on your phone when you wake

**Looking at the clock brings the panic of "only X hours left", and the light wakes you further.** Keep clocks out of sight. **The rule is not to spend awake time in bed.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜It's my age; nothing can be done

**Age plays a part**, but **alcohol, caffeine, the bedroom and time in bed** can all be changed. **Snoring, breathing pauses and frequent night-time urination** are matters for a doctor.

## Misunderstanding 5｜An over-the-counter sleep aid solves it

Over-the-counter sleep aids in Japan (睡眠改善薬, suimin kaizen-yaku, sold at drugstores and based on antihistamines) are **for temporary insomnia** and **not recommended for continued use.** **Insomnia lasting three months or more is a matter for a doctor.**
