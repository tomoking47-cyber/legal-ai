# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about "tech neck" (straight neck)?｜Japan Questions #244

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/tech-neck` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Neck strain from looking down at a smartphone is called "tech neck" around the world. In Japan it spread under the names "sumaho-kubi" (スマホ首, smartphone neck) and "straight neck" (ストレートネック), and you will hear both from colleagues and at the pharmacy. Here we explain what the terms mean and what you can actually do about it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "straight neck is a diagnosis and needs medical care." "Straight neck" is an informal term for a neck that has lost some of its natural curve; it is not an official diagnosis. In most cases it is stiffness in the neck and shoulders from posture and habit, and the basic measures are raising the screen, taking breaks and drawing the neck back. Pain or numbness that persists is a matter for an orthopaedic clinic (整形外科, seikei-geka; covered by Japanese health insurance).

## The numbers

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Weight of the head | **About 4–6 kg** (roughly 8–10% of body weight) |
| Estimated load on the neck (from a research model) | About 5 kg upright; **about 12 kg at 15° forward tilt, 18 kg at 30°, 22 kg at 45°, 27 kg at 60°** (Hansraj, 2014) |
| Average smartphone use | **About 3–4 hours a day** (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications survey on media use; over 5 hours for people in their teens and 20s) |
| Guideline for work with display screens | **No more than 1 hour of continuous work, with a 10–15 minute break in between**; **top of the screen below eye level**; **viewing distance 40 cm or more** |

- **"Straight neck" is not a formal diagnosis**; it is an informal term for a reduced forward curve (lordosis) of the cervical spine. **Many people show it on imaging yet have no symptoms**, so it is not in itself a "disease".
- **Neck stiffness, headaches and tight shoulders** are generally attributed to muscle fatigue from **long periods of leaning forward**. **Numbness, weakness or pain spreading into the arm** may point to a **disorder of the cervical spine** and needs checking at an orthopaedic clinic.

> **[Verify]** The load figures are calculated values from Hansraj KK (Surgical Technology International, 2014), not measurements. Check usage time against the latest Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications survey.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Straight neck is dangerous if not corrected

**A reduced curve with no symptoms is not something that needs medical care.** What matters is **the symptoms (stiffness, pain, headache)**, and most of these ease with **posture and breaks**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Neck-rolling stretches solve it

**Rolling the neck suddenly or with momentum** puts load on the cervical spine. Movements that **rest the muscles at the back of the neck** — **tucking the chin slightly, drawing the shoulder blades together, opening the chest** — are generally recommended instead.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Hold the phone at eye level and the problem is solved

For long periods **your arms tire and the phone drops anyway.** What works in practice is **elbows against your body, screen between chest and chin height**, and **at a desk, a stand or riser to lift the screen**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Change the pillow and the problem is solved

**The pillow affects the angle of the neck while you sleep**, but **the hours spent leaning forward during the day** are generally described as having the larger effect. Review both (see our article on pillows).

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is a young person's problem

**People in their teens and 20s use phones the most**, but **in middle age and beyond it overlaps with age-related changes in the cervical spine**, so symptoms are described as appearing more readily. **Numbness or arm pain** is a reason to see a doctor at any age.
