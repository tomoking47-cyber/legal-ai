# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about foot baths and half-body baths?｜Japan Questions #245

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/foot-baths-and-half-baths` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan, where soaking up to the shoulders is the norm, the foot bath (足湯, ashiyu) and the half-body bath (半身浴, hanshin-yoku — sitting in water up to the solar plexus) spread as gentler ways of warming up. The "Japanese foot bath" is known abroad too, but care is needed in how its benefits are described. Here we separate what public sources support from what is shared as everyday wisdom.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a half-body bath detoxes you or burns fat." The solid advantages of half-body and foot baths are that water pressure puts less strain on the heart, so older or less robust people warm up more easily. Weight lost through sweating is water, and dehydration is a risk. The guide is around 40°C (104°F) for 15–20 minutes, and remember to drink water.

## What to consider

| Method | What it is | Advantages | Cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full bath** | Up to the shoulders. The Consumer Affairs Agency's guide is **41°C (106°F) or below, about 10 minutes** | Warms you quickly | **Hot water and long soaks are the main cause of bathing accidents** |
| **Half-body bath** | Up to the solar plexus. **38–40°C (100–104°F), 15–20 minutes** | **Less water pressure on the heart**. Warms you gradually | **The upper body gets cold**, so put a towel over your shoulders. Dehydration if too long |
| **Foot bath** | Up to the calves. **40–42°C (104–108°F), 10–15 minutes** | Can be done fully clothed. **An alternative on days you are not well enough for a bath** | Some people feel heat poorly in the feet (**people with diabetes should take care with temperature**) |

- Deaths during bathing are estimated at **about 19,000 a year** (Consumer Affairs Agency, based on research by the Tokyo Metropolitan Institute for Geriatrics and Gerontology), mostly **older people in hot water for long periods**. **Half-body and foot baths are described as one option for lowering that risk.**
- **"Detox" and "fat burning"** are not medical terms; **what sweat carries out is water and salt**. **Weight lost in the bath is dehydration**, and it returns when you drink.
- Under the Hot Springs Act, **"therapeutic effects of hot springs"** may be displayed only at hot-spring facilities; **no health claims may be made for bathing or foot baths at home.**

> **[Verify]** Check the bathing-death estimate against Consumer Affairs Agency publications, and the temperature and time guides against the Consumer Affairs Agency and the Japanese Society of Balneology, Climatology and Physical Medicine.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The more you sweat in a half-body bath, the more weight you lose

**The weight lost is water.** The risks of **dehydration and blood-pressure swings** are larger, so the guide is **20 minutes or less, drinking water as you go**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The longer you soak, the warmer you get

**If body temperature rises too far it leads to dizziness, light-headedness and dehydration.** **Get out when sweat beads on your forehead** — the rule of thumb shared among people living in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The hotter the foot bath, the better it works

**The skin of the feet gets used to heat, and burns can go unnoticed.** **Check with your hand: 40–42°C.** **If diabetes or another condition has dulled the feeling in your feet, use a thermometer.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜A half-body bath is safe for older people for hours

**The load from water pressure is lower**, but **temperature and time act in the same way.** **41°C or below, 20 minutes or less, and tell a family member before you get in.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜A bath additive adds health effects

**No health claims may be made for bathing at home** (see our article on bath additives). **Quasi-drug bath additives** (医薬部外品, a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines) are limited to their approved claims and **do not add effects to bathing itself.**
