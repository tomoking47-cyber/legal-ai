# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about protein intake?｜Japan Questions #246

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/protein-intake` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The market for protein products has grown worldwide, and in Japan too "protein deficiency" is widely talked about. Here we set the national reference values against what people in Japan actually eat, and look at where the real problems lie — above all among older people.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "Japanese people are short of protein, so they need protein supplements." The National Health and Nutrition Survey shows that average adult intake is above the recommended amount. A shortfall becomes a problem for older people whose appetite is shrinking and for people on extreme diets, and the answer is one main dish (主菜, shusai — meat, fish, egg or soy) at every meal.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Japanese people lack protein

**The adult average is above the recommended amount.** Those most likely to fall short are **older people with small appetites, people who skip breakfast, and people on extreme diets**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Without protein powder you cannot get enough

**One serving of meat, fish, egg, soy or dairy at every meal** gets most people to the recommended amount. Powder is **a supplement for when food alone is difficult**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more you eat, the more muscle you build

Anything above **about 1.2–2.0 g per kg of body weight** (depending on how active you are) is generally described as being used for energy rather than built into muscle. **People with reduced kidney function may need to limit protein**; ask a doctor.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Older people should cut down on meat

**The opposite.** A protein shortfall in later life is generally linked to **loss of muscle mass (sarcopenia and frailty)**, and the reference intakes set a higher target for older people at **15–20% of total energy**. **Foods that are easy to chew (minced meat, fish, egg, tofu)** are the practical route.

## Misunderstanding 5｜One big protein meal is enough

**Spreading protein across three meals** is generally described as more efficient than loading it into one. **Breakfast is where protein is most often missed** (see our article on breakfast).
