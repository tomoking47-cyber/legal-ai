# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about snacking?｜Japan Questions #247

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/snacking` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Whether snacking is a bad habit divides opinion in every country. In Japan the government's Food Balance Guide gives an actual figure — sweets and sweetened drinks up to about 200 kcal a day — and sets out how to think about amount and timing. Here is that framework, with Japanese snack examples you will find in any convenience store.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "all snacking is bad." The Food Balance Guide places sweets and sweetened drinks at about 200 kcal a day, to be "enjoyed in moderation." The issues are type, amount and timing: an amount that does not spoil the next meal, eaten three hours or more before it.

## The numbers

| Item | Details | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Guide for sweets and sweetened drinks | **About 200 kcal a day** | Food Balance Guide (MHLW and Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries) |
| What 200 kcal looks like | **About 35 g of chocolate (roughly two-thirds of a 50 g bar)**, **one dorayaki (red-bean pancake)**, **about 35 g of crisps (a little over half a 60 g bag)**, **about three cans of sweetened coffee**, **3 senbei (rice crackers)** | Standard Tables of Food Composition in Japan |
| People who snack | **About 70% of adults** (men about 60%, women about 80%); **about 20% snack every day** (private survey) | MyVoice, "Survey on snacking" [間食に関するアンケート調査] |
| Sugar guide | WHO: **free sugars below 10% of total energy (ideally below 5%)** | WHO |

| Smarter choices | Examples |
|---|---|
| Foods with **protein or fibre** | Nuts (unsalted, about 25 g a day), yoghurt, cheese, boiled egg, fruit |
| **Timing** | Around 3 pm. **Three hours or more before dinner**. Avoid late at night |
| **Decide the amount** | **Put it on a plate**, not straight from the bag. Keep small packs, not large ones |
| **Drinks** | Sweetened drinks are "invisible snacks". Switch to water or tea |

> **[Verify]** Check the 200 kcal figure against the wording of the Food Balance Guide. The sugar figure is from the WHO guideline (2015).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Stop snacking and you lose weight

**Stronger hunger at the next meal can lead to overeating.** **A light snack that keeps meal sizes steady** makes the total easier to control — the experience shared among people who have tried it.

## Misunderstanding 2｜"Calorie off" or "low sugar" means you need not worry

**"Off" (オフ) is a comparative claim, not zero.** Read the **energy and carbohydrate figures** on the label. **Drinks with artificial sweeteners** also call for care, as they are generally described as keeping you accustomed to sweetness.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Fruit is unlimited

Fruit **contains sugars, and the Food Balance Guide's figure is about 200 g a day**. If you eat it as a snack, stay **within that amount**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Nuts are healthy, so eat plenty

**Nuts are high in fat: 25 g is about 150–180 kcal.** **Unsalted, in small packs**, and decide the amount in advance.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A late snack is fine as long as it is not right before bed

**Eating in the 2–3 hours before bed is generally described as lowering sleep quality** (see our article on eating before bed). **Save the evening snack for the next morning** — a practice many people here follow.
