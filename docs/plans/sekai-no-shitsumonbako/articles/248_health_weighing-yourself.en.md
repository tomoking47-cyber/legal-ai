# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about weighing yourself?｜Japan Questions #248

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/weighing-yourself` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The bathroom scale is the most familiar health device we own, yet "when should I weigh myself", "should I do it every day" and "can I trust the body-fat reading" are asked in every country. Body-composition scales (体組成計, taisosei-kei) are common in Japanese homes, so here is how to read them sensibly.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "your weight is the same whenever in the day you measure it." Weight swings 1–2 kg over a day, and home body-fat estimates shift by several percentage points with your hydration. Weigh yourself under the same conditions every day (morning, after using the toilet, before breakfast, same clothing) and watch the weekly trend, not the daily number.

## What to consider

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Daily variation | **About 1–2 kg** (food, fluids, toilet, sweat) |
| Conditions for weighing | **Morning, after getting up, after using the toilet, before breakfast**, same clothing (underwear), **same scale on the same hard, flat floor** |
| Frequency | **Every day** is generally described as the best way to notice change in weight management. **Ignore daily ups and downs; look at the weekly average** |
| Home body-fat % (bioimpedance method) | **Shifts by several % with body water**. Avoid after a bath, exercise or a meal. **Only compare readings taken under the same conditions** |
| BMI | **Weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²**. Japanese cut-offs: **under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to under 25 normal, 25 and over obese** |
| Target | Health Japan 21 (third term) targets **the share of people with a BMI of 18.5–24.9**. **Underweight (especially young women and older people) is also a concern** |

- Home body-composition scales are covered by **JIS standards and the Measurement Act as weighing scales**; body fat and other readings are **estimates** and the devices are not medical devices.
- The **National Health and Nutrition Survey** shows **about 20% of women in their 20s are underweight (BMI under 18.5)**, a figure the government lists as a national concern. **Undernutrition among older people** is another.

> **[Verify]** Check the BMI categories against the Japan Society for the Study of Obesity, and the underweight share against the latest National Health and Nutrition Survey.

## Misunderstanding 1｜I am 1 kg up on yesterday, so I have gained fat

**That is within a day's normal range.** **Salty meals, fluids and bowel movements** move it. **Judge by the weekly average.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Body-fat % is an exact figure

Home readings are **estimates**. **They shift by several % with hydration**, so **only look at the trend under identical conditions**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Daily weighing makes you obsess, so once a month is enough

**Research on weight management generally finds that weighing daily or several times a week helps people notice change.** The premise is a way of looking that **does not react to every number (the weekly average)**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A low BMI means you are healthy

**Underweight (BMI under 18.5) is linked to lower bone mass, menstrual irregularities and undernutrition**, and is flagged as a concern in national health targets. **"The thinner the better" is not the official position.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜The scale works anywhere you put it

**On carpet or tatami mats it cannot measure accurately.** Use a **hard, flat floor**. **Low batteries** also skew the reading.
