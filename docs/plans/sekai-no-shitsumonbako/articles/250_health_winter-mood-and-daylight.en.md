# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about daylight hours and mood?｜Japan Questions #250

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/winter-mood-and-daylight` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Low mood in winter, struggling to get up, craving sweet things — the "winter depression" known in Scandinavia is talked about in Japan too, in the regions with the shortest daylight. If you have moved here from a sunnier country, the dark winters of the Sea of Japan coast can come as a surprise. Here we separate what belongs to medicine from what you can do with light in daily life.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "winter low mood is all in your head, and you just have to put up with it." When daylight hours shorten, the body clock is more easily disrupted, and Japan's official sleep guide recommends deliberately making time for morning light. However, low mood, loss of interest or disruption to daily life lasting two weeks or more is a matter for a doctor.

## The numbers

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Regional differences in sunshine (December normals) | **Tokyo about 175 hours** / **Sapporo about 90 hours** / **Niigata about 60 hours** / **Kōchi about 200 hours** (Japan Meteorological Agency) |
| Seasonal mood change | Depressive symptoms that **start in autumn or winter and lift in spring** are known in medicine as "seasonal affective disorder" (winter depression). **More common at high latitudes and where sunshine is scarce** |
| Light levels | Outdoor morning light is **several thousand to over 10,000 lux, even under cloud**. Indoor lighting is around **300–500 lux** |
| Sleep Guide 2023 | **Getting morning light after waking** settles the body clock and **helps you feel sleepy at night** |
| When to see a doctor | **Two weeks or more** of near-daily **low mood or loss of interest and pleasure**, **changes in sleep or appetite**, **disruption to daily life** |

- Winter sunshine on the Sea of Japan side (Niigata, Akita, Toyama and others) is **about a third of Tokyo's**, and **winter mood and sleep problems** are a local talking point.
- **Bright light therapy** is carried out in medical settings; **the "light alarm clocks" sold in shops are not medical devices** (the light intensity and method of use differ).

> **[Verify]** Check the sunshine normals with the Japan Meteorological Agency's "Historical weather data search", and the description of seasonal affective disorder against MHLW e-Health Net and the Japanese Society of Mood Disorders.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Winter low mood is a matter of willpower

**Changes in daylight, temperature and activity affect the body clock and mood** — this is established in medicine. **Tackling it as an environmental problem** is the practical approach.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Indoor lighting is enough

**Indoor lighting is a few tens of times weaker than outdoor light.** **Eating breakfast by the window after getting up, or walking outside for 15–30 minutes**, is the method shared among people living in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 3｜If you live in a low-sunshine region, nothing can be done

By **deliberately getting morning light, going out during the day, keeping up exercise and fixing a waking time**, many people get through the winter.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Craving sweets means a lack of grit

**Increased craving for carbohydrates is a recognised feature of winter depression.** **Regular meals** (do not skip breakfast) flatten the swings.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It lifts in spring, so there is no need to see anyone

If you have **low mood lasting two weeks or more, disruption to daily life, or thoughts of wanting to die**, do not wait for the season: go to a **doctor or a helpline** (Yorisoi Hotline 0120-279-338; Kokoro no Kenkō Sōdan unified mental-health line 0570-064-556). **This article is no substitute for a diagnosis.**
