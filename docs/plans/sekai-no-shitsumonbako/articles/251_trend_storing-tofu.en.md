# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing tofu?｜Japan Questions #251

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-tofu` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Tofu is an everyday food far beyond Japan now, but it still prompts two questions: how long does it keep once opened, and why does it sit in water? Japanese supermarkets sell several kinds of tofu with different date labels, which is where the confusion starts. Here is how the types and the labelling system work.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "tofu is a soy product, so it keeps well." Tofu sitting in water is one of the most perishable foods you can buy; once opened, use it the same day or the next. Keep it in the fridge with fresh water, and check the label to see whether it can be eaten without heating. Filled tofu (充填豆腐, jūten tōfu) keeps comparatively long while sealed.

## Types and storage

| Type | What it is | Date while sealed | Once opened |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Momen (firm) and kinugoshi (silken) tofu in a water pack** | Sits in water | **Use-by date (消費期限, shōhi kigen), a few days** (3–7 days from manufacture) | **Same day to the next day**. **Change the water in a clean container and refrigerate** |
| **Filled tofu (充填豆腐, jūten tōfu)** | Soy milk and coagulant are sealed in the container, then heated. No water | **Best-before date (賞味期限, shōmi kigen), a few weeks to 2 months** (varies by product) | Same day to the next day once opened |
| Grilled tofu (yaki-dōfu), thick fried tofu (atsuage), thin fried tofu (aburaage) | Processed | Follow the label (a few days) | Use soon. Aburaage can be frozen |
| Kōya-dōfu (freeze-dried tofu) | Dry good | **Several months to 1 year** | Keep away from moisture |

- Tofu labelling is governed by the **Food Labelling Act** and shows **a use-by or best-before date, the storage method and the ingredients (origin of the soybeans and whether they are genetically modified)**.
- Tofu is **heated during manufacture**, so **within the date, if the label says "そのままお召し上がりいただけます" (can be eaten as is), it can be eaten without cooking**. **If it has been open for some time, or the water has turned slimy, heat it** or throw it away.
- **Freezing drives out the water and leaves a spongy texture** (using it this way as "kōri-dōfu" in simmered dishes is a piece of everyday Japanese know-how).

> **[Verify]** The date guidelines are general figures based on the labelling of the Japan Tofu Association and major makers. The label takes priority.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Tofu keeps for days as long as it sits in water

**Changing the water every day does not stop the tofu itself deteriorating.** Once opened, **use by the next day**. **A sour smell, sliminess or cloudy water** are signs to throw it away.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It is better to drain the pack water and store the tofu dry

**The basic method is to keep it in clean water in the fridge**, which stops it drying out and protects the surface. **Do not reuse the water from the pack; use fresh water.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Filled tofu is lower quality because it is "full of additives"

Filled tofu is simply **made differently**: **it is heated after sealing, which is why it keeps longer**. The ingredients are basically **soybeans, water and a coagulant**, much the same as other tofu.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Past the date is fine if you cook it

**A use-by date (消費期限) is the limit for eating the food safely**, and food past it is **not recommended even if heated.** A best-before date (賞味期限, used on filled tofu) is a quality guideline; **if unopened and stored as instructed**, it is often still fine a little past the date, but check its condition.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Frozen tofu is ruined

**The texture changes, but there is a method: drain, freeze, thaw and use in simmered or stir-fried dishes.** It takes on a **meat-like texture**, a money-saving and food-waste-reducing idea shared among people living in Japan.
