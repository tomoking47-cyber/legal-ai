# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing yoghurt and using it after opening?｜Japan Questions #252

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-yogurt` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Yoghurt is the classic example of a food people assume "is fermented, so the date does not matter." Japanese supermarkets sell it mostly in large 400 g tubs, which raises the question of how long it lasts once opened. Here is how to handle an opened tub, what the liquid on top is, and how to judge a passed date, based on how Japanese labelling works.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "yoghurt is fermented, so it cannot go off." However many lactic acid bacteria it contains, once opened it picks up bacteria and mould from the air; a large tub should be finished within 2–3 days. The clear liquid on the surface (whey) is a normal component with nutrients in it, and there is no need to pour it away.

## What to judge by

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Storage | **Refrigerate (10°C / 50°F or below)**. Avoid the door pocket, where the temperature rises easily |
| Best-before date while sealed | Usually **2–3 weeks** from manufacture. **If unopened and refrigerated, it is often still fine a few days past the date** (check its condition) |
| Once opened (large 400 g tubs etc.) | **2–3 days**. **Serve with a clean spoon; do not eat straight from the tub** |
| Liquid on the surface | **Whey (乳清)**. Contains protein and calcium. **Stir it back in** |
| Signs to throw it away | **Too sour, bitter, pink/black/green spots (mould), a bulging lid, a smell of alcohol** |
| Freezing | Possible, but **the texture changes** (it separates). Eating it as frozen yoghurt is one option |

- Most yoghurt is labelled **"fermented milk" (発酵乳, hakkō-nyū)**, defined in Japan's Ministerial Ordinance on Milk and Milk Products as **non-fat milk solids of 8.0% or more and 10 million or more lactic acid bacteria per 1 mL**.
- Claims such as **"reaches the gut alive"** are allowed only on products approved or notified as **Foods with Function Claims or Foods for Specified Health Uses (FOSHU, トクホ)**, two Japanese regulated categories; **ordinary yoghurt cannot carry them** (Health Promotion Act; Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations).
- **A best-before date (賞味期限, shōmi kigen) is the limit for eating the food at its best**; it differs from a use-by date (消費期限) (see the article on date labelling).

> **[Verify]** The standard for fermented milk is in the Ministerial Ordinance on Milk and Milk Products Concerning Compositional Standards; check the after-opening guideline against makers' labels and Q&A pages.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The liquid on top means it has gone off

**It is whey.** **Pouring it away loses nutrients.** Stir it in or eat it as it is. **Cloudy liquid with a strong sour smell** is a different matter.

## Misunderstanding 2｜One day past the best-before date, throw it away

**If unopened and refrigerated, it is often still fine a few days past the date.** Judge by **appearance, smell and taste**. **Once opened, 2–3 days regardless of the date.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Eat from the tub with a spoon and put the rest back in the fridge

**Bacteria from a spoon that has touched your mouth get in and speed up spoilage.** **Serve a portion first.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜It is fine to say that eating yoghurt sorts out your gut

**Specific effects may only be stated on products that have been approved or notified.** Within the law, ordinary yoghurt is positioned as "one of the fermented foods".

## Misunderstanding 5｜All yoghurts contain the same bacteria

**The strains differ from product to product** (Lactobacillus bulgaricus, Streptococcus thermophilus, bifidobacteria, Lactobacillus gasseri, etc.). **Which suits you varies from person to person**; the practical approach is to choose **one you can keep eating**.
