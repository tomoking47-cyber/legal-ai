# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about choosing cooking oil?｜Japan Questions #253

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/choosing-cooking-oil` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Which oil is good for you" is one of the most argued-over food topics in the world. Japan adds a name of its own, "salad oil" (サラダ油, sarada-yu), and the shelves hold plenty of others: olive, sesame, rice bran. Here we set out only what the public standards and labelling rules actually say.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "there is a clear line between good oils and bad oils." Japan's Dietary Reference Intakes give a target for total fat (20–30% of energy) and an upper limit for saturated fat (7% or less); the basic question is not "which oil" but "how much, and for what." The practical answer is to separate oils that suit heating from oils that suit raw use.

## The numbers and how to use each oil

| Item | Details | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Target for fat | **20–30% of total energy** | Dietary Reference Intakes for Japanese (2025 edition) |
| Saturated fatty acids | **7% or less of total energy** (age 18 and over) | Same |
| n-3 fatty acids (alpha-linolenic acid, EPA, DHA) | Adequate intake **about 2.0–2.2 g for men, about 1.6–2.0 g for women** (varies with age) | Same |
| 1 tablespoon of oil (12 g) | **About 110 kcal** | Standard Tables of Food Composition |

| Oil | Main fatty acids | Suits | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Salad oil (a blend of rapeseed, soybean, etc.)** | Mono- and polyunsaturated | Stir-frying, deep-frying, raw | Under JAS (Japanese Agricultural Standards), "salad oil" is a grade name for **oil refined enough to stay clear at low temperatures** |
| **Olive oil** | Oleic acid (monounsaturated) | Raw, stir-frying | **"Extra virgin" is an international standard; Japan's JAS categories are "olive oil" and "refined olive oil"** |
| **Sesame oil** | Linoleic and oleic acid | Flavouring, stir-frying | The aroma is lost with heat |
| **Rice bran oil** | Oleic and linoleic acid | Deep-frying, stir-frying | Generally described as resistant to oxidation |
| **Perilla (egoma) oil and flaxseed oil** | **Alpha-linolenic acid (n-3)** | **Raw only** | **Do not heat; once opened, keep refrigerated for 1–2 months** |
| **Coconut oil** | **About 90% saturated fat** | — | Watch the **upper limit for saturated fat** |

> **[Verify]** The intake figures are from the 2025 edition of the Dietary Reference Intakes; check the JAS categories against the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries' "Japanese Agricultural Standard for Edible Vegetable Oils".

## Misunderstanding 1｜"Salad oil" is an oil for raw vegetables

Under JAS, **"salad oil" is a grade name for oil refined so that it does not cloud at low temperatures**; it does not limit the use. **It can be used for stir-frying and deep-frying too.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Olive oil is all-purpose and ideal for heating

Extra virgin **loses its flavour compounds with heat, and its smoke point is somewhat lower than refined oil**. It suits **raw use and finishing**; the usual practice is **refined oil for high-temperature deep-frying**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Perilla and flaxseed oil are healthy for stir-frying

**Alpha-linolenic acid oxidises easily with heat and light and is not suited to heating.** **Pour it on raw**, **refrigerate after opening**, and **use it up within 1–2 months**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Coconut oil is a healthy oil

It is **about 90% saturated fat**, the oil with the biggest effect on the upper limit in the Dietary Reference Intakes (7% or less). **We could find no public basis for calling it "good for you".**

## Misunderstanding 5｜The less oil, the better

**Fat is an essential nutrient and is needed to absorb fat-soluble vitamins.** The target is **20–30% of total energy**, and **extreme restriction is not recommended.**
