# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about oil splatter and fire when deep-frying?｜Japan Questions #257

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/deep-frying-safety` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Deep-fried food is a staple of Japanese home cooking. It is also why "the hob" (こんろ, konro) is always near the top of the causes of house fires in Japan, most of them "tempura oil fires". The same accidents happen everywhere, and misunderstandings about how to put the fire out make the damage worse.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "if the oil catches fire, throw water on it." Water turns to steam instantly at the oil's temperature and flings burning oil across the kitchen, spreading the fire. The basic sequence is: turn off the heat, cover with a lid or a large wet towel, then use a fire extinguisher rated for oil fires. Prevention means not letting the oil get too hot: frying happens at around 180°C, and oil ignites by itself at about 370°C.

- **Si-sensor hobs hold the oil temperature in deep-fry mode and cut the flame if it overheats.** **Older hobs and portable cassette-gas stoves do not have this function.**
- **Oil splatter** is caused mainly by **water in the food**. **Pat food dry, knock the frost off frozen food, and do not overload the pan relative to the amount of oil.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Put an oil fire out with water

**Never use water.** The oil scatters and the flames reach the ceiling. **Turn off the heat and cut off the air with a lid or a large wet towel (wrung out)**, then use a **fire extinguisher (household type, rated for oil fires)**. **Tipping in mayonnaise or vegetables is not reliable, and the fire service does not recommend it.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It is fine to step away for a moment

**Most hob fires happen because the cook left the kitchen.** The phone, a visitor, the laundry: **do not leave while deep-frying**. If you must go, **turn off the heat**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜If it starts smoking, turn up the heat and fry faster

**Smoke is the sign that the oil has passed its smoke point** and **ignition is close**. **Turn off the heat at once and let the oil cool.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Less oil is safer

**A small amount of oil heats up faster, so it reaches ignition sooner.** The safe side is **no more than half the depth of the pan, but enough oil**, with **a thermometer or the Si sensor's deep-fry mode**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Fire extinguishers are for businesses, not homes

**A household fire extinguisher rated for oil fires, or an aerosol-type extinguisher**, costs a few thousand yen, and the fire service's advice is **one in the kitchen**. Check the **expiry date (usually 5–10 years)**.
