# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about using a toaster oven?｜Japan Questions #258

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/toaster-oven-safety` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The toaster oven (オーブントースター) is one of the most common cooking appliances in Japanese kitchens, used not only for bread but for gratins, mochi rice cakes and frozen foods. If you have moved to Japan you may well have one in your flat. "Can I put foil in it?" and "what actually catches fire?" are frequent questions, and NITE (Japan's National Institute of Technology and Evaluation) reports accidents every year.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a toaster is a weak heat source, so anything is safe inside it." The heating elements run at several hundred degrees, and fat from food or crumbs touching them will ignite. The basics: empty the crumb tray regularly, catch fatty foods on foil or a dish, watch for spitting from bread spread with butter or margarine, and do not leave it unattended while it is on.

## What to judge by

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Element temperature | **Quartz-tube and halogen elements have a surface temperature of several hundred degrees**. **Food, paper or foil coated in fat that touches them can ignite** |
| **Crumbs** | Crumbs and fat collected in the tray are **the most common cause of fire** (NITE). **Clean it once a week** |
| **Aluminium foil** | **Allowed on most models**, but **it must not touch the elements or cover the whole tray** (heat builds up). **Check the manual** |
| Mochi (rice cakes) | **They puff up and touch the element**, then ignite. **Low position, centre of the rack, do not look away** |
| Paper and baking parchment | **Burns above its heat rating (usually 250°C)**. **Some products are marked "not for toaster ovens"** |
| Fatty foods (bacon, karaage fried chicken) | **Fat drips onto the element**. **Catch it on a dish or foil** |
| Power | **1,000–1,400 W**. **Avoid extension leads and multi-way adaptors** |

- NITE receives **continual reports of fire and smoke from toaster ovens**; the main causes are **"crumb build-up", "mochi or other food touching the element" and "leaving it unattended while on"**.
- Toaster ovens fall under the **Electrical Appliance and Material Safety Act (the PSE mark)**, and **the prohibited items in the manual** (what must not go inside) differ from model to model.

> **[Verify]** Check the accident-cause classification against NITE's product accident information. For foil and parchment, follow the manual for your own model.

## Misunderstanding 1｜You must never put aluminium foil in a toaster oven

**Most models allow it.** What is prohibited is **placing it so that it touches the element** and **covering the whole tray**. People **confuse this with microwaves**; a toaster oven uses electric heating elements, so metal itself is not a problem. **Check the manual.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Crumbs only scorch; they are not dangerous

**Fat-soaked crumbs carbonise and ignite**; this is the typical accident. **Pull out the tray and empty it, and wipe it once a week.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Mochi is safe on the rack

**It puffs up, touches the upper element** and burns. **Put it on foil, in the centre of the rack, and keep watching**; **once it puffs up, open the door and check.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Once the timer is set you can walk away

**There are cases of fire breaking out while the timer was running.** **Stay within sight for a few minutes of cooking.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜An old toaster oven is fine until it stops working

**A worn element, a cracked door glass or a damaged cord** can lead to accidents. **Odd noises, odd smells or a door that will not shut** are signs to replace it. Also check **recall notices** (the Consumer Affairs Agency's recall information site).
