# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about safe cooking at a campsite?｜Japan Questions #260

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/camp-cooking-safety` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japan's camping boom has made outdoor cooking an everyday pastime, and two kinds of accident have risen with it: food poisoning, and carbon monoxide poisoning inside tents. The points below follow the warnings issued by Japan's public bodies. Here is what to be extra careful about precisely because you are outdoors.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "cooking over a fire outdoors is safe." Outdoors you cannot refrigerate, hand-washing is difficult and heating is often uneven, so the risk of food poisoning is higher than at home. On top of that, using a stove, charcoal or heater inside a tent or a car causes carbon monoxide poisoning, and deaths occur every year.

## Two kinds of accident

### Food poisoning

| Situation | Points | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Carrying food | **Cooler box with ice packs or ice**. **Meat and fish at the bottom, separated from other food**. Keep it at **10°C or below** | MHLW "Six points" |
| Hand-washing | If **running water and soap** are not available, **wet wipes plus alcohol**. **Always after touching raw meat** | Same |
| Cooking | **Centre at 75°C for at least 1 minute**. **A charred surface on the barbecue and a raw centre are two different things**. **Campylobacter from chicken** is the typical case | Same |
| Chopsticks and tongs | **Separate ones for raw meat and for eating** | Same |
| Leftovers | **The rule is to throw them away, not take them home**. **Under a blazing sun, 2 hours is enough to make them dangerous** | Same |

### Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning

| Situation | Points |
|---|---|
| **Stoves, charcoal, wood-burning stoves or cassette-gas heaters inside a tent or car** | **Prohibited** (stated on the product label too). **CO is colourless and odourless**, so you do not notice |
| Ventilation | "It is fine if you ventilate" is **wrong**. **Do not use outdoor combustion appliances inside a tent** |
| Accidents | **Deaths from carbon monoxide poisoning inside tents every year** (reported by the Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE) |
| Disposing of charcoal | **Put it out completely in a charcoal extinguishing pot (火消し壺, hikeshi-tsubo) or with water**. **Do not bury it in the ground** (it smoulders for hours and causes burns) |

> **[Verify]** Check the food-poisoning temperatures against the MHLW, the CO poisoning cases against Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE warnings, and charcoal disposal against individual campsites and the Ministry of the Environment.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If the outside of the barbecue meat is cooked, so is the inside

**Over charcoal, the surface chars first while the centre stays raw.** **Cut thick pieces to check**, and **cook chicken until the centre is white**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Food in a cooler box is safe all day

**Opening and closing, and the outside air, push the temperature up.** **Use plenty of ice or ice packs, keep the box in the shade, and open it less often.** **Use the meat and fish first.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜A stove inside the tent is fine because there are gaps for air

**Gaps do not ventilate enough to stop CO, and because it is colourless and odourless you get drowsy without noticing.** **Combustion appliances stay outside the tent.** More people now carry a **CO alarm**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Take the leftover food home and eat it tomorrow

**Food that has sat for hours under a blazing sun is dangerous even if it looks unchanged.** The rule is **do not take it home, even if it was cooked**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Buried charcoal goes out

**Buried charcoal smoulders for hours, and there are accidents where someone steps on it the next day and is burned.** **Seal it in a charcoal extinguishing pot**, or **douse it with water until it is completely out and take it to the designated disposal point**.
