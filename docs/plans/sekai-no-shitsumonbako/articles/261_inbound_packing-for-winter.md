# Q. What do people get wrong about packing for a Japanese winter?｜Japan Questions #261

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/packing-for-winter` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan's winter confuses packers because it is several climates at once: Hokkaido at −10°C, Tokyo dry and sunny at 5–10°C, Okinawa at 18°C — and indoors, trains heated to 25°C while old houses sit at 12°C.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japan is uniformly cold and wet in winter. The Pacific side (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka) is dry and sunny with cold nights; the Sea of Japan side and Hokkaido have heavy snow. What every visitor needs is layers that come off on heated trains, a coat that folds, footwear for icy pavements in the north, and moisturiser for the dry air.

## The climates (January averages)

| City | Daily high / low | Precipitation | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sapporo** | **−1°C / −7°C** | Snow, typically **50–100 cm on the ground** (deepest in February) | Ice on pavements; **spiked boots or grip attachments** |
| **Tokyo** | **10°C / 1°C** | **Very dry**, 5–8 sunny days out of 10 | Humidity often 30–40% in the afternoon (monthly average about 50%); static, chapped lips |
| Kyoto | 9°C / 1°C | Occasional snow | Colder than Tokyo indoors; temples are unheated |
| Kanazawa / Niigata | 6°C / 1°C | **Heavy snow or rain most days** | Waterproof shoes essential |
| Fukuoka | 10°C / 4°C | Cloudy, some snow | — |
| Naha (Okinawa) | 19°C / 15°C | — | Light jacket only |

- Indoors: **trains and shops 22–26°C**, **traditional inns and old homes 10–15°C** (see the kotatsu article). The coat goes on and off ten times a day.
- Tokyo's January **relative humidity averages about 50% outdoors and 20–30% indoors** with heating — bring lip balm and moisturiser, or buy them at any drugstore.
- Snow-country footwear: locals use **winter-soled shoes or clip-on spikes**, sold at convenience stores in Hokkaido for **¥1,000–2,000**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Pack for one cold country

Check your route: **Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka in January is dry, bright and cold at night** — a good coat and layers suffice. **Add Hokkaido or the Alps** and you need snow-rated footwear and gloves.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Bring a heavy parka and thick sweaters

You will be **hot on every train and in every shop**. Locals wear a **thin heat-retaining base layer, a light mid layer and a coat** that they carry indoors (see the layering article). Two thin layers beat one thick one.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Snow means slush; trainers will do

Hokkaido pavements are **polished ice**; visitors fall in numbers each winter (Sapporo's fire department takes well over 1,000 people a season to hospital after falls on snowy roads — a record 1,795 in FY2023). **Non-slip soles or clip-on spikes**, and walk with short steps.

## Misunderstanding 4｜It rains, bring a big umbrella

On the Pacific side, **December and January are the driest months**; an umbrella is rarely needed. On the Sea of Japan side, **snow and wind make umbrellas useless** — a waterproof hooded coat works better.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You can buy warm clothes cheaply anyway

You can — Japanese fast-fashion chains sell excellent heat-retaining underwear for **¥1,000–2,000** — but **sizes run small** (see the sizing article). Buy base layers here; bring your coat and shoes.
