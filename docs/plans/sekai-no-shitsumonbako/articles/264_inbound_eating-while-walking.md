# Q. What do people get wrong about eating while walking in Japan?｜Japan Questions #264

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/eating-while-walking` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The idea that "eating while walking is rude in Japan" circulates as an absolute. It is closer to a local ordinance in a few streets and a general preference elsewhere — and it collides with the fact that Japan sells more street food than ever.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that eating while walking is forbidden everywhere. It is discouraged in crowded shopping streets, and a few tourist districts (Kamakura's Komachi-dōri since 2019, parts of Kyoto's Nishiki Market) have local ordinances or posted requests against it — mainly because of litter and stains on kimono. The workable rule: buy, step aside, eat where you bought it, dispose of the wrapper there.

## The rules, such as they are

| Setting | Norm |
|---|---|
| **Shopping streets and markets** | Eat **standing next to the stall** that sold it; the stall takes the wrapper. Many now post "no walking while eating" signs |
| **Kamakura, Komachi-dōri** | **Municipal ordinance (April 2019)** asks visitors to refrain from eating while walking — a request, not a fine |
| **Kyoto, Nishiki Market** | Shops ask customers to eat on the spot; "no eating while walking" signage since 2019 |
| **Festivals (matsuri)** | Eating while wandering among food stalls is **normal** |
| **Trains** | Local trains: no. **Shinkansen and long-distance limited expresses: yes**, ekiben is the point |
| **Streets in general** | Ice cream or a drink is fine; a full meal draws looks |

- The practical reasons: **few public bins** (see the rubbish bins article), **crowding**, and complaints from **kimono-rental visitors and shop owners about stains**.
- **Convenience stores** increasingly provide **an eat-in counter** for exactly this; using it also changes the tax rate to 10% (see the consumption tax article).

> **[Verify]** The Kamakura ordinance date and wording with Kamakura City; Nishiki Market's request with the market association.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is a national law

It is **a local ordinance in a handful of places and a norm elsewhere**. Nobody is fined; you may be asked to stop.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Street food is therefore rare

Japan has **more street food than ever** — markets, festivals, station snacks. The convention is to **eat it where you buy it**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Eating on any train is fine

**Commuter trains, no** (smell and crumbs in a packed car). **Shinkansen and reserved-seat expresses, yes** — station bento (ekiben) exist for that.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You can just find a bench

Benches are scarce in shopping districts. **Parks, station waiting areas, department-store rest corners and convenience-store eat-in counters** are the places.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The rule is about manners only

It is largely about **rubbish**. If you eat somewhere, **take the wrapper back to the seller or carry it** — bins are rare, and litter is what turned a norm into ordinances.
