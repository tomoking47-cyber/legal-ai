# Q. What do people get wrong about convenience-store food and onigiri?｜Japan Questions #265

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/convenience-store-food` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese convenience-store food has a global reputation, mostly deserved. What visitors miss is how the system works — daily deliveries, strict date labels, the heating counter, the onigiri wrapper — and what is actually the best value.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that convenience-store food is a fallback. The food is delivered up to three times a day, date-labelled to the hour, and engineered to be eaten within it; staff will heat anything; and the onigiri wrapper is a small piece of design that keeps seaweed crisp until you pull the tabs. The value is in the fresh chilled shelves, not the snacks.

## How it works

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Stores | **about 56,000** nationwide; the big three (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson) hold ~90% |
| Deliveries | **2–3 times a day** for chilled food; onigiri and bento carry a **consumption-by date and time** (消費期限) |
| Onigiri | **¥130–250**; the **three-part wrapper** keeps the nori separate — pull tab 1 down the middle, then pull 2 and 3 sideways |
| Heating | **"Atatamemasu ka?" (shall I heat it?)** — free, at the till; bento come out in about 1–2 minutes |
| Hot counter | Fried chicken, croquettes, oden (winter) at the till |
| Eat-in | Many stores have a counter; **eating in changes the tax to 10%** |
| Discounts | Some chains sticker **near-date items 20–30% off** in the evening |

- Common onigiri fillings printed in kanji: **鮭 (salmon), ツナマヨ (tuna mayo), 梅 (pickled plum), 昆布 (kelp), おかか (bonito), 明太子 (spicy cod roe), 高菜 (mustard greens), 赤飯 (red-bean rice)**.
- **Chilled noodles, salads and sandwiches** are made in dedicated factories with a **~24–48-hour shelf life** — this is why they taste fresh.
- **Food waste** at convenience stores is a recognised problem; the chains have extended some shelf lives and introduced evening markdowns since 2019.

> **[Verify]** Store counts with the Japan Franchise Association; prices are 2025 ranges.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is junk food

The shelves are dominated by **rice, fish, vegetables, tofu, salads and soups**. The snack aisle exists; it is not the point.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The date label is a suggestion

Onigiri and bento carry a **consumption-by (消費期限) date and time**. Eat by it. Staff **pull items from the shelf** at the time printed.

## Misunderstanding 3｜You have to eat it cold

**Ask for heating** — any bento, pasta or curry. Staff heat it in a commercial microwave calibrated for the packaging. Onigiri can be heated too (some types are labelled for it).

## Misunderstanding 4｜The onigiri wrapper is a puzzle

Numbers **1, 2, 3** are printed on it. **Pull 1 straight down** to split the film, **then pull 2 and 3 outward**. The nori is then between rice and your hand — crisp.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Prices are the same as a supermarket

**Roughly 20–40% higher** for equivalent items (see the supermarket article). You pay for hours and location; for a single lunch it does not matter.
