# Q. What do people get wrong about sales seasons and fukubukuro in Japan?｜Japan Questions #270

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/sales-seasons-and-lucky-bags` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan has two big sale seasons and one ritual — the New Year "lucky bag" — that looks like a gimmick and is actually a serious retail event with queues from before dawn. Knowing the calendar changes what a shopping trip costs.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that discounts in Japan are rare and small. Two clearance seasons — early January and early July — cut fashion prices by 30–70%, and fukubukuro (sealed lucky bags sold on 1–3 January) typically contain goods worth two to three times the price. Outside those windows, "sale" signs are modest; tax-free is the year-round mechanism.

## The calendar

| When | What | Typical discount |
|---|---|---|
| **1–3 January** | **Fukubukuro** (lucky bags) at department stores, fashion brands, electronics, coffee chains; **New Year sales** begin | Bag contents worth **2–3× price**; sales **30–50%** |
| **Mid-January to February** | Winter clearance | **50–70%** on coats and knitwear |
| **Late June to July** | **Summer sale** (bargain sale) | **30–50%**, deepening in late July |
| August | Late summer clearance | 50–70% |
| **Late November** | "Black Friday" adopted by some chains since ~2016 | 10–30%; less significant than in the US |
| Mid-March / mid-September | Season transitions; quiet markdowns | 20–30% |

- **Fukubukuro** date to Edo-period kimono shops; modern versions range from **¥1,000 coffee-chain bags to ¥100,000+ department-store bags**, and some contain **experiences** (a flight, a car lease). Many stores now **show the contents** or offer "mieru fukubukuro" (visible bags); others keep the mystery.
- Queues for popular bags form **before dawn on 1 January**; some brands run **online lotteries in December** instead.
- **Tax-free** (see that article) applies on top of sale prices where the ¥5,000 threshold is met.

> **[Verify]** Sale timing varies by retailer; fukubukuro practices with department-store announcements each December.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Japan does not really do sales

The **January and July clearances are deep**; the country simply does not run continuous "sale" pricing. Time a fashion-shopping trip to **early January or early July**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Fukubukuro are full of unsellable stock

Some are. The reputable ones — department stores, major brands, coffee chains — are **loss leaders** meant to build goodwill, with **contents worth several times the price**. Check whether the store shows the contents; **returns are not accepted**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜You can pick up a good bag mid-morning on 2 January

**Popular bags sell out within the first hour of 1 January.** For a specific brand, **check for an online lottery or pre-order** in December.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Black Friday is the big one

It exists at some chains but is **minor**; the **New Year** is the retail peak. Do not plan around November.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Sale goods are not eligible for tax-free

**They are**, as long as the same-day, same-shop total meets **¥5,000** before tax. Sale plus tax-free is the best price you will get in Japan.
