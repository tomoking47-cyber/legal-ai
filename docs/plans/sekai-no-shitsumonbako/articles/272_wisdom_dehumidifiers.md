# Q. What do people get wrong about dehumidifiers in Japanese homes?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #272

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/dehumidifiers` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A dehumidifier is standard household equipment in Japan in a way it is not in most of Europe or North America. The reasons are the rainy season, indoor laundry drying and mould — and the machines are built for those jobs specifically.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a dehumidifier is a luxury for damp basements. In Japan it is used to dry laundry indoors in a few hours, to keep a closet or bedroom below the mould threshold in June and September, and to reduce condensation in winter. Choosing the type (compressor, desiccant or hybrid) matters because their strengths differ by season.

## The types

| Type | How | Best in | Weakness | Power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Compressor** | Cools air to condense water (like a fridge) | **Summer, rainy season** (warm, humid) | Weak below about 15°C; heavier; louder | Low: **150–300 W** |
| **Desiccant (zeolite)** | Absorbs moisture into a wheel, heats to release it | **Winter** (works in the cold); quiet; light | **Raises room temperature 3–8°C**; uses more power | High: **300–700 W** |
| **Hybrid** | Both, switching by temperature | Year-round | Price and size | Varies |
| Air-conditioner "dry" mode | Cools and condenses | Summer | Cools the room; cannot be aimed at laundry | — |

- Capacity is quoted in **litres per day** (e.g. **6–12 L/day** for a room; **16–20 L/day** for laundry rooms). Japanese models add a **"laundry mode"** with a swinging louvre that directs dry air at hanging clothes; a load dries in **2–4 hours**.
- Target: **relative humidity 40–60%**. **Mould accelerates above 70%**, and the space inside closets and behind furniture runs higher than the room.
- Running cost (Tokyo 2025, about ¥31/kWh): compressor **¥5–9 per hour**, desiccant **¥10–20 per hour**.

> **[Verify]** Power and capacity figures are typical manufacturer ranges; electricity rates change.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Air conditioning does the same job

An air conditioner on "dry" **cools the room** and cannot be aimed at laundry; in a cold rainy June evening, cooling further is unpleasant. A dehumidifier **removes water without cooling** (desiccant types add warmth, which helps laundry).

## Misunderstanding 2｜Bigger capacity is always better

Capacity is measured at **27°C and 60% humidity**; a compressor model rated 10 L/day may produce **a fraction of that at 12°C**. Match the **type to the season** you need it most.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Run it with the windows open

It then dehumidifies **the outdoors**. **Close the room**, and for laundry, **hang clothes with space between them** directly in the airflow.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Empty the tank when it is full

The machine **stops automatically** when the tank fills — often at 2 a.m. in the rainy season. **Continuous-drain models** connect a hose to a bathroom drain and never stop.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Winter does not need one

**Condensation on windows and in north-facing closets** is a winter problem, and compressor units are weak in the cold — this is why **desiccant models dominate winter sales** in Japan.
