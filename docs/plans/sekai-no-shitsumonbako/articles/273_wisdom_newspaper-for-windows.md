# Q. What do people get wrong about cleaning windows with newspaper?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #273

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/newspaper-for-windows` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Cleaning glass with crumpled newspaper is a technique Japanese households have used for generations and one that survived into the microfibre era. It is often dismissed as folklore. It works for a specific reason, and it has a specific limit.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that newspaper cleans glass because of some magic in the ink. Newsprint works because it is a dense, lint-free, slightly absorbent paper that dries the glass without leaving fibres, and the small amount of oil in the ink gives a faint polish. It does not clean heavy grime; it finishes glass that has already been wiped with water. Microfibre does the same job with less mess, but the newspaper method costs nothing and uses waste.

## How the method works

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Wipe | Wet cloth (water, or water with a drop of detergent) to lift dust and grime; wring hard |
| 2. Dry-polish | **Crumpled newspaper**, dry or barely damp, in circles then straight strokes |
| 3. Check | Angle of light shows streaks; re-polish with a fresh sheet |
| When | **Cloudy day or shade** — direct sun dries cleaner before you wipe, causing streaks |
| Frequency | Inside monthly; outside at the **year-end clean** and before pollen season |

- Newsprint is **unbleached, low-lint paper with a matte surface**; the oil-based ink component leaves a very thin film that **repels light dust for a few days**. Colour pages and glossy inserts do not work as well.
- **Modern soy-based inks** transfer less to hands; older inks could smudge white frames — wipe frames separately with a cloth.
- **Squeegee** is the professional tool; the **microfibre cloth (dry) is the modern household equivalent**; newspaper is the zero-cost version and reuses waste paper that goes to recycling anyway.

> **[Verify]** The mechanism is a widely repeated explanation in household-cleaning literature; newspaper ink composition with the Japan Newspaper Publishers & Editors Association.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Newspaper replaces a cleaner

It **finishes** glass. Grime, fingerprints and kitchen grease need **water and a little detergent first**; newspaper alone smears them.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wet the newspaper thoroughly

**Barely damp or dry.** Soaked newsprint disintegrates and leaves pulp. Use it as the drying and polishing stage.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any paper works

**Kitchen paper and tissues shed lint**; glossy magazines smear. Newsprint's density is the point.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The ink will stain the glass

**It does not** — the trace film is what gives the polish and is invisible. It can mark **white PVC frames and sills**; wipe those with a cloth.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Sunny days are best for windows

Sun **dries the water before you can wipe it**, leaving streaks and mineral marks. **Cloudy, dry days** — Japan's December — are ideal, which is why windows are part of the year-end clean.
