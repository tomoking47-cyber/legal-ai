# Q. What do people get wrong about the Japanese shoe cabinet (getabako)?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #275

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/shoe-cabinets` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Because shoes come off at the door, every Japanese home has a shoe cabinet in the entrance — and every home has the problem of what happens inside it in a humid climate. The solutions are a small system of their own.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a shoe cabinet is just a cupboard by the door. In Japan it is where shoes rest between wearings, in a space that is often the most humid in the home. The habits that keep it working: shoes go in only after a night airing, one pair per person stays out, desiccant and charcoal live inside, and the doors are opened regularly.

## The system

| Practice | Detail | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Air before storing** | Shoes worn today stay **out in the genkan overnight**; they go in the cabinet tomorrow | Feet release about **200 mL of sweat a day**; a damp shoe in a closed cabinet grows mould and odour |
| **One pair out per person** | The genkan floor shows one pair each, toes toward the door | Tidiness rule taught to children; also a fire-escape habit |
| **Desiccant and charcoal** | Silica-gel or calcium-chloride packs; **bamboo charcoal** for odour | Cabinet humidity often exceeds 70% in June and September |
| **Ventilate** | Doors open on dry days; some cabinets have louvred doors or a vent | Air movement |
| **Rotate** | Do not wear the same shoes two days running | Drying time |
| **Season out** | Off-season shoes cleaned and boxed with desiccant, top shelves | Space |

- New-build homes commonly have a **floor-to-ceiling shoe cabinet or walk-in "shoes cloak"** with a **coat hook and umbrella slot** — an evolution of the genkan (see that article).
- The **getabako** (literally "geta box") name survives from wooden-sandal days; **school shoe cabinets** with a slot per pupil are where most Japanese first learn the routine.
- **Mould conditions** — above 70% humidity, 20–30°C — are met inside a closed cabinet holding damp shoes for much of the year.

> **[Verify]** Sweat figures are standard physiology references; mould thresholds with the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's mould guidance.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Put shoes away as soon as you take them off

**Let them dry overnight first.** The cabinet is for rested shoes, not for shoes that have just done a day's walking.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A shoe cabinet keeps the entrance tidy by hiding everything

Hiding damp shoes in a closed box **creates the smell** the cabinet was meant to prevent. Tidy is one pair per person out, the rest **dry and inside**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Deodoriser solves odour

Odour is bacteria in **moisture**. **Dry the shoes** (see the shoe storage article) and put **desiccant** in the cabinet; charcoal or deodoriser is the finishing touch.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cabinets need no maintenance

**Wipe the shelves seasonally**, replace desiccant every few months (many packs change colour when saturated), and **check for mould on the back wall** — the coldest, dampest surface.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is only for shoes

Modern designs hold **umbrellas, the shoehorn, slippers for guests, the emergency bag (see that article) and keys** — the genkan is the household's transit hub, and the cabinet is its storage.
