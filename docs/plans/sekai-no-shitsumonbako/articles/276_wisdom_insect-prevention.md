# Q. What do people get wrong about keeping insects out of a Japanese home?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #276

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/insect-prevention` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese summers bring mosquitoes, cockroaches, and the small moths that eat wool. The household response is layered — screens, coils, traps, repellents — and much of it has a regulatory basis visitors do not see: many of these products are classified as quasi-drugs.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese homes are pest-free because they are clean. They are pest-managed: every window has a screen, mosquito coils and plug-in repellents run from June to September, cockroach bait stations sit under the sink, and food waste is sealed and frozen or taken out on collection day. Insecticides for hygiene pests are regulated as quasi-drugs, and "do not mix, ventilate, keep from children" is printed on all of them.

## The layered approach

| Pest | Prevention | Control | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mosquitoes** (June–October) | **Screens (amido) on every window**, no standing water on balconies, fans (mosquitoes are weak fliers) | **Mosquito coils (katori senkō)**, **plug-in liquid or mat vaporisers**, body repellent (DEET or icaridin) | Repellents are **quasi-drugs**; concentration limits apply (DEET up to 30% since 2016) |
| **Cockroaches** | Seal food, **wipe grease**, empty drain baskets, **freeze meat scraps until collection day** | **Bait stations (poison gel/tablets)** under sink and behind fridge; **sticky traps** | Baits are quasi-drugs; place out of children's reach |
| **Clothes moths and carpet beetles** | Wash before storing; **repellent on top of stored clothes** | See the moth-repellent article | Do not mix repellent types |
| **Fruit flies / drain flies** | Rinse recyclables, dry the sink at night, cover fruit | Drain cleaning; traps | Summer only |
| **Ants** | Wipe sugar and juice; seal entry gaps | Bait | — |
| **Centipedes, giant hornets (rural)** | Screens, seal gaps, do not disturb nests | Call the municipality or a specialist for hornet nests | Hornet stings can be dangerous |

- Insecticides and repellents for **hygiene pests** (mosquitoes, flies, cockroaches, fleas) are **quasi-drugs under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act**; products for other insects (ants, centipedes) are regulated as household goods. The label states the classification.
- **Katori senkō** (the pyrethrum spiral coil) was invented in Japan in **1890 (stick) and 1895 (spiral)**; the modern versions use synthetic pyrethroids, and the ceramic pig holder (katori-buta) is a summer icon.
- **Fumigation cans** (smoke or mist type) for whole-room treatment require **leaving the room for 2–3 hours, covering fish tanks and food, and ventilating** — and warning neighbours in a building, since smoke can trigger alarms.

> **[Verify]** Classification and DEET limits with MHLW; coil history with the manufacturers' association; municipal hornet-nest services vary.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Clean homes have no cockroaches

Cockroaches follow **moisture and grease**, and come in through **drains and gaps** regardless. **Bait stations placed preventively in May** are what Japanese households do; they do not wait to see one.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Mosquito coils are dangerous smoke

They burn a **low dose of pyrethroid** and have been used for over a century; the label says **ventilate and keep from infants and pets**. **Plug-in liquid vaporisers** are the smokeless alternative most flats use.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Screens are optional

**Every window has one**, and it stays closed even when the window is open. Balcony doors get a **magnetic screen curtain**. This is the single most effective measure.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Kitchen waste can wait for collection day

In summer it **breeds flies within a day**. **Rinse containers, wrap meat and fish scraps and freeze them** until the collection morning — a widely shared habit.

## Misunderstanding 5｜All bug sprays are the same

A **mosquito spray, a cockroach spray and a wasp spray have different ingredients, ranges and hazards**; using an aerosol near a **gas flame or pilot light** has caused fires. **Read the label**; do not spray toward food or a stove.
