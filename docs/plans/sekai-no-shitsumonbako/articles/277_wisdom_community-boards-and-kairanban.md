# Q. What do people get wrong about neighbourhood associations and the kairanban?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #277

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/community-boards-and-kairanban` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

New residents in Japan are handed a clipboard of notices to read and pass to the next house, and are asked to join a neighbourhood association and pay a small fee. It looks like bureaucracy or an intrusion. It is the layer of local life that runs the rubbish site, the festival and the disaster response.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that neighbourhood associations are compulsory or meaningless. They are voluntary (membership has fallen to about 70% nationally), fee-based (typically ¥3,000–6,000 a year), and they manage the things a municipality does not: the rubbish collection point, the festival, street lights in some areas, and — most importantly — the evacuation roll call after an earthquake. The kairanban is their newsletter, passed hand to hand.

## How it works

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Chōnaikai / jichikai** (neighbourhood association) | Voluntary body of **50–500 households**; elected officers rotate; **about 70% of households belong** (71.7% in 2020, down from 78.0% in 2010; a 1970 survey put participation around 90%) |
| **Fees** | **¥3,000–6,000 a year** typically, collected by a block leader (hanchō) |
| **Kairanban** | A clipboard of notices — collection schedule changes, festival dates, fire drills, crime alerts — **read, stamp or sign, pass to the next house within a day** |
| **Duties** | Rotating **rubbish-site cleaning**, crossing-guard shifts near schools, **festival preparation**, **disaster drill participation** |
| **Disaster role** | The association **keeps the roster used to check who is missing** after an earthquake; runs the local **evacuation site** with the municipality |
| Alternatives | Many associations now use **LINE groups or apps** alongside the clipboard |

- Municipalities delegate real functions to associations: **placement and cleaning of the rubbish collection point**, **installation of street lights and mirrors**, **distribution of city newsletters**. Non-members often may still use the collection point, but disputes arise.
- After the **1995 Kobe earthquake, about 8 in 10 people rescued from collapsed houses were pulled out by family or neighbours**, not by emergency services — the statistic that anchors the associations' disaster role.
- Membership decline is a recognised policy issue; the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications has published guidance on **making participation easier for renters, single people and foreign residents**.

> **[Verify]** Membership rates and the Kobe rescue figure with the Cabinet Office disaster-prevention white papers and MIC surveys on community associations.

## Misunderstanding 1｜You must join

**Voluntary.** But the rubbish site, the disaster roll call and the goodwill of neighbours run through it. Renters in large apartment buildings are often covered by the **building's own management association** instead.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The kairanban is junk mail

It carries **the collection-schedule change, the water-outage notice and the fire-drill date**. Read it the day it arrives and pass it on; holding it for a week is the classic newcomer's offence.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The fee is a tax

It funds **festival costs, street-light electricity in some areas, condolence gifts, disaster supplies, and the association's insurance**. Accounts are usually reported at the annual meeting.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Foreign residents are not welcome

Many associations **actively want them** — membership is falling and they need hands. Language is the barrier; municipalities increasingly publish **multilingual guides to association life**. Turning up to the rubbish-site cleaning once buys a great deal of goodwill.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is only about the festival

The **disaster function** is the reason policymakers care: in a major earthquake the association is who knows that the elderly person in the corner house is alone, and who opens the evacuation site.
