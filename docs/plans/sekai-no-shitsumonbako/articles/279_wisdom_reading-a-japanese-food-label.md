# Q. What do people get wrong about reading a Japanese food label?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #279

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/reading-a-japanese-food-label` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese packaged food carries one of the world's more detailed labels: two kinds of date, a full ingredient list in descending order, allergens in brackets, and a nutrition panel. Residents read it in a glance; newcomers see a wall of kanji. Six fields cover almost everything.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the label is unreadable without Japanese. Six fixed items appear on every packaged food under the Food Labelling Act, always in the same frame: name, ingredients, contents, date (with a marker saying which kind), storage method, and manufacturer. Learn the six headings and the two date words, and the rest is detail.

## The six fields (一括表示, the boxed panel)

| Heading | Reads | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **名称** | meishō | Product type (e.g. 清涼飲料水 soft drink; 菓子 confectionery) |
| **原材料名** | genzairyō-mei | **Ingredients in descending order by weight**; additives after a slash "／"; **allergens in brackets** e.g. （小麦・乳成分を含む） |
| **内容量** | naiyōryō | Net weight or volume |
| **賞味期限 / 消費期限** | shōmi / shōhi kigen | **Best-before** (quality) / **Use-by** (safety) — see below |
| **保存方法** | hozon hōhō | Storage: 常温 room temp, 要冷蔵 refrigerate (10°C以下), 冷凍 frozen (−18°C以下) |
| **製造者 / 販売者** | seizōsha / hanbaisha | Manufacturer or seller, with address |

### The two dates

| Word | Meaning | Applies to | After the date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **賞味期限** | **Best before** — the maker's quality date | Foods keeping more than ~5 days: snacks, canned, dry, frozen | Usually still edible; judge by condition |
| **消費期限** | **Use by** — safety limit | Perishables: bento, sandwiches, fresh cakes, raw meat | **Do not eat** |

- Dates read **year.month.day** (e.g. 2026.09.08 or 26.9.8); long-life items may show **year and month only**.
- **Nutrition panel (栄養成分表示)**: energy (エネルギー kcal), protein (たんぱく質), fat (脂質), carbohydrate (炭水化物), **salt equivalent (食塩相当量)** — mandatory since April 2020; per serving or per 100 g as stated.
- **Origin**: since 2022, the **country of origin of the main ingredient** is required on all processed foods (原料原産地).

> **[Verify]** Field requirements with the Consumer Affairs Agency's Food Labelling Standards; nutrition and origin labelling dates with CAA notices.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The date is a use-by date

Most packaged foods carry **賞味期限 (best before)** — a quality date. **消費期限 (use by)** is the safety one, and it is only on perishables. Learn to tell **味 (taste)** from **費 (consume)**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Ingredients are in random order

**Descending by weight**, like most countries. Additives come **after the slash (／)**. The first three ingredients tell you what the food mostly is.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Allergens are somewhere else

They are **in brackets inside the ingredient list** or summarised at its end: **一部に〇〇を含む** ("contains, in part, …"). Eight are mandatory (see the allergen article).

## Misunderstanding 4｜"Salt" will be listed as sodium

Japanese panels show **食塩相当量 (salt equivalent) in grams** — directly comparable to the 7 g daily target — not sodium in milligrams.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A camera translator is enough

It helps with ingredients. For **dates and storage**, learn the two kanji pairs and 要冷蔵 (refrigerate) — they are the fields where a mistake matters.
