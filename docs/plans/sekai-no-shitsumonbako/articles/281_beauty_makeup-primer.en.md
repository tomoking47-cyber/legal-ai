# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about make-up primer?｜Japan Questions #281

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/makeup-primer` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Do I actually need a primer?" is a frequent question in overseas communities. In Japan, primers (化粧下地, keshō shitaji) have become multi-purpose — sunscreen, colour correction and pore cover in one tube — so the question here is more often "which one do I pick?". This article sorts out what a primer does and how Japanese law classifies it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "primer is a ritual before foundation, and you can skip it." A primer's practical jobs are to help foundation adhere and last, to even out uneven colour and texture, and, in many products, to block UV. If you rely on it for sun protection, quantity is the key point: you need more primer than you would use of foundation.

## What a primer does

| Role | What it does | Points to note |
|---|---|---|
| **Adhesion and staying power** | Balances sebum and dryness to give foundation an even base | Choose by skin type (a sebum-absorbing type for oily areas, a moisturising type for dry areas) |
| **Colour correction** | Redness → green, dullness → purple/pink, yellowish dullness → blue tones | The idea is to cancel with a "complementary colour". Too much changes your whole complexion |
| **Texture cover** | Silicone-based products that fill pores and fine lines (for spot use) | Thick layers are a cause of make-up breaking down |
| **UV protection** | Many primers carry an SPF/PA rating | **The effect depends on the amount applied** (**about 0.8 g for the whole face** is the measurement standard). **If you use it in place of sunscreen, use the full amount** |

- Primer is a **cosmetic** under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law), so the effects it may claim are limited to a list of 56 items ("conditions the skin", "prevents sunburn", etc.). **"Makes pores disappear" is not permitted.**
- The SPF/PA rating of a **sunscreen primer** is based on the **measurement standards of the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association (JCIA)**. **The SPF of primer plus foundation does not add up** (see the article on SPF in foundation).
- Japanese primers are finely divided into **"tone-up", "anti-sebum" and "glow" types**, and **using different types by zone (a sebum-control type on the T-zone only)** is common practice among people living in Japan.

> **[Verify]** Check the 56 items against MHLW notice Yakushoku-hatsu 0721 No. 1, and the SPF measurement standard against the JCIA's published materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If you wear sunscreen you don't need primer, or if you wear primer you don't need sunscreen

**Both can be true — if you use enough.** If you use sunscreen as your primer, apply **about 0.8 g** over the whole face. **If you rely on a single primer, you need the same amount.** **The usual way of spreading a small amount of primer thinly will not give you the SPF on the label.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Primer goes evenly over the whole face

**Dividing the face by zone** is how people in Japan use it. **A sebum-control type on the T-zone, a moisturising type on the cheeks, colour correction under the eyes**, and so on. **The total amount is roughly the size of one pearl** as a general guide.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Colour-correcting primer makes skin whiter

It works by **cancelling with a complementary colour**; **too much and your face looks chalky or dull.** Use **a small amount** on the areas that bother you only.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Put foundation on straight after primer

Leaving **1–2 minutes for the primer to settle** is generally described as making foundation less likely to slide. **Let a sunscreen primer dry first.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Primer is a burden on the skin, so give your skin a rest

Primer is a cosmetic, and **the idea of "resting" the skin has no medical definition.** If you get skin problems, review the **ingredients and the amount**, and check **how you remove it** (see the article on cleansing).
