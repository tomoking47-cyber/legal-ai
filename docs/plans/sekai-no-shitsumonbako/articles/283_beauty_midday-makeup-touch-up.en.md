# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about touching up make-up during the day?｜Japan Questions #283

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/midday-makeup-touch-up` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan, touching up make-up in the lunch break is a widespread habit, and stations and offices have well-equipped powder rooms. In many other countries "the same make-up all day" is the norm, and visitors ask "why touch up at all?" and "doesn't it end up caked on?".

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "touching up means adding another layer on top." Layering alone mixes with sebum, goes patchy and builds up thick. The basic order is: "press away sebum and sweat with a tissue or sponge → a little moisturising mist or milky lotion on dry areas → add a thin layer only where it has broken down." It takes about 5 minutes.

## The steps

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Press away sebum and sweat with a tissue or blotting paper (あぶらとり紙, aburatori-gami)** — do not rub | Layering over sebum gives patchiness and creasing |
| 2 | **Where make-up has creased, smooth it lightly with a cotton bud or sponge carrying a little milky lotion (乳液, nyūeki)** | The lotion dissolves the old foundation and blends it in |
| 3 | **A moisturising mist on dry areas, or a thin film of milky lotion with a fingertip** | Against afternoon dryness |
| 4 | **A thin layer of powder foundation or face powder only where it has broken down** | Do not layer over the whole face |
| 5 | **Add colour back to eyes and lips** | The areas that fade fastest |
| Time | **About 5 minutes** | Lunch break |

- Midday touch-ups took root in Japan because of an environment that breaks make-up down: **hot, humid summers** and **mask-wearing**.
- **Hygiene of the sponges and puffs used for touch-ups** (see the article on make-up brushes): **left damp inside a pouch** is the condition bacteria need to multiply. Many people carry **disposable sponges and individually wrapped cotton buds**.
- If you want the touch-up to double as **re-applying SPF**, one method is to use **a face powder or spray with SPF** over your make-up (see the article on SPF in foundation).

> **[Verify]** The steps are based on the general guidance of cosmetics makers and the beauty industry.

## Misunderstanding 1｜When it breaks down, put more foundation on top

**Layering over sebum gives patchiness and a caked look.** First **remove the sebum**, then **smooth the creased areas**, and finally **add only in patches**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Blotting paper should take off every trace of oil

**Removing too much causes dryness, and the sebum comes back in the afternoon.** **Press the T-zone lightly** and leave it there.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Touching up takes a long time

**It is a 5-minute routine.** The point is **not to redo the whole face, only the parts that have broken down**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜For a mist, just decant your toner into a spray bottle

**Ordinary toner moved into a spray bottle grows bacteria easily, and the spray droplets are coarse,** so over make-up it causes breakdown. Use **a mist labelled as usable over make-up**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Not touching up is better for the skin

Rather than whether you touch up or not, what matters for the skin is **not leaving sebum sitting on it for hours**. **Just pressing it away** is a form of care in itself.
