# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about looking after a hairbrush?｜Japan Questions #285

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/hairbrush-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The hairbrush is the tool we use every day and almost never wash. "How often should I wash it?" and "how do I wash each material?" are standing questions in overseas communities too. In Japan there is a long tradition of caring for boar-bristle brushes, and this article draws on it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "once the loose hair is out, the brush is clean." A brush collects sebum, styling products and dust, and all of it goes back into your hair. As a guide: remove loose hair every time, wash once a week to once a month (depending on the material), and dry completely before use. Keep water to a minimum with natural-bristle brushes.

## Care by material

| Material | Removing loose hair | Washing | Drying | Points to note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plastic (cushion brush, vented "skeleton" brush)** | Every time | **Once a week**, in water with a little neutral detergent or shampoo | Bristles facing down | Water that gets into the holes of the cushion dries slowly |
| **Natural bristle (boar)** | Every time, **with a brush cleaner or comb** | **About once a month**: **dip only the bristle tips in diluted shampoo and shake.** **Do not wet the base** | Bristles down, **air-dry completely out of direct sun** | Water and heat make bristles fall out and crack the wooden base |
| **Wooden pins (paddle brush)** | Every time | Wipe with a well-wrung cloth. **Avoid washing in water** | — | Wood swells and cracks |
| **Round brush (metal barrel)** | Every time | With neutral detergent | Dry completely | Metal rusts |
| Comb | Every time | Once a week with detergent | — | — |

- The dirt on a brush is mainly **sebum, styling products, dead skin and dust**, and **left damp it becomes a cause of mould**. **Dry the brush after combing wet hair.**
- **Natural-bristle brushes are generally described as producing less static and spreading sebum evenly along the hair.** In Japan they are a traditional tool alongside the **boxwood comb (つげ櫛, tsuge-gushi — a wooden comb cared for with camellia oil)**.
- A brush is **for one person only**. **Scalp conditions (head lice, ringworm)** can spread through sharing. **Head lice occur among children in Japan every year**, and guidance from the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and others is to **avoid sharing brushes, hats and towels**.

> **[Verify]** The care methods are general methods based on brush makers' guidance. Check the head-lice advice against the materials of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Bureau of Public Health.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Remove the loose hair and it is clean

**Sebum and styling products build up at the base of the bristles as a white, powdery deposit.** Regular **washing** is needed.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Soak a natural-bristle brush whole in water

**The wooden base cracks and the bristles fall out.** **Dip only the bristle tips in the washing liquid and keep the base dry.** Use a comb-shaped brush cleaner for everyday dirt.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A hairdryer is the quick way to dry it

**Heat frizzes natural bristles and warps plastic.** **Air-dry with the bristles facing down.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜One brush is fine for the whole family

It passes on **sebum, dandruff, head lice and ringworm (a relative of the athlete's-foot fungus)**. **One brush per person** — **the same goes for hats and towels**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A brush lasts until it breaks

**A brush whose rounded pin tips have come off scratches the scalp.** Replace it when **the bristles splay, the tips come off or the base cracks**. **1–2 years for plastic, and up to 10 years for natural bristle depending on care**, are the figures usually quoted.
