# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing perfume and fragrance?｜Japan Questions #287

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/storing-fragrance` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"How many years does perfume keep?" and "should I keep it in the fridge?" come up again and again on overseas forums. Japan's hot, humid summers and strong sunlight make the question more pressing here. This article sets out how to store a fragrance so that it keeps its scent.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "perfume is alcohol, so it never goes off and stays the same for years." Fragrance oils oxidise with light, heat and air; the colour darkens and the scent changes. Once opened, 1–3 years is the guide, and the basic rule is "away from direct sunlight, in a dark place with little temperature change, cap firmly closed." The fridge is not recommended, because of condensation from temperature swings and scent transferring to food.

## Points for judgement

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| What degrades it | **UV light, heat (30°C / 86°F and above), temperature swings, air (oxidation)** |
| Where to keep it | **A dark, cool place with a stable temperature** (wardrobe, drawer). **Bathroom, windowsill and car are unsuitable** |
| Guide after opening | **1–3 years** (depends on the type of scent and on storage). **Citrus scents degrade fastest; woody and oriental scents tend to last longer** |
| Signs of degradation | **Colour darkens (yellow → amber), top notes smell vinegary or sour, scent goes flat** |
| The box | **Keeping it in its outer box** is the simplest way to block light |
| Expiry labelling | Cosmetics **need no expiry date if they are stable for 3 years or more**. The **batch code (lot number)** can sometimes be used to estimate when it was made |

- In Japan perfume is a **cosmetic** under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (some products are sold as general goods), and **full ingredient labelling** is compulsory. **Alcohol (ethanol) helps preserve it** but **does not stop the fragrance oils oxidising.**
- **Decanting into an atomiser** exposes the liquid to **air and light, so it degrades faster than the bottle.** **Decant only as much as you will use in a few weeks.**
- Perfume is **a hazardous material (alcohols) under the Fire Service Act once ethanol content reaches 60% or more**: **keep it away from open flames**, **international-flight carry-on is limited to 100 mL per container** (domestic flights follow different rules — containers up to 0.5 L, 2 L in total), and **perfume with a high alcohol content cannot be sent through Japan Post** (it is a prohibited item under the Postal Act; solid perfume and alcohol-free types are fine). Some private courier firms accept it on condition of ground transport (see the article on posting parcels).

> **[Verify]** The guide for opened bottles is based on general industry and maker guidance. Check the hazardous-material handling against the Fire Service Act and airline rules, and check whether it can be posted against the latest guidance from Japan Post and each courier firm.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It lasts longer in the fridge

**Taking it in and out causes condensation from the temperature difference, and the scent transfers to food.** A dark place at room temperature is enough. **A dark place at constant temperature, like a wine cellar,** is ideal but not essential.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The bathroom is the natural place for it

**Steam makes the bathroom hot and humid, and a window adds UV.** Every condition for degradation is present. Move it to **a bedroom drawer**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A darker colour means it has matured

It is **the colour of oxidation**, and the scent has changed. **A sour top note** means it has degraded. Some people **stop wearing it on the skin and use it as a room fragrance** instead.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Once opened, you have to use it up quickly

**With good storage it keeps for 1–3 years.** **Closing the cap firmly and putting it back in the box** alone makes a difference.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Miniatures and atomisers are handy for storage

**Decanting from the bottle speeds up degradation.** Carry **only a few weeks' worth**. **Unopened miniatures** are a different matter (unopened, they keep like the full bottle).
