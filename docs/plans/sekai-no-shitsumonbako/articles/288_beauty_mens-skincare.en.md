# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about men's skincare?｜Japan Questions #288

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/mens-skincare` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The men's skincare market has grown fast in Japan, and visitors ask "what are Japanese men actually using?". This article looks at whether men's and women's skin differ, and what the minimum routine is, using public sources and the general explanations given in dermatology.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "men's skin is tough, so it needs no skincare." Men's skin tends to produce more sebum and hold less water, and daily shaving damages the outer layer. The minimum is three steps: "wash the face (no more than twice a day) → moisturise → sunscreen." A product's gender label is not a legal category, so choose by ingredients and texture.

## Points for judgement

| Item | General tendency (varies by person) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Sebum | Generally described as **about twice that of women** | Wash **no more than twice a day**; do not over-wash |
| Water content | Tends to be **lower than women's** (partly because many men do not moisturise) | **Do not skip moisturising** (milky lotion or gel) |
| Shaving | **A daily blade scrapes the outer layer: dryness, redness, folliculitis** | **Shaving gel, a clean blade, moisturise after shaving** |
| UV | Many men spend long hours outdoors | **Sunscreen** (the Ministry of the Environment manual makes no distinction by sex) |
| Ageing | Spots and dullness follow the same mechanism in men and women | UV protection is the most effective prevention |

- Japan's men's cosmetics market **has grown since the 2010s, and some surveys report that about 60% of men in their twenties do skincare daily** (industry surveys).
- **"Men's" and "for men" are not legal categories**; the regulation and the permitted claims (the list of 56 items) for a cosmetic are the same. **Men's products tend towards a cooling feel (menthol) and sebum control**, which may not suit men with dry skin.
- The Ministry of the Environment's "UV Environmental Health Manual" recommends **sunscreen, a hat and a parasol regardless of sex**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Washing the face is enough for a man

**Washing away sebum and then applying nothing is generally described as causing dryness, which makes the skin produce even more sebum.** Adding **a single milky lotion (乳液, nyūeki) or gel** changes that.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Men cannot use women's products

**The regulation for cosmetics is the same.** The only difference is **preference in scent and texture**, and **there is no problem using the moisturiser your family already has.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Redness after shaving is razor burn and cannot be helped

It can be greatly reduced by **a clean blade, shaving gel, shaving in the direction of hair growth, and moisturising afterwards**. If **red bumps persist**, it may be folliculitis; see a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Sunscreen is for women

**UV damage does not depend on sex, and the longer you spend outdoors the more it builds up.** **Men's risk of pigmentation spots and skin cancer** is dealt with in the same way in the Ministry of the Environment manual. **Non-sticky gel sunscreens** are the ones men in Japan tend to choose.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Skincare is unnecessary when you are young

**Accumulated UV exposure is the main cause of spots and wrinkles from middle age onwards.** The general dermatological explanation is that **the most effective care is sunscreen from a young age**.
