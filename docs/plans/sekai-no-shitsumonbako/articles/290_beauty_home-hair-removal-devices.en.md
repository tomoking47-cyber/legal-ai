# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about home hair-removal devices?｜Japan Questions #290

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/home-hair-removal-devices` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The market for home light-based (IPL) hair-removal devices has grown in Japan, and they are popular purchases for visitors too. But the expectation — "same results as a salon", "permanent hair removal" — does not match how these devices are actually classified, and the Consumer Affairs Agency has issued warnings.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a home device gives permanent hair removal." Permanent hair removal destroys the follicle; that is a medical procedure, and only lasers at a medical institution may do it. Home light devices are low-output "hair reduction" appliances, sold as general goods, and may only be described as "removing" or "reducing" hair. Burns and pigmentation have been reported, so following the manual's forbidden areas and the interval between sessions is the starting point.

## Classes and incidents

| Class | Who | How it works | What it can do | Legal status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Medical laser hair removal** | **Doctors and nurses (medical institution)** | High-output laser destroys the follicle | **Permanent hair removal** (suppresses regrowth long-term) | **Medical procedure** (MHLW notice, 2001) |
| **Salon light hair removal** | Beauticians | Lower-output light (IPL etc.) | **Hair reduction** (not permanent) | Illegal if it destroys the follicle. **The words "permanent hair removal" may not be used** |
| **Home light device** | You | Light at still lower output | **Hair removal and reduction** | **General goods (雑貨, zakka — neither a medical device nor a quasi-drug)** |

- The Consumer Affairs Agency and the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan have received reports of **burns, blisters, pigmentation and eye pain from home devices**, and **have issued warnings since 2019**. Most cases were caused by **ignoring the interval between sessions, using the device on forbidden areas (parts of the face, mucous membranes, moles, freshly tanned skin), or turning the output up too high**.
- **Problems with salon hair removal** are also reported continuously, and **claims of "permanent hair removal" or "equivalent to medical" are regarded as problematic under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations and the Medical Care Act**.
- **Use on the VIO area (the Japanese term for the bikini line and intimate area) and on the face is forbidden by some products.** Products carry instructions about **protective goggles and shielding the eyes**.

> **[Verify]** Check the scope of medical procedures against the MHLW notice (Iseii-hatsu No. 105, 2001), and the incident cases against the published materials of the Consumer Affairs Agency and the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A home device gives permanent hair removal

**It does not.** Permanent hair removal is **for medical lasers only**. Home devices **reduce** hair, and the assumption is that **hair returns once you stop**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Maximum output works faster

That is **the main cause of burns, blisters and pigmentation**. Follow **the manual's output setting (graded by skin tone) and the interval between sessions (for most devices, once every 2 weeks)**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It can be used on tanned skin

**The light reacts to melanin, so tanned skin carries a high burn risk**, and products forbid it. **In summer, sunscreen and shade are a precondition for use.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The face and VIO area can be done the same way

**Each product has its own forbidden areas.** **Around the eyebrows, near the eyes, mucous membranes, moles and tattoos** are forbidden by most products. **Follow the manual.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜A salon is safe and permanent

**Salon light hair removal is hair reduction**, and may not be described as permanent. **Understand the difference from a medical institution, and check the contract and cancellation terms (the cooling-off right under the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions).** **For problems, call the consumer hotline on 188 (Japanese-language line to your local consumer affairs centre).**
