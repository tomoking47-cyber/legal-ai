# Q. What do people get wrong about looking after a suit?｜Japan Questions #292

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/suit-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Wearing a suit every working day is deeply rooted in Japan, so a good deal of know-how on making a suit last has built up. Abroad, the recurring questions are "how often should I dry-clean it?" and "is it fine to wear the same suit every day?".

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a suit needs no care unless it looks dirty, and then it goes to the cleaner". A wool suit takes on moisture and loses its shape every time it is worn; worn on consecutive days it cannot recover, and repeated dry-cleaning strips the natural oils and dulls the fabric. The basics are: rotate 2–3 suits, brush it and air it overnight when you get home, and dry-clean once or twice a season.

## Everyday care

| When | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| After getting home | **Jacket on a thick, shaped hanger; trousers on a clip hanger or one that grips the hems.** **Brush with a clothes brush, top to bottom** | Removes dust and pollen, settles the fibres |
| Overnight | **Air it in a well-ventilated spot out of the sun** (don't put it straight in the wardrobe) | Lets sweat and moisture out. Wool absorbs moisture |
| Rotation | **Don't wear the same suit two days running. Rotate 2–3** | Wool fibres **recover their shape and spring in 1–2 days** |
| Creases | **Hang it in the bathroom for 1–2 hours (steam)**, or **hold a garment steamer just off the fabric** | Moisture lets wool fibres relax back |
| Trouser crease | **Iron with a pressing cloth on medium (two dots)**, with steam | Prevents shine |
| Cleaning | **Once or twice a season, and before storing.** **In a sweaty summer, ask for a "sweat removal" (汗抜き) or wet clean** | Dry-cleaning cannot remove sweat (water-soluble) |

- Most suits are **wool (or a wool blend)**, and the **care label mostly reads "do not wash at home; dry-clean (P)"**. **A "washable suit" carries a machine-wash symbol** and can be washed at home.
- **Shine** is where friction has flattened the fibres (see the ironing article). It appears on the **elbows, knees and seat**. **Steaming from a distance and lifting the fibres with a brush** can make it less noticeable.
- **Storage**: **a non-woven garment cover, moth repellent placed above the suit, plastic dry-cleaner's cover removed.** Moths eat wool.

> **[Verify]** Check the meaning of the symbols against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page. Care frequency is based on general guidance from the textile and dry-cleaning industries.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Wearing the same suit every day is fine

**Wool needs 1–2 days to recover.** Worn day after day, **the knees and elbows bag out, creases stay, and the suit's life shortens.** **Rotating 2–3 suits** is the basis of a long life.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The more often you dry-clean, the cleaner it is

**Dry-cleaning strips oils; repeated, it dulls the fabric.** **Once or twice a season**; day to day, **brush and air**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dry-cleaning removes sweat

**Dry-cleaning (solvent) suits oily dirt; sweat (water-soluble) stays behind.** At the end of summer, ask for **"sweat removal" (汗抜き, ase-nuki) or wet cleaning**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Iron the creases out

**A hot iron directly on wool causes shine.** Use **bathroom steam, or a steamer held off the fabric**. If you must iron: **pressing cloth, medium heat**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A wire hanger is good enough

**The shoulders lose their shape and the jacket gets hanger marks.** Use **a thick wooden or plastic hanger that matches the shoulder width.** **Hanging trousers by the hems with clips** helps the knees recover.
