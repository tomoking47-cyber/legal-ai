# Q. What do people get wrong about getting creases out of shirts and jackets?｜Japan Questions #293

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/removing-wrinkles-with-steam` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

On a business trip, or in a rushed morning, how to remove creases without an iron is a question asked in every country. In Japan, garment steamers have become common, and "how do they differ from an iron?" and "what can I use one on?" are the new questions.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "you need an iron and an ironing board to remove creases". A crease is the fibre's memory of a shape, and it releases with moisture, warmth and weight. Bathroom steam, a garment steamer, or a spray bottle and a hanger will remove most creases. But a "press" — the sharp fold on cotton or linen — can only be made with an iron.

## Method by method

| Method | Suited fabrics | Steps | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hang in the bathroom** | Wool, polyester, blends | **1–2 hours in the steamy bathroom after a bath or shower**, then move to a dry place | Deep creases in cotton and linen do not release easily |
| **Garment steamer** | Wool, polyester, silk (check the label) | **Hold it a few cm off the fabric**, work top to bottom, pulling the fabric gently with the other hand | **Cannot press (make a fold).** Weak on deep cotton creases |
| **Spray bottle + hanging** | Cotton, polyester | Dampen the whole garment, **straighten it and let its own weight pull it as it dries** | Takes time to dry |
| **Iron** | Cotton, linen (three dots), blends (two dots) | Set the temperature by the number of dots on the label. **Use a pressing cloth** | Time and space |
| Hair dryer | Emergencies | Dampen the spot, blow warm air and smooth it by hand | Local only; watch the heat |

- **Creases form when fibres move under moisture and heat and lock into shape as they dry.** **The same principle reverses them**: add moisture, straighten, dry.
- **A steamer is not a substitute for an iron**: it is good at **relaxing fabric and plumping it back**; **creating folds and crisp flatness** is the iron's job. The usual division is **iron for shirt collars and front plackets, steamer for jackets and knitwear**.
- **Steamers can spit hot steam or hot water and cause scalds.** Manufacturers' instruction manuals list the same precautions: **do not overfill the tank, do not tilt it too far, never point it at a person**.

> **[Verify]** Check the ironing symbols against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page, and steamer accidents against NITE's product-accident information.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Only an iron removes creases

**Moisture and weight** release most of them. **Hanging the garment in bathroom steam** is the standard trick of Japanese business travellers.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A steamer fully replaces an iron

**It cannot press or make folds.** It is for **relaxing fabric back into shape**; **for a crisp cotton shirt, use an iron**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The closer the steam to the fabric, the better it works

**Hold it away.** **Pressed against the fabric it leaves water marks, and discolours silk and rayon.** **A few cm off, top to bottom.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Steam is safe on any material

**Leather, synthetic leather, prints, fused interlinings and water-repellent finishes** can be damaged by steam. **If the label says no steam, follow it.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Wear it damp and body heat will smooth it out

**The shape as it dries is what sets**, so if you wear it and move about, **the wearing creases lock in**. **Hang it to dry before putting it on.**
