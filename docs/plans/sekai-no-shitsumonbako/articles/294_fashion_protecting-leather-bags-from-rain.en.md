# Q. What do people get wrong about protecting a leather bag from rain?｜Japan Questions #294

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/protecting-leather-bags-from-rain` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In rainy Japan, you cannot avoid a leather bag getting wet at some point. "What do I do once it's wet?" and "does waterproofing spray damage leather?" are asked in every country. Once you know what a rain mark actually is, the way you respond changes.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "once leather gets wet, it's finished". The problem is not the wetting itself but uneven drying (a partly wet patch leaves a tide-mark, the "rain stain") and the loss of oils once it dries. The basics: if it gets wet, press the whole bag at once with a dry cloth → shape it and dry in the shade → once fully dry, apply cream. For prevention, apply a thin coat of waterproofing spray outdoors.

## What to go on

| Situation | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Just wet** | **Press with a soft dry cloth to lift the water** (don't rub). **If only part of it is wet, lightly dampen the whole bag with a wet towel to remove the boundary** (prevents rain marks) | Leaving it, hair dryer |
| **Drying** | **Stuff it with paper to hold the shape; 1–2 days in a well-ventilated shady spot** | Direct sun, heater |
| **Once dry** | **A thin layer of leather cream** (the oils have been lost) | Over-applying |
| **Prevention** | **Fluorine-based waterproofing spray (フッ素系防水スプレー), outdoors, 30 cm away, two thin coats.** **Test on a hidden spot** | Spraying indoors (inhalation accidents) |
| By material | **Suede and nubuck: a spray made for them**; **patent leather: no spray, just wipe**; **synthetic leather: resists water but hydrolyses** | Cream on suede |

- A **rain mark** forms when **oils and dyes inside the leather migrate to the boundary between the wet and dry areas, leaving an edge.** **Dampening the whole surface evenly and drying it** can make the mark less visible.
- **Respiratory injury from inhaling waterproofing sprays (those containing fluororesin, silicone resin and the like)** is something the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government have warned about. **Outdoors, upwind, with a mask** is the rule.
- **Colour transfer**: a wet leather bag can **stain light-coloured clothing**. On rainy days, **keep a wet bag from resting against your clothes**.

> **[Verify]** Check waterproofing-spray accidents against the National Consumer Affairs Center's published material. Care methods are based on guidance from the Japan Leather and Leather Goods Industries Association and others.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Leather must never get wet

**Leather is animal skin; getting wet does not in itself destroy it.** The issues are **how it dries and its oils**. **Handled properly, it usually recovers.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wipe only the wet patch

**A patch that dries on its own leaves a rain-mark edge.** **Dampen the whole bag lightly, then dry it evenly** — the method the leather industry also describes.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dry it quickly with a hair dryer

**Sudden heat hardens, shrinks and cracks leather.** **Air-dry** for 1–2 days.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Waterproofing spray damages leather

**Fluorine-based leather sprays are designed to repel water while letting the leather breathe.** **Silicone-based sprays can block breathability**, so choose one **labelled for leather** and **test it on a hidden spot** first.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Not using a leather bag on rainy days is the only answer

With **waterproofing spray, a bag cover (or rain cover), and knowing what to do when it gets wet**, it can be used every day. Switching to **patent leather, synthetic leather or nylon** on rainy days is another habit shared among people living in Japan.
