# Q. What do people get wrong about using a fabric shaver (lint remover)?｜Japan Questions #295

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/using-a-lint-shaver` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The fabric shaver (毛玉取り器, *kedama-tori-ki* — an electric tool whose rotating blade shaves off pills) is a fixture in Japan, sold everywhere from 100-yen shops to electronics stores. Abroad, people are wary that it will "cut holes in the clothes". Here are the conditions for using one safely.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "press the shaver on hard and it works faster". Pressing shaves into the weave itself and causes holes and thinning. The basics: spread the fabric on a flat surface, hold the shaver lightly so it floats, move it in circles, and do not use it on loose-knit or thin fabrics.

## What to go on

| Tool | Suited fabrics | Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Electric fabric shaver (rotating blade)** | **Flat cotton, acrylic or polyester fabrics** (sweatshirts, fleece, socks) | **Don't press; float it.** Keep away from **buttons, zips and embroidery**. **Loose-knit fabric gets pulled into the blade** |
| **Pilling brush (animal bristle)** | Wool and cashmere knits | Lightly, with the nap. Also **tidies the fibres** |
| **Small scissors** | Large pills, a few of them | **Cut only the pill, without pulling the fabric** |
| **Safety razor (T-shape)** | Flat cotton fabric (emergency) | A new blade cuts too deep. **Glide lightly.** Risky |
| **Sponge (rough side of a washing-up sponge)** | Light pilling | Don't over-rub |

- Pills form when **friction tangles the surface fibres into balls** (see the pilling article). **Removing them tidies the garment for now, but unless you reduce the cause (friction in wear and in the wash) they come back.**
- Some electric shavers have **a "fabric thickness" setting or a height-adjustment ring**: **set it high for knits, low for flat fabrics**. **Neglect blade replacement and cleaning** and it stops cutting and snags instead.
- Complaints that **"the shaver made a hole"** typically involve **pressing too hard, thin fabric, or working near buttons**.

> **[Verify]** Follow the instruction manual for how to use the product. The explanation of pilling is the general one given by textile organisations.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Press harder and it removes more

**It shaves the weave and opens holes.** **Float it lightly, in circles.** **Anything left, catch on a second pass.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜It works on any garment

**Thin fabrics, loose knits, stretch fabrics, lace and embroidered areas** get pulled in or holed. **Flat fabric of medium weight or heavier** is what suits it.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Remove the pills and they won't come back

**The cause is friction.** **Wash inside out, use a mesh laundry bag, and reduce rubbing from your bag** to slow their return.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A 100-yen shaver is dangerous

**Technique matters more than price.** **A model with no height adjustment needs a careful floating touch**, so for a first shaver **one with an adjustment ring** gives fewer failures.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Pick knit pills off by hand

**Pulling draws out the surrounding fibres and makes more pills.** **Cut only the pill with scissors**, or **tidy with a brush**.
