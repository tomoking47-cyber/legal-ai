# Q. What do people get wrong about looking after a belt?｜Japan Questions #296

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/belt-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

A belt is fastened and loosened every day — the hardest-worked leather item most people own. Cracking, stretched holes and discolouration around the buckle are complaints the world over. In Japan, people share care habits for extending a belt's life.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a belt is a consumable, not something you look after". A leather belt's life changes by years if you wipe it and apply cream once a month, avoid always using the same hole, and hang it rather than storing it rolled up. Discolouration around the buckle is a reaction between sweat and metal, and wiping slows it down.

## Care and storage

| Item | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | When you take it off, **wipe the inside (sweat) and the outside with a dry cloth** | Salt from sweat and skin oil cause cracking |
| **Monthly** | **A thin coat of leather cream, then buff dry** | Replaces the oils |
| **Holes** | **Don't always fasten at the same hole** (use the ones either side as your body changes) | One hole stretches and tears |
| **Storage** | **Hang it with the buckle outward**, or **lay it flat**. **Don't roll it up in a drawer** | Rolling sets a bend and cracks it |
| **Rotation** | Alternate two or more | Lets the leather recover |
| **Buckle** | **Metal polish can strip the surface plating**, so **dry wiping** is the rule | Discolouration is a reaction with sweat |
| **Synthetic leather** | Wipe only. **Hydrolysis makes it peel within a few years** | Nature of the material |

- **Cracks in a leather belt come from drying out and repeated bending.** **Storage that avoids folds** and **oils** are the prevention.
- **Green deposits around the buckle (verdigris)** are **a reaction between copper-containing metal and sweat.** If **a cloth wipes them off**, there is no problem. If **your skin becomes irritated**, a metal allergy (nickel etc.) is possible — have it checked at a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).
- Belts fall under the **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (miscellaneous manufactured goods)** and carry **a material label (cowhide, synthetic leather, etc.)**.

> **[Verify]** Check the scope of the labelling obligation against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Quality labelling rules for miscellaneous manufactured goods", and the metal-allergy explanation against Japanese Dermatological Association material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Belts aren't something you maintain

**A leather belt is a leather product, like shoes and bags.** **Monthly cream** adds years to its life.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Always fastening at the same hole is correct

**A single hole stretches and tears.** **Alternate between the neighbouring holes, and adjust as your weight changes.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Rolled up in a drawer is compact

It causes **a set bend and cracking**. **Hang it** (belt hanger or hook), or **lay it flat**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Polish the buckle with metal polish to make it shine

**Most buckles are plated, and abrasive polish strips the plating.** **Wipe dry; for dirt, use a cloth dampened with diluted neutral detergent.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜A synthetic-leather belt is tougher than leather

**Synthetic leather (polyurethane) hydrolyses in humidity, and the surface peels within a few years even if it is never used.** It is **cheap and stands up to rain**, but **for long use, choose leather**.
