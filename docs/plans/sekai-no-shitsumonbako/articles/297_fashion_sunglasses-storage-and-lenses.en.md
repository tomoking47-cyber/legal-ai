# Q. What do people get wrong about sunglass lenses and storage?｜Japan Questions #297

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/sunglasses-storage-and-lenses` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Sunglasses are often assumed to protect the eyes in proportion to how dark they are. In fact it is the UV-blocking rate, not the tint, that protects your eyes. Japan has a JIS standard for sunglasses, and knowing how to read its labels changes how you choose. Storage precautions are covered too.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the darker the lens, the more UV it blocks". UV protection comes from the lens material and coating and has nothing to do with the depth of tint. A dark lens without UV protection is actually worse: the pupil opens wider and lets in more UV. Choose by the label (UV transmittance 1.0% or less, UV400, etc.), and store the glasses away from heat and scratches.

## Reading the label (JIS T 7333)

| Label | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **UV transmittance 1.0% or less (紫外線透過率1.0%以下)** | **Blocks 99% or more of UV.** Japan's JIS label | |
| **UV400** | **Blocks UV up to a wavelength of 400 nm** (international / industry label) | |
| **Visible light transmittance (可視光線透過率)** | The **darkness** of the lens. **For driving, 8% or more** (JIS: under 8% is unsuitable for driving); **for night driving, 75% or more** | |
| Polarised (偏光) | Reduces reflected glare (water, road surface) | LCD screens can be hard to see |
| Photochromic (調光) | Tint changes with the amount of UV | Changes little inside a car (UV-cut glass) |

- **JIS T 7333 (sunglasses)** specifies labelling for **UV transmittance, visible light transmittance and suitability for driving**, among other things. **Even with cheap fashion sunglasses, check for a JIS label or a "UV cut" (UVカット) label.**
- The Ministry of the Environment's "UV Environmental Health Manual" lists risks such as **cataract and pterygium** (a growth on the surface of the eye) from UV, and recommends **UV-blocking sunglasses and a hat**. **A shape that fits the face closely and blocks light from the side** is effective.
- **Plastic lenses are heat-sensitive (around 60°C)**; **left on a car dashboard, the coating cracks and the lens warps**.

> **[Verify]** Check the labelling items and figures in JIS T 7333, and the wording of the Ministry of the Environment manual.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The darker the lens, the better it protects

**UV protection has nothing to do with tint.** A dark lens without UV protection **opens the pupil and lets more UV in**, so it is worse. Look for **"UV transmittance 1.0% or less" or "UV400"** on the label.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Cheap sunglasses don't block UV

**Regardless of price, if it is labelled, it blocks UV.** The differences are **lens distortion, durability and frame quality**. **Avoid unlabelled ones.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any sunglasses will do for driving glare

**Visible light transmittance under 8% is unsuitable for driving** (JIS). **Dark lenses are dangerous at night and in tunnels.** **Polarised lenses can make the sat-nav screen hard to read.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Keeping them in the car is handy

**A car interior in summer reaches 60–70°C**, **cracking the coating on plastic lenses and warping the frame**. **Take them with you, in a case.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Wipe the lenses on your shirt

**Dust scratches them and the coating peels.** As with spectacles, **rinse with water first, then use a lens cloth** (see the glasses-care article). **Don't put them in the case lens-down.**
