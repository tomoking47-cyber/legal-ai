# Q. What do people get wrong about smelly shoes?｜Japan Questions #299

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/shoe-odour` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan, where you take your shoes off indoors, smelly shoes are a worry that other people discover at the entrance hall (玄関, genkan). "Will deodorant spray solve it?" and "is it fine to wear the same shoes every day?" are asked abroad too. Knowing where the smell comes from changes the approach.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the smell is the smell of foot sweat itself". Sweat is almost odourless; the smell comes from substances (isovaleric acid and others) produced when bacteria break down skin oil and dead skin in the damp inside of a shoe. The order of measures is: dry the shoes (don't wear them on consecutive days; take out the insoles), keep the feet clean (wash between the toes; fresh socks every day), and use deodorisers and drying agents only as a back-up.

## Causes and measures

| Cause | Mechanism | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Moisture** | Feet produce about 200 mL of sweat a day. Inside a shoe it reaches **about 90% humidity and 35°C** | **After a day's wear, dry for 1–2 days**; **take out the insoles**; **drying agents, newspaper, wooden shoe trees** |
| **Bacteria** | Break down skin oil and dead skin, releasing **isovaleric acid, acetic acid and others** | **Fresh socks every day**; **wash between the toes and around the nails**; **care for hard skin** (see the heel article) |
| **Material** | Synthetic leather and rubber breathe poorly and dry slowly | **Breathable materials**; **rotation** |
| **Socks** | Synthetics dry fast but hold odour; cotton absorbs but dries slowly | **A moisture-wicking, quick-dry blend**; **five-toe socks (5本指ソックス)**; **carry a spare pair** |
| Washable shoes | Trainers etc. | **Wash monthly and dry completely** (see the trainers article) |

- **Deodorant sprays and deodorisers** are a back-up that **breaks down or absorbs the odour molecules**; **unless you reduce the bacteria and the moisture, the smell is back the next day.**
- **Baking soda (重曹, jūsō)**, mildly alkaline, **neutralises acidic isovaleric acid**, so **putting it in a cloth bag inside the shoe** is a method shared among people living in Japan. **Loose powder leaves a white residue.**
- Putting a **10-yen coin (copper)** in the shoe is folk practice based on **copper's antibacterial property**; its **effect is generally described as limited**. **Newspaper, which draws out moisture for the time being**, is more reliable.
- If **the smell is strong and the skin peels or itches**, it may be **athlete's foot (足白癬, tinea pedis)** — see a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).

> **[Verify]** Sweat volume and in-shoe temperature and humidity are general figures. Check the odour compounds against Japanese Dermatological Association and textile-industry material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Deodorant spray solves it

**It only holds the smell down for a while.** **Dry, wash, change socks** comes first.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wearing the same shoes every day is fine

**The inside of a shoe does not dry overnight.** **Rotating 2–3 pairs** is the single most effective measure.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sun-drying the shoes kills the bacteria

**Direct sun hardens leather and degrades synthetic leather.** **Shade with good airflow, insoles out.** **UV only kills what is on the surface.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Washing your feet with soap is enough

Bacteria stay **between the toes, around the nails and in the hard skin on the heels**. **Wash between the toes, dry completely, then put on socks.** **Keep nails short.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Smell is down to your constitution, nothing to be done

**It is a moisture-and-bacteria problem**, and **shoes, socks and how you wash** change it a great deal. **If it doesn't improve, or the skin peels or itches**, see **a dermatologist**.
