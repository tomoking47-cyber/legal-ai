# Q. What do people get wrong about putting on and lacing shoes?｜Japan Questions #300

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/how-to-lace-and-wear-shoes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan you take your shoes off and on many times a day, so habits such as treading down the heel or slipping shoes on and off still laced come easily. They affect both the life of the shoe and the health of the foot. How to wear shoes is asked more often than you might expect in every country. Here is the general guidance of Japan's footwear industry and foot specialists.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "put your foot in, tie the laces, and you're wearing the shoe". The correct way is to seat the heel first, then tighten the laces from the toe end, so that the heel does not lift and there is about 1 cm of room at the toe. Treading down the heel, or wearing shoes with the laces loose, is generally described as distorting the shoe and increasing the strain on the toes and knees.

## The basics of putting shoes on

| Step | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Loosen the laces and put your foot in** | Forcing it stretches the opening |
| 2 | **Tap the heel lightly on the floor to seat your heel in the heel of the shoe** | The position where the foot does not slide forward |
| 3 | **Tighten the laces in order from the toe end** | Holds the instep evenly |
| 4 | **Tie. Leave room for one finger** | Too tight restricts blood flow and causes numbness |
| 5 | **Loosen the laces to take the shoes off** | Pulled off still tied, the opening and heel break down |
| Size | **About 1 cm at the toe (捨て寸, sute-zun, "spare length")**, **no pressure across the width**, **try on in the evening** (feet swell) | The toes can move |

- **Treading down the heel crushes the heel counter (the stiffener in the heel), and the shoe stops supporting the foot.** A crushed counter does not recover.
- **Wearing shoes with the laces loose** lets **the foot slide forward and pushes the toes against the front**, and is generally described (by the Japanese Society for Footwear Medicine and others) as one factor in **bunions (hallux valgus), ingrown toenails and knee strain**.
- Habits of people living in Japan: **on days with a lot of taking off and on, wear shoes that need no tying (loafers, slip-ons)**, or **switch to elastic laces or quick-release fasteners**. **Keep a shoehorn (靴べら, kutsu-bera) at the entrance.**
- **Children's shoes**: **check the size every 3–6 months** (growth), and **pull the Velcro strap tight before fastening it**.

> **[Verify]** The effects on the foot are based on general explanations from the Japanese Society for Footwear Medicine and the Japanese Orthopaedic Association. The sizing guide (sute-zun) is footwear-industry convention.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Treading on the heel doesn't change the shoe

**The heel counter crushes and does not recover.** The shoe loses its ability to support the foot, and **its life shortens sharply**. Use a **shoehorn**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Tie the laces once and never loosen them

**Taking shoes off and on while tied stretches the opening and breaks down the heel.** **Loosen them every time you take the shoes off.** **If that is a chore, choose shoes without laces.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜A bigger shoe is more comfortable

**The foot slides inside and the toes are pushed forward.** **Seat the heel, 1 cm at the toe** is the guide. Check the **width** too.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The tighter, the more stable

It **restricts blood flow, causing numbness and cold feet.** **Room for one finger.** **People with a high instep can relieve the pressure by skipping an eyelet or two** when lacing.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Buy children's shoes a little big so they last longer

**Shoes that are too big are generally described as a cause of falls and foot deformity.** **0.5–1 cm of room at the toe**, **check every 3–6 months**. Make a habit of **pulling the Velcro strap tight before fastening it.**
