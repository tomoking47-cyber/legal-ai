# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how bright a bedroom should be?｜Japan Questions #302

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/bedroom-lighting` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Should I sleep in total darkness, or is a small night light fine?" is asked all over the world. In Japanese homes the standard ceiling light has a built-in night-light setting, the small orange bulb called the *mame-denkyū* (豆電球, "bean bulb"), and how to use it is a common question.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "brightness only matters for falling asleep." Light is the strongest signal that moves the body clock: light at night makes sleep shallower, and light in the morning sets your waking rhythm. The guide is to dim the lights 1–2 hours before bed, keep the bedroom dark (if that worries you, a weak warm-coloured night light at floor level), and open the curtains in the morning to let the light in.

## In numbers

| Situation | Guide | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 hours before bed | **Dim the lights (indirect lighting, warm colour)**. **Avoid phone and PC screens, or dim them** | Sleep Guide 2023 |
| Bedroom (while asleep) | **As dark as possible**. If needed, **a weak warm-coloured light at floor level (a few lux)** | Same as above |
| Morning | **Open the curtains after getting up and take in the light**. Even on a cloudy day, outdoors is **several thousand lux** | Same as above |
| Typical indoor lighting | **300–500 lux** (living room); offices about **750 lux** (JIS Z9110) | JIS |
| Night light (mame-denkyū) | **1 to a few lux** (varies by product) | — |

- **Bluish-white light ("daylight colour", a high colour temperature) is generally described as promoting wakefulness, while warm light ("bulb colour") has a smaller effect.** Use **warm "bulb colour" lighting in the bedroom** (on Japanese bulb packaging: 電球色, *denkyū-shoku*).
- **Light at night** is known to **suppress melatonin (a hormone involved in sleep)**, and **several studies find that the habit of sleeping in a bright room lowers sleep quality**.
- For older people who **get up to use the toilet at night**, **a weak light at floor level prevents falls better than total darkness**, so **a warm-coloured light with a motion sensor** is practical.

> **[Verify]** Check the statements on light in the Sleep Guide 2023, and the illuminance standards in JIS Z9110.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Once you get used to a bright room, it makes no difference

**Brightness acts on the body clock; it is generally described as not a matter of getting used to it.** **Even if you sleep, the sleep is shallower.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜A night light is dim, so it is no problem

**A weak, warm-coloured light at floor level is generally described as having little effect.** **Avoid white light near the face or from the ceiling.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜If you dim the screen, a phone is fine right before bed

**The problem is not only brightness but the wakefulness caused by the content.** **Staying off screens for the hour before bed** is the sleep guide's basic position (see our article on phones before bed).

## Misunderstanding 4｜In the morning, it is better to wake slowly in a dark room

**Morning light is the strongest signal for setting the body clock.** **Opening the curtains, sleeping with them slightly open, or a dawn-simulating light alarm (*hikari mezamashi*)** are the everyday tricks people in Japan use.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Blackout curtains are always the right answer

They are effective at **blocking city light at night**, but they **also block the morning light**, so many people **open them at their wake-up time** or combine them with **timer-driven curtains or a light alarm**.
