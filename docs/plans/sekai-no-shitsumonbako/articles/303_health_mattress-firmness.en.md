# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about mattress firmness?｜Japan Questions #303

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/mattress-firmness` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The belief that "a hard mattress is good for your back" is especially deep-rooted in Japan, tied to the tradition of a thin futon (*shikibuton*) laid on tatami. Outside Japan the opposite view, "the softer, the more comfortable", is common. Here we sort out only what can be said with confidence, from back-pain research and the general guidance of the bedding industry.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a hard mattress is best for lower-back pain." Research has more often found that a "medium-firm" mattress gives better results for sleep and pain in people with back pain than an extremely hard one. The basic rule is a firmness that keeps the spine in its natural S-curve when you lie down and lets you turn over easily; that depends on your weight and sleeping position.

## Points to judge by

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Research | A widely cited randomised trial (Kovacs et al., *Lancet* 2003) found a **medium-firm** mattress better than a firm one for **pain (in bed and on rising) and disability** in patients with back pain |
| Guide | **No gap under the lower back and back when lying on your back**; **spine parallel to the floor when lying on your side**; **easy to turn over** |
| Body weight | **Lighter people tend to suit a softer mattress**, **heavier people a firmer one**, in terms of how far the body sinks |
| Sleeping position | **Side sleepers need enough softness for the shoulder and hip to sink**; **back sleepers, slightly firmer** |
| Futon on tatami | **A thin futon is too hard and concentrates pressure on the hips and shoulders.** Use **3–5 cm or more of thickness**, or **a mattress underneath** |
| Replacement | **Sagging (body impressions that do not spring back), hips sinking, back pain in the morning**. Guide: **7–10 years** |

- Japan's traditional **shikibuton is thin and hard**, which is generally described as the origin of the idea "hard = good for the back"; but **today's general guidance from orthopaedics and the bedding industry is "medium"**.
- **The Japanese Orthopaedic Association** advises, on back pain, that **bedding should "not let the body sink in, and should make turning over easy"**.
- **The combination with your pillow** decides the alignment of neck and spine (see our article on pillows).

> **[Verify]** The research citation is Kovacs FM et al., Lancet 2003; 362:1599-1604. Check the Japanese Orthopaedic Association's wording.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A hard floor or board is good for back pain

**Too hard, and pressure lands on single points at the hips and shoulders; you turn over more and sleep more shallowly.** In the research, **medium-firm** came out ahead.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The softer, the more comfortable and the better for your body

**Sink in too far and the lower back is held in a bent position, and turning over becomes harder.** One cause of a stiff lower back in the morning.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more expensive the mattress, the better for your body

**Price and fit are two different things.** Fit depends on **weight, sleeping position and preference**; the practical approach is to **lie on it in the shop for 10 minutes or more** and **choose a product you can return if it does not suit you**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A mattress never needs replacing

**Sagging reduces how well it works.** **7–10 years**, or **when body impressions no longer spring back**. **Rotating it head-to-foot and flipping it regularly** makes it last longer.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Laying the futon directly on tatami is the correct Japanese way

**A single thin futon is often too hard**; the usual practice today is to **put a mattress or a thick under-pad beneath it**. **Moisture control (a slatted *sunoko* base, or lifting and airing the futon)** is also needed.
