# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about chewing your food well?｜Japan Questions #305

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/chewing-well` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Chew each mouthful 30 times" is a rule repeated in Japanese food education (*shokuiku*), and people from abroad often ask whether it really means anything. Here we look at how the number of chews has changed over history, and at what public sources allow us to say.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "how many times you chew is a matter of taste." In a modern meal the number of chews has fallen to less than half of the pre-war figure, and chewing is involved in digestion, feeling full, oral health and saliva production. "30 chews per mouthful" is the guide used in a Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare campaign (Kaming 30), and simply choosing foods that need chewing is a measure in itself.

## In numbers

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Chews per meal (estimate) | **Pre-war about 1,400** → **today about 600** | Estimates in dentistry and nutrition science (research by Shigeru Saito and others) |
| Guide | **30 chews per mouthful** (**"Kaming 30" (噛ミング30, *kamingu sanmaru*)**, since 2009) | MHLW |
| 8020 campaign | Keep **20 of your own teeth at age 80**. **About half** of people achieve it (2022 Survey of Dental Diseases) | MHLW, Japan Dental Association |
| Saliva | **1–1.5 L a day**. Chewing increases the flow, which works for **digestion, cleansing and neutralising acid** | General physiology |
| Fast eating and obesity | **Several Japanese studies find a higher risk of obesity among fast eaters** | Studies linked to the National Health and Nutrition Survey |

- **The fall in the number of chews** is generally attributed to **more soft foods (bread, hamburger steak, noodles)** and **shorter meal times**.
- **The feeling of fullness is generally described as reaching the brain 15–20 minutes after a meal starts**, and fast eating is explained as leading to overeating because **you finish before you feel full**.
- **In children's oral development** ("underdeveloped oral function", recognised as an insurable diagnosis in 2018), **too little chewing experience** is also named as a problem.

> **[Verify]** The chew-count figures are researchers' estimates. Check the 8020 achievement rate against the latest Survey of Dental Diseases, and Kaming 30 against MHLW material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜How many times you chew has nothing to do with digestion

**Chewing breaks food down and lets the enzyme in saliva (amylase) start working.** Swallowing food whole is generally described as a burden on the stomach and gut.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Counting to 30 at every mouthful is unrealistic

**Counting is not the point; choose foods that need chewing and the number rises by itself.** **Root vegetables, mushrooms, seaweed, brown rice, and cutting food into larger pieces** are the tricks used in Japanese food education.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Soft food is gentler on the body

**For chewing strength, the jaw and saliva, fewer chances to chew are generally described as a disadvantage.** **For older people too, a diet that needs chewing, for as long as they can chew,** is recommended to maintain oral function.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Eating fast does not make you fat if the amount is the same

**Research shows that fast eating delays the feeling of fullness, so the amount tends to rise.** There are also reports that **eating the same amount slowly is more satisfying**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Chewing has nothing to do with healthy teeth

**Chewing produces saliva that washes the mouth, and stimulates the tissues around the teeth.** That is why the **8020 campaign** has "keeping teeth you can chew with" as its goal.
