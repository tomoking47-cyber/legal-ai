# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how much sugar to eat?｜Japan Questions #306

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/sugar-intake` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

How much sugar people eat is a public-health issue worldwide; the WHO says "free sugars below 10% of total energy, and preferably below 5%". Japan's Dietary Reference Intakes set no numerical target for sugars, and the difference causes confusion.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "Japan has no sugar standard, so there is no need to worry." Japan's Dietary Reference Intakes (2025 edition) do not set a target for sugars, but the WHO's "below 10%" (about 50 g on 2,000 kcal; 25 g at 5%) is widely used as a reference value. A single sweet drink often exceeds 25 g, and "cutting down on drinks first" is described as having a large effect.

## In numbers

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| WHO guide | **Free sugars below 10% of total energy (strong recommendation); below 5% (conditional recommendation)** | WHO (2015) |
| For a person on 2,000 kcal | **10% = about 50 g / 5% = about 25 g** | Calculation |
| Japan's Dietary Reference Intakes | **No numerical target for sugars** (carbohydrate 50–65% of total energy) | MHLW (2025 edition) |
| Japanese sugar intake | Supply of **about 15–17 kg per person per year (about 45 g/day)**. Actual intake in surveys is from about **6–8 g (sugar and sweeteners only)** upwards | Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries; National Health and Nutrition Survey |
| Nutrition labelling | **Declaring "sugars" is voluntary.** "Carbohydrate" is mandatory | Consumer Affairs Agency |

| Drink or food | Approximate sugars |
|---|---|

## Misunderstanding 1｜Japan has no standard, so there is no problem

**The absence of a standard is generally explained by a lack of data in Japanese surveys to measure sugar intake accurately.** **The WHO guide** is the de facto reference.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The sugar in fruit should be cut too

The WHO's "free sugars" means **added sugars plus the sugars in juice, honey and syrups**; **the sugars in whole fruit and milk are not included.** Fruit: **about 200 g a day** (Japanese Food Guide Spinning Top).

## Misunderstanding 3｜"Low-carb" or "zero sugars" means there is nothing to worry about

**"Zero sugars" (糖類ゼロ) means less than 0.5 g per 100 mL**, and **artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols** are used instead. Some hold the view that care is needed because of **getting accustomed to sweetness**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Brown sugar, honey or fruit sugar is the healthy choice

**All of them are sugars** and count among the WHO's free sugars. **Their mineral content is tiny**, and no reason to eat more of them in place of sugar.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Give up sweets and your sugar goes down

**The biggest source is drinks.** **Swapping sweetened drinks for water or tea** is generally described as more effective than going without snacks.
