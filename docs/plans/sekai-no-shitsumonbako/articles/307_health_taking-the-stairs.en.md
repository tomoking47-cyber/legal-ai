# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about taking the stairs?｜Japan Questions #307

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/taking-the-stairs` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Take the stairs, not the lift" is health advice heard everywhere, but in Japan station stairs are part of daily life, and many people climb several hundred steps a day. Here we look at the value of stairs as exercise, and at the misunderstandings about their effect on the knees.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "stairs are bad for your knees, so avoid them." For healthy knees, stairs are a short burst of high-intensity physical activity (about 4.0–8.8 METs going up depending on pace, about 3.5 METs coming down), and the easiest way to follow the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare guide's advice to "move even a little more than now". If your knees hurt, avoid going down, use the handrail and talk to a doctor.

- The 2023 guide promotes **"+10 (Plus Ten)" = move 10 minutes more than now**, with **"stairs instead of the lift"** as its flagship example.
- For people with **knee osteoarthritis**, **the load of going down can worsen symptoms**, and orthopaedic clinics sometimes advise **"stairs going up, lift going down"**.
- **Stair accidents**: the Consumer Affairs Agency lists **falls on stairs among older people** high among accidents in the home, and recommends **handrails, lighting and non-slip strips**.

> **[Verify]** Check the MET values against the METs table of the National Institute of Health and Nutrition, the knee-load multiples against general orthopaedic explanations, and the accidents against Consumer Affairs Agency material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Stairs are bad for the knees

**For healthy knees, they are exercise.** If a knee is **painful or swollen**, **avoid going down, use the handrail and see a doctor**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜If you walk 10,000 steps a day, you do not need stairs

**Stairs are high intensity and earn physical activity in a short time.** The guide recommends **both step count and intensity**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Going down is easy, so it is not exercise

**Going down is exercise in which the muscles produce force while lengthening (eccentric)**; it **does build strength**, but **the load on the knee is greater than going up**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Only people in a hurry use the station stairs

**Climb 200 steps a day in total and that alone is about 5–10 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous exercise.** Part of the physical activity of Japan's city dwellers is made up of station stairs.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Older people should avoid stairs

**Stairs are effective for keeping strength and balance**, but there is a **risk of falls**. **Always use the handrail, on a well-lit staircase, without hurrying.** **Fit handrails and floor-level lights on the stairs at home.**
