# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about a stiff lower back?｜Japan Questions #308

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/lower-back-stiffness` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Lower-back pain is the most-reported symptom among Japanese men and the second among women (Comprehensive Survey of Living Conditions), and the most common physical complaint worldwide. The long-running misunderstanding, "rest or keep moving?", has been settled by clinical guidelines.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "when your back is stiff, rest and lie down." Japan's clinical guideline for low back pain recommends staying active rather than resting when there are no signs of serious disease ("red flags"). The basics are "do not hold one position", "keep it warm" and "move gently"; numbness, fever, problems passing urine, or pain after an injury are reasons to see a doctor.

## Points to judge by

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rate of people reporting back pain | **About 90 men and about 110 women per 1,000 population** (1st or 2nd most common symptom) | Comprehensive Survey of Living Conditions (2022) |
| Back pain with an identifiable cause | **About 15%**. The remaining **about 85% is "non-specific low back pain"** (no cause found on imaging) | Clinical Practice Guideline for Low Back Pain 2019 |
| Rest or activity | **Staying active is recommended.** **Long rest delays recovery** | Same as above |
| Heat or cold | The general advice is **warmth for chronic stiffness**, and **cold for the first 1–2 days of acute severe pain (*gikkuri-goshi*, a "thrown-out" back)** | Orthopaedics |
| **Reasons to see a doctor (red flags)** | **Numbness, weakness in the legs, problems with urination or bowel movements, fever, weight loss, pain after an injury, severe pain even at rest, a history of cancer** | Guideline |

- **Sitting for long periods** is one of the main causes of a stiff lower back (see our article on sitting too much). **Stand up once every 30 minutes to an hour.**
- As **exercise to prevent back pain**, short routines proposed by orthopaedic surgeons, such as **"Kore-dake Taisō" ("just this one exercise", a backward bend of the lower back)**, are widespread in Japan. **Avoid any movement that makes the pain worse.**
- **A back support belt (corset)** is an aid in the acute phase; **long-term daily use is not recommended** because of concern about **loss of muscle strength**.

> **[Verify]** Check the symptom rate against the latest Comprehensive Survey of Living Conditions, and the recommendations against the Japanese Orthopaedic Association / Japanese Society of Lumbar Spine Disorders *Clinical Practice Guideline for Low Back Pain 2019*.

## Misunderstanding 1｜When your back is stiff, lie down and rest

**For back pain without red flags, the guideline shows that staying active leads to faster recovery.** **Keep up everyday movement within the limits of the pain.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜An X-ray or MRI will find the cause of back pain

**In about 85% of cases, imaging cannot identify a cause.** Changes visible on imaging (such as disc degeneration) are **common in people with no pain at all**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Massage or a *seitai* (manipulation) clinic will fix it

**There is temporary relief**, but if **the cause of the stiffness (posture, sitting too much, lack of exercise)** remains, it comes back. The National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan has also received **complaints of symptoms made worse by forceful manipulation**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Strengthen your abdominal muscles and back pain disappears

**Core strength is one factor, but the clear preventive effect shown in research is for "exercise in general"**; no particular abdominal exercise is the single answer. **Abdominal exercises while in pain** can also make it worse.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Back pain is just age; nothing can be done

**It increases with age, but back pain is also common in people in their 20s–40s, and sitting too much, lack of exercise and stress are generally described as factors.** **Moving, warmth and changing position** apply at any age. **With red flags, see a doctor whatever your age.**
