# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about air conditioning and your health?｜Japan Questions #309

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/air-conditioning-and-health` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Air conditioning is bad for you" and *reibō-byō* (冷房病, "air-con sickness") are phrases heard all over Japan, and visitors ask why Japanese people seem to dislike air conditioning. Meanwhile, about 40% of heatstroke cases happen indoors. Here we sort out how to use air conditioning, based on public sources.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "air conditioning is bad for the body, so put up with the heat." The Ministry of the Environment and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare recommend using air conditioning to prevent heatstroke; most heatstroke deaths are indoors, among older people who were not using it. "Reibō-byō" is not a medical diagnosis but a popular name for feeling unwell from over-cooling and temperature swings; the fix is "set temperature and airflow direction", not "switching it off".

## Misunderstanding 1｜Using air conditioning weakens the body

**Most heatstroke deaths happen indoors where air conditioning was not in use.** **Using it is what protects your health.** Deal with discomfort through **settings and airflow direction**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜28°C is the temperature to set on the air conditioner

**28°C is a guide for room temperature**, not the set temperature. **Check with a thermometer in the room**, and **if humidity is high, go a little lower**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Older people cope well with heat, so they do not need it

**The opposite.** Older people **feel heat less and sweat less**, so they **slip into heatstroke without noticing**. **"Air conditioning even if you do not feel hot"**; more families living apart now **keep watch remotely with a thermometer-linked monitor**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Air conditioning gives you a cold

**Air conditioning itself does not cause infections.** **Chill and dry air can lower the body's defences**, so **avoid direct airflow, humidify, and switch it off or use the timer at night** (but **night-time heatstroke also happens**, so on hot nights the Ministry of the Environment's advice is to **leave it on low**).

## Misunderstanding 5｜A fan is enough

**When the air is hot, a fan only blows hot air at you.** The Ministry of the Environment recommends **air conditioning once room temperature passes 28°C**. Use **the fan to support the air conditioning** (circulating the air).
