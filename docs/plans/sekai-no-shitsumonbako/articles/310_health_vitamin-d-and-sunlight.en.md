# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about vitamin D and sunlight?｜Japan Questions #310

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/vitamin-d-and-sunlight` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The claim that "sunscreen leaves you short of vitamin D" is raised in many countries as a counter to sun-protection campaigns. In Japan the 2025 Dietary Reference Intakes raised the adequate intake for vitamin D, and interest has grown.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "sun protection and vitamin D cannot go together." The Ministry of the Environment and the National Institute for Environmental Studies explain that the sunlight needed to make vitamin D is covered by short everyday exposure (a few to ten-odd minutes on a clear summer day, with only hands and face uncovered). Even if you use sunscreen, everyday exposure plus food (fish, mushrooms) can reach the adequate intake.

## In numbers

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Adequate intake (adults) | **9.0 μg/day** (raised from 8.5 to 9.0 in the 2025 edition) | Dietary Reference Intakes for Japanese (2025 edition) |
| Tolerable upper intake | **100 μg/day** | Same as above |
| Actual intake (age 20 and over) | **About 6–7 μg** (higher in people who eat fish) | National Health and Nutrition Survey |
| Sunlight needed | **Clear July day at noon, face and backs of both hands**: **about 3–4 minutes in Tsukuba**, **about 5–6 minutes in Sapporo** for the equivalent of 10 μg. **At noon in December: about 76 minutes in Sapporo, about 8 minutes in Naha** | National Institute for Environmental Studies (2013) |
| Foods | **Salmon, 1 fillet (80 g) about 26 μg**; **Pacific saury (sanma), 1 fish about 16 μg**; **kikurage wood-ear mushroom (2 g dried) about 1.7 μg**; **dried shiitake (3 pieces) about 0.4 μg**; **1 egg about 1.3 μg** | Food composition tables |

- **A 2013 study by the National Institute for Environmental Studies and Tokyo Kasei University** published, **by season and region, the sunlight exposure time needed to produce 10 μg of vitamin D**. The range is wide: **a few minutes in summer, more than an hour in northern Japan in winter**.
- **The Ministry of the Environment's UV manual** states that **UV protection and getting enough vitamin D are not in conflict**, and that **everyday exposure plus food** is enough.
- Groups where **vitamin D shortage** is a concern: **people who spend their days mostly indoors, northern Japan in winter, older people, people who do not eat fish, and infants** (paediatricians advise vitamin D supplementation for breastfed babies).

> **[Verify]** Check the adequate intake against the 2025 Dietary Reference Intakes, the sunlight exposure times against the National Institute for Environmental Studies release (2013), and the food values against the Standard Tables of Food Composition in Japan (8th revised edition).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Sunscreen causes vitamin D deficiency

**Everyday exposure (face, hands, a few minutes on the commute) plus food is enough.** The Ministry of the Environment's position is to **recommend UV protection while covering vitamin D from food and everyday exposure**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sunbathe longer and you make more

**Production levels off at a certain amount, and any further UV is only a burden on the skin.** **In summer, a few to ten-odd minutes** is enough.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sunlight through a window works too

**UVB, the part of sunlight involved in making vitamin D, barely passes through window glass.** Little is produced sitting by a window indoors.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Take plenty in supplements and you will be healthier

There is a **tolerable upper intake of 100 μg**, and **excess causes hypercalcaemia (too much calcium in the blood)**. **Exceeding the limit with a normal diet plus a supplement** is rare, but **for high-dose supplements, ask a doctor or pharmacist**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Japanese people eat fish, so they are never short

**Fish consumption is falling**, and **younger generations are generally described as taking in less than the adequate intake**. **Fish two or three times a week, mushrooms and eggs** are the realistic way to get enough.
