# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing cheese?｜Japan Questions #312

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-cheese` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Cheese consumption is rising in Japan too, and two questions come up again and again: "Do I throw it out when mould appears?" and "Can I freeze it?" Processed cheese, the type found in most Japanese homes, and imported natural cheese need different handling. Here is how to read a Japanese cheese label and how long each type keeps.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "all cheese can be stored the same way." Processed cheese, the type most widely sold in Japan, has been melted at high heat and keeps fairly well after opening; natural cheese contains live cultures, and depending on the type it lasts anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. Unintended mould on the surface of a hard cheese can be cut away with a generous margin and the rest used; for soft cheese, the rule of thumb is to throw it out.

## Types and storage

| Type | Character | After opening (fridge) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Processed cheese** (slices, "6P" wedge packs) | Natural cheese **melted with heat** and reshaped. Cultures are inactive | **1–2 weeks** (individually wrapped pieces: until the best-before date while unopened) | Dries out. **Eat an individually wrapped piece the day you open it** |
| **Hard and semi-hard** (Parmesan, Cheddar, Gouda) | Low moisture | **2–4 weeks** | Drying, mould. **Rather than cling film alone, use baking paper plus cling film, or cheese paper** |
| **Soft and fresh** (mozzarella, ricotta, cottage) | High moisture | **A few days** (**2–3 days after opening**) | Spoils easily. Keep anything sold in liquid in its liquid |
| **White mould** (Camembert, Brie) | Keeps ripening | **1–2 weeks** (turns runny as it ripens) | Discard when the ammonia smell gets strong |
| **Blue** (Gorgonzola, blue cheese) | The blue mould is edible | **2–3 weeks** | Its mould spreads easily to other foods. Seal tightly |
| **Grated and shredded cheese** | Large surface area | **Refrigerate after opening, 2–3 weeks**. **Can be frozen** | Humidity causes mould and clumping |

- Under Japan's **Ministerial Ordinance on Milk and Milk Products (乳等省令)**, cheese is classified as either **"natural cheese" or "processed cheese"**, and the label tells you which (look for the line 種類別, "type").
- **Mould on cheese**: **on a hard cheese, unintended mould can be cut away with a 1–2 cm margin and the rest used**, according to general guidance; **mould on soft or shredded cheese has spread through the whole piece, so discard it** (this matches the general advice of the US Department of Agriculture).
- **Freezing**: **shredded and pizza cheese freeze well** (heat straight from frozen). **Blocks change texture** (crumbly), so keep them for cooked dishes. **Soft cheeses do not freeze well.**

> **[Verify]** Check the classification under the Ministerial Ordinance on Milk and Milk Products with MHLW, and the handling of mould against general food-safety guidance (USDA, MAFF).

## Misunderstanding 1｜If mould appears, throw the whole thing away

**A hard cheese can be used after cutting away a generous margin**, according to general guidance. **Soft cheese, shredded cheese and slices: discard.** **The mould that belongs on white-mould and blue cheeses is edible.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wrapping it tightly in cling film is best

**Wrapped tightly, moisture is trapped and mould appears more easily.** **Wrap in baking paper or cheese paper first, then loosely in cling film or a container** — the general advice from cheese specialists.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Cheese cannot be frozen

**Shredded, grated and blocks meant for cooking can be frozen.** **The texture changes**, so **frozen cheese is not for eating as it is**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Processed and natural cheese are the same

**Processed cheese has been heated so its cultures are inactive**, which is why it keeps. **Natural cheese has live cultures and keeps ripening.** Check the **"type" (種類別) line on the label**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cheese past its best-before date is dangerous

**Unopened and refrigerated, a hard cheese can be judged by its condition even past the date.** **With soft and fresh cheese, keep to the date.** Discard for **ammonia smell, sliminess or unexpected mould**.
