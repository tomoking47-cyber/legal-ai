# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about storing salt and sugar?｜Japan Questions #313

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-salt-and-sugar` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Salt and sugar are seasonings that "do not go off", but clumping, dampness and insects are problems in every country. Japan's humid climate makes the clumping worse, and the standard Japanese white sugar behaves differently from the granulated sugar many readers grew up with. Here is why each one clumps and how to bring it back.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "salt and sugar clump for the same reason." Salt clumps because it absorbs moisture; sugar clumps because it dries out. So the fixes are opposites: dry the salt (toast it, add a desiccant) and give the sugar a little moisture (a crust of bread, a spray of water). Neither has to carry a best-before date, and the basic rule for both is an airtight container at room temperature.

## How salt and sugar differ

| Item | Salt | Sugar (jōhakutō, 上白糖 — standard Japanese white sugar) |
|---|---|---|
| Why it clumps | **Absorbs moisture, then the crystals stick together as it dries** | **The syrup (invert sugar) coating the grains dries out and glues the crystals together** |
| How to loosen it | **Dry-toast in a frying pan**, **warm briefly in the microwave** (tens of seconds), **add a desiccant** | **Give it a little moisture**: **leave a crust of bread or a piece of apple peel in the container overnight**, **one spray from a mister**, **lay a damp, well-wrung cloth over it** |
| Storage | **Airtight container, desiccant, away from the hob** | **Airtight container, somewhere not too dry** (not the fridge, which dries it out) |
| Date | **No obligation to show a best-before date** (quality changes very little) | Same |
| Insects | Do not go for it | **Rarely go for the sugar itself**, but ants get into opened bags |

- Under the **Food Labelling Standards (食品表示基準)**, **foods whose quality changes very little — salt, sugar, ice cream, chewing gum and the like — may omit the date label.**
- **Highly refined salt (table salt, 食塩) stays free-flowing more easily; coarse salt and sun-dried salt (粗塩・天日塩) contain more minerals and moisture and clump more readily.**
- **Jōhakutō (上白糖) is a sugar particular to Japan**: **it is coated with invert-sugar syrup ("bisco") to keep it moist**, so **it clumps when it dries out**. **Granulated sugar (グラニュー糖) clumps far less.**
- **A household trick for keeping salt loose**: **drop in a few grains of toasted rice** (they absorb moisture) — shared among people living in Japan.

> **[Verify]** Check the exemption from date labelling in the Consumer Affairs Agency's Food Labelling Standards. The properties of jōhakutō follow materials from the Japan Sugar Refiners' Association.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Clumped salt and clumped sugar are loosened the same way

**Dry the salt, dampen the sugar** — the opposite of each other. **Sugar heated in the microwave melts into caramel**, so do not try it.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sugar keeps longer and insect-free in the fridge

**The fridge is dry, and the sugar clumps.** **An airtight container at room temperature** is the basic rule. **If ants worry you, an airtight seal** is enough.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Salt and sugar have a best-before date too

**There is no labelling obligation, and most products carry no date.** As long as there is **no damp, no absorbed smell and nothing foreign in it**, they can be used for years.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Clumped salt has lost quality

**It is a moisture problem; the composition has not changed.** Dry it and it comes back. **The same goes for sugar.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Next to the hob is the convenient place

**Steam and heat make salt clump and sugar melt or clump.** Keep them **in a drawer away from the hob**, with **only the amount you use in a small container**.
