# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about kitchen-cloth and paper-towel hygiene?｜Japan Questions #314

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/kitchen-cloths-and-paper-towels` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The kitchen cloth — fukin (布巾) — has been used in Japanese homes for generations, yet it is one of the most bacteria-laden things in the whole kitchen. More and more households are switching to paper towels. Here is where the hygiene line sits for both, with the Japanese labels to look for.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a fukin is clean once you rinse it in water." A damp cloth has every condition bacteria need to multiply, and rinsing alone does not reduce them. The basics are: separate cloths by job (dishes, worktop, hands); wash with detergent after use and hang to dry; boil or bleach a few times a week; and if you cannot dry them, use paper towels.

## What to go on

| Every time | **Wash with detergent, wring hard and hang up** (spread out somewhere airy) | Same |
| A few times a week | **Boil (5 minutes), or chlorine bleach (label concentration, a few minutes) then rinse**; **the microwave (wet, 1–2 minutes)** is also given as a method | Same, plus general household-hygiene guidance |
| Replace | **When it smells, darkens or wears thin.** **Monthly to every few months** | — |
| Paper towels | **Disposable, so hygienic.** Suited to **raw meat and fish juices, oil and spills** | General food-hygiene guidance |

- **Surveys by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and others** report cases where **kitchen cloths and sponges carried more bacteria than the chopping board or the sink**.
- **Paper towels are household goods, not "utensils" under the Food Sanitation Act**, but **for uses that touch food directly (as a drop-lid on a simmering pot, in the microwave)** choose a product **labelled for food use (食品用)**. **Products made only from recycled paper may not be food-grade.**
- **Boil only cotton or linen cloths** (synthetics melt). **Never mix chlorine bleach with other cleaning products.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Rinsing a cloth in water is enough

**Water does not reduce bacteria.** **Wash with detergent and dry**, and **boil or bleach regularly**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜One cloth does the worktop and the dishes

**It carries worktop dirt (raw-meat juice, oil) onto the dishes.** **Separate by job.** **The towel you dry your hands on is separate too.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Switch to paper towels and everything is safe

**They win on hygiene**, but **check for the "food use" label if you use them in the microwave or as a drop-lid**. **Leave a wet paper towel lying around for hours** and you are back where you started.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Heating a cloth in the microwave sterilises it

**A wet cotton cloth for 1–2 minutes** is one method, but **a dry cloth or a synthetic one can catch fire**. Only under the conditions **cotton, wet, briefly**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Bleach it and it lasts for months

**When the fibres are worn, the dirt no longer comes out and the smell will not go: replace it.** **About once a month** as a guide. **Old cloths go on cleaning duty.**
