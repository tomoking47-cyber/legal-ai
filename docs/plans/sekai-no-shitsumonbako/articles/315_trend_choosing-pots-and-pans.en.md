# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about choosing pot and pan materials?｜Japan Questions #315

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/choosing-pots-and-pans` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Pans range from "used for decades" to "replaced every year", and the material decides how each one behaves. Questions such as "is aluminium bad for you?" and "does stainless steel stick?" come up from newcomers to Japanese kitchen shops, where aluminium saucepans and induction (IH) hobs are far more common than in many countries. Here is the answer from Japanese regulation and the properties of each material.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "some materials are safe and others are dangerous." Cookware sold in Japan meets the standards of the Food Sanitation Act, so used as labelled there is no built-in safety gap between materials. The real differences are how heat travels, weight, upkeep and which dishes suit — and no single material does everything.

## Comparing materials

| Material | Heat conduction | Good for | Poor at | Upkeep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aluminium** | **Fast, light** | Boiling, soups, simmering (the yukihira-nabe, Japan's everyday spouted saucepan) | Blackens with acid or alkali. Food sticks when frying | Dark marks come off with vinegar or lemon |
| **Stainless steel** | **Slow, uneven** (single layer). **Multi-ply (aluminium core) is even** | Stews, holding heat, stands up to acid | Single layer scorches and sticks easily | Tough, dishwasher-safe |
| **Iron** | High heat retention | Frying, stir-frying, deep-frying | Rust, heavy, long cooking of acidic food | Seasoning with oil, drying (see our iron frying-pan article) |
| **Enamel (iron with a glass coating)** | Holds heat | Stews, acidic dishes (tomato), jam | Rusts if chipped, cracks if cooled suddenly, **not for the microwave** | Wash gently |
| **Copper** | **Fastest** | Jam, egg dishes, deep-frying | Expensive, verdigris, needs re-lining when the tin inside wears off | Polishing |
| **Non-stick (fluoropolymer coating)** | Depends on the base metal | Frying, low-oil cooking | **Breaks down when heated empty (above 260°C)**, metal utensils, high heat | Soft sponge; lasts 1–3 years |
| **Earthenware (donabe) and heat-resistant glass** | Slow, holds heat | Hotpot, rice | Sudden cooling, heating empty | See our donabe article |

- Products on sale meet the **Food Sanitation Act standards for utensils, containers and packaging** (which include metal leaching tests). **The Food Safety Commission of Japan has assessed that using aluminium pans poses no problem under normal use** (most aluminium intake comes from food itself).
- **Fluoropolymer (PTFE) non-stick coatings** **start to break down above 260°C and give off harmful gases above 350°C**, so **do not heat the pan empty or preheat on high** — the warning from the Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE (Japan's product-safety agency). **PFOA (a processing aid) was phased out by the major Japanese makers in the 2010s.**
- **Induction (IH) compatibility** depends on the material: **iron, magnetic stainless steel, and aluminium marked IH-compatible (with a steel plate in the base)** work; **plain aluminium, copper and donabe (unless marked compatible)** do not.

> **[Verify]** Check the aluminium assessment against Food Safety Commission of Japan materials, and the fluoropolymer temperatures against Consumer Affairs Agency, NITE and manufacturer materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Aluminium pans are bad for you

**Very little leaches out in normal cooking**, and **the Food Safety Commission considers health effects unlikely**. The advice not to leave **strongly acidic dishes sitting in the pan for long** is about **discolouration and taste**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Stainless steel does not stick

**Single-layer stainless heats unevenly and scorches easily.** Choose **multi-ply (with an aluminium core)**, and **preheat well and let the oil coat the surface**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Non-stick pans are fine at high heat

**Heating empty or preheating on high takes the pan past 260°C, and the coating degrades and breaks down.** **Medium heat or lower, put the food in before heating, no metal utensils.** **Replace the pan when scratches or peeling show.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜An expensive multi-ply pan does everything

**Multi-ply stainless suits stews, but iron is better for frying and enamel for acidic simmered dishes.** **Owning 2–3 types for different jobs** is the realistic answer.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Any pan works on an induction hob

You need **a material a magnet sticks to (iron, magnetic stainless) or an "IH-compatible" label**. **Many aluminium, copper, heat-resistant glass and donabe pans are not compatible.** **A pan with a warped base will not respond either.**
