# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about heat levels for stir-frying?｜Japan Questions #317

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/stir-fry-heat` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Stir-fry fast on high heat" is a rule that spread from Chinese restaurant cooking, and a home hob with a frying pan is a different matter. Japanese gas hobs also have a safety sensor that turns the flame down on its own, which puzzles many newcomers. Here we look at the output of a household hob, the heat limits of non-stick coatings, and what makes oil spit.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "at home too, the right way is to blast it on high heat." A professional Chinese wok burner has several times the output of a domestic hob; trying the same thing on a home hob's high setting degrades the non-stick coating, sends the oil smoking, and leaves the outside burnt and the inside raw. At home, the conditions for a crisp result are: preheat properly on medium, do not overfill the pan, and drain the ingredients.

## In numbers

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Domestic gas hob output | **About 2,000–4,200 kcal/h** (high-output burner). A commercial Chinese wok burner is **10,000–30,000 kcal/h** |
| Induction (IH) hob | **Maximum 2,000–3,000 W**. Heats only the pan base; **the sides do not get hot** |
| Non-stick coating heat limit | **Starts to degrade at about 260°C**; reached when heated empty or preheated on high |
| Smoke point of cooking oil | **About 230–250°C** (refined oils) |
| Ideal stir-fry temperature | **170–200°C** (just before the oil starts to smoke lightly) |
| Amount of food | **Up to 1/2–2/3 of the pan base.** **More and the temperature drops, water comes out and the food stews** |

- **Si-sensor hobs (fitted to Japanese gas hobs since 2008)** **automatically turn the flame down at about 250°C**, so **"the flame weakens even though I set it to high" is the sensor at work**. Some models have a **high-temperature stir-fry mode** that overrides it for a while, but **this is not for non-stick pans**.
- **Iron frying pans** withstand high heat and **suit high-heat stir-frying** (see our iron frying-pan article). **Non-stick: medium heat at most.**
- **The main cause of spitting oil is water.** **Drain vegetables; thaw frozen ingredients or knock off the frost.**

> **[Verify]** Check the output figures against hob manufacturers and the Japan Gas and Kerosene Appliances Industry Association (JGKA), and Si-sensor behaviour against Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The higher the heat, the crisper the result

**With a domestic hob, "a proper preheat and small batches" does more than high heat.** Overfilling the pan **drops the temperature and draws out water** — the main cause of a soggy stir-fry.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Preheat a non-stick pan on high

**Heating it empty on high passes 260°C and degrades the coating.** **Preheat on medium, add the oil, then heat.** **If it smokes, it is too hot.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The hob turning itself down is a fault

**The Si sensor has triggered at about 250°C.** It is also **a sign that the oil is too hot**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Induction is too weak for stir-frying

**Induction is about 90% thermally efficient against about 40–55% for gas, and the pan base gets plenty hot.** **Lifting the pan stops the heating**, so **stir with the pan sitting on the hob** — that is the induction method. Use **a flat-based IH-compatible pan**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜More oil stops food sticking

**Sticking comes from too little heat and too much water.** **About a tablespoon of oil, and preheat well.** **With an iron pan, do abura-gaeshi (油返し — swirl hot oil around the pan, then pour it off)** (see our iron frying-pan article).
