# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about using and cleaning the fish grill?｜Japan Questions #318

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/fish-grill` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The fish grill built into a Japanese gas hob is the fitting that makes visitors ask, "What is this drawer for?" It is a small broiler that sits under the burners, and most rental kitchens have one. Here is how to use it, the cleaning method everyone dreads, and the fire warnings that come with it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the fish grill is only for fish, and cleaning is such a chore that it is better left unused." The grill is a high-heat direct-flame oven that does toast, vegetables, chicken and mochi as well as fish. Cleaning becomes easy if you do it straight after use, keep water or a purpose-made sheet in the drip tray, and soak the rack in baking-soda water. Using the grill with oil pooled in the drip tray is a cause of fires.

## Use and care

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Types | **Water type** (fill the drip tray with water; cuts spitting and smoke) / **Waterless type** (no water needed; easier to clean, but the oil has to be dealt with) |
| Temperature | **About 300–400°C on double-sided models.** **Preheat 1–2 minutes** |
| Beyond fish | **Toast (1–2 minutes), vegetables (green peppers, aubergine), chicken, mochi (rice cake), aburaage (fried tofu), grilled rice balls (yaki-onigiri)** |
| Cleaning (drip tray) | **Straight after use, once cool.** **Water type: pour off the water and wash with detergent** before the oil sets. **Lining with aluminium foil or a purpose-made grill sheet** makes it easier (**on a water-type grill, check the manual allows it**) |
| Cleaning (rack) | **Soak 30 minutes in baking-soda water (重曹, jūsō; 40°C / 104°F, 2 tablespoons per litre), then sponge.** **Baking-soda paste for burnt-on bits** |
| Cleaning (interior and door) | Monthly, with neutral detergent. **For fish smell, grill used tea leaves or coffee grounds in the tray after use** — a household trick |

- **Grill fires**: the Tokyo Fire Department reports **oil pooled in the drip tray catching fire** every year, and warns: **"do not use the grill with oil pooled in the tray", "use the specified amount of water in a water-type grill", "do not leave the grill unattended"**.
- **Grills on Si-sensor hobs have overheat protection and an auto-off timer** (most **switch off automatically after 15–20 minutes**).
- **Grill smell** comes mainly from **oil in the tray and burnt residue on the rack**. **Cleaning straight after use** works best.

> **[Verify]** Check fire cases against Tokyo Fire Department fire statistics, and grill safety features against JGKA and manufacturer materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The grill is for fish only

**It is a direct-flame oven above 300°C**: **toast in 1–2 minutes**, and good for vegetables, chicken and mochi. Many people in Japan **skip the toaster and use the grill instead**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Clean it once a month in one go

**Oil sets as it cools, and pooled oil causes fires.** **Straight after use, once cool** is the only easy way. **Line the tray with a sheet or foil** (check the manual).

## Misunderstanding 3｜The water in the tray can be skipped

**A water-type grill is designed so the water suppresses spitting and smoke and prevents fire.** **Always use the specified amount.** **Never add water to a waterless grill** (it causes faults).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Mask the smell with a deodoriser

**The source is oil and burnt residue.** **Clean first.** **Grilling tea leaves or coffee grounds** is the finishing touch after cleaning.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Foil can line any grill

**Foil in the drip tray can stop the temperature sensor working correctly and let trapped oil residue smoke or catch fire**, and **blocking the air inlet or outlet can cause incomplete combustion (carbon monoxide) or overheating**; **some models forbid it**. **Check the instruction manual.** **If you line the rack with foil, punch holes so the oil drains.**
