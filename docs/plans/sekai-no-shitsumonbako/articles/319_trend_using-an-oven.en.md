# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about using an oven in Japan?｜Japan Questions #319

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/using-an-oven` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Most home ovens in Japan are combination microwave-ovens (ōbun renji, オーブンレンジ), which differ from the large ovens common abroad in capacity, temperature and how fast they preheat. If a recipe from home comes out wrong here, this is usually why. Here is what changes the result and what to do about it.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "follow the recipe's temperature and time and it bakes the same." Depending on the model, the actual temperature inside can be 10–30°C off the display, and skipping the preheat, the shelf position and opening the door all change the result a lot. The basics: always preheat, and give it 5–10 minutes beyond the signal; check once with an oven thermometer; turn the tray partway through; judge by colour and a skewer.

## What to go on

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Preheating | **Always. After the oven signals ready, wait a further 5–10 minutes** so the whole cavity settles (combination models have thin walls and lose heat quickly) |
| Temperature error | **Some models run 10–30°C off the display.** **Check once with an oven thermometer (a few hundred yen)** |
| Tray | **A dark tray browns more**, **a light tray less**. **Upper shelf for colour, lower shelf for slow, thorough baking** |
| Door | **Each opening drops the cavity temperature by tens of degrees.** **Check as little as possible, and quickly** |
| Fan (convection) mode | Set **10–20°C below the recipe** as a guide (the heat hits harder) |
| Combination microwave-oven | **After oven use the cavity is hot and the microwave function is unavailable for a while.** **Small cavity, one tray** |
| Safety | **The door glass is hot during and just after use** (in National Consumer Affairs Center tests, the centre of a hob grill door window reached **about 150°C at most**, and stayed **above 50°C for about 15 minutes after use**). **Beware of child burns** (National Consumer Affairs Center); **containers that are not heat-resistant (ordinary glass, plastic) must not go in** |

- Japanese **combination microwave-ovens** mostly have **20–30 L cavities** — **smaller, with wider temperature swings**, than the **60–70 L built-in ovens** common abroad.
- **Baking paper (kukkingu shīto, クッキングシート; rated to about 250°C)** **burns above its rated temperature**, so **do not use it for recipes at 250°C or above**, and **do not let it touch the heating element**.
- **Aluminium foil: not in microwave mode, fine in oven mode** (take care when switching modes on a combination model).

> **[Verify]** Follow your manufacturer's instruction manual for capacity and temperature behaviour. Check burn accidents against Consumer Affairs Agency materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Preheating can be skipped

**Without preheating, dough spreads and fails to rise, or the inside dries out before the outside browns.** **Waiting a few more minutes after the signal** is the reality with a Japanese home oven.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The display temperature is accurate

**A 10–30°C error, depending on the model, is well known.** **Check once with an oven thermometer** and learn your own oven's "correction figure".

## Misunderstanding 3｜Open the door often to check

**Every opening drops the temperature, causing uneven baking and a poor rise.** **Look through the window; open once, near the end of the baking time.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Any glass dish can go in the oven

**Glass, plastic and some ceramics without a heat-resistance label crack.** **Check for an "oven safe" (オーブン使用可) label.** **Donabe and enamel are mostly fine** (check the label).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Overseas recipes bake the same here

**A built-in oven abroad and a Japanese combination oven differ in cavity size and heat stability.** **Check with a thermometer, adjust the tray's position and orientation, judge by colour** — the practice shared in Japanese households.
