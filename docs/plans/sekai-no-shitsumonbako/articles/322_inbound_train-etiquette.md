# Q. What do people get wrong about train etiquette in Japan?｜Japan Questions #322

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/train-etiquette` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese trains carry tens of millions of people a day in near silence, and visitors sense a rulebook they cannot see. Most of it reduces to a few habits about sound, space and the order of boarding — plus a couple of formal rules about seats and cars.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the rules are about being quiet. They are about not imposing on others in a space where nobody can leave: phones silent and calls not taken, backpacks worn on the front or in the rack, priority seats given up, eating avoided on commuter lines, and lines on the platform respected. None of it is law except the ban on smoking; even women-only cars rest on voluntary cooperation rather than a legal ban, though operators expect men to move.

## The conventions

| Situation | Norm |
|---|---|
| **Phone** | **Silent mode; no voice calls**. Texting and video with earphones are fine. Near priority seats, older signage asks to switch off (now relaxed to "set to silent" on most lines) |
| **Talking** | Low voices; groups of tourists at normal volume are the most common complaint |
| **Backpacks** | **On the front, in the overhead rack, or between the feet** on crowded trains |
| **Boarding** | Stand in the **painted lines**, **let passengers off first**, board without pushing |
| **Priority seats (優先席)** | For the elderly, pregnant, disabled, those with infants; **give them up**, and do not sit in them when the car is crowded |
| **Women-only cars (女性専用車)** | Marked pink; **weekday mornings** (and some evenings) on many urban lines; men board by mistake and are asked to move |
| **Eating** | **Not on commuter trains**; **yes** on the Shinkansen and reserved limited expresses |
| **Luggage** | Large cases: rack, behind the last seat, or forwarded (see the luggage article) |
| **Rush hour** | **7:30–9:00** in Tokyo; avoid with luggage or children if you can |

- The **Yamanote Line** carries **over 3 million passengers a day**; **Shinjuku Station roughly 2.7–3.5 million** across its five operators, the world's busiest (Guinness). Density is why the norms exist.
- **Smoking is banned** on all trains and platforms; **drinking alcohol** is legal but frowned on except on long-distance trains.
- Operators run **"manner" posters** each season; the current top complaints are **noise from phones and earphones, backpacks, and blocking the doors**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Silence is the rule

**Quiet is the norm, not silence.** Couples chat, children talk. What draws looks is **volume that fills the car** and **voice calls**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Priority seats are first-come

They are **for people who need them**, and giving one up when someone boards is expected. On a near-empty train, sitting there is tolerated; stand when it fills.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any car is fine in the morning

**Women-only cars** run on many lines in the morning rush; the car and platform are marked pink. Men who board by mistake are politely asked to move at the next stop.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The doors will wait

Doors close on a schedule; **running for a closing door** is the cause of most platform incidents and is what the safety announcements are about. There is another train in 3 minutes.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Everyone is asleep, so it is relaxed

Sleeping on trains is normal; **taking up two seats, spreading luggage, or a loud phone** while others try to rest is what breaks the unspoken deal.
