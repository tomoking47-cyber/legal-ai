# Q. What do people get wrong about taxis in Japan?｜Japan Questions #323

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/taxis-in-japan` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese taxis are clean, honest and expensive by Asian standards, and they have quirks — the door that opens itself, the driver who may not know your hotel, the late-night surcharge — that repeatedly surprise visitors. Ride-hailing exists, but differently.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that taxis are a last resort. For groups of three or four over short distances, a taxi is often cheaper than four train fares, and it is the practical option late at night when trains stop around midnight. Do not touch the rear door (it is automatic), have the destination in Japanese, and expect to pay by card or IC in most city cabs.

## The facts

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Fares (Tokyo, 2025) | **¥500 for the first 1.096 km**, then **¥100 per 255 m**; **+20% from 22:00 to 05:00** |
| Payment | **Cash, credit card, IC card, QR** in almost all Tokyo cabs; rural areas cash-heavy |
| Doors | **Rear left door opens and closes automatically** — driver-operated. Do not pull or slam |
| Hailing | Street hail (red light 空車 = vacant), taxi stands, or apps (**GO, S.RIDE, Uber — which dispatches licensed taxis**) |
| Tipping | **Not practised** |
| Destination | Show the **address in Japanese or a map pin**; hotel names in English are often not recognised |
| Ride-hailing | **Private-car ride-sharing was limited until 2024**; "Japan-style ride-share" now allows taxi companies to use private drivers **in limited areas and hours** |
| Trains stop | **Roughly 00:00–00:30**; taxis are the way home after |

- Taxi fares are **regulated by region**; Tokyo's 2022 revision set the current base. **Toll roads are added** to the meter.
- **Drivers are licensed** (Class 2 licence) and cabs display the **company and driver ID**; overcharging is rare, and **lost property is reliably recovered** via the company.
- **Uber in Japan dispatches licensed taxis** at metered fares in most cities; it is an app for hailing, not a discount.

> **[Verify]** Fare figures with the Tokyo Hire-Taxi Association and MLIT fare notices; ride-share rules with MLIT's 2024 announcements.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Taxis are always the expensive option

For **3–4 people on a 2–3 km trip**, the fare (**¥1,000–1,500**) is comparable to train tickets and door to door. Late at night they are the only option.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Open the door yourself

The **rear left door is automatic**; the driver opens and closes it. Grabbing it can damage the mechanism and irritates drivers more than anything else.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The driver will know your hotel

Drivers know **districts and landmarks**, not every hotel name in English. **Show the address in Japanese** or a map pin; the hotel card from reception is ideal.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Uber is cheaper than a taxi

In Japan, **Uber dispatches regular taxis at regular fares** in most cities. Use it for the **hailing convenience and card payment**, not for savings.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You should tip

**No.** Round-up is sometimes refused. Say "arigatō gozaimashita" and take your receipt (レシート) — useful if you leave something behind.
