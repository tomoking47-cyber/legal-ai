# Q. What do people get wrong about smoking rules in Japan?｜Japan Questions #326

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/smoking-rules` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan's smoking rules have inverted in a decade: it was once easy to smoke in a restaurant and forbidden on the street; now indoor smoking is largely banned and street smoking is restricted by local ordinance. Visitors arrive with the old picture.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japan is smoker-friendly. Since April 2020, smoking is banned inside most restaurants, bars, offices and public buildings (with sealed smoking rooms and some small pre-existing bars as exceptions), and many city centres ban smoking while walking on the street, with fines of ¥1,000–2,000. Smoking happens in designated booths and rooms; heated-tobacco products follow similar rules.

## The rules

| Place | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Restaurants, bars, cafés** | **No smoking indoors** since 1 April 2020, except in a **sealed smoking room** (no food or drink inside) or in **small bars (under 100 m², existing before 2020) that opt out and display a sign** | Health Promotion Act amendment |
| **Offices, hotels' common areas, shops, stations** | No smoking except designated rooms | same |
| **Hospitals, schools, government buildings** | **No smoking on the premises** | same |
| **Hotel rooms** | **Smoking rooms still exist**; book 禁煙 (non-smoking) explicitly | — |
| **Streets** | **Walking while smoking banned** in many central wards and cities (Chiyoda since 2002; most of central Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto, Nagoya) — **fine ¥1,000–2,000** | Local ordinances |
| **Designated smoking areas** | Booths and marked areas near stations; **apps and maps list them** | — |
| **Heated tobacco (IQOS etc.)** | Treated as tobacco; **allowed in some "heated-tobacco rooms" where eating is permitted**; banned where cigarettes are | Health Promotion Act |
| Taxis, trains, planes | **Banned** | — |

- **Smoking rate**: **about 16% of adults** (men 25.6%, women 6.9%) in 2023, the latest survey, down from **~50% of men in 2000** (National Health and Nutrition Survey).
- Tokyo's own **Ordinance to Prevent Passive Smoking** (April 2020) is stricter than the national law for restaurants with employees.
- **Ashtrays on the street are rare**; carry a **portable ashtray** (sold at convenience stores) if you smoke — littering butts is fined in ordinance areas.

> **[Verify]** The 2020 amendment details with MHLW; ward ordinances with each municipality; smoking rates with the latest National Health and Nutrition Survey.

## Misunderstanding 1｜You can smoke in izakaya and bars

**Mostly not since 2020.** Only **small pre-existing bars that opted out** (sign at the door) and **sealed smoking rooms** allow it. Ask before lighting up.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Outdoors is free

**Walking while smoking is banned in most city centres** by ordinance, with on-the-spot fines. Use the **marked smoking areas** — usually near stations.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Heated tobacco is exempt

It is **tobacco under the law**. Some venues have **heated-tobacco-only rooms** where you can also eat; otherwise it follows cigarette rules.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hotels are all non-smoking now

**Smoking rooms persist**, especially in business hotels. **Request non-smoking (禁煙) when booking**; a smoking room's odour is hard to miss.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Nobody smokes any more

**About one in six adults** does; smokers have moved to **booths, rooms and the balcony**. The infrastructure exists — it is just not where visitors expect.
