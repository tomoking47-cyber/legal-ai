# Q. What do people get wrong about temple and shrine etiquette?｜Japan Questions #327

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/temple-and-shrine-etiquette` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Visitors spend more time at shrines and temples than anywhere else in Japan and worry most about doing something wrong there. The good news: the sites are used to visitors, the rules are few, and the biggest mistake is not knowing which of the two you are in.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that shrines and temples are the same kind of place with the same rituals. Shrines are Shinto (torii gate, purification basin, clap twice); temples are Buddhist (gate with statues, incense, no clapping). Both expect quiet, no food, hats off in halls, and photography only where allowed. Everything else — the coin, the bow, the omamori — is optional and forgiven.

## Shrine vs temple

| | **Shrine (神社, jinja)** | **Temple (寺, tera / -ji)** |
|---|---|---|
| Religion | Shinto | Buddhist |
| Entrance | **Torii** gate (two posts, two crossbars) — bow once, walk to the side of the path | **Sanmon** gate, often with guardian statues |
| Purification | **Temizuya basin**: ladle in right hand, rinse left, switch, rinse right, pour into left palm to rinse mouth (spit beside the basin), rinse ladle handle | Same basin at some temples; **incense** at the burner — waft smoke toward yourself |
| Prayer | **Coin → bow twice → clap twice → pray → bow once** (二拝二拍手一拝) | **Coin → bow, hands together, no clapping** |
| Coin | Any; **¥5 (go-en, "good connection")** is traditional | Same |
| Photography | Grounds usually fine; **not inside the main hall**, not of worshippers; **some areas signed 撮影禁止** | Same; **many temple interiors and statues banned** |
| Extras | Omamori charms, ema plaques, omikuji fortunes (tie a bad one to the rack) | Same, plus goshuin stamp books (**¥300–500**) |

- Japan has about **80,000 shrines and 77,000 temples** (Agency for Cultural Affairs, Religious Yearbook).
- **Goshuin** (calligraphy stamps) are religious records, not souvenirs; use a **dedicated book (goshuin-chō)**, not a notebook.
- **Kyoto's Gion** banned **photography on private lanes in 2019** and, from **April 2024, barred tourists from entering the private alleys altogether** after harassment of geiko; signs cite a **¥10,000** fine.

> **[Verify]** Site counts with the Agency for Cultural Affairs Religious Yearbook; Gion rules with the local council.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Clap at temples

**Clapping is Shinto.** At a temple, **hands together silently**. Getting it backwards is the most common visible error and nobody will say anything.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Skip the basin — it is ceremonial

It is **the etiquette that marks a visitor as respectful**, takes 20 seconds, and no one cares if you do it imperfectly. **Do not drink from the ladle.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Walk down the middle of the path

The centre of the approach is **for the deity**; visitors walk **to one side**. A small thing; residents do it automatically.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Photograph everything

**Grounds, yes; interiors and statues often not; people praying, never.** Look for the sign. Drones are banned at nearly all sites.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The coin has to be big

**¥5 is the tradition** for its pun on "connection"; the amount is irrelevant. Throwing coins hard, or from a distance, is what looks bad.
