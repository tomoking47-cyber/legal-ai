# Q. What do people get wrong about staying at a ryokan?｜Japan Questions #328

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/staying-at-a-ryokan` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A night at a traditional inn is on most itineraries and generates more anxious questions than any other part of a trip: what to wear, when to bathe, why dinner is at 18:00, whether to tip. A ryokan is a schedule and a set of rooms used differently through the evening, and knowing that removes the anxiety.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a ryokan is a hotel with tatami. It is closer to a hosted evening: check-in around 15:00, bath before dinner, dinner served in your room or a dining room at a fixed early hour while your room is converted from sitting room to bedroom, breakfast at a fixed hour, check-out at 10:00. The price is per person and includes two meals. The yukata is your outfit for all of it.

## The evening

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| **15:00–16:00** | Check-in; tea and a sweet in your room; **shoes off at the genkan**, slippers in corridors, **socks or bare feet on tatami** |
| **Before dinner** | **Bathe** in the communal bath (see the onsen article); change into the **yukata** provided — left side over right, obi at the hip |
| **18:00–19:00** | **Dinner** (kaiseki, many small courses) in the room or a dining room; **fixed time chosen at check-in**; sake and beer charged extra |
| During dinner or after | Staff **lay the futon** — the sitting room becomes the bedroom |
| Evening | Second bath is normal; some baths switch gender by time — check the sign |
| **07:30–08:30** | **Breakfast** at a fixed time (grilled fish, rice, miso soup, pickles; Western option at many) |
| **10:00** | Check-out |

- **Pricing is per person, including dinner and breakfast** — typically **¥15,000–40,000 per person**, higher at famous onsen. A room for two is priced as two people.
- **Yukata** may be worn to dinner, in corridors, to the bath and, at onsen towns, **in the street**. Wear the **tanzen** (padded jacket) over it in winter.
- **Tipping is not expected**; a small gift (kokorozuke, in an envelope) to the room attendant is a fading custom at high-end inns and entirely optional.
- Dietary needs (**allergies, no fish, halal**) must be **communicated when booking**; kaiseki menus are fixed and prepared in advance.
- **Late arrival** after the dinner hour means **no dinner and often no refund**; call if delayed.

> **[Verify]** Price ranges are typical; conventions with the Japan Ryokan and Hotel Association.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Dinner at 18:00 is negotiable

**The kitchen cooks for the whole house at once**; you choose 18:00 or 19:00 at check-in. Arriving late forfeits the meal.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Yukata is a bathrobe for the room

It is **your outfit for the whole stay** — dinner, corridors, the bath, the street in onsen towns. **Left over right** (right over left is for the dead).

## Misunderstanding 3｜You can order from a menu

**Kaiseki is a set course**; there is no menu. State **allergies and restrictions at booking**, not at the table.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Someone will disturb you laying the futon

Staff lay it **while you are at dinner or the bath**; if you dine in-room, they do it right after, taking a few minutes. It is part of the rhythm, not an intrusion.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Tip the attendant

**Not expected.** A sincere "gochisōsama deshita" after dinner and "osewa ni narimashita" at check-out is the currency.
