# Q. What do people get wrong about home-made miso and kōji?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #336

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/homemade-miso-and-koji` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Making miso at home has revived in Japan since the 2010s — "miso-making workshops" fill every January. Abroad it reads as advanced fermentation. It is soybeans, salt and kōji left alone for six months; the skill is patience and keeping air off the surface.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that miso is hard to make. The ingredients are three, the active work is one afternoon, and the result needs six to twelve months of being ignored in a cool place. The one variable people get wrong is salt: too little and it spoils, too much and it is inedible. Kōji — rice inoculated with the Aspergillus oryzae mould — is bought ready-made and does the work.

## The recipe and the numbers

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ingredients (a typical batch) | **Soybeans 1 kg (dry)**, **rice kōji 1 kg**, **salt 400–450 g** — final salt about **11–12%** |
| Day 1 | Soak beans 18 h → boil 3–4 h until crushable → mash → mix with kōji and salt → pack into a container **pressing out air** → salt on the surface → weight on top |
| Container | Enamel, food-grade plastic, or ceramic; **never metal** in contact (salt corrodes) |
| Where | **Cool, dark, stable** (a closet, under the stairs); **not the fridge** (too slow) |
| Wait | **6 months** (light, sweet) to **12 months** (darker, deeper); start in **January–February** so summer's heat does the work |
| Surface | **Tamari (dark liquid)** rising is normal; **white film** is yeast — remove; **coloured mould** — remove with margin |
| Yield | About **4 kg** of miso |
| Salt in finished miso | **10–13%** (commercial white miso is 5–7%, red 11–13%) |

- **Kōji (麹)** is rice (or barley or soy) cultured with **Aspergillus oryzae**, designated Japan's "national fungus" by the Brewing Society of Japan. Its enzymes break starch into sugar and protein into amino acids — the source of miso's sweetness and umami. It is sold **fresh or dried** at supermarkets and online.
- **Shio-kōji (salt kōji)** — kōji, salt and water fermented a week — is the entry-level product: a marinade and seasoning that tenderises meat and fish. Ratio **kōji 200 g : salt 60 g : water 300 mL**, stirred daily for **7–10 days**.
- **Amazake** (sweet kōji drink) is kōji and rice at **55–60°C for 8–10 hours** — a rice cooker on "keep warm" with the lid open is the household method.
- **Safety**: the **salt content and the anaerobic packing** are the preservation system. Low-salt experiments spoil; **under 10% is not recommended** for room-temperature ageing.

> **[Verify]** Ratios are traditional ranges published by miso associations; commercial miso salt ranges with the National Federation of Miso Manufacturers Cooperatives.

## Misunderstanding 1｜You need to culture the mould yourself

**Kōji is bought.** Home kōji-making is a separate, fussier hobby (35°C, 48 hours, precise humidity). Miso-making starts from purchased kōji.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Less salt is healthier

**Salt is the preservative**; below about 10% the batch spoils at room temperature. Reduce salt in the **soup**, not in the miso.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Mould on top means failure

**Some surface yeast or mould is normal** after months; scrape off with a margin. What is underneath, sealed from air, is fine. Pack tightly and salt the surface to minimise it.

## Misunderstanding 4｜It must be kept cold

**Room temperature ageing is the point**; fermentation needs summer warmth. The fridge is for **after** it is done, to stop it darkening further.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Kōji is only for miso

**Shio-kōji** as a marinade and **amazake** as a drink are the quick wins, made in a week and a night respectively — which is why kōji sits in the refrigerated aisle of every supermarket.
