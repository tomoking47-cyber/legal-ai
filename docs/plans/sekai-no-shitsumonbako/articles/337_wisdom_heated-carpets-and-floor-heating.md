# Q. What do people get wrong about heated carpets and floor heating in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #337

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/heated-carpets-and-floor-heating` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan heats the floor rather than the air more than most countries — with hot-water floor heating in new homes, and electric heated carpets and rugs in older ones. To visitors it seems a luxury; it follows from sitting and sleeping close to the floor.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that floor heating is an indulgence. In a country where people sit on the floor, eat at low tables and sleep on futon, warming the floor is the efficient way to warm the person. Hot-water floor heating is standard in new houses; the electric heated carpet (hot carpet) is the cheap retrofit. Both come with a specific hazard — low-temperature burns from lying on them too long.

## The options

| Type | How | Cost to run | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hot-water floor heating (yuka-danbō)** | Pipes under the floor, heated by a gas or heat-pump unit | **¥50–150 per day** for a living room, depending on fuel | Standard in new detached houses; slow to warm (1 h), even heat, silent |
| **Electric floor heating** | Heating film or cable under flooring | Higher than hot-water for large areas | Retrofit in flats |
| **Hot carpet (electric heated rug)** | A rug with heating wires; **2 or 3 zones** switchable | **¥6–23 per hour** at full power (200–740 W); zones save power | The cheap option; put a **rug on top** to retain heat |
| **Heated seat cushions / small mats** | Personal | ¥1–3 per hour | Desk work |
| **Kotatsu** | See that article | Very low | Heats under a table only |

- The **Ministry of the Environment's Warm Biz** guidance emphasises **warming feet and the lower body**; floor heating aligns with this — comfort at a **room temperature 2–3°C lower** than with air heating.
- **Low-temperature burns**: a hot carpet surface sits at **40–45°C**; **sleeping on it or sitting motionless for hours** can burn skin (see the hot-water-bottle article for the time-temperature table). NITE and the Consumer Affairs Agency issue winter warnings, especially for **the elderly, infants, and anyone who has drunk alcohol**.
- **Furniture** on a hot carpet traps heat — manufacturers list **do not place heavy furniture, futon or cushions** over the heating area for long periods.
- **Hot-water floor heating** is compatible with **tatami** (special thin tatami) and **wood**; some flooring warps with electric mats — check the flooring specification.

> **[Verify]** Running costs with the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy's household energy guides; burn warnings with NITE and the Consumer Affairs Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Heating the floor is a luxury

It is the **logical way to heat a room where people live at floor level**. Air heating at 20°C leaves the floor at 15°C; floor heating warms where you sit.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Hot carpets are safe to sleep on

They are **the classic low-temperature burn**: 40–45°C for hours. **Never sleep on one; do not let a child nap on one; switch off or use the timer.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Turn it to maximum for quick warmth

**Floor heating warms slowly** (an hour for hot-water systems); hot carpets warm in minutes at medium. **Timers** are the tool: hot-water systems on 30 minutes before you get up.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Any rug on top ruins it

A **thin rug on a hot carpet** improves comfort and reduces power use by holding heat. **Thick futon or heavy furniture** on the heating area is what the manuals forbid.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Floor heating alone heats the home

It heats **the room it is in**, and the person on it. Japanese homes still heat **by room**, not centrally; the corridor and bathroom stay cold, hence slippers and the bathroom heater (see the bath-safety article).
