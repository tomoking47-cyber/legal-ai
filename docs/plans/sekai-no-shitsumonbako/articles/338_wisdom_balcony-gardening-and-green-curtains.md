# Q. What do people get wrong about balcony gardening and green curtains in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #338

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/balcony-gardening-and-green-curtains` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese balconies grow things — herbs, tomatoes, and every summer a wall of bitter gourd or morning glory trained up a net over the window. The "green curtain" is a government-promoted cooling measure, not a hobby, and the balcony rules that govern it are strict.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the plants on Japanese balconies are decoration. The green curtain of climbing vines is a shading and evaporative-cooling device promoted by the Ministry of the Environment and municipalities, which hand out seeds; it lowers the window's surface temperature by several degrees. Growing on a balcony also means obeying condominium rules: nothing on the rail, drains clear, nothing that blocks the emergency partition.

## The green curtain

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Plants | **Gōya (bitter gourd)**, **morning glory (asagao)**, **cucumber**, **passionfruit**, **loofah (hechima)** — fast annual climbers |
| Set-up | **Net (10 cm mesh) fixed from the balcony rail or planter to a hook above the window**; planters **30–40 L** with 2–3 plants per metre; sow **May**, cover the window by **July** |
| Effect | Municipal measurements: **window surface 5–10°C cooler**, indoor **1–3°C cooler**, with the cooling of transpiration added to shade | 
| Water | **Daily, morning**; a **30 L planter dries in a day in August** |
| End | Cut down in **October**; **seeds saved**; the net stored |
| Promotion | The **Ministry of the Environment's "Green Curtain" campaign** and many cities (Itabashi, Yokohama, Kyoto) distribute gōya seeds and run contests |

## Balcony rules (typical condominium bylaws)

- The balcony is **common property for exclusive use**: **no fixed structures**, **nothing hung on or over the rail**, **the emergency partition and hatch must stay clear**, **drains must not be blocked** (soil and leaves cause leaks into the flat below).
- **Weight**: balconies are designed for about **180 kg/m²**; a wet 40 L planter is **~50 kg** — spread them out.
- **Soil disposal**: used soil is **not household waste** in most municipalities — reuse it or use a garden-centre take-back.

> **[Verify]** Temperature effects with municipal green-curtain reports (e.g. Itabashi Ward); load figures with the Building Standards Act; bylaws with your building's management rules.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The vines are decoration

They are a **cooling device**: exterior shade stops solar gain before the glass (see the summer heat article), and the leaves cool the air by evaporation. The aubergine and cucumber harvest is a bonus.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Hang planters on the rail for space

**Almost universally prohibited**: a falling planter is the condominium's nightmare. Planters sit on the floor; nets fix to hooks on the building side or to free-standing frames.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any climber works

**Fast annuals with big leaves**: gōya is the standard because it grows 3 m in two months, resists pests and heat, and is edible. Ivy and perennial climbers damage walls and are banned.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Watering every few days is enough

In August a balcony planter **dries in a day**; the vine wilts by noon. **Morning watering, saucers or self-watering planters**, and mulch.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Dump the old soil in the park

**Illegal dumping.** Soil is not collected as rubbish in most cities; **refresh it with compost and reuse**, or use a garden centre's take-back service.
