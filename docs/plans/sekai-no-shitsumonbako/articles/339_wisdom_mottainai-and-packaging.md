# Q. What do people get wrong about "mottainai" and Japanese packaging?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #339

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/mottainai-and-packaging` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan is known abroad for two contradictory things: a word, mottainai, that means "what a waste", and packaging so elaborate that a single sweet arrives in three layers. Both are real, and the tension between them is where policy has moved in the last decade.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that mottainai is a slogan about recycling. It is an older sense of regret at wasting the value in a thing — food, cloth, time — that shows up as leftovers used, clothes repaired and furoshiki reused. The packaging culture runs on a different logic: wrapping as respect and hygiene. The plastics law of 2022 and the 2020 bag charge are where the two meet: the wrapping is being thinned, the regret is being harnessed.

## The two sides, in numbers

- **Mottainai** in the household: **leftover rice becomes onigiri or fried rice**, vegetable scraps become dashi, old towels become zōkin (see that article), clothes are repaired (see the repair article). It is **an attitude toward value**, older than the environmental movement.
- **Packaging** in Japan grew from **gift culture (wrapping as courtesy)**, **hygiene** (individually wrapped sweets stay clean in humidity), and **retail logistics** (convenience-store items handled by many hands). Individually wrapped cookies in a box in a bag in a paper bag was normal until 2020.
- Since 2020: **bags cost ¥3–5**, **convenience stores ask before giving cutlery**, **hotels moved amenities to lobby bars**, **department stores offer "simple wrapping"**. **Over-packaging complaints** now appear in consumer surveys as a reason to avoid a product.

> **[Verify]** Waste figures with UNEP and the Ministry of the Environment; bag-refusal rates with the MoE's 2020–21 surveys; food loss with MAFF's latest estimate.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Mottainai means recycling

It means **regret at waste** — of food, effort, things. Recycling is one response; **not buying, using up, repairing and repurposing** are the older ones.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Japan is a low-waste society

**Per-capita plastic waste is among the highest in the world.** What Japan does well is **sorting and collection** (see the waste article); what it did badly was **generation**, and that is what the 2020–22 laws target.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The wrapping is pointless

It grew from **courtesy and hygiene**; the individually wrapped sweet stays fresh in a humid summer and can be shared at the office. The excess is real; the reasons were not frivolous.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Nothing has changed

**Bag refusal over 70%, cutlery on request, hotel amenities off the room, thinner gift wrapping.** The change since 2020 is visible at every till.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is a Japanese concept only

Wangari Maathai carried the word to the UN in 2005 as a summary of **reduce, reuse, recycle and respect**. The household habits it names exist in every frugal culture; Japan simply kept the word.
