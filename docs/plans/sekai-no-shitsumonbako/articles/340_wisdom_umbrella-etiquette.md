# Q. What do people get wrong about umbrella etiquette in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #340

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/umbrella-etiquette` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan uses an estimated 120–130 million umbrellas a year, and on a rainy day the country runs on a set of umbrella conventions — where to leave it, how to carry it, what to do with the drip — that a newcomer breaks without noticing.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that an umbrella is a personal item with no social rules. In Japan it becomes public the moment you enter a building: it goes in the stand or a bag at the door, it is never opened indoors or dripped on a train floor, it is carried tip-down and close to the body, and — the hardest rule — you do not borrow one from the stand that is not yours, even on a downpour.

## The conventions

| Situation | What people do |
|---|---|
| **Entering a shop, office, restaurant** | **Umbrella stand at the door** (often with a lock and numbered key at larger shops) or **the plastic sleeve dispenser** — sleeve it before entering |
| **Convenience stores and stations** | Sleeve dispensers or drip-drying racks; **never carry a dripping umbrella through the aisles** |
| **On the train** | **Furled, in its sleeve or wrapped**; tip down; not resting on the seat or a neighbour's leg. **Folding umbrellas go in a pouch** in the bag |
| **Walking** | **Tip down, close to the body**; never swung by the handle or carried horizontally under the arm (the tip is at a child's eye level) |
| **Passing someone under an eave** | Tilt your umbrella **away** from them; the one on the outer side steps into the rain briefly |
| **Sharing** | **Aigasa** (two under one umbrella) is a romantic trope, not a norm; offer a spare rather than a share |
| **Someone else's umbrella** | **Do not take it**, even a ¥500 clear one; umbrella theft from stands is petty crime but widely resented |
| **After** | **Open it to dry at home** (see the umbrella-care article); a wet umbrella furled overnight rusts and smells |

- **Consumption: about 120–130 million umbrellas a year**, of which a large share are **¥500 clear vinyl umbrellas** bought at convenience stores when rain starts — many abandoned; some cities now run **umbrella-sharing schemes** (iKasa, with app unlock at stations, ¥140/day).
- **Umbrella theft** from stands is common enough that people **mark handles with tape** or use **lockable stands**; leaving a good umbrella in an open stand is considered careless rather than trusting.
- **Plastic sleeves** at shop entrances are a waste issue; many chains switched to **drip-drying racks or reusable sleeves** under the 2022 plastics law.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bring the umbrella in with you

**Stand at the door, or sleeve it.** A wet umbrella against the shelves or a restaurant floor is the visitor's most frequent small offence.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Open it inside the doorway

**Open it outside, after stepping out** — opening indoors sprays the entrance and hits people behind you. Step out, then open.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Carry it horizontally under your arm when furled

**The tip points backwards at hip height — a child's face height.** Carry it **vertical, tip down**, against your side.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A cheap clear umbrella in the stand is fair game

**No.** The person who left it will come back for it. If you are caught out, **buy a ¥500 one**; they are at every convenience store, which is why the stands are full of them.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Share your umbrella with the stranger next to you

Offering shelter is kind, but **aigasa is intimate** in Japanese culture; the natural offer is **to lend a spare** or to walk them to the nearest shop. Reading the situation matters more than the rule.
