# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about reading cosmetic ingredient lists?｜Japan Questions #341

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/reading-cosmetic-ingredient-lists` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Since 2001 every cosmetic sold in Japan has had to list all of its ingredients, so packaging carries dozens of names — in Japanese. Where to look, and whether the order means anything, are questions asked in Japan and abroad alike. This article explains the Japanese rules and how to use the list even if you cannot read every word.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "fewer ingredients means safer, more means riskier." The list runs in descending order of amount (ingredients at 1% or below may be in any order), and the first few lines decide what the product is. The number of ingredients has no link to whether it irritates. The practical reason to read the list is to know, and avoid, the ingredients that do not suit you.

## The labelling rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Full ingredient labelling** | Mandatory since **April 2001** (Article 61 of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act, Japan's cosmetics and drugs law). **Every ingredient must be listed** |
| **Order** | **Descending order of amount.** However, **ingredients at 1% or below may appear in any order.** **Colourants are grouped at the end** |
| Ingredient names | **The Japan Cosmetic Industry Association (JCIA) "labelling names for cosmetic ingredients"** (Japanese names based on INCI). Examples: 水 (water), グリセリン (glycerin), BG (butylene glycol), エタノール (ethanol) |
| Quasi-drugs (医薬部外品, a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines) | **Active ingredients are listed first under "有効成分" (active ingredient)**, the rest under "その他の成分" (other ingredients). **Full labelling is a voluntary industry standard** (most makers follow it) |
| Carry-over ingredients | Trace preservatives and similar contained in a raw material **need not be listed** (they are there to stabilise the raw material) |
| "無添加" (additive-free) and "フリー" (-free) | **No legal definition** (see the article on additive-free claims) |

### How to read it

| Where to look | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **First 3–5 lines** | The product's **base** (water, glycerin, BG, ethanol, oils). **If ethanol is near the top, it is a light, refreshing type, and some people feel it sting** |
| **Middle** | Moisturising and skin-conditioning ingredients, thickeners, emulsifiers |
| **1% or below (latter part)** | Fragrance, preservatives (phenoxyethanol, parabens) and **most of the advertised "hero" ingredients (〇〇エキス, "… extract")** |
| **End** | Colourants (赤〇号 "Red No. X", 黄〇号 "Yellow No. X", iron oxides) |

> **[Verify]** Check the legal basis for labelling and its exceptions (order of ingredients at 1% or below, carry-over) against the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare notices (Iyakushin-hatsu No. 163 and others) and JCIA material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A product with many ingredients is risky

**The number of ingredients and irritation are unrelated.** **Knowing one ingredient that does not suit you** is more useful. **Photograph the ingredient list of any product that gave you a rash and take it to a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance)** — it helps identify the cause.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The "contains X extract" in the advert is the main ingredient

Most such ingredients **appear in the latter, 1%-or-below part of the list.** **The claim is true as far as the label goes, but the amount is usually small.** **The top 3–5 lines** are the substance of the product.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The English INCI name and the Japanese label name are different ingredients

They are **the Japanese names for the same ingredients** (e.g. Butylene Glycol = BG, Tocopherol = トコフェロール). **When comparing with products from home, use the JCIA correspondence table.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜"Alcohol-free" means there is no ethanol in it

**In cosmetics "alcohol" usually means ethanol**, but **stearyl alcohol and behenyl alcohol (fatty alcohols, in practice oils)** are different substances and are sometimes outside the scope of a "-free" claim. **Look at where ethanol sits in the list.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Reading the list tells you how well it works

**What you learn is the composition**; **how much effect you get depends on the amounts, the formulation and your skin.** **Reading the list in order to avoid ingredients** is the practical use that dermatology clinics also recommend.
