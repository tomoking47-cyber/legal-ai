# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about applying toner with cotton or with your hands?｜Japan Questions #342

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/cotton-vs-hands-for-toner` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Cotton pad or hands?" is the longest-running argument in Japanese skincare. Outside Japan the question often starts one step earlier: what is a toner (化粧水, keshōsui — the watery lotion applied after washing) for at all? Both methods have a case, and the choice depends on the state of your skin.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "one of them is the right answer." Cotton spreads toner evenly and can also be used for wiping, but it invites friction and over-use of product. Hands cause less friction and make the amount easier to judge, but coverage can be patchy. The general dermatological advice is "either — but press, do not rub or pat." For dry or sensitive skin, hands are the safer choice.

## Comparison

| Item | Cotton | Hands |
|---|---|---|
| Evenness | **Easy to spread**, reaches details (sides of the nose, under the eyes) | Tends to be patchy |
| Friction | **Rubbing with the fibres irritates.** Small if you **press rather than slide** | **The least** |
| Amount | **The cotton absorbs it, so you use more** (**about a 500-yen coin's worth, until the pad is soaked through**) | **A small amount is enough** (from a 100-yen coin's worth) |
| Hygiene | **Disposable, so clean** | **Wash your hands first** |
| Wiping | **Yes** (wipe-off toners assume cotton) | No |
| Skin it suits | Normal or oily skin; wiping purposes | **Dry, sensitive or easily reddened skin** |
| Cost | Cotton pads | None |

- General guidance from bodies such as the Japanese Dermatological Association describes **most skincare irritation as coming from "rubbing, tapping and patting"**; **the point is "press it in", not which tool you use.**
- In cosmetics, **"penetration" (浸透)** means **as far as the stratum corneum (the outermost layer of skin)** under the industry's advertising guidelines. **Claims that cotton pushes it deeper, or that body heat from the hands carries it in, have no official basis.**
- **A cotton mask (a pad soaked in toner and left on for a few minutes)** **starts to draw moisture out of the skin once it begins to dry**, so the usual guidance is to remove it after **about 3 minutes** (the same logic as in the sheet mask article).

> **[Verify]** Check the Japanese Dermatological Association statements and how the advertising guidelines handle "penetration". The amounts are makers' guidance.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cotton makes it penetrate better

**Penetration goes only as far as the stratum corneum**, whichever tool you use. **Evenness** is cotton's advantage; **less friction** is the hands'.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Applying with your hands puts bacteria on your face

**Washed hands are fine.** Cosmetics are **formulated with preservatives**, and **clean hands** are the method dermatologists take for granted.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Patting improves circulation

**Tapping can cause redness and pigmentation** and is not recommended by dermatologists. **Press.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The longer a cotton mask stays on, the better

**Once it starts to dry it pulls moisture out of the skin.** Remove after **about 3 minutes** and **seal with a milky lotion (乳液, nyūeki) or cream.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Any cotton will do

**Fluffy, stiff or thin cotton causes friction and leaves fibres behind.** Use **cosmetic cotton pads (thick and lint-free)**, and **place them on the skin rather than sliding them.**
