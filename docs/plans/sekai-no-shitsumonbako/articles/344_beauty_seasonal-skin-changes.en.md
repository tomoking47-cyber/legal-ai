# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about skin at the change of seasons?｜Japan Questions #344

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/seasonal-skin-changes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"My skin plays up when the seasons change" is a feeling widely shared in Japan, where four distinct seasons bring large swings in humidity. Many newcomers notice it in their first year. This article sets out what actually changes, and what to adjust, using public weather data and general dermatological explanations.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the change of seasons is a special allergy period." Mostly it is a pile-up of sudden shifts in humidity and temperature, changing UV levels, pollen and dust, and the switch between heating and air-conditioning, so that the skin's water and oil balance cannot keep up. The response is "adjust how much you moisturise", "keep up sun protection" and "do not add several new products at once." Redness or itching that persists is a matter for a dermatology clinic.

## What changes (Tokyo climate normals)

| Seasonal switch | Change | Effect on skin (general explanation) |
|---|---|---|
| **Winter → spring (Feb–Apr)** | Humidity stays in the **50s (%)** while temperatures rise; **UV climbs sharply (March is about the same as September)**; **cedar (sugi) and cypress (hinoki) pollen** | Sebum increases while dryness lingers; pollen brings redness and itching |
| **Spring → summer (Jun–Jul)** | Humidity **above 75%**, temperatures of **30°C (86°F)**, sweat and sebum | Greasiness, irritation from sweat, dampness under masks |
| **Summer → autumn (Sep–Oct)** | Humidity and temperature drop sharply; **summer's UV damage shows on the surface** | Dryness, rough texture, dullness |
| **Autumn → winter (Nov–Dec)** | Humidity **below 50%**, and **20–30%** indoors with the heating on | Dryness, flaking, itching |

- **UV levels rise sharply from March and peak in May–August** (Japan Meteorological Agency). **Assuming the spring sun is "still weak"** is the classic reason people start sunscreen too late.
- **Skin trouble in hay-fever season** (pollen dermatitis, 花粉皮膚炎) typically shows as **redness and itching around the eyes, on the cheeks and on the neck**, and is well known to dermatologists.
- **Switching between heating and air-conditioning**: indoor humidity changes sharply just after the heating or the cooling goes on, so this is also **the time to review humidifying and moisturising.**

> **[Verify]** Check the Japan Meteorological Agency climate normals (humidity, temperature) and monthly UV data. The explanation of pollen dermatitis is based on Japanese Dermatological Association material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The change of seasons is when skin "gets weaker"

**Your skin does not change its nature; the environment changes suddenly.** The practical response is to **look at the environment (humidity, UV, pollen) and adjust how much you moisturise and protect from the sun.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜When skin flares up, fix it with new products

**Adding several new products at once makes the cause impossible to find.** While the skin is irritated, **stick to a minimum of products you know**, and **once it settles, add one at a time.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Spring UV is still weak

**UV in March is about the same as in September.** **Sunscreen from February–March** is the general dermatological advice.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You do not need moisturiser in summer

**Air-conditioning dries the air and sweat evaporates water from the skin**, so skin dries in summer too. **Keep moisturising with a lighter product** (see the oily skin article).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Seasonal flare-ups settle on their own, so leave them

**Most do settle once the skin adjusts**, but **redness, itching or bumps lasting more than 2 weeks** may be pollen dermatitis or contact dermatitis. **Have a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance) check.**
