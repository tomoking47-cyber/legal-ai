# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about bathing and dry skin?｜Japan Questions #346

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/bath-and-skin` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan, where most people soak in a bath every day, complaints that "the bath dries my skin" cluster in winter. Abroad the standard advice is "keep showers short." This article sets out how bath temperature, time and washing method relate to the skin's moisture.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a bath moisturises the skin." The stratum corneum (the outermost layer of skin) takes in water while you are in the bath, but evaporation afterwards can leave the skin drier than before, and the more that hot water, long soaks and over-washing have stripped the skin's oils, the more pronounced this is. The guide figures are "around 40°C (104°F), about 10 minutes", "do not over-wash" and "moisturise within 5–10 minutes of getting out."

## Points to weigh up

| Item | Guide | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Water temperature | **Around 40°C (104°F)** (**41°C (106°F) or below, about 10 minutes** is the guide for preventing bathing accidents) | 42°C (108°F) and above strips sebum easily and leads to itching |
| Time | **10–15 minutes** | Long soaks wash out the water-holding substances in the stratum corneum |
| Washing | **With foam, using your hands or a soft towel.** **Do not scrub with a nylon towel** | Friction causes dryness and pigmentation (friction melanosis) |
| How often to wash with soap | **Oily areas (face, armpits, feet, groin) every day; arms and legs can have water-only days if you have not sweated** | General dermatological guidance |
| After the bath | **Moisturise within 5–10 minutes** (some people apply it in the bathroom) | Evaporation leaves skin drier than before — "over-drying" |
| Bath additives (入浴剤, nyūyokuzai) | **Moisturising types (ceramides, oils)** suit people with dry skin. **Check the label on sulphur and carbonated types** | See the bath additives article |

- The Japanese Dermatological Association's lifestyle guidance for **sebum deficiency (xerosis, dry skin)** lists **avoiding hot water, long soaks, nylon towels and over-washing, and moisturising straight after the bath.** It is one of the main causes of **winter itching in older people.**
- **Bathing accidents** (hot water, long soaks) point to the same guide figures from a different angle (see the bath temperature article).
- **Soap or body wash on the whole body every day is not necessary** — a view set out in dermatological material (see the body wash article).

> **[Verify]** Check the Japanese Dermatological Association's lifestyle guidance on sebum deficiency and the Consumer Affairs Agency's guide figures on bathing accidents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A long soak moisturises the skin

**Evaporation afterwards can leave it drier than before.** **10–15 minutes, then moisturise straight away.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Hotter water gets you cleaner

**42°C and above strips sebum and causes itching.** **Around 40°C** removes dirt perfectly well.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Scrubbing hard with a nylon towel is the Japanese way

**Dermatologists know it as a cause of friction melanosis (pigmentation).** **Roll the foam over the skin with your hands or a soft towel.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Moisturising an hour after the bath is fine

**Over-drying sets in within 5–10 minutes.** More people now apply moisturiser **straight after leaving the bathroom, or inside it.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Itching means dirt is left on the skin

**The main cause of winter itching is dryness**, and **over-washing makes it worse.** **Itching that continues despite moisturising** should go to a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).
