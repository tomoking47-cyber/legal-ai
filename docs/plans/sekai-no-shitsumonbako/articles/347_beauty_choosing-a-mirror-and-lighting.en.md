# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about choosing a mirror and lighting for make-up?｜Japan Questions #347

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/choosing-a-mirror-and-lighting` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Make-up that looked perfect at home looks different outside" is a universal experience. The cause is usually not the mirror but the colour and direction of the light. In Japan the typical bluish-white "daylight colour" ceiling lights in homes, and the lighting in the washroom (洗面所, the sink area), play a part.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "make-up mistakes are the mirror's fault." What decides the finish is the colour temperature of the light (bluish "daylight colour" or warm "bulb colour"), its brightness, and its direction (from above only, or from in front of the face). The basics are "neutral white close to natural light (around 5,000 K)", "even light from in front of the face" and "a final check in the kind of light you will be seen in."

## Colour and direction of light

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Colour temperature** | **Bulb colour (電球色, about 2,700–3,000 K)**: warm and strongly yellow → foundation looks darker and redness is hard to see ／ **Neutral white (昼白色, about 5,000 K)**: close to natural light → **suited to make-up** ／ **Daylight colour (昼光色, about 6,500 K)**: bluish-white → **common in Japanese washrooms and living rooms.** Makes the complexion look pale, so people over-apply blusher |
| **Direction** | **Light from above only**: shadows under the eyes and nose → people over-apply concealer ／ **Light from in front**: no shadows, closer to reality |
| **Brightness** | Washroom lighting is often about **300–500 lux.** **For make-up, 500–1,000 lux on the face** is the guide (too bright and you cannot see unevenness) |
| **Colour rendering (Ra)** | **Ra 90 or above** shows colours accurately. Ordinary LEDs are **around Ra 80** |
| **Mirror** | **A flat mirror without distortion**; **a magnifying mirror is for details (brows, eyes) only.** Doing the whole face in a magnifying mirror leads to heavy application |
| **Checking outside** | Final check in **natural light by a window**, or in **the light where you are going (office neutral white, evening street light)** |

- Japanese homes commonly have **daylight-colour (bluish-white) ceiling lights**, and **washrooms tend to be daylight colour too**; people often say **the complexion looks pale, so they add too much red.**
- **The colour temperature and colour rendering of LED lights are printed on the product** (classified under JIS Z9112, the Japanese Industrial Standard). **Most "make-up mirrors" are Ra 90 or above and around 5,000 K.**
- **Changing the washroom light to neutral white (5,000 K), or adding a front light beside the mirror** — a tip that has spread among people living in Japan.

> **[Verify]** Check the colour temperature classes against JIS Z9112 and the illuminance guide figures against JIS Z9110.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Buying an expensive mirror solves it

**A flat mirror without distortion is enough.** What makes the difference is **the colour and direction of the light.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The brighter the better

**Too bright and you cannot see unevenness or heaviness, so it looks heavy outside.** **Around 500–1,000 lux, evenly.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Daylight colour (bluish-white light) is the same as natural light

**Daylight colour is 6,500 K, bluer than actual daytime natural light (roughly 5,000–5,500 K).** The complexion looks pale, and **blusher and lipstick end up too heavy.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Finish the whole face in a magnifying mirror

**Magnifying mirrors are for details.** Looking at the whole face magnified leads to **over-application.** **Whole face at 1×, brows and eyes magnified.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Perfect at home means perfect outside

**Outdoor light is not the same as home light.** **A final check in natural light by a window**, or **in light of the colour temperature you will be under**, is the reliable way.
