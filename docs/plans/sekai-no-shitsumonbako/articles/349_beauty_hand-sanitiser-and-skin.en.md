# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about hand sanitiser and sore hands?｜Japan Questions #349

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/hand-sanitiser-and-skin` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Since 2020 alcohol sanitiser has been part of everyday life in Japan — there is a bottle at almost every shop entrance — and complaints of sore, cracked hands have risen. This article sets out when to wash and when to sanitise, what concentration to use, and how to protect your skin, based on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's approach to hand hygiene.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "washing and then sanitising every time is the safe option." The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) makes washing with running water and soap the basic method, and alcohol sanitiser the fallback for when you cannot wash. Doing both every time stacks up irritation and makes sore hands worse. The guide is "wash if there is dirt, sanitise if there is none", "rub sanitiser in until it is dry" and "moisturise in between."

## Points to weigh up

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Basics | Wash **with running water and soap for about 30 seconds** | MHLW |
| Where sanitiser fits | A means for **when you cannot wash.** **If there is visible dirt, wash** | Same |
| Alcohol concentration | **60–95% (around 70% is the guide).** **Wet hands dilute it** | Same, WHO |
| Method | **A generous amount in the palm (a 500-yen coin's worth); rub over the whole hand until dry (15–30 seconds).** **Do not wipe it off** | Same |
| Sore hands | **Alcohol strips sebum; repeated use leads to dryness and cracking.** **Products with added moisturisers**, and **moisturising after sanitising** | Dermatology |
| Category | **Alcohol hand sanitiser is a "medicine" (医薬品) or a "quasi-drug" (designated quasi-drug, 指定医薬部外品 — a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines).** **Different from general goods labelled "除菌" (anti-bacterial)** | MHLW |
| Fire | **Alcohol is flammable.** **Do not use or store it near flames.** **Do not inhale sprays** | Fire and Disaster Management Agency |

- **When hands become sore the skin's defences drop and bacteria settle more easily**, according to general guidance, so **moisturising is generally described as part of hygiene** (see the sore hands article).
- **General-goods "anti-bacterial sprays" and "anti-bacterial wipes" (除菌, jokin) are not medicines or quasi-drugs, and the claims they may make are restricted.** For sanitising hands, look for the label **"指定医薬部外品" (designated quasi-drug) or "医薬品" (medicine).**
- **Children**: **swallowing sanitiser** and **getting it in the eyes** have been reported. **Keep it out of reach.**

> **[Verify]** Check the basics of hand hygiene against the MHLW "Hand washing and hand sanitising" material, and the concentrations against the WHO guidelines.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Washing and sanitising every time is the cleanest

**Irritation stacks up and hands become sore**, and **sore hands are worse for hygiene too.** **If you can wash, just wash**; **sanitise when you cannot.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The higher the concentration, the better it works

**60–95% is the effective range**, and **higher concentrations are generally described as evaporating faster and working less well without some water present.** **Around 70% is the guide.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜A quick spray and a shake of the hands is enough

**Rub in for 15–30 seconds until dry.** **Too little, or wiping it off straight away,** reduces the effect.

## Misunderstanding 4｜"除菌" (anti-bacterial) on the label means the same as sanitising

**"除菌" is wording for general goods**, and **is regulated differently from "消毒" (disinfect) or "殺菌" (kill bacteria) on medicines and quasi-drugs.** For hands, look for **at least a designated quasi-drug label.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜You just have to put up with sore hands

Many people improve with **sanitiser containing moisturisers, hand cream after sanitising, cotton gloves and intensive moisturising at night.** **Cracking, bleeding or strong itching** should go to a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance).
