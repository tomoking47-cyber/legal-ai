# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about "organic" and "natural" cosmetics?｜Japan Questions #350

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/organic-and-natural-cosmetics` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Organic cosmetics are a global market, but Japan has no certification scheme for organic cosmetics, so the label is close to self-declared. This article sets out how the situation differs from the certifications you may know from home, and what to look for on a Japanese shelf.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a cosmetic labelled 'organic' in Japan has been certified." Japan has no official definition or certification for organic cosmetics (the Organic JAS mark for food does not apply to cosmetics). If a product carries an overseas private certification mark (COSMOS, ECOCERT, USDA, etc.) you know which standard it meets; "organic" or "natural" without a mark is wording with no legal backing.

## The systems compared

| Item | Japan | Overseas (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| Definition of organic cosmetics | **No official definition or certification** | **COSMOS (EU, private)**: sets the share of organic ingredients, etc. ／ **USDA Organic** (US Department of Agriculture; applies its food standard to cosmetics) ／ **ECOCERT, NATRUE** and others |
| "Organic JAS" (有機JAS, Japan's organic food mark) | **Food and agricultural products only.** Cannot be used on cosmetics | — |
| "Natural", "自然派" (nature-based), "plant-derived" | **No definition** | ISO 16128 (**a method for calculating a natural-origin index**; not a certification) |
| Regulation of claims | **The Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations (景表法, Japan's consumer advertising law)** — unsupported claims of superiority are prohibited; **the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association's advertising guidelines** | Each country's advertising rules |
| Safety | **Organic or natural origin has no link to mildness.** **Allergy to essential oils and plant extracts** is well known to dermatologists | The same |

- **Private bodies such as the Japan Organic Cosmetics Association have their own standards**, but **they have no legal force, and each body has its own mark.**
- **ISO 16128 (2016–2017)** is an international standard that **defines "natural", "natural-origin", "organic" and "organic-origin" ingredients and sets out how to calculate their share of a product.** It is **a calculation rule, not a certification**, and is the basis for claims such as "95% natural-origin index."
- **Essential oils** are **a group of ingredients that dermatologists frequently see as causes of allergic contact dermatitis** (lavender, tea tree, citrus and others).

> **[Verify]** Check the scope of Organic JAS with the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, the content of ISO 16128 in the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association explanation, and the approach of the Premiums and Representations Act with the Consumer Affairs Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜"Organic" on the label means certified

**Japan has no organic certification for cosmetics.** Without **an overseas certification mark (COSMOS etc.)** it is **self-declared.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Organic or natural means gentle on the skin

**Natural ingredients (essential oils, plant extracts) are among the main causes of allergy.** **"Gentle" and "natural" are unrelated**, and **synthetic ingredients are sometimes less irritating.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜"95% natural-origin" means the other 5% is dangerous

It is **a figure calculated under ISO 16128**, and **water counts as natural-origin.** **The remaining 5% does not mean "dangerous"**; it is usually necessary ingredients such as preservatives.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Preservative-free organic products are higher quality

**Without preservatives, microbes multiply more easily and the product keeps for less time after opening.** They assume **small sizes, refrigeration and quick use** (see the additive-free article).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Organic means it works better

**A cosmetic may claim only the 56 permitted items, organic or not.** **It cannot claim to "heal" or to "work"**, and price and efficacy are separate things.
