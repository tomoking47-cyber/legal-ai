# Q. What do people get wrong about washing towels and towel smell?｜Japan Questions #351

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-towels` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"I washed it and it still smells" and "my towels have gone stiff" are complaints heard everywhere. In Japan's hot, humid climate they happen especially easily. This article sorts out a common misunderstanding about fabric softener, and what is known about the bacteria behind the smell (*Moraxella*).

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "use fabric softener every time and towels stay fluffy and clean". Softener coats the fibres, lowers absorbency and gets in the way of washing out dirt. The main cause of the smell is bacteria left after washing (such as *Moraxella*) multiplying while the towel stays damp. The fixes are: hang towels as soon as the wash ends; wash at 40–60°C or soak in oxygen bleach; use a tumble dryer; and use softener only once every few washes.

## What to base your decision on

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cause of the smell | **Skin oil and dead skin that the wash did not remove are broken down by bacteria (*Moraxella* and others) while the towel is damp**. **This is the main cause of "damp-dry smell" (生乾き臭, namakawaki-shū)** |
| Detergent | **The stated dose**. **Too much leaves residue in the fibres, which feeds the bacteria** |
| Fabric softener | **Coats the fibres, so absorbency and cleaning both drop**. **If you use it, once every few washes, at the stated dose** |
| Temperature | **Hot water at 40–60°C (104–140°F)** removes bacteria and skin oil more easily (**check the care label**). **A soak in powdered oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai)** also works |
| Drying | **Hang the wash as soon as it finishes** (leaving it in the drum lets bacteria multiply). **A tumble dryer (60°C or above) reduces bacteria** |
| How to hang | **Spread out, with air moving through**. **Shake the towel a few times before hanging to lift the pile** |
| When to replace | **Smell comes back after washing, no longer absorbs, fraying**. A rough guide is **1–2 years** (depends on how often it is used) |

- Research by detergent makers has shown that ***Moraxella*** breaks down skin oil and gives off the substance behind the **"old dishcloth smell"**. It can be reduced by **heat of 60°C or above, oxygen bleach, and thorough drying**.
- **Stiffness** comes from **the pile (the loops) lying flat** and from **detergent and skin oil left in the fibres**. **Softener makes a towel feel soft for a while, but the real fix is washing and rinsing properly**.
- **Most towel care labels (Japan uses the international JIS L0001 symbols)** show **a wash tub with a number (40 or 60) and a tumble-dry symbol**. **If the label says 60, a hot wash is allowed**.

> **[Verify]** The explanation of *Moraxella* is based on research published by detergent makers (Kao, Lion and others). Check the care symbols against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Fabric softener makes towels clean

**Softener has no cleaning power.** Because it **lowers absorbency and gets in the way of removing dirt**, the guide for towels is **once every few washes**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜More detergent will get rid of the smell

**Too much detergent leaves residue after rinsing and makes things worse**. **The cause is the bacteria left behind**, and the answer is **heat, bleach and drying**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Damp-dry smell goes away once the towel dries

**The bacteria stay in the dry towel and smell again as soon as it gets wet.** Reset the towel with **a soak in oxygen bleach (40–50°C, 30 minutes to 1 hour) or a wash at 60°C**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Towels can go in with the rest of the wash

**Towels carry a lot of skin oil and dead skin, and can pass bacteria to other clothes**. **Washing towels together on their own in hot water** is a method widely shared among people living in Japan.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If the fluffiness will not come back, the towel is finished

Usually **the pile is flattened or detergent is left in the fibres**. It often comes back if you **add a rinse, shake before hanging, or give it a short spell in the dryer**. **When a towel stops absorbing, it is at the end of its life**.
