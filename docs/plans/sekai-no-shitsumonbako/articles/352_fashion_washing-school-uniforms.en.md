# Q. What do people get wrong about caring for Japanese school uniforms?｜Japan Questions #352

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-school-uniforms` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Most Japanese junior-high and high-school students wear a uniform, and the family looks after it for three to six years. "Can it be washed at home?" and "Is it fine to wear the same one every day?" are questions we hear especially often from foreign families with children in Japanese schools.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "uniforms are dry-clean only and cannot be washed at home". Most recent school uniforms are polyester blends, and if the care label shows a washing-machine symbol they can be washed at home. The key points are: check the label; use a laundry net and a gentle cycle; reshape before hanging; and deal with shiny patches. Combine a dry-clean at the end of each season with daily brushing and airing in the shade.

## A care guide

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Fabric | Today's uniforms are **mainly polyester (from 50% to all polyester, some with wool)**. Most carry a **"washable" label allowing home washing** |
| How often to wash | **Shirts, blouses and polo shirts every day**; **trousers and skirts about once a week; blazers once a month to a few times a season** |
| How to wash | **Follow the label: laundry net, gentle cycle, neutral (中性) detergent**. **Fold pleated skirts along the pleats before putting them in the net** |
| How to hang | **Blazers on a thick hanger; trousers clipped by the hem and hung; skirts clipped at the waistband** |
| Daily care | **Brush and air in the shade after school** (the same as a suit). **Pre-wash shirt collars and cuffs** |
| Shiny patches | **Where the child sits (seat and elbows)**. **Hold a steam iron just above the fabric and lift the fibres with a brush** |
| Dry-cleaning | **At the end of the season (the seasonal wardrobe change, 衣替え)**. Ask for **sweat removal (汗抜き, ase-nuki)** |
| Size | **Check the size once a year while the child is growing**. **Let out the sleeves and hems — uniforms are made with extra fabric sewn in for this** |

- School uniforms fall under the **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (Japan's labelling law)**, so they carry **a fabric composition label and a care label**. Products marked **"washable" (ウォッシャブル)** can go in the washing machine.
- **Yellowing on shirt collars and cuffs** (see the article on yellowing) and **antiperspirant build-up** (see the article on underarm yellowing) are especially common in growing children.
- **A uniform jacket and trousers or skirt typically cost ¥30,000–50,000 together**, so **three years of care has a direct effect on the household budget**. **Reuse (uniform banks, hand-me-downs from graduates)** is spreading.

> **[Verify]** Check fabric trends and prices against school-uniform makers and the national school-supplies association; check the symbols against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Uniforms cannot be washed at home

**If the label shows a washing-machine symbol, they can.** Most current uniforms are polyester blends and **washable** is the norm. **Even a blazer may be machine-washable, depending on the label**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wearing the same trousers or skirt every day is fine

**Worn day after day, the knees and seat stretch, go shiny and pick up sweat smell**. **With two pairs, rotate them**; with one, **brush and air it in the shade after school**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Washing a pleated skirt makes the pleats disappear

**Polyester pleats are heat-set and stay in if you wash as the label says.** **Fold along the pleats into a net, spin briefly, and hang clipped at the waistband**. **Iron on low with a pressing cloth**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Shiny patches come out in the wash

**A shiny patch is fibre that has been crushed flat**, and washing does not undo it. **Hold a steam iron just above it and lift the fibres with a brush**. **Prevention is rotation and how the child sits**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The only option is to buy a new uniform

**Letting out the sleeves and hems** and **adjusting the waist** are built into most uniforms. **Uniform reuse** is spreading through schools, local councils and parent associations.
