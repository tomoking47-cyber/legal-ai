# Q. What do people get wrong about jeans sizing and shrinkage?｜Japan Questions #353

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/jeans-sizing-and-shrinkage` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"They got tight after one wash" and "the size on the label means something different in every brand" are complaints heard worldwide. Japanese denim from Okayama and Hiroshima is known internationally, and Japan has a strong culture of raw (unwashed) denim, so shrinkage is discussed here in more detail than in most places.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "jeans always shrink a full size when washed". Whether they shrink depends on how the fabric was finished: pre-shrunk (Sanforized) fabric shrinks 1–3%, while untreated raw denim shrinks 5–10% in the first wash. Even in inch sizes, the actual measurements differ from brand to brand, so the reliable method is to measure the actual waist in centimetres and compare.

## The numbers

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Pre-shrunk (Sanforized)** | Most jeans sold in shops. **Shrinkage in the wash 1–3%** (1–2 cm on an 80 cm waist) |
| **Not pre-shrunk (raw denim, "shrink-to-fit")** | **Shrinks 5–10% in the first wash** (**3–8 cm** at the waist and in length). **Buying a larger size to allow for shrinkage** is the assumption |
| Inch sizes | Waist **W28–W36** etc. = **about 71–91 cm**. But **a brand's actual measurement is normally the inch size plus 2–5 cm** ("bigger than the label") |
| Stretching | **Stretch 1–3 cm with wear** (cotton stretches and does not recover well). **Stretch blends (1–3% polyurethane/elastane) stretch even more** |
| Tumble dryer | **High heat causes heavy shrinkage and degrades stretch fabric** |
| Japanese denim regions | **Ibara and Kurashiki (Kojima) in Okayama Prefecture, Fukuyama in Hiroshima Prefecture** — **most of Japan's denim fabric**. Known for **selvedge denim (赤耳, "red ear")** |

- **Sanforizing** is **a finish that mechanically shrinks the fabric in advance so that it stays stable** (developed in the US in the 1930s). **Untreated "shrink-to-fit"** denim is **bought, then wetted so it shrinks to the wearer's body**, a way of buying that is well established among Japanese enthusiasts.
- **Size labels follow industry custom; there is no legally unified standard** (JIS L4004 classifies body measurements; the garment's actual measurements are up to each maker).
- Under the **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (Japan's labelling law)**, **the fibre composition (all-cotton, cotton 98% polyurethane 2%, etc.) and the care label** are compulsory. **There is no duty to state "pre-shrunk"**, so ask the shop staff or look for **a tag note saying "may shrink when washed" (洗濯で縮む場合があります)**.

> **[Verify]** Shrinkage rates are general figures from the textile industry and denim makers. Check the regional information against industry publications from Okayama and Hiroshima Prefectures.

## Misunderstanding 1｜All jeans shrink when washed

**Pre-shrunk jeans shrink 1–3%**, which wear recovers. **Raw denim shrinks 5–10%**, which you allow for when buying. Check **the tag and the brand's description**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜W30 means an actual 76 cm waist

**An actual measurement 2–5 cm larger than the inch size** is normal, and **it differs by brand**. **Lay a pair you own flat, measure the waist and double it (×2)**, then compare in centimetres.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Buy an exact fit and they will not stretch

**Cotton stretches 1–3 cm with wear and does not recover well**. **"Slightly tight breaks in within a few days"** is the denim convention. **Stretch blends stretch easily and recover easily**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜If they shrink, the dryer will stretch them back

**The dryer makes shrinkage worse.** **Wearing them damp, or pulling them into shape as they dry**, can recover a few centimetres (**raw denim shrinkage does not come back**).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Japanese denim shrinks more than other denim

**It is a difference in how the fabric was finished**: **selvedge raw denim is often not pre-shrunk**, so it shrinks. **Sanforized Japanese denim** behaves like any other. A **"one-wash" (ワンウォッシュ) label** means the jeans have already been washed once and shrunk.
