# Q. What do people get wrong about insoles?｜Japan Questions #354

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/insoles-and-foot-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese shoe shops and drugstores (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.) sell an enormous range of insoles, and people ask us "which should I choose?" and "will they help my foot pain?". This article separates what an off-the-shelf insole can do from what belongs to medicine (prescribed orthotic insoles).

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "put in an insole and foot pain is solved". Off-the-shelf insoles are general goods for cushioning, fit, odour control and size adjustment, and by law they cannot claim medical effects. Correcting a foot deformity or pain is the province of orthotic insoles (足底板, sokuteiban) made to a doctor's prescription, which Japanese health insurance covers. Choose a shop-bought insole by purpose (cushioning, size adjustment, arch support, odour control), by a thickness that fits the shoe, and by material.

## Shop-bought insoles versus medical orthoses

| Category | What it is | Purpose | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shop-bought insole (general goods)** | Cushioning, arch support, size adjustment (thickness), odour and sweat absorption, heel-height adjustment | **Comfort, fit, hygiene** | A few hundred to a few thousand yen |
| **Shop-bought (classed as a medical device)** | Some products sold as "orthotic insoles" are notified as **Class I (general) medical devices** | Spreading pressure under the sole | A few thousand yen up to the ¥10,000s |
| **Medical orthotic insole (an orthosis)** | **Made by a certified prosthetist-orthotist to a doctor's prescription**, from a mould of the foot | **Correction and off-loading for deformities and conditions** (flat feet, bunions, plantar fasciitis, etc.) | **Covered by health insurance** (you pay 10–30% of a cost in the tens of thousands of yen) |

- Shop-bought insoles are **general goods or Class I medical devices**, and cannot make claims such as **"fixes pain" or "corrects bunions"** (under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act and the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, Japan's advertising law). **"Less tiring" and "comfortable"** are as far as they may go.
- **Arch-support types (which support the instep)** can **cause pain or discomfort if they do not match your foot**. **Break them in for short periods**, and **take them out if the discomfort continues**.
- Against **moisture inside shoes** (see the article on shoe smell), **an insole you can take out and dry** has a hygiene advantage. **Take it out and dry it every day**.
- If **pain in the heel, sole or big toe continues**, see **an orthopaedic clinic (整形外科, covered by Japanese health insurance)**. **Orthotic insoles made on a doctor's prescription are insured**.

> **[Verify]** Check the classification of shop-bought insoles (general goods / Class I medical device) in the PMDA medical-device database, and the insurance coverage of orthotic insoles in the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's reimbursement documents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜An insole will solve foot pain

**Shop-bought products have no such effect and may not claim one.** **If pain continues, see an orthopaedic clinic**, and if needed get **orthotic insoles (insured)**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Arch support is good for everyone

**Instep height varies a great deal between people, and support that does not fit causes pain**. **Try it for short periods and stop if it feels wrong**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The thicker the insole, the better the cushioning

**Too thick and the shoe becomes tight, the heel lifts and you get blisters**. **Take out the shoe's original insole and replace it**, or choose **a thin one**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Once it is in, an insole can stay there

**Sweat and skin oil make it unhygienic, and the cushioning flattens.** **Take it out and dry it every day, and replace it every few months to a year**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If the shoe does not fit, an insole is enough to fix it

**It works for half a size, but a difference of a full size or more affects how you walk**. **Get the shoe size right first** (see the article on how to wear shoes).
