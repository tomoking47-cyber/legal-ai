# Q. What do people get wrong about clothing collection and recycling in Japan?｜Japan Questions #355

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/clothing-donation-and-recycling` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese households part with around 460,000 tonnes of clothing a year that ends up incinerated or otherwise disposed of. This article looks at the gap between "old clothes get recycled" and what actually happens, and at how to dispose of clothes from home, using figures from the Ministry of the Environment.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "put old clothes in a collection and they will be recycled". By the Ministry of the Environment's estimate, about 55% of the clothing households part with is thrown away as burnable, non-burnable or mixed waste; reuse and recycling together account for about 40%, and fibre-to-fibre recycling is a tiny fraction. The realistic order is: wear it longer, pass it on for reuse, then use a collection point. Your local council's sorting rules are the first thing to check.

### How to dispose of clothes

| Method | Detail | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Council textile collection (古布, kofu — "old cloth")** | **Many local councils collect "old cloth and old clothes" as a recyclable**. **Wash and dry them, put them in a clear bag**. **Do not put them out on a rainy day** | Many councils exclude **stained, torn or wet items, underwear and socks** |
| **In-store collection** | Collection boxes run by clothing brands and shopping centres | Sometimes **limited to the shop's own brand** |
| **Reuse (second-hand shops, flea-market apps)** | Items in good condition | — |
| **Wiping rags and reclaimed fibre** | Items from council collections that cannot be reused become **industrial wiping rags (ウエス, uesu) or wadding** | — |

## Misunderstanding 1｜Put it in a collection and it gets recycled

**About 60% is incinerated, and under 1% is recycled fibre-to-fibre**. **Reuse (someone else wears it)** is the most valuable way to part with clothing.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Anything can go in the textile collection

**Stained, torn or wet items, underwear and socks** are excluded by many councils (they are rubbish). **Washed and dried** is the basic condition. **Put it out on a rainy day and the whole bag becomes rubbish**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Donating is always better than throwing away

**Exporting second-hand clothing overseas has been linked to problems for local textile industries and with waste in the receiving countries**. Give priority to **reuse and collection points inside Japan**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Fast fashion is the only problem

**Buying 18 items a year and leaving 25 unworn** is the average, and points to **how we buy and how long we wear**. **"Wear one item for longer"** is the Ministry of the Environment's first message.

## Misunderstanding 5｜What one person can do is small

**Wear things longer (care and repair), pass them on for reuse, do not buy what you will not wear** — the Ministry of the Environment estimates that **wearing clothes for just one more year cuts waste by tens of thousands of tonnes**. The care tips in this series of articles exist for exactly that reason.
