# Q. What do people get wrong about sewing on buttons, hemming and other repairs at home?｜Japan Questions #357

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/sewing-repairs-at-home` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In parts of the world, "a button came off, so throw it away" has become a normal choice. Japan has kept a culture of mending: home economics is compulsory, so everyone learns to hand-sew, and 100-yen shops stock a full range of repair supplies. This article covers the basic repairs and where to hand over to a professional.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "repairs need sewing skill". Sewing on a button, hemming with a blind stitch and stopping a fray are jobs anyone can do with a needle, thread and ten minutes; in Japan every child learns them from the fifth year of primary school. If hand-sewing is hard for you, there is iron-on hemming tape, iron-on repair patches and fray-stop liquid. Replacing zips, leather goods and holes in knitwear are jobs for a repair shop.

## Repairs you can do at home

| Problem | Method | Tools | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Button came off** | **Use doubled thread, pass through 4–5 times in an × or = pattern, then wind thread round the base to make a "shank"** (room for the thickness of the fabric) | Needle, thread (button thread or hand-sewing thread), a toothpick (sets the shank height) | 5 min |
| **Hem came down (blind hem stitch, まつり縫い)** | **Catch tiny stitches on the inside so the thread hardly shows on the outside**. Or use **iron-on hemming tape (裾上げテープ, suso-age tēpu)** | Needle and thread, or tape and an iron | 15–30 min |
| **Seam coming apart** | **Backstitch** along the original seam line | Needle and thread | 10 min |
| **Small hole or moth hole (knitwear)** | **Close it from the back with matching or near-matching yarn**. **Darning (weaving thread across the hole)** has become popular in recent years | Needle, yarn, a darning mushroom (or a light bulb) | 20–40 min |
| **Hole (trouser knee, elbow)** | **Iron-on repair patch (補修布, hoshū-nuno) from the back**, or **sew on a patch** | Repair patch, iron | 10 min |
| **Frayed thread end** | **Fray-stop liquid (ほつれ止め液, hotsure-dome eki)** | Liquid | 1 min |
| **Zip runs stiffly** | **Rub candle wax or a zip lubricant on the teeth** | — | 1 min |

### What belongs to the repair shop (お直し, o-naoshi)

**Zip replacement, adjusting the waist or shoulder width, leather and fur, large tears, big holes in knitwear, suit hems and sleeves** — a repair shop (common in stations and shopping centres) charges **a few hundred to a few thousand yen**. **Free or discounted hemming at the shop where you bought the item** is normal.

- **Home economics** is compulsory under the national curriculum: **hand-sewing (buttons, running stitch, backstitch, overcast stitch) and the sewing machine in primary years 5 and 6**, and **garment making in junior high school**. **For boys and girls alike**.
- **The repair corner of the 100-yen shop** (needles, thread, repair patches, hemming tape, fray-stop, buttons) has drawn attention from abroad as **a distinctive feature of Japanese household-goods shopping**.

> **[Verify]** Check the home-economics content against the Ministry of Education (MEXT) curriculum guidelines. The repair methods are the standard procedures in home-economics teaching materials and the textile industry.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Repairs need sewing skill

**Sewing on a button and blind-hemming are jobs primary-school children learn**. **Needle, thread, ten minutes**. **Watch one video and you can do it first time**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜If you cannot hand-sew, the only option is to throw it away

**Hemming tape, repair patches and fray-stop liquid** need **only an iron or a dab of liquid**. **The 100-yen shop has them all**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A button just needs thread through it

**Without a "shank" (a short column of thread), the button sits flat against the fabric, is hard to fasten and the thread soon breaks.** **Sew with a toothpick under the button, then wind the thread round the base**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Repair shops are expensive

Rough guide: **hemming ¥500–1,500, a button ¥200–500, zip replacement ¥2,000–5,000**. Judged against **the price of the garment and how many more years you will wear it**, most repairs are worth doing.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A jumper with a hole is finished

**Darning** (weaving thread across the hole) can be **invisible, or done in coloured thread as decoration**, and is spreading in Japan too. **A small moth hole takes 20 minutes**.
