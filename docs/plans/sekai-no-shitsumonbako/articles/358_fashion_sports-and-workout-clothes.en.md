# Q. What do people get wrong about washing sportswear and its smell?｜Japan Questions #358

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/sports-and-workout-clothes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

With the spread of running and gym-going, the complaint that polyester sportswear "smells even after washing" is shared worldwide. This article works through the fix from the nature of the fibre and what is known about the bacteria behind the smell.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "sportswear is quick-drying, so smell will not linger". Polyester does not absorb water, but it readily holds on to skin oil, and bacteria grow on that oil until the smell is fixed in the fabric. The fixes are: wash as soon as you take it off (do not leave it); no fabric softener; lukewarm water around 40°C with oxygen bleach or a sportswear detergent; and wash it inside out.

## What to base your decision on

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Why it smells | **Polyester repels water but readily holds skin oil**. **Cotton releases skin oil together with the water**; polyester keeps it. **Bacteria break down the leftover oil and produce the smell** |
| Leaving it | **Sweat-soaked kit left in a bag or basket for a few hours** is the ideal condition for growth. **Wash as soon as you get home**; if you cannot, **spread it out to dry** |
| Detergent | **A mildly alkaline liquid detergent or a "sportswear" detergent** (breaks down skin oil). **No fabric softener** (its coating worsens absorbency and oil build-up, and seals the smell in) |
| Temperature | **Around 40°C (104°F)** (check the label; **polyurethane/elastane blends are often labelled 30°C or below**) |
| Soaking | **30 minutes in lukewarm water with liquid oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) dissolved in it**. **No chlorine bleach** (colour loss, fibre damage) |
| Inside out | **Skin oil is on the inside**. **Turn inside out and use a laundry net** |
| Drying | **Quick-drying, so a few hours in the shade is enough**. **A tumble dryer damages stretch fabric** (check the label) |
| Anti-odour finishes | **"Antibacterial deodorising" finishes wear off gradually with washing** (some labels give a guide number of washes) |

- **Damp-dry smell is attributed mainly to *Moraxella***, while detergent-maker research shows that **the smell of worn, sweaty clothing comes mainly from *Micrococcus* bacteria**, with short- and medium-chain fatty acids as the odour compounds (see the article on towels).
- **Polyurethane (stretch) blends** are **degraded by heat and chlorine**, so **no hot tumble drying and no chlorine bleach** (the same as swimwear, see that article).
- **A soak in baking soda (重曹, jūsō, mildly alkaline)** also works on skin oil, but **oxygen bleach is generally described as working on the bacteria as well**.

> **[Verify]** The link between polyester's oil retention and bacteria is based on textile science and detergent-maker research. Check the symbols against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" page.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Quick-drying fabric does not smell

**The water dries but the skin oil stays, and bacteria grow on it.** **A wash that removes the oil** is the key.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Cover the smell with fabric softener fragrance

**Softener coats the fibres and seals in the oil and the smell.** **"Do not use on sportswear"** is the makers' usual guidance.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Keep it in the bag until it goes in the machine

**Sealed and wet is the condition bacteria need**. **Wash as soon as you get home**, or **spread it out to dry first**, then put it in the basket.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hot water removes both bacteria and oil

**The label's upper limit is often 30°C or below for polyurethane blends and about 40°C even for polyester**; **heat degrades the stretch**. **Stay at the labelled temperature and add oxygen bleach**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once the smell will not come out, the garment is finished

**An oxygen-bleach soak resets most of it**. **If the smell still comes back, oil and bacteria are fixed deep in the fibre**, and it is time to replace the item. **Prevention (wash at once, no softener)** works best.
