# Q. What do people get wrong about reading the fabric composition label on clothes?｜Japan Questions #360

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/clothing-and-static-fabrics-choice` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"綿 全量" (all cotton), "ポリエステル65% 綿35%" (polyester 65%, cotton 35%) — the composition label on a garment decides how to wash it, how it feels and how long it lasts, yet few people read it. This article explains how the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (Japan's labelling law) works and what each fibre is good and bad at.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "natural fibres = luxury and comfort, synthetics = cheap and unpleasant". The composition label is a statement of fact required by law, and every fibre has strengths and weaknesses. Cotton absorbs but dries slowly; polyester dries fast but holds skin oil; wool is warm but shrinks. Choosing the fibre that suits the purpose, and caring for it as the label says, is the basis of wearing clothes for a long time.

## How the label works

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Legal basis | **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (Textile Goods Quality Labelling Regulations)**: **fibre composition (%), care label and the labeller's name** are compulsory |
| Composition | **Fibre name and percentage**, **largest first**, e.g. **"綿 全量" (all cotton), "ポリエステル65% 綿35%" (polyester 65%, cotton 35%)**. **Lining and filling are labelled separately** |
| Exceptions | **Range labelling such as "wool 90% or more"**; **fibres under 5% may be grouped as "other" (その他)** |
| Designated terms | **Cotton (綿), linen/hemp (麻), wool (毛), silk (絹), rayon, cupro, polyester, nylon, acrylic, polyurethane** and so on: **the names fixed by law** must be used (**"コットン" and "ウール" may be added in brackets**) |

## Main fibres and their properties

| Fibre | Good at | Bad at | Care |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cotton (綿)** | Absorbency, feel on the skin, hard-wearing | **Slow to dry, shrinks, creases** | Easy to wash. Shrinks in the dryer |
| **Linen / hemp (麻)** | Breathable, absorbent, quick-drying | **Creases, shrinks, fluffs** | Hand-wash or gentle cycle |
| **Wool (毛)** | Warm, springy, creases recover | **Shrinks (felting), moths** | Hand-wash if the label allows |
| **Silk (絹)** | Sheen, lightness, warmth | **Weak against water, light, sweat and rubbing** | Neutral detergent if hand-washable (see the silk article) |
| **Polyester** | **Quick-drying, hard-wearing, crease-resistant** | **Holds skin oil, static, pilling** | Easy to wash |
| **Nylon** | Hard-wearing, light, stretchy | Yellows with heat and UV | Low temperature |
| **Acrylic** | Light, warm, wool-like | **Pilling, static** | Easy to wash |
| **Rayon** | Drape, absorbency, cool feel | **Shrinks and weakens in water** | Hand-wash or not washable (see label) |
| **Polyurethane (elastane)** | Stretch | **Degrades with heat, chlorine and age (2–3 years)** | 30°C or below, no bleach |

> **[Verify]** Check the labelling rules against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Textile Goods Quality Labelling Regulations". The fibre properties are the standard descriptions given by textile associations.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Natural fibres are always better

**It depends on the purpose.** **Quick-drying polyester for sportswear, cotton for underwear, wool for a winter coat**. Choose "because it is natural" and you end up with a T-shirt that will not dry or a shirt full of creases.

## Misunderstanding 2｜All-cotton does not shrink

**Cotton shrinks a few per cent in the wash and more in the dryer.** **Natural fibres** are the more shrink-prone group.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Polyester is cheap stuff

It is **a performance fibre — quick-drying, durable, crease-resistant** — and the mainstay of **sports, outdoor and school-uniform clothing**. **Its weaknesses are holding skin oil and static** (see the articles on sportswear and static).

## Misunderstanding 4｜If the percentages are given, you know how to wash it

**The composition is a clue to care, but the care label decides**. **With a blend, follow the more delicate fibre** (if there is wool in it, handle it as wool).

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is fine to cut the label off

**Cut off the composition and care labels and a dry-cleaner may refuse the item, and there is nothing to judge by if something goes wrong**. **If you must cut it, photograph it first**, or **move where it is sewn in**.
