# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about sparkling water?｜Japan Questions #361

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/sparkling-water` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Unsweetened sparkling water (炭酸水, tansansui) took off in Japan in the 2010s, and you will find it in every convenience store and vending machine. People ask whether it can replace plain water, and whether it harms bones or teeth. The same questions come up abroad. Here is what public sources let us say.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "sparkling water dissolves your bones and teeth." Unsweetened sparkling water (water plus carbon dioxide, nothing else) is only weakly acidic, and no evidence has been confirmed that it affects bone. Its effect on teeth is generally described as very small compared with fizzy drinks that contain sugar or acidulants. The points to watch are: tell it apart from sweetened and acidulated products, and remember that a large amount before a meal fills you up and can change how much you eat.

## Points to weigh

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| pH of unsweetened sparkling water | **Roughly 4–5** (weakly acidic; it varies with the product and how strong the carbonation is). Cola and similar drinks are **about 2.5–3**; lemon juice is **about 2** |
| Teeth | **Enamel is generally described as starting to dissolve at a pH of about 5.5**, and **unsweetened sparkling water sits below that**, but **the scale of its effect differs from fizzy drinks with sugar and citric acid**. The general dental advice is **do not hold it in your mouth for long, and drink water afterwards** |
| Bone | **No evidence has been confirmed that carbonation itself lowers bone density.** Past studies linked cola consumption with bone density, but **phosphoric acid, caffeine and cola replacing milk** are described as the factors |
| Hydration | **If unsweetened, it counts the same as water.** It falls **within the thinking of the "Drink Water for Your Health" campaign, run since April 2024 by the Ministry of the Environment and the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism** |
| Stomach | Carbonation **expands the stomach and gives a temporary feeling of fullness**. Studies find that **a large amount before a meal reduces food intake**, while **a small amount stimulates appetite** (varies by person) |
| Labels | **"炭酸水" (tansansui, sparkling water) is unsweetened**; **"炭酸飲料" (tansan inryō, carbonated drink) usually contains sugar, flavourings and acidulants**. **A "lemon flavour" product is still sparkling water if it is unsweetened** |

- **The Japanese market for unsweetened sparkling water grew more than fivefold in the 2010s** (beverage-maker and industry estimates). It moved **from a mixer for spirits to an everyday drink**.
- **"強炭酸" (kyō-tansan, "strong carbonation")** means a higher carbon dioxide pressure (gas volume); **the ingredients are the same**.
- People with **gastro-oesophageal reflux (heartburn)** may find that **carbonation brings on symptoms more easily**. Ask a doctor.

> **[Verify]** Check the pH figures against general measured values, the effect on teeth against materials from the Japan Dental Association and similar bodies, and the effect on bone against nutrition research reviews.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Sparkling water dissolves bone

**No evidence has been confirmed that the carbonic acid formed from carbon dioxide dissolves bone.** The cola effect seen in past studies is attributed to **phosphoric acid, caffeine and cola replacing milk**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It dissolves your teeth

**Unsweetened sparkling water is only weakly acidic, and its effect differs greatly from fizzy drinks with sugar and citric acid.** **Do not hold it in your mouth for long, and drink water before bed.** That is enough.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Only plain water counts for hydration

**If unsweetened, it hydrates just like water.** **It contains no caffeine, and it works at room temperature too.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜"Flavoured" means it contains sugar

**There are many unsweetened flavoured sparkling waters.** **Check "糖類" (sugars) and "エネルギー" (energy) on the nutrition label.** **Products labelled "炭酸飲料" (carbonated drink) usually do contain sugar.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Sparkling water makes you fat / makes you slim

**If unsweetened, it has 0 kcal**, so there is nothing to make you gain weight. **There is also no evidence of a weight-loss effect**: its effect on how much you eat **works in opposite directions depending on amount and timing**.
