# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about deep breathing and relaxation?｜Japan Questions #363

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/breathing-and-relaxation` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Take a deep breath to calm down" is advice heard the world over. In Japan it is built into stress programmes at schools and workplaces. This article separates what is solid from what is overstated, using materials from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and standard physiology.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "deep breathing gets rid of illness and stress." What deep breathing reliably does is this: a slow, long out-breath temporarily lowers heart rate and blood pressure and gives a feeling of tension loosening. That makes it a useful everyday tool for "switching gears", but anxiety, insomnia or low mood that persists belongs to medical care. The differences between methods ("belly breathing", "in for 4, out for 8") matter less than being able to keep it up.

## Points to weigh

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | **A slow out-breath shifts the balance towards the parasympathetic nervous system, and heart rate and blood pressure fall temporarily.** Breathing is generally described as **the only route into the autonomic nervous system that you can control consciously** |
| Example methods | **Breathe in for 4 seconds, out for 8 (make the out-breath long)**; **belly breathing (the abdomen expands)**; **slow breathing at about 6 breaths a minute** |
| Time | **1–3 minutes** to feel a change. **Long sessions or overly deep breathing cause hyperventilation (dizziness, tingling)** |
| MHLW's position | The **Kokoro no Mimi** portal (the MHLW's mental-health site for working people) presents breathing methods, muscle relaxation and stretching as **relaxation techniques** |
| Limits | If **anxiety, insomnia or low mood lasts 2 weeks or more, or gets in the way of daily life**, go to **a medical or counselling service** |
| Occasions | **Before a meeting, before bed, on the train, when you feel anger** — shared among people in Japan as "wait 6 seconds" or "one breath" |

- **Several studies show that slow breathing (about 6 breaths a minute) raises heart-rate variability and is linked to relaxation.** **They do not show that it makes go away illness or removes stress.**
- **Hyperventilation**: **repeated fast, deep breaths** lower carbon dioxide in the blood, causing **dizziness, tingling in the hands and palpitations**. The key is **"slow, with a long out-breath", not "deep"**.
- **Mindfulness and meditation** are methods in the same family. **Research shows a certain effect on reducing anxiety and stress**, but they are **described as no substitute for medical care**.

> **[Verify]** Check the relaxation section of the MHLW's Kokoro no Mimi portal. The physiological explanation is a general description.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Deep breathing makes stress go away

It is a tool for **temporarily loosening tension in the body**. **It does not change the source of the stress (work, relationships). If the strain persists, seek counselling or see a doctor.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The deeper and bigger the in-breath, the better

**The key is "breathe out slowly and for a long time", not "breathe in".** **Repeated fast, deep breaths cause hyperventilation**, bringing on dizziness and palpitations instead.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It only works with the "correct" method (belly breathing, 4-7-8, etc.)

**What the methods share is a slow out-breath and regular practice; the differences matter less.** **Use whichever form is easiest for you.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Deep breathing is all in the mind; nothing happens in the body

**The temporary fall in heart rate and blood pressure can be measured.** **It is a physiological response, not a matter of attitude.** That is also why it has limits.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If you cannot sleep, keep deep breathing and you will drop off

**It is one way of helping you fall asleep**, but **breathing while "trying hard to sleep" can backfire**. **If you cannot sleep, get out of bed** (see our article on waking in the night). **Persistent insomnia belongs to medical care.**
