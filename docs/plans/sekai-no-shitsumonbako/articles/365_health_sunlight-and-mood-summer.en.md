# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about UV rays and how you feel (summer sun and fatigue)?｜Japan Questions #365

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/sunlight-and-mood-summer` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"The sun wears me out" and "I feel drained after being outside in summer" are feelings shared by almost everyone who spends a summer in Japan. The effects of ultraviolet (UV) rays and the effects of heat and dehydration tend to get mixed together. This article uses public sources to pull them apart.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "summer fatigue is caused by UV rays." The main causes of tiredness after a day outside are the strain of regulating body temperature in the heat, and dehydration. UV rays mainly affect the skin and eyes. UV protection (hat, parasol, sunscreen, sunglasses) protects skin and eyes; heat protection (fluids, salt, shade, air conditioning) is needed separately.

## Keep the causes separate

| Factor | Effect on the body | Countermeasures |
|---|---|---|
| **UV rays** | **Skin (sunburn, dark spots, skin cancer); eyes (cataracts, pterygium)**. **Effects on immunity** have also been noted | Hat, parasol, clothing, sunscreen, sunglasses (Ministry of the Environment manual) |
| **Heat (temperature, humidity, radiant heat)** | **Strain on temperature regulation → fatigue, heatstroke** | **Fluids and salt, shade, rest, air conditioning** (Ministry of the Environment heatstroke prevention) |
| **Dehydration** | **Sluggishness, headache, poor concentration** | **Small, frequent drinks** (1.2 L a day plus what you sweat) |
| **Lack of sleep (summer nights)** | **A hot bedroom means shallow sleep → tiredness the next day** | **Air conditioning while you sleep (26–28°C / 79–82°F, left on at a gentle setting rather than on a timer)** (Sleep Guide) |
| **Temperature gap with air conditioning** | A general explanation says **a gap of 5–7°C or more between indoors and outdoors strains the autonomic nervous system** | Carry a layer; ease the setting before going out |

- **UV Index**: Japanese summers reach **8–10 ("very high")**. The Ministry of the Environment's guide is **take precautions at 3 or above, and avoid going out in the middle of the day at 8 or above**.
- The phrase **"UV fatigue" (紫外線疲れ)** is in use, but **we could not confirm any public source showing that UV rays directly cause whole-body fatigue.** The **sluggishness and fever** after severe skin inflammation (sunburn) are **the body's general response to a burn**.
- The idea that **UV entering the eyes affects the whole body ("sunburn through the eyes")** rests on **reports at the level of animal experiments**. **The Ministry of the Environment manual recommends sunglasses to prevent eye disease.**

> **[Verify]** Check the UV Index guide against the Ministry of the Environment's *UV Environmental Health Manual 2020*, and heat measures against the Ministry's heatstroke prevention information.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Being in the sun tires you because of the UV

**The main causes of tiredness are heat and dehydration.** **UV affects skin and eyes.** Take both sets of precautions, separately.

## Misunderstanding 2｜With sunscreen on, going out in summer is safe

**Sunscreen only protects the skin**; **it does not prevent heatstroke**. **Fluids, salt, shade and rest** are separate.

## Misunderstanding 3｜On a cloudy day neither UV nor heat is a problem

**Under cloud, UV is still 50–80% of a clear day** (Japan Meteorological Agency). **Humid, cloudy days also bring many cases of heatstroke** (sweat does not evaporate).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Sunglasses prevent eye fatigue

**The main purpose of sunglasses is to protect the eyes from UV (preventing cataracts and so on).** **Reducing glare** does relate to tiredness, but **it is a different matter from eye strain at a screen** (see our article on eye strain).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Summer fatigue goes away once autumn comes

**Accumulated lack of sleep and dehydration over the summer** is what Japanese people call **natsubate** (夏バテ, summer fatigue; see that article). **Getting the bedroom temperature, fluids and meals right during the summer** is the foundation.
