# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about fat intake?｜Japan Questions #368

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/fat-intake` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Fat makes you fat" and "cut out oil and you will lose weight" are stubborn misunderstandings; the opposite idea, "good fats are unlimited", is also spreading. This article sets out the approach of the Dietary Reference Intakes for Japanese (2025 edition) and shows where fat actually piles up on a Japanese table, which is useful if you are eating out or shopping here.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "fat should be cut as far as possible." The Dietary Reference Intakes set fat as a range, 20–30% of total energy, and describe it as essential for essential fatty acids and for absorbing fat-soluble vitamins. The key is amount and type: keep saturated fat (meat fat, butter, coconut oil) to 7% or less, make sure of the n-3 fats from fish, and watch the "hidden fat" in fried food, snacks and processed meat.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cut out fat and you lose weight

**Fat is an essential nutrient**, and **extreme restriction leads to shortages of fat-soluble vitamins and essential fatty acids**. **Overall energy balance** decides your weight.

## Misunderstanding 2｜"Good oils" are unlimited

**Even olive oil is about 110 kcal per tablespoon.** **Choosing the type and managing the amount are two separate things** (see our article on cooking oils).

## Misunderstanding 3｜Japanese food is low in fat

**Traditional washoku is low in fat**, but **today's Japanese fat intake is about 28–29%, close to the upper limit**. **Fried food, mayonnaise, snacks and processed meat** are the drivers.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Trans fats are a big problem in Japan too

**The Food Safety Commission assesses the Japanese average intake as below the WHO guide (under 1%).** **There is no labelling requirement either.** **In Japan, saturated fat is the bigger issue.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Fish oil is still fat, so hold back

**The n-3 fats in fish (EPA, DHA) have a set adequate intake and are fats you should make sure of.** **Fish 2–3 times a week** is the practical route. **Food before supplements** is the rule.
