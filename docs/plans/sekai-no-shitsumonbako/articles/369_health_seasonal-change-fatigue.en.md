# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about feeling unwell when the seasons change?｜Japan Questions #369

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/seasonal-change-fatigue` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"I always feel off when the seasons change" is something you will hear constantly in Japan, where spring and autumn bring sharp swings in temperature, pressure and daylight. This article looks at what those swings do to the body, and how far the popular Japanese phrase "disturbed autonomic nerves" (自律神経の乱れ) can be taken.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "feeling unwell at the change of seasons is either imaginary or a disease called 'autonomic dysfunction' (自律神経失調症)." Sudden temperature gaps, pressure changes and shifts in daylight hours strain temperature regulation and the sleep rhythm, and sluggishness, headaches and drowsiness become more likely. That is a physiological response many people have. The measures are: adjust clothing and bedding, fix your wake-up time and get morning light, and take a bath. Anything that lasts 2 weeks or more, however, belongs to medical care.

## What is happening

| Change | Effect on the body (general explanation) | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily temperature swing (10°C or more)** | **Strain on temperature regulation** → sluggishness, tiredness. **Chilly mornings and evenings stiffen the neck and shoulders** | **Adjust with layers** (keep the "three necks" — neck, wrists, ankles — warm); match bedding to the season |
| **Pressure changes (spring and autumn fronts, typhoons)** | Many people report **headaches, joint pain and dizziness** ("weather sickness" 気象病 / "weather pain" 天気痛). One theory is that **a pressure sensor in the inner ear is involved** | **Get enough sleep**, **prepare using the forecast**, **see a doctor if the pain is severe** |
| **Change in daylight hours** | **Body clock drifts** → hard to get up, hard to sleep at night | **Fix your wake-up time; get morning light** |
| **Pollen and dust** | Nose, eye and skin symptoms | See our article on pollen |
| **Life changes (April and September)** | Stress of job transfers and the new school term (Japan's school and business year starts in April) | Rest and sleep |

- **"Autonomic dysfunction" (自律神経失調症, jiritsu shinkei shitchō-shō)** is **a term used customarily in Japan**; **it is not an established disease name matching the international classification (ICD)**. It is used as an umbrella for symptoms; **identifying the cause and managing it is a job for a medical institution**.
- **"Weather sickness" and "weather pain"** are **terms that spread in Japan in the 2010s**. **Research links falling pressure with headaches and dizziness**, but **the mechanism is still being worked out**.
- **Feeling unwell in autumn** often overlaps with **carried-over summer fatigue (natsubate, 夏バテ)** (see that article).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Feeling off at the change of seasons is imaginary

**Temperature gaps, pressure and daylight are measurable changes in the environment**, and they strain temperature regulation and sleep. **Adjusting clothing, bedding and wake-up time to the environment** is the practical response.

## Misunderstanding 2｜I have caught a disease called "autonomic dysfunction"

**It is an umbrella term for symptoms, not an established disease name.** Behind the symptoms there may be **thyroid problems, anaemia, depression or a sleep disorder**. **If it persists, have the cause checked at a medical institution.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Weather headaches are all in your head

**Research links pressure changes with headaches**, and "weather sickness" is gaining recognition. **People with migraine especially.** **If the pain is severe or frequent**, see a headache clinic or a neurologist.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Get fit and the seasons will stop affecting you

**A habit of exercise helps stabilise temperature regulation and sleep**, but **it does not make the effects of pressure and daylight disappear**. **Overdoing exercise tires you out instead.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Only spring and autumn need care

**The rainy season, typhoons and winter temperature swings** cause the same problems by the same mechanism. **Noticing "big temperature swings this week" in the forecast** is the measure people in Japan share.
