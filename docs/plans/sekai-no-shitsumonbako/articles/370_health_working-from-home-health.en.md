# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about staying healthy when working from home?｜Japan Questions #370

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/working-from-home-health` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Working from home became established in Japan from 2020. Alongside "no commute, how relaxing" came back pain, eye strain, too little movement and isolation. This article sets out the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) telework guideline and how to set up a workable space in a Japanese home, where desks and dining tables are often small and low.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "working from home is easier on the body." The commute walk (several thousand steps a day) disappears, posture collapses when you work at the dining table or on the sofa, and the natural punctuation of breaks vanishes, so sitting time and eye strain both increase. The MHLW telework guideline gives targets for desk, chair, lighting and screen position, and calls for a break every hour.

## Guideline targets (MHLW Telework Guideline and Guideline on Work with Information Devices)

| Item | Target |
|---|---|
| **Chair** | Height-adjustable, **soles of the feet flat on the floor**, with a backrest. With a dining chair, add **a cushion and a footrest** |
| **Desk** | **A height at which your elbows are at 90 degrees** (roughly **60–72 cm**). Make up for a low dining table with **a monitor stand** |
| **Screen** | **Top edge below eye level**, **viewing distance 40 cm or more**, **laptop on a stand with an external keyboard** to raise your line of sight |
| **Lighting** | **300 lux or more on the work surface**; avoid reflections on the screen (do not sit with your back to a window) |
| **Breaks** | **No more than 1 hour of continuous work, then a 10–15-minute pause.** **Break up sitting every 30 minutes to 1 hour** |
| **Temperature and humidity** | **Room temperature 18–28°C (64–82°F), humidity 40–70%** (the office hygiene standards as a guide) |
| **Movement** | **The commute walk (several thousand steps) is gone** → **deliberately go for a walk, take the stairs** (Physical Activity Guide 2023) |
| **Working practices** | **A clear start and finish to the day, reporting your breaks**, **preventing long hours** (Telework Guideline) |

- The MHLW's *Guideline for the Appropriate Introduction and Implementation of Telework* (2021, **revised July 2025**) says **setting up the work environment is the responsibility of both employer and worker**, and provides **a checklist for the home environment**.
- **Using a laptop as it is** is **the biggest cause of neck and shoulder strain** (see our article on "smartphone neck"). **An external keyboard plus the screen on a stand** makes a big difference on its own.
- **Isolation and mental health**: the guideline calls for **opportunities for communication to be maintained** and points to **contact points (the occupational physician, the Kokoro no Mimi mental-health portal)**.

> **[Verify]** Check the guideline figures (desk height, lux, temperature and humidity) against the MHLW materials and the Guideline on Work with Information Devices.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Working from home is easy on the body

**The commute walk disappears, sitting increases and posture slips.** **Pay deliberate attention to steps, breaks, and the desk and chair.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The dining table and a laptop are fine

**The table is too high, the chair too low and the laptop pulls your gaze down** — **the classic recipe for neck, shoulder and back strain**. **A monitor stand, an external keyboard and a footrest** fix it for a few thousand yen.

## Misunderstanding 3｜I can take breaks whenever I like, so no problem

**Without punctuation, breaks usually get fewer, not more.** **Set a timer to stand every hour**, and **go outside at lunch**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜I can make up the missing exercise at the weekend

**Weekday sitting time is a separate problem from exercise** (see our article on sitting too much). **Spread it out with a daily walk, stairs and housework.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜My home set-up is my own responsibility

**The guideline sets out roles for both employer and worker**, and asks that **rules on lending equipment and covering costs** be agreed between management and staff. **This is something you can raise with your company.**
