# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how often to shop and food waste?｜Japan Questions #372

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/shopping-frequency-and-food-waste` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Buying in bulk saves money." "Shopping every day is fresher." Opinions on how often to shop differ from country to country. Japan has its own conditions — small fridges, a supermarket close by, many dual-income households — and the government has set the question out in terms of food loss (食品ロス, shokuhin rosu, the Japanese term for food waste). Here is what the numbers say.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "bulk buying is always economical." Buying in bulk lowers the unit price, but once you exceed your fridge's capacity, food goes unused and gets thrown away — feeding straight into the main causes of household food waste (leftovers, expired items, over-trimming). For a Japanese household, "a planned shop once or twice a week, plus topping up nearby for what runs short" is realistic, and knowing what is in your fridge works for both saving money and cutting waste.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bulk buying saves money

**Even at a lower unit price, anything you cannot use up is thrown away — a loss.** The ceiling is **what fits your fridge and one week's meals.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Shopping every day means no waste

**Daily shopping adds impulse buys and the cost of travel and time.** **A planned shop once or twice a week, plus topping up** is what most households actually do.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Avoid items near their date and reach to the back

**If you will eat it soon, the front (the nearer date) is fine, and it reduces the shop's waste** ("temae-dori"). The test is **whether you can use it up at home before the date.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Buy when it is cheap and freeze it — nothing is wasted

**Frozen food you "cannot remember buying" never gets used and is thrown away from the back of the freezer.** **Write the date on it and use frozen food within 2–3 weeks** (see our freezing article).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Food waste is a problem for shops and restaurants

**Household waste is about half of the total (2.24 of 4.61 million tonnes).** **"Forgot I had already bought it" is the main cause,** so knowing what is in your fridge is the most effective measure.
