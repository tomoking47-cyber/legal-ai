# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about cooking for one?｜Japan Questions #373

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/cooking-for-one` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Single-person households are about 38% of all households in Japan (2020 Census) — the largest group. If you live alone here, you may feel that "cooking one portion costs more than buying" or that "ingredients always go to waste". We answer with the official approach to nutrition and the practices Japanese households actually use.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "cooking for one is expensive and wasteful, so buying ready-made is the rational choice." The waste comes from trying to cook a single portion every time. Switch to "cook 2–3 portions and split them between fridge and freezer" and "buy ingredients in combinations you can use up", and cost, nutrition and time all improve. For nutrition, it is enough to aim for the Japanese Food Guide's "staple, main dish, side dish" within each meal.

| Shape of one meal | **Staple (shushoku, rice or bread), main dish (shusai), side dish (fukusai)** together (**a soup with vegetables in it counts as a side dish**) | Japanese Food Guide Spinning Top (食事バランスガイド) |

| Practice | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Cook in batches and split** | **Cook 3–5 portions of rice and freeze them**; **make 2–3 portions of each dish** (see our batch-cooking article) |
| **Combinations you can use up** | A quarter cabbage → stir-fry, miso soup, salad ／ one block of tofu → half as hiyayakko (chilled tofu), half in miso soup |
| **Frozen and pre-cut vegetables** | **Even at a higher price, they come out cheaper if nothing is thrown away.** Frozen broccoli, spinach, mixed vegetables |
| **Soups packed with ingredients** | Vegetables, tofu and mushrooms in miso soup or soup → **doubles as side dish and soup** |
| **One pot or one frying pan** | Less washing up |
| **How to use ready-made food (nakashoku, 中食)** | **Buy the main dish and add a vegetable side and a soup.** Watch the **salt in deli dishes (sōzai, 惣菜)** |
| **Buy seasonings in small sizes** | They deteriorate after opening (see our seasonings article) |

## Misunderstanding 1｜For one person, buying is cheaper

**People living alone who rely on ready-made and restaurant food tend to spend more on food than those who mostly cook** (Family Income and Expenditure Survey). **"Cook in batches and split"** brings the unit cost of home cooking down.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Unless I cook one portion each time, food is wasted

**Cooking 2–3 portions and keeping them in the fridge (2–3 days) or freezer (2–3 weeks)** is the standard pattern for cooking alone. **Cooking every time leaves more ingredients over.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜One person cannot use up vegetables

**Frozen vegetables, pre-cut vegetables and soups full of ingredients** solve this. **Buy a whole cabbage or carrots and turn them into 3–4 dishes.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜A balanced diet is hard

Just check **whether each meal has a staple (rice), a main (meat, fish, egg or tofu) and a side (vegetables).** **Put vegetables in the miso soup and it counts as the side.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Deli food and convenience-store food are bad for you

**Buy the main dish and add vegetables and a soup,** and **there is no nutritional problem.** Keep an eye on **salt (a guide of 2–3 g per meal) and how often you eat fried food.**
