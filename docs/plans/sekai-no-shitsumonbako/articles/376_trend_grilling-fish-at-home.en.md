# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about grilling fish at home?｜Japan Questions #376

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/grilling-fish-at-home` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Grilled fish is a staple of the Japanese breakfast, yet fewer households grill it at home: "the skin sticks", "the smell lingers", "I am afraid of undercooking it". Here is how to choose between the built-in fish grill, a frying pan and an oven, and where the food-safety line sits.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "fish can only be cooked in the fish grill." (Most Japanese gas hobs have a pull-out grill drawer, the sakana-yaki grill.) A frying pan lined with baking parchment or foil, or a countertop oven toaster, cooks fish just as well and gives off less smell. The points that apply everywhere are "pat dry", "skin side first", "do not move it" and "cook through to the centre (75°C for 1 minute, or until the flesh turns white and lifts off the bone)". Frozen fillets are thawed before grilling.

## By equipment

| Equipment | Method | Advantages | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fish grill** (built into the hob) | Preheat 1–2 min; **skin side up (single-sided grill) or double-sided**; medium heat **8–12 min** | Direct flame, good browning; fat drips away | Smell and cleaning (see our grill article) |
| **Frying pan** | Line with **baking parchment (kukkingu shīto, クッキングシート) or fish-grilling foil**; **skin side down, medium heat 4–5 min → turn, 3–4 min**; a lid helps cook it through | **Does not stick, less smell, easy to clean** | Parchment's heat limit (250°C); never heat an empty lined pan |
| **Oven toaster** (small countertop toaster oven) | On foil, **10–15 min**; keep it off the heating elements | Easy | Dripping fat can catch fire (see our toaster article) |
| **Oven** | 200–220°C, **12–15 min** | Cooks several at once | Preheat |

## Points that apply everywhere

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Pat dry** | With kitchen paper. **Salt the fish, leave 10 minutes and wipe off the moisture** — less fishy smell, crisper skin |
| **Skin side first** | Stops the skin shrinking and curling, and crisps it. **Score the skin** to reduce curling |
| **Do not move it** | Sticking is caused by **moving the fish too soon.** **Once browned, it releases by itself** |
| **Is it cooked?** | **The flesh turns white and opaque and lifts off the bone.** **Centre at 75°C for at least 1 minute** (MHLW) |
| **Frozen fillets** | **Thaw in the fridge first.** **Grilled from frozen, the outside burns while the inside stays raw** |
| **Anisakis** (a parasite in some fish) | **Killed by heating (70°C or above, or 60°C for 1 minute)** (see our fish-storage article) |

- **The smell of grilled fish** comes mainly from **the fish's fat and the charring of the skin.** **Use a frying pan with parchment**, **turn the extractor fan to high before you start**, and **wash the grill's drip tray straight after.**
- **Himono (干物, salted dried fish) is high in salt** (one butterflied dried horse mackerel, aji no hiraki, 60–85 g edible portion, carries about 1–1.5 g salt equivalent). Keep it **within your salt target.**

> **[Verify]** Check the heating temperature and the anisakis temperatures against Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) materials. Grilling times are guides and vary with the thickness of the fillet.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Fish can only be cooked in the fish grill

**A frying pan lined with baking parchment** is now one of the mainstream methods in Japanese homes. **Less smell, easy to clean.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The skin sticks because of the fish

**Moisture and moving it too soon** are the causes. **Pat dry, skin side first, leave it alone.** **Lining the pan makes it certain.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Afraid of undercooking, so grill it for a long time

**Overcooking makes the flesh dry and shrunken.** The guide is **white, opaque flesh that lifts off the bone.** **For thick fillets, put a lid on and steam-grill.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Frozen fillets can be grilled from frozen

**The outside burns while the inside stays raw.** **Thaw in the fridge** (move it there the night before).

## Misunderstanding 5｜The smell is unavoidable

**A frying pan with parchment, the extractor fan on first, and washing the drip tray straight away** cut it greatly. **Dry-heating used tea leaves or coffee grounds in the grill after cooking fish** is a trick shared among people living in Japan.
