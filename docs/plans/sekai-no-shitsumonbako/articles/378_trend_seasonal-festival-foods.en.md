# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about Japanese festival foods (Setsubun, Hinamatsuri, Tanabata, the winter solstice and more)?｜Japan Questions #378

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/seasonal-festival-foods` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japan's annual festivals each come with a set food, and visitors often ask "why that food, on that day?" This article covers the origins, what actually happens today (buying versus making, food waste), and safety — beans that small children can choke on, and mochi rice cakes that older people can choke on.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "festival foods are ancient rules, the same across Japan." Most spread from the Edo period (1603–1868) onward, they differ by region, and some — like ehōmaki — were commercialised nationwide only from the 1990s. Their practical value is "a seasonal marker: eating the season's ingredients together as a family". On safety, two official warnings stand out: Setsubun beans (do not give them to children aged 5 and under) and New Year mochi (choking among older people).

## The main festival foods

| Festival | Date | Food | Origin / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Setsubun** (start of spring) | Around 3 February | **Roasted soybeans (one per year of your age), ehōmaki (a thick sushi roll eaten facing the year's lucky direction), sardines** | Beans ward off evil. **Ehōmaki went nationwide in the 1990s through convenience stores.** **Do not give beans to children aged 5 and under** (Consumer Affairs Agency: choking and aspiration accidents) |
| **Hinamatsuri** (Girls' Day) | 3 March | **Chirashi-zushi (scattered sushi), hamaguri clam soup, hishi-mochi (diamond-shaped rice cakes), hina-arare (sweet rice puffs), shirozake (sweet white sake; amazake for children)** | A clam's two shells fit only each other = a good marriage |
| **Tango no Sekku** (Children's Day) | 5 May | **Kashiwa-mochi (rice cake in an oak leaf, Kantō), chimaki (rice dumpling in bamboo leaf, Kansai)** | The oak keeps its old leaves until new buds appear = the family line continues |
| **Tanabata** (Star Festival) | 7 July | **Sōmen (thin wheat noodles)** | From the Chinese sweet sakubei (索餅) |
| **Doyō no Ushi no Hi** (midsummer "Day of the Ox") | Late July | **Eel (unagi)** | Traced to an Edo-period advertising idea (attributed to Hiraga Gennai). **Eel is an endangered species** (Ministry of the Environment Red List) |
| **Jūgoya** (harvest moon) | September–October | **Tsukimi dango (moon-viewing rice dumplings), taro, chestnuts** | Thanks for the harvest |
| **Tōji** (winter solstice) | Around 22 December | **Kabocha squash, a yuzu bath**, foods with "n" in their name | Winter nutrition (kabocha is rich in beta-carotene); a play on words for good luck |
| **New Year's Eve** | 31 December | **Toshikoshi soba** (year-crossing buckwheat noodles) | Long and thin, and easily cut = cutting off the year's misfortune |
| **New Year** | 1–3 January | **Osechi (boxed New Year dishes), zōni (mochi soup), mochi** | See our osechi article. **Choking on mochi** (Consumer Affairs Agency: older people) |
| **Nanakusa** (seven herbs) | 7 January | **Nanakusa-gayu (seven-herb rice porridge)** | Rests the stomach after New Year |

- **Ehōmaki food waste** makes the news every year, and MAFF has asked the industry for **pre-order sales and appropriate production volumes** (since 2019).
- **Choking on beans**: the Consumer Affairs Agency says **"do not give hard beans or nuts to children aged 5 and under"** (in 2021 the age was raised from 3 to 5). **In the windpipe they cause choking and pneumonia.**
- **Eel**: **the Japanese eel is listed as Endangered (category IB) on the Ministry of the Environment Red List** (since 2013).

> **[Verify]** Check the age limit for beans against the Consumer Affairs Agency alert (2021), the ehōmaki request against MAFF materials, and the eel's classification against the Ministry of the Environment Red List.

## Misunderstanding 1｜All festival foods are ancient traditions

**Ehōmaki dates from the 1990s; midsummer eel from Edo-period advertising.** **The "tradition" varies in age**, but their value as a seasonal marker is the same.

## Misunderstanding 2｜At Setsubun, children eat one bean for each year of their age

**Do not give them to children aged 5 and under** is the Consumer Affairs Agency's warning. **Choking and pneumonia from aspiration** happen every year. **Throw individually wrapped beans, and let the adults eat them.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The whole country eats the same thing

**Kashiwa-mochi (Kantō) versus chimaki (Kansai)**, **the shape of the mochi and the type of soup in zōni**, **sardines at Setsubun** — **regional differences are large.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Festival food should be home-made, not bought

**Most chirashi-zushi, ehōmaki and osechi are bought.** The essence is **"a marker eaten together as a family"**; whether you buy or make is a means.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You should eat eel on Doyō no Ushi no Hi

**The Japanese eel is an endangered species**, and **alternatives (anago conger eel, saury, or aubergine in the same kabayaki glaze) are spreading.** There is also a move to **choose eel from managed (certified) stocks if you do eat it.**
