# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about handling takeaway and delivery food?｜Japan Questions #379

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/takeout-and-delivery-food-safety` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Takeaway and delivery became part of everyday life in Japan from 2020, and with them came the questions "how many hours is it safe?" and "can I eat it tomorrow?". Here is the answer, based on the food-poisoning prevention approach of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and the hygiene rules that apply to the businesses.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "it was made in a restaurant, so it is safe to leave at home." The moment the food leaves the shop, temperature control passes to you; beyond 2 hours at room temperature (1 hour in summer) it sits too long in the range where bacteria multiply. The basics are "eat it as soon as you receive it", "if you keep leftovers, refrigerate and eat by the next day", "reheat until hot in the centre" — and never carry raw items (sashimi, raw salad) over to the next day.

## What to base your judgement on

| Item | Detail | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Time at room temperature | **Eat within 2 hours** (**within 1 hour if the air temperature is 30°C or above**). Bacteria multiply **between 10 and 60°C, and fastest at 30–40°C** | MHLW, food-poisoning prevention |
| Leftovers | **Refrigerate straight away (10°C or below), eat by the next day.** **Divide into small portions to cool** | Same |
| Reheating | **75°C at the centre for at least 1 minute** (**until the whole dish is hot, stirring as you go**) | Same |
| Never carry over | **Sashimi and raw sushi, raw vegetable salad, soft-cooked egg dishes, cream-based dishes, onigiri rice balls shaped by hand** | Same |
| The business side | **Since June 2021, hygiene management along HACCP lines has been mandatory.** **Labelling takeaway food with a use-by date and storage method is not compulsory for over-the-counter sales, but guidelines call for it** | MHLW |
| Delivery | **The longer the delivery, the longer the food sits in the danger range.** **Check whether an insulated hot or cold bag is used**, and **eat as soon as it arrives** | — |
| Containers | Check for a **"microwave-safe" mark (電子レンジ可).** **Plastic containers deform when oily food is heated in them** | See our storage-container article |

- In **2020 MHLW issued an alert on "preventing food poisoning from takeaway and delivery"**, asking **businesses to label food "eat today" and pack cooling packs, and consumers to "eat straight away or refrigerate".**
- The mechanism is the same as **food poisoning from bentō lunch boxes** (see our bentō article): **food that is safe when it leaves the kitchen is decided by the temperatures it meets afterwards.**
- **Takeaway curry and stew** are the classic case for **Clostridium perfringens** (see our slow-cooking article), and **leaving them at room temperature is the greatest risk.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Restaurant food is safe to leave out at home

**Temperature control passes to the consumer the moment the food leaves the shop.** **Within 2 hours (1 hour in summer).**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Anything is fine the next day if it was in the fridge

**Do not carry over raw food, soft-cooked eggs or cream-based dishes.** **Cooked dishes: by the next day, reheated through to the centre.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The microwave kills the bacteria

**If the food is only partly warm, the centre is still cold.** **Stir and heat the whole dish.** **Spores (Clostridium perfringens) survive**, so **not leaving the food out comes first.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Delivery is safe because the rider keeps it cool

**Whether food is kept hot or cold varies by platform and shop.** **Eating as soon as it arrives** is the only sure method. In **summer, you may decide against ordering raw food** for delivery.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The container can go straight into the microwave

Check for the **"microwave-safe" mark.** **Oily dishes can exceed the container's heat limit.** **Transferring to a plate** is the safe option.
