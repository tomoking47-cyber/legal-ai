# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about handling and storing kitchen knives safely?｜Japan Questions #380

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/kitchen-knife-safety-and-storage` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Separately from our article on sharpening, this one answers the safety questions: "isn't a sharp knife dangerous?", "how should I store it?", "when can a child start using one?". It draws on the kinds of knife found in Japanese homes and on accident data from the Consumer Affairs Agency, and includes a note on Japan's knife-carrying law.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a dull knife is safer." A dull knife needs force, slips, and is a cause of cut hands. The basics of safety are: "use a sharp knife, with the correct grip and the 'cat's paw', on a stable cutting board"; "wash it, dry it straight away and store it with the blade covered"; and "children can start in the early years of primary school, with an adult watching."

## What to base your judgement on

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sharpness and safety | **A dull knife needs force and slips, cutting your hand.** **A sharp knife needs little force and is easier to control** |
| Grip | **Hold the handle; do not rest your index finger along the spine of the blade (in ordinary homes, a plain handle grip).** **The other hand is the "cat's paw" (neko no te, 猫の手): fingers curled, holding the food with the first knuckles** |
| Cutting board | **A stable surface; a damp cloth underneath stops it sliding.** **The right height is one where the tip of the blade reaches the board with your arm extended** |
| Washing | **Straight after use, blade pointing away from you, sponge working from the spine side.** **Never leave it in the sink** (the classic accident: cutting your hand on a blade you cannot see) |
| Drying and storage | **Wipe and dry straight away** (rust). **Cover the blade (knife block, sheath; on a magnetic strip, never with the edge facing out).** **Do not put it loose in a drawer** |
| Children | **From the early years of primary school (about age 6–8), with an adult alongside.** Start with **a children's knife (rounded tip).** **Teach the "cat's paw" first** |
| Types | **Santoku (all-purpose), nakiri (vegetables), deba (fish), yanagiba (sashimi), petty (small utility).** **Stainless steel resists rust; carbon steel (hagane) cuts better but rusts** |
| Disposal | **Wrap the blade in newspaper, write "刃物" (hamono, "blade") on it, and put it out with your municipality's non-burnable or hazardous waste** |

- Accident data (the Consumer Affairs Agency's accident information databank, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's near-miss survey "Dangers lurking in the kitchen" and the like) report kitchen cuts most often **"while washing up", "from a knife left in the sink" or "when the cutting board slipped".**
- **Food-poisoning prevention**: **after cutting raw meat or fish, wash the knife with detergent and pour boiling water over it** (the same as in our cutting-board article). **Use separate knives for meat/fish and vegetables**, or **cut vegetables first, then meat and fish.**
- **The Firearms and Swords Control Act (銃刀法, jūtōhō), Article 22**: **carrying a blade whose blade body exceeds 6 cm in length without a legitimate reason is prohibited** (**scissors, folding knives and the like are exempted by cabinet order up to a blade body of 8 cm**). **Carry a newly bought knife home in its box or packaging**, and **for outdoor use keep it in a case and be clear about your purpose.**

> **[Verify]** Check the accident data against Consumer Affairs Agency materials, and the provisions of the Firearms and Swords Control Act against National Police Agency materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A dull knife is safer

**The opposite.** **Force, then a slip** is the pattern of accidents. **Sharpen regularly** (see our sharpening article).

## Misunderstanding 2｜Leave it soaking in the sink and wash it later

**The classic accident: cutting your hand on a blade you cannot see.** **Wash and dry it straight after use.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Do not let children touch knives

**From the early years of primary school, with an adult watching and a children's knife.** **The "cat's paw" first.** Japanese food education holds that **a child never allowed to touch a knife becomes an adult who does not know how to handle one.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜A magnetic knife holder is convenient and safe

Mounting it so **the edge is exposed and faces outward** is dangerous. **Edge towards the wall, at a height children cannot reach.** **Homes with children: a knife block with a door.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Just throw an unwanted knife in the bin

**Wrap it thickly in newspaper, write "刃物" (blade) and "キケン" (danger) on it, and put it out with your municipality's sorted waste.** This is a shared rule in Japan **to protect the people who collect the rubbish from injury.**
