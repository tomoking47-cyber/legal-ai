# Q. What do people get wrong about buying toiletries in Japan?｜Japan Questions #382

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/toiletries-to-buy-locally` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Half a suitcase of toiletries is unnecessary for Japan — almost everything is sold at any convenience store or drugstore, often in travel sizes, and often better. A few items are genuinely hard to find or different, and those are the ones worth packing.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese toiletries will be unfamiliar or hard to find. Drugstores stock everything a traveller needs, labelled in Japanese but recognisable by shape and pictures, and hotels supply the basics. What is worth bringing: deodorant if you rely on a strong antiperspirant, tampons with applicators if you prefer them, contact lens solution if you use a specific brand, and any medication (see the medicine article).

## Easy to buy vs worth packing

| Buy here (any drugstore or convenience store) | Japanese label | Note |
|---|---|---|

| Shampoo, conditioner, body wash | シャンプー / コンディショナー / ボディソープ | **Pump bottles and refill packs**; travel sets (トラベルセット) at convenience stores |
| Face wash, cleanser, toner, moisturiser | 洗顔 / クレンジング / 化粧水 / 乳液 | Excellent and cheap (see the skincare names article) |
| Sunscreen | 日焼け止め | Among the best in the world; see the SPF/PA article |
| Razors, shaving cream | カミソリ / シェービング | Disposable razors everywhere |
| Sanitary pads | 生理用ナプキン | Wide range; **tampons (タンポン) exist but fewer brands, often without applicator** |
| Lip balm, hand cream | リップクリーム / ハンドクリーム | Cheap, good |
| Cotton buds, cotton pads, tissues, wet wipes | 綿棒 / コットン / ティッシュ / ウェットティッシュ | Pocket tissues free at stations |

| Pain relief, cold medicine | 頭痛薬 / 風邪薬 | OTC classes; see that article |

| Worth packing | Why |
|---|---|
| **Deodorant / antiperspirant** | Japanese products are **milder** (roll-ons and sprays; few strong sticks). Bring yours if you rely on it |
| **Tampons with applicators** | Available but limited; pads dominate |
| **Specific contact lens solution or medication** | Brands differ |
| **Large-size shoes/clothes items** | See the sizing article |
| **Strong-hold hair products for very thick or curly hair** | Range is oriented to straight hair |

- Convenience stores sell **single-use travel sizes** (shampoo, toothbrush kits, razors) at **¥100–400**; hotels supply toothbrush, razor, comb and shampoo (see the hotel article).
- **Drugstore chains** are on every high street and open **until 22:00 or later**; many in tourist areas have **English-speaking staff and tax-free counters** (see the drugstores article).

> **[Verify]** Product availability is observational; tax-free rules with the National Tax Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Pack everything, nothing will be familiar

**Everything basic is available**, identifiable by shape and picture, and often of higher quality than at home. Pack for **your specific needs**, not for the category.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Japanese deodorant works like Western antiperspirant

**It is milder.** Sprays and roll-ons dominate, and strong aluminium-based sticks are rare. **Bring your own** if it matters to you.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Feminine products will be a problem

**Pads are excellent and everywhere**; tampons exist (fewer brands, mostly non-applicator). **Bring applicator tampons** if you prefer them.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hotel amenities are the only option late at night

**Convenience stores are 24 hours** and stock travel-size everything. Drugstores close late.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Buying toiletries is a waste of tax-free allowance

Toiletries count as **consumables** for tax-free (¥5,000+ same day, sealed until departure under the current rules; the packaging requirement ends 1 November 2026 — see the tax-free article). A drugstore haul of skincare and sunscreen is a common tax-free purchase.
