# Q. What do people get wrong about when to take shoes off in Japan?｜Japan Questions #383

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/shoes-off-situations` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Visitors know shoes come off at the door of a home. What they do not expect is the number of other places — fitting rooms, temple halls, some restaurants, clinics, school gyms — and the small rituals of the threshold that mark someone as knowing what they are doing.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the shoes-off rule applies only in homes. It applies wherever there is a raised floor or a step at the entrance: ryokan, temple interiors, tatami rooms in restaurants, fitting rooms, clinics, gyms, some izakaya. The cues are a step up, a row of slippers, or a shoe locker. The technique: step out of shoes onto the raised floor without stepping back onto the lower floor in socks, turn shoes to face the door, and never wear toilet slippers outside the toilet.

## Where shoes come off

| Place | Shoes | Slippers provided? | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Homes, ryokan, minshuku** | Off at the genkan | Yes (corridor slippers); **separate toilet slippers** | Step up, shoe cabinet |
| **Temple halls, some shrines' inner areas** | Off; carry in a bag or leave on racks | Sometimes | Sign, racks of shoes, plastic bags |
| **Restaurants with tatami / zashiki seating** | Off | Sometimes | Raised floor, shoe lockers with wooden keys |
| **Izakaya (some)** | Off at entrance | — | Lockers at the door |
| **Fitting rooms** | **Off before stepping onto the raised floor** | — | Raised platform, sometimes a mat |
| **Clinics, dentists, some pharmacies** | Off; slippers provided | Yes | Shoe rack at door |
| **Schools, gyms, some museums** | Off; indoor shoes or slippers | Yes or bring | Shoe lockers |
| **Castles, historic houses** | Off; **socks recommended** | Sometimes plastic bags for your shoes | Signage |
| Offices, shops, stations, most restaurants | **Shoes on** | — | Level floor |

- The rule is about **the boundary between outside (dirty) and inside (clean)**; the step up marks it (see the genkan article). **Stepping back down onto the lower floor in socks** then onto the raised floor is the classic error.
- **Socks**: many places expect socks, and **bare feet on tatami** or in a temple can be frowned on; **holes in socks are noticed**. Carry a spare pair in spring and summer if you wear sandals.
- **Fitting rooms**: the raised floor is treated as indoors — **shoes off, and do not let clothes touch the floor**; many shops provide a **face cover (フェイスカバー)** to protect garments from makeup.

> **[Verify]** These are conventions; temple and castle rules are posted at each site.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Only in homes

**Any raised floor**: temples, tatami restaurants, fitting rooms, clinics. **Look for the step and the slippers.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sandals in summer avoid the issue

**Bare feet on tatami and in temples are unwelcome.** Carry **socks in your bag** in summer.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Toilet slippers are just slippers

**They stay in the toilet.** Wearing them out into the corridor is the mistake every guest makes once and remembers.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Step out of your shoes any way you like

**Step up directly out of the shoes**, then **turn to face the room and nudge the shoes to point at the door** with your hand (or kneel to do it in a formal setting). Never step back down in socks.

## Misunderstanding 5｜In a fitting room, shoes can stay on if you are quick

**The raised floor is indoors.** Shoes off on the lower level, step up; staff will ask otherwise. Slip-on shoes make shopping easier for exactly this reason.
