# Q. What do people get wrong about what a Japanese convenience store can do?｜Japan Questions #385

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/convenience-store-services` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Food gets the attention; the services are the reason residents visit a convenience store several times a week. For a visitor, the same counter and machines solve a surprising share of travel problems — printing, cash, tickets, parcels — if you know they exist.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a convenience store is a shop. It is also an ATM that takes foreign cards, a post office counter, a printer and scanner, a ticket agency for concerts and buses, a bill-payment point, a parcel drop for luggage forwarding and online returns, a free toilet, and a source of hot water and Wi-Fi. Most of it works without Japanese if you know which machine to stand in front of.

## What the counter and machines do

| Service | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **ATM (foreign cards)** | Seven Bank ATMs (7-Eleven) and Lawson/Japan Post ATMs accept **Visa, Mastercard, Plus, Cirrus, UnionPay**; **English menus** | Fee ~¥110–220 per withdrawal plus your bank's charge; limit often ¥100,000 |
| **Multi-copy machine** | **Print from a USB or smartphone** (app or QR upload), **copy, scan to USB, print photos**; **print boarding passes and e-tickets** | Colour A4 ~¥50–60; **print a PDF via "netprint"/"PrintSmash" apps** |
| **Ticket terminal (Loppi / multi-copy)** | **Concert, theme park, museum, highway bus, some sports tickets**; pay at the counter | Some need a Japanese phone number |
| **Parcels** | **Send TA-Q-BIN luggage forwarding**; **drop online-shopping returns**; **receive parcels** ordered to the store | See the luggage article |
| **Bill payment** | Utility bills, taxes, online orders with a barcode slip — **cash at the counter** | Residents' use |
| **Post** | **Stamps and postcards**; a **mailbox** outside many stores | Not international parcels (post office) |
| **Toilet** | Free, clean, usually available (**ask: "toire ii desu ka?"**) | Some city-centre stores restrict |
| **Hot water, microwave, cutlery** | For instant noodles and bento bought there | Ask at the counter |
| **Wi-Fi** | Free with registration (each chain's app or email) | Patchy but useful |
| **Photo ID** | Some multi-copy machines print **passport-style photos** | For residence-card renewals etc. |
| **Eat-in counter** | Tables and chairs; **tax 10% instead of 8%** if you use them | See the tax article |

- Japan has about **56,000 convenience stores**; **Seven Bank alone runs over 27,000 ATMs**, and they are the most reliable foreign-card cash source in the country (see the cash article).
- **Printing a document from your phone**: upload to the chain's print service (7-Eleven "netprint", FamilyMart/Lawson "PrintSmash" or "network print"), receive a code, enter it at the machine — **no Japanese needed beyond the English menu**.
- The counter staff **cannot** exchange currency, sell SIM cards for tourists at all stores, or help with lost property beyond the store.

> **[Verify]** ATM fees and limits with Seven Bank / Lawson Bank; service availability varies by store and chain.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is just a shop

The **ATM, printer, ticket machine and parcel counter** are why residents go. Visitors mostly need the **ATM and the printer**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜I cannot use the copy machine without Japanese

**Every multi-copy machine has an English menu.** Printing from a phone via the chain's app is a five-minute task.

## Misunderstanding 3｜ATMs only take Japanese cards

**Seven Bank and Japan Post/Lawson ATMs accept most foreign cards**, in English, at almost any hour. Your home bank's fee is the larger cost.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You need a post office to send luggage ahead

**Any convenience store with a Yamato counter** takes a suitcase for next-day delivery to a hotel or airport. Fill in the slip (staff help).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Toilets are for customers only

Most stores allow use; **asking is polite**, and buying a drink is the natural thank-you. **Some central-Tokyo stores lock theirs** — look for the sign.
