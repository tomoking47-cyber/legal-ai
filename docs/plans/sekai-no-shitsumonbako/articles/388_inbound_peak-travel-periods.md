# Q. What do people get wrong about Golden Week, Obon and New Year travel?｜Japan Questions #388

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/peak-travel-periods` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Three times a year most of Japan travels at once, and visitors who arrive in the middle of it find full trains, doubled hotel prices and closed businesses. The dates are fixed and known years ahead; planning around them is the single easiest way to improve a trip.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japan is a year-round destination with even demand. Three domestic holiday peaks — Golden Week (late April to early May), Obon (mid-August) and New Year (late December to early January) — move tens of millions of people, sell out reserved Shinkansen seats a month ahead, and push hotel prices up 50–200%. Sightseeing is not impossible, but it is crowded and expensive, and some businesses close for New Year.

## The three peaks

| Period | Dates | What happens | Advice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Golden Week** | **29 April – 5 May** (four public holidays; often a 7–10-day block) | **Domestic travel peak**; Shinkansen reserved seats sell out **a month ahead**; hotels in Kyoto, Kanazawa, Hakone at **2–3× price**; expressways jammed (**40 km queues**) | Book everything 1–2 months ahead or **avoid**; big cities are actually quieter (Tokyo empties) |
| **Obon** | **around 13–16 August** (not a public holiday, but most companies close) | Return to hometowns; **outbound trains and roads jammed 10–13 Aug, inbound 15–17 Aug**; heat at its peak | Stay in one place; **visit ancestral festivals (bon odori)** |
| **New Year** | **29 December – 3 January** | Outbound 28–30 Dec, return 2–4 Jan; **shops, museums, restaurants and government offices close 31 Dec–3 Jan** (department stores reopen 1 or 2 Jan); shrines packed | Plan for closures; see the New Year article |
| Secondary | **Silver Week (September, when holidays align)**, **school holidays (late July–August, late March–early April)**, **cherry blossom (late March–early April)**, **autumn leaves in Kyoto (mid–late November)** | Hotel prices up; Kyoto foliage weekends rival Golden Week | Book Kyoto in November early |

- **Golden Week transport**: JR Group reports **about 10–12 million Shinkansen passengers** over the period; reserved seats on the Tokaido line hit **100% on peak days**.
- **Hotel prices**: Kyoto averages roughly **¥15,000–20,000** in an ordinary month and **¥40,000+** on peak Golden Week and November foliage weekends (market data).
- **Public holidays** in Japan number **16 a year**; when one falls on a Sunday, Monday is a substitute holiday, which is how long weekends form.

> **[Verify]** Holiday dates with the Cabinet Office's list of national holidays; passenger figures with JR Group's seasonal reports.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Holidays make Japan more festive to visit

**Golden Week and Obon are domestic travel, not festivals.** You compete with 100 million residents for trains and rooms. The exception is Obon's **bon odori dances** in towns.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Everything is open at New Year

**Most businesses close 31 December to 3 January**; museums and many restaurants for longer. Convenience stores, department stores (from 1 or 2 Jan) and shrines are open. Plan meals.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Unreserved Shinkansen seats are a fallback

**Unreserved cars are standing-room-only** on peak days, with queues on the platform. **Reserve a month ahead**, or travel on the peak-flow's counter-direction.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Tokyo is worst during Golden Week

**Tokyo empties** as residents leave; museums and restaurants are quieter than usual. The crowds are in **Kyoto, resorts and on the roads**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cherry blossom season is the only expensive spring window

**Golden Week follows a month later** and is more expensive for hotels. **Mid-April to late April** between the two is a relative lull.
