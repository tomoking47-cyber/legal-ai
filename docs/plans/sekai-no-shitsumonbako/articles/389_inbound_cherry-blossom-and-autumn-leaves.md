# Q. What do people get wrong about cherry blossom and autumn foliage timing?｜Japan Questions #389

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/cherry-blossom-and-autumn-leaves` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The two seasons that sell Japan — sakura in spring and kōyō in autumn — are also the ones most likely to disappoint through timing. The bloom lasts about a week in any one place, the date moves by up to two weeks year to year, and the forecasts that residents rely on are not the ones visitors read.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that cherry blossom is "late March to early April" as a fixed window. Full bloom in Tokyo has ranged from 22 March (2021, 2023) to about 4 April (2024) in the last decade, lasts about seven days, and moves north by roughly a week per 300 km. The Japan Meteorological Corporation and Weathernews issue forecasts from January that update weekly; booking flexible dates or a route that follows the front is how residents handle it. Autumn foliage is more predictable, peaking in Kyoto in the second half of November.

## Cherry blossom (Somei-yoshino)

| Place | Typical full bloom | Range (recent decade) |
|---|---|---|
| Fukuoka | ~28 March | 22 Mar – 3 Apr |
| **Tokyo** | **~30 March** | **22 Mar – 5 Apr** |
| **Kyoto / Osaka** | **~2 April** | 27 Mar – 8 Apr |
| Kanazawa | ~7 April | — |
| Sendai | ~10 April | — |
| **Sapporo** | **~1 May** | late Apr – early May |

- **Duration**: first bloom to full bloom **about 5–7 days**; full bloom holds **about a week**, less in rain or wind. The petal-fall ("sakura fubuki") is itself a favourite moment.
- **Forecasts**: the JMA stopped official forecasts in 2010; **private forecasters — Japan Meteorological Corporation (JMC), Weathernews, Weather Map and the Japan Weather Association (tenki.jp)** — publish forecasts from **January**, updated weekly, based on winter cold and spring warmth. They have been **within 2–3 days** in most recent years.
- **Trend**: full bloom in Tokyo is now **about 10 days earlier than in 1953** (JMA records); 2021 and 2023 were record early (22 March).

## Autumn foliage (kōyō)

| Place | Typical peak |
|---|---|
| Hokkaido (Daisetsuzan) | mid–late September |
| Nikko, Japan Alps | mid–late October |
| **Tokyo** | **late November – early December** |
| **Kyoto** | **mid – late November** (the most crowded fortnight of the year) |

- Foliage is **more predictable** than blossom (driven by temperature drop) and **lasts 2–3 weeks**; Kyoto temples run **evening illuminations** in November.

> **[Verify]** Bloom dates with the JMA's phenological records (さくらの開花日) and JMC forecasts; foliage timing with the same providers.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Book for "the first week of April"

**Tokyo's full bloom has fallen anywhere from 22 March to 5 April**; Kyoto a few days later. **Book flexible dates**, or plan a **route north** (Kyoto → Tokyo → Tohoku) that gives several chances.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The forecast is what the government says

The **JMA ended forecasts in 2010**; the useful ones are **JMC and Weathernews**, in English, from January. Check weekly.

## Misunderstanding 3｜One tree in bloom means the season has started

**"Kaika" (first bloom) is declared when 5–6 flowers open on the reference tree**; **full bloom (mankai) is about a week later**. Aim for mankai plus a few days.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Miss it and the trip is ruined

**Late blossoms (yaezakura, double-flowered) bloom 1–2 weeks after Somei-yoshino**; **Sapporo peaks in early May**. And the petal-fall week is considered by many the finest.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Autumn is the quiet alternative

**Kyoto in the second half of November is as crowded and expensive as Golden Week.** Tokyo, Nikko in October, and Tohoku are less so.
