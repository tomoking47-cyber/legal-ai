# Q. What do people get wrong about chopsticks and Japanese table manners?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #391

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/chopsticks-and-table-manners` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Chopsticks are the part of Japanese table manners visitors worry about most and get wrong least — nobody minds a clumsy grip. The rules that matter are about what chopsticks mean, not how they are held: a handful of gestures that reference death or greed, and the phrases that open and close a meal.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the skill is in holding chopsticks. The etiquette is in a few taboos — standing them upright in rice (a funeral offering), passing food chopstick to chopstick (a funeral rite), pointing, stabbing, licking, hovering — and in the frame around the meal: "itadakimasu" before, "gochisōsama" after, the bowl lifted to the mouth, the soup drunk from the bowl. Learn those and the grip is forgiven.

## The taboos (kirai-bashi) and why

| Do not | Name | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Stand chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice** | tate-bashi | This is the **offering to the dead** at a funeral |
| **Pass food directly from your chopsticks to someone else's** | hiroi-bashi / awase-bashi | Mirrors **passing cremated bones** at a funeral. Put it on a plate instead |
| Point at people or dishes | sashi-bashi | Rude |
| Stab food | tsuki-bashi | Childish |
| Hover over dishes deciding | mayoi-bashi | Greedy |
| Lick or suck them | neburi-bashi | — |
| Rest them across the bowl | watashi-bashi | Signals "I am finished" |
| Pull a dish toward you with them | yose-bashi | — |
| Use as a skewer or drumstick | — | — |
| Rub disposable chopsticks together | — | Implies the restaurant gave cheap ones |

## The frame

| Moment | What is done |
|---|---|
| Before | **"Itadakimasu"** (I humbly receive) — often with a small bow; at home, everyone waits to start together |
| Serving | **Rice bowl in the left hand, lifted to the chest**; soup bowl lifted to the mouth — **bowls are held, plates are not** |
| Soup | **Drink from the bowl**; chopsticks pick out the solids |
| Sharing plates | Use the **serving chopsticks (toribashi)** if provided; otherwise **turn your own around** is old-fashioned and now discouraged — just use them |
| Rice | Do not pour soy sauce on plain rice; do not leave grains (finish the rice) |
| Sushi | Fingers or chopsticks both fine; **dip fish side, not rice**; eat in one bite |
| Noodles | **Slurping is normal**; it cools and aerates |
| After | Chopsticks **on the rest (hashioki) or back in the wrapper**, parallel; **"Gochisōsama deshita"** to the cook or staff |

- Japan uses about **20 billion disposable chopsticks a year** (about 97% imported); reusable chopsticks are the household norm, each person with **their own pair**.
- **Children learn from about age 3** with training chopsticks; **holding them near the tip** is considered poor form, **about a third from the top** is standard.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bad technique offends

**Nobody is offended by a clumsy grip.** Standing chopsticks in rice or passing food chopstick-to-chopstick is what draws a wince — because of what it references.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Passing food with chopsticks is a kindness

It **mirrors the funeral rite of passing bones**. Put the piece on the other person's plate.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Lifting bowls is rude

**Bowls are meant to be lifted** — rice and soup to the mouth. **Plates stay on the table.** Hunching down to the bowl is the rude version.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Turn your chopsticks around to serve from a shared dish

**Old advice, now discouraged** (the end you held is not clean either). Use **serving chopsticks** if provided; otherwise use your own without fuss.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Itadakimasu is a prayer

It is **thanks to everyone and everything that produced the meal** — farmers, cook, the animal or plant. Non-religious; everyone says it, in restaurants too, quietly.
