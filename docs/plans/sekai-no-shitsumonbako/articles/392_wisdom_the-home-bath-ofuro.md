# Q. What do people get wrong about the Japanese home bath (ofuro)?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #392

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-home-bath-ofuro` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The Japanese bathroom is designed around one idea that the rest of the world does not share: the bath is for soaking, not washing, and one tub of hot water serves the whole family in sequence. Once that is understood, the layout, the equipment and the etiquette all make sense.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the Japanese bath is a bathtub in a bathroom. It is a soaking tub — deep, short, kept at 40°C or more — in a wet room where you wash outside the tub first, so the water stays clean for the next person and for reuse in the laundry. The toilet is in a separate room. Modern units reheat and refill automatically, the bath has a lid, and the whole family bathes the same evening in order.

## How it works

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Layout** | **Wet room (bathroom)** with a drain in the floor; **tub** along one wall; **shower and tap at low height** with a stool; **changing area** outside; **toilet in its own room** |
| **Tub** | **Deep (about 55–60 cm) and short**; sit with knees bent, water to the shoulders. Holds **about 200 L** |
| **Temperature** | **40–42°C** is typical; **41°C or below, 10 minutes** is the safety guidance for older people |
| **Sequence** | **Wash and rinse thoroughly outside the tub** (stool, hand shower) → **soak** → get out, no need to rinse the bath water off |
| **Family order** | Traditionally father/guests first, children, then whoever heats the water last; today, **whoever is ready**; the water is **shared, not refilled** |
| **Lid** | Keeps the water hot between bathers and overnight (for laundry reuse — see that article) |
| **Controls** | **Auto-fill and reheat** at the touch of a button (**oidaki** reheat); voice announces when ready; **timer** to have the bath ready on return |
| **Cleaning** | Daily quick rinse; **mould is the enemy**: fan on during and 1–2 hours after; **squeegee walls**; weekly scrub |

- **About 95% of Japanese homes have a bath**; **most people bathe daily**, in the evening; the **average bath water volume is about 200 L** and the **hot-water bill for bathing is the largest share of household energy after heating** (Agency for Natural Resources and Energy).
- **Bath accidents**: about **19,000 deaths a year** are estimated to occur in or around the bath, mostly **older people in winter**, from the temperature shock and heat; the guidance is **warm the changing room, 41°C or lower, 10 minutes, tell someone** (see the bath temperature article).
- **Bath water reuse** for laundry and the **emergency water** role (200 L for flushing after a quake) are why the tub is not drained until the next bath.

> **[Verify]** Bath-ownership and bathing habits with the Housing and Land Survey and industry surveys; accident estimates with the Consumer Affairs Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜You wash in the bath

**Never.** Wash **outside** with the hand shower and stool, then soak. The tub water is shared and should stay clean.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Each person runs a fresh bath

**One tub serves the household** the same evening, kept hot by the lid and the reheat function. Draining after one use is wasteful and unusual.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The toilet is in the bathroom

**Separate rooms** in almost all Japanese homes (unit baths in small flats and business hotels are the exception). The wet room is for bathing only.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Hotter is better

**42°C and above, and long soaks, are the pattern in bath deaths.** 40–41°C, 10 minutes, and warm the changing room first in winter.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The bath is a luxury that could be skipped

For most households the evening bath is **the daily reset**: warmth for sleep (see the bath-timing article), family time, and 200 L of stored water. Skipping it is what would feel like a hardship.
