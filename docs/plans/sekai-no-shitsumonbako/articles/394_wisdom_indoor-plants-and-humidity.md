# Q. What do people get wrong about indoor plants in a Japanese home?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #394

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/indoor-plants-and-humidity` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese homes keep plants — bonsai, kokedama, the ubiquitous pothos — but the humid climate and small rooms change the rules that houseplant guides written for dry Western homes assume.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that houseplant advice is universal. In Japan's rainy season and summer, indoor humidity is already 70–80%, so misting and pebble trays add mould risk rather than help; in winter, heated rooms drop to 20–30% and the plants dry out. The Japanese approach: fewer, smaller plants; well-draining soil; water by weight not schedule; and treat the balcony as the greenhouse.

## The climate problem

| Season | Indoor humidity (Tokyo, typical) | Plant risk | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rainy season (June–July)** | **70–85%** | **Root rot, fungus gnats, mould on soil, powdery mildew** | Water less; **no misting**; fan or dehumidifier; move to the balcony under cover |
| Summer (July–September) | 65–80% | Heat above 35°C on a closed-up balcony; leaf scorch | Shade; water early morning; bring tender plants in |
| Autumn | 50–65% | Fine | — |
| **Winter (heated rooms)** | **20–35%** | **Drying, spider mites**, cold draughts by windows at night | Water when dry; **keep away from heater blast and single-glazed windows**; humidifier helps plants and people |

- The **plants that thrive** in Japanese flats are the ones sold everywhere for that reason: **pothos, sansevieria, monstera, ZZ plant, ficus, peace lily, ivy** — tolerant of low light and irregular water.
- **Soil**: Japanese gardening centres sell **"kanyō-shokubutsu no tsuchi"** (houseplant mix) with **akadama (fired clay granules)** for drainage — the traditional bonsai medium that suits the humidity.
- **Kokedama** (moss-ball plants) and **bonsai** are outdoor or windowsill plants that need light and airflow; keeping bonsai indoors permanently is the most common killer of them.
- **Mould and mosquitoes**: **standing water in saucers breeds mosquitoes** (dengue prevention notices from the Ministry of the Environment) and **soil mould**; empty saucers, top-dress with grit.

> **[Verify]** Humidity ranges with JMA normals and indoor-environment surveys; dengue/mosquito guidance with the Ministry of the Environment.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Mist your plants for humidity

**In June you are misting into 80% humidity** — the result is mould. Misting is a winter-only habit in Japan, if at all.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Water on a schedule

**Water when the pot is light or the top 3 cm are dry**; in the rainy season that may be every two weeks, in a heated winter room every four days. Lift the pot.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Bonsai are indoor plants

**Almost all bonsai are outdoor trees** that need light, cold dormancy and airflow. Indoors they decline within months. Windowsill or balcony, brought in only for display.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A saucer of water keeps the roots moist

**Standing water is root rot plus mosquito larvae** — a public-health concern in summer. Empty saucers after watering.

## Misunderstanding 5｜More plants make a small flat healthier

**Plants add humidity and mould risk in a small humid room**; a few, well placed with airflow, beat a jungle. The Japanese habit of **one plant per surface**, in a pot that fits the space, is climate-driven.
