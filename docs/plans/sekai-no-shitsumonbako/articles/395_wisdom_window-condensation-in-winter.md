# Q. What do people get wrong about winter window condensation in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #395

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/window-condensation-in-winter` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Wake up in a Japanese flat in January and the windows are streaming. Condensation is the winter counterpart of the rainy season's damp — the moisture is the same, it just has somewhere to land — and it feeds the mould that appears on window frames and curtains by spring.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that condensation is a nuisance to wipe up. It is the visible sign that indoor humidity is meeting a cold surface, and left alone it grows mould on frames, curtains and the wall behind furniture. The causes are single-glazed aluminium windows (still common), indoor drying, unvented heaters and breathing; the fixes are ventilation, moving furniture off exterior walls, wiping or absorbing the water each morning, and — for owners — the subsidised switch to double glazing.

## Why it happens and what to do

| Cause | Detail | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Single glazing and aluminium frames** | Standard in most homes built before ~2010; the frame conducts cold; the glass surface can drop below 5°C while the room is 20°C | **Inner window (uchimado) or double glazing** — **national subsidies covered up to half the cost in 2023–25**; **insulating film or bubble sheet** as the cheap version |
| **Indoor humidity from living** | A family adds **several litres of water vapour a day** (breathing, cooking, bathing, indoor laundry) | **Ventilate** (two windows, 5–10 min, twice a day); **bathroom fan on**; **dehumidifier for laundry** |
| **Unvented combustion heaters** | Kerosene and gas fan heaters release **about 1 L of water per litre of fuel** | Ventilate hourly (also for CO); or switch to air-conditioner/heat pump |
| **Furniture against exterior walls** | Cold, still air behind it condenses | Leave **5–10 cm** gap; check behind the wardrobe in February |
| **Curtains** | Trap moist air against cold glass; **mould on the hem** by spring | Open curtains in the morning; wash them in spring |
| The water itself | — | **Wipe each morning** with a squeegee or cloth; **condensation-absorbing tape** on the frame bottom; **keep the sill dry** |

- **Mould conditions** are met on a window frame at **70%+ local humidity**; the black mould on frames and rubber seals is a common spring cleaning task.
- **Insulation standards**: only about **13% of existing homes** meet the 1999 standard (see the kotatsu article); **windows are the largest heat-loss path**, and the government's **2023–25 "Advanced Window Renovation" subsidy** paid **up to ¥2 million per home** to switch to insulated glass.
- **Inner windows (uchimado)** — a second sliding window inside the existing frame — are the renter-friendly option (removable) and cut condensation dramatically.

> **[Verify]** Subsidy figures with MLIT/METI/MoE joint window-renovation programme; insulation statistics with MLIT.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is just water — wipe it and move on

**It is the mould condition being met every night.** Wipe it, yes, but also **ventilate and move the wardrobe** off the outside wall.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Heating the room more will dry the glass

**Warmer air holds more water and the glass stays cold**; condensation often gets worse. **Reducing humidity and insulating the glass** is the fix.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Kerosene heaters dry the air

**They add about a litre of water vapour per litre of fuel** (and CO2 and some CO). The dry feeling is warmth, not low humidity. Ventilate hourly.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Nothing can be done in a rental

**Insulating film, bubble sheet, thick curtains kept open by day, absorbent tape, furniture off the wall, a dehumidifier** — all removable. Ask the landlord about **inner windows**; some accept them.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Double glazing is a luxury

**Windows are the biggest heat-loss path** and the state has been **paying half the cost**. For owners, it is the highest-return home upgrade in Japan; it also ends the streaming windows.
