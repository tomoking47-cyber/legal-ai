# Q. What do people get wrong about seasonal decorations (hina dolls, koinobori, New Year) and storing them?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #398

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/seasonal-decorations-and-storing-them` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japanese homes decorate for the seasons — New Year pine, Girls' Day dolls, Boys' Day carp streamers, summer wind chimes — then put it all away, and where it goes is a real question in a small home. The customs come with rules about timing, and the storage comes with rules about humidity.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that seasonal decorations are casual. Each has a display window (put out too late is careless, taken down too late is superstitiously bad luck), most are handed down, and the storage — a hina doll set is a dozen figures in silk and lacquer — is a humidity-management problem. Households air them before packing, use camphor and desiccant, box them in paulownia or paper, and keep them off the floor.

## The calendar and the storage

| Decoration | Display | Take down | Storage notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Year: kadomatsu (pine), shimekazari (rope), kagami-mochi** | **From about 26–28 December** (not the 29th — "double suffering" — nor the 31st, "one-night decoration") | **7 January** (Kanto) or 15 January (Kansai); **burned at the shrine's Dondo-yaki bonfire** in mid-January | Not stored; new each year |
| **Hina dolls (Girls' Day, 3 March)** | **Early February to 3 March** | **Promptly after 3 March** — folklore says late removal delays the daughter's marriage; **on a dry day** | **Air the dolls, wipe with a soft brush, wrap faces in tissue, a single doll-specific repellent placed near the top of the box and never touching the dolls (camphor or an odourless 人形用 product — never two kinds together), silica gel; paulownia (kiri) boxes**; store **high and dry, not the floor or near a bathroom**; **check once a year in autumn** |
| **Koinobori (carp streamers) and kabuto (helmet), Boys' Day 5 May** | Mid-April to 5 May | After 5 May | Streamers washed and dried completely; helmet dusted, silica gel |
| **Tanabata (7 July)** | Bamboo with paper wishes | Same day or next | Bamboo disposed; papers kept or burned |
| **Summer: wind chimes (fūrin), sudare blinds** | June to September | September | Blinds dried before storage |
| **Obon (mid-August)** | Family altar decorations, lanterns | After Obon | Lanterns boxed |
| **Christmas** | Late November to 25 December — **taken down on the 26th** to make way for New Year | 26 December | The one-day switch is a Japanese peculiarity |

- **Hina sets** range from **¥50,000 to ¥1 million+**; they are **gifts from the maternal grandparents** and kept for decades — the storage rules exist because **silk, lacquer and hair-glue** decay in humidity and are eaten by insects.
- **Camphor (shōnō)** is the traditional repellent for dolls and kimono; **do not mix with paradichlorobenzene or naphthalene** (they liquefy together and stain — see the moth-repellent article).
- **Dondo-yaki / Sagichō**: shrines and neighbourhoods burn New Year decorations and last year's charms in **mid-January**; do not put them in ordinary rubbish if a bonfire is available.

> **[Verify]** Display customs vary by region; storage guidance with doll makers' associations (e.g. Japan Doll Association).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Put decorations up when you feel like it

**Each has a window and a folklore.** New Year on the 26th–28th, not the 29th or 31st; hina dolls down promptly after 3 March; Christmas gone on the 26th.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Throw New Year decorations in the bin

**They go to the shrine bonfire (Dondo-yaki) in mid-January** — a neighbourhood event with mochi roasted in the embers. Municipal rubbish is the fallback.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Dolls go in a box in the closet like anything else

**Silk, lacquer and hair need dry air, no light, repellent and a high shelf.** Air them on a dry day before packing; a set stored damp is ruined by the next February.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Any moth repellent works for dolls

**One doll repellent only, never two types together.** Camphor is the traditional choice; doll makers and the Japan Doll Association now point to odourless doll-specific repellents (人形用). Place it near the top of the box, not touching the dolls — too much, or two kinds mixed, discolours faces and silk.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A small flat cannot keep a hina set

**Compact sets (a prince and princess pair, 30 cm) and glass-case sets** exist for exactly this; the storage box doubles as the display stand. Storing it on the **top shelf of the oshiire** with desiccant is the standard answer.
