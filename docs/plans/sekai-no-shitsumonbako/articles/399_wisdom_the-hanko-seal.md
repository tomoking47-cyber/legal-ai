# Q. What do people get wrong about the hanko (personal seal)?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #399

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-hanko-seal` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The small carved seal pressed in red ink is where Japan signs. Foreigners find it archaic; the government has been removing it from paperwork since 2020; and yet every household still has two or three. What it is for, what changed, and what a newcomer actually needs are three different questions.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the hanko has been abolished. What happened in 2020–21 is that the government removed the seal requirement from about 99% of administrative procedures and many companies dropped it internally. The registered seal (jitsuin) remains legally central for property, vehicles and major contracts; banks still use a bank seal for many account operations; and the everyday seal (mitome-in) survives for deliveries and internal forms. A resident needs one; a visitor needs none.

## The three seals

| Seal | Japanese | Use | Registration | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Registered seal** | **実印 jitsuin** | **Property purchase, vehicle registration, mortgages, wills, company formation, major contracts** — with a **seal certificate (印鑑登録証明書)** from the municipality | **Registered at the municipal office**; one per person; must be unique, usually full name, 8–25 mm | ¥3,000–30,000+ (hand-carved) |
| **Bank seal** | **銀行印 ginkō-in** | **Opening accounts, withdrawals at the counter, transfers** at many banks (online banks may not require) | Registered with the bank | ¥2,000–10,000 |
| **Everyday seal** | **認印 mitome-in** | **Acknowledging deliveries, internal approvals, minor forms** | None; **ready-made stamps (¥100–500)** with common surnames sold at 100-yen shops | ¥100–1,000 |
| **Shachihata** | シャチハタ (self-inking) | Same as mitome-in **but not accepted where a proper impression is required** (banks, registration) | — | ¥500–1,500 |

- **The 2020 review** (Cabinet Office regulatory-reform team; the Digital Agency was only created in September 2021): seal requirements were abolished, or set for abolition, in **14,909 of 14,992 administrative procedures — 99.2%, with only 83 kept**; the remaining ones are mostly **real-estate and vehicle registration** where the jitsuin and certificate serve as strong identity proof.
- **Foreign residents** can register a jitsuin in **katakana, alphabet or kanji** matching the residence card; many municipalities accept alphabet seals. A **signature** is legally valid for most contracts, but **banks and landlords may still ask for a seal** — a mitome-in in katakana (**¥1,000–3,000, made in a day**) removes friction.
- **Delivery acknowledgement**: since 2020 most couriers accept **a signature or contactless delivery**; the mitome-in by the door is fading.
- **Security**: the jitsuin and its certificate together can transfer property — **store the jitsuin separately from the certificate card**, and never lend it.

> **[Verify]** Reform figures with the Cabinet Office / Digital Agency's 2020–21 seal-requirement review; registration rules with your municipality.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Hanko were abolished in 2020

**Administrative forms mostly dropped them; property, vehicles and many bank operations did not.** The jitsuin is as legally weighty as ever where it remains.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A visitor needs one

**No.** Tourists sign. Even short-term residents can manage with a signature at most places; a **¥1,000 katakana mitome-in** is a convenience, not a requirement.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any seal works for anything

**A self-inking Shachihata is refused** for banking and registration because the rubber face deforms. Jitsuin and bank seal should be **different seals**, and the jitsuin used only for registered matters.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Losing a seal is like losing a pen

**Losing the jitsuin is like losing a signature that anyone can forge perfectly.** Report to the municipality, cancel the registration, register a new one. Keep it away from the certificate card.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Seals are a formality with no logic

A registered seal plus municipal certificate is a **two-factor identity check** that predates digital ID by centuries; the reform kept it exactly where the stakes justify it. The everyday seal, by contrast, is going the way of the fax.
