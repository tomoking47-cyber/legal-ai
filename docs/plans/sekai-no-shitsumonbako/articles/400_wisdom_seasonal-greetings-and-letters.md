# Q. What do people get wrong about Japanese seasonal greetings and the letter calendar?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #400

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/seasonal-greetings-and-letters` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan still sends paper: New Year cards in the billions, mid-summer greetings, thank-you notes for gifts, and formal letters that open with a line about the weather. Visitors see quaint stationery; residents see a calendar of obligations with fixed phrases and deadlines — and a system that is shrinking but not gone.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese letter-writing is free-form politeness. It is a calendar with set windows and set phrases: New Year cards arrive on 1 January (posted by 25 December), summer greetings change name on a fixed date (shochū-mimai until about 7 August, zansho-mimai after), thank-you notes for gifts go within days, and a formal letter opens with a seasonal phrase chosen from a list for that month. Email and messaging have absorbed most of it; the phrases moved with them.

## The calendar

| Greeting | When | Deadline / rule | Volume or note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nengajō (New Year card)** | Arrives **1 January** | **Post by 25 December** for delivery on New Year's Day; **not sent to a household in mourning** (they send a mochū-hagaki in November–December) | **About 560 million for 2027** (initial issue 555.92 million, announced 31 August 2026 — a 16th straight annual fall), down from about 4.4 billion at the 2003–04 peak |
| **Kanchū-mimai (midwinter greeting)** | **8 January – 3 February** | For late replies and to mourning households | — |
| **Shochū-mimai (midsummer greeting)** | **From the end of the rainy season / early July to about 7 August (Risshū)** | Postcards with summer motifs; "Are you bearing the heat?" | Declining but persistent |
| **Zansho-mimai (late-summer greeting)** | **8 August – end of August** | Same card, different heading — sending "shochū" after Risshū is a known error | — |
| **Ochūgen / oseibo thank-you** | Within **a few days** of receiving a gift | A phone call or card; the return gift (okaeshi) is not required for these seasonal gifts | See the gift article |
| **Thank-you for a wedding/funeral gift** | Within **a month** | With the return gift (hangaeshi) | — |
| **Formal letter opening (kisetsu no aisatsu)** | Any formal letter | **A weather phrase for the month** (e.g. "In this season of fresh greenery…" for May) followed by an inquiry after the recipient's health | Lists exist for all 12 months; business email uses shorter fixed forms ("Osewa ni natte orimasu") |

- **Japan Post's nengajō stamps** (with a lottery number) go on sale **1 November**; cards posted **15–25 December** are held and delivered on 1 January. The **"otoshidama lottery"** on the stamp number pays small prizes.
- The **mourning notice (mochū-hagaki)** tells friends not to send a New Year card that year; the etiquette is to send a **kanchū-mimai in January** instead.
- **Digital shift**: **LINE New Year stickers and messages** now dominate among under-40s; companies have largely stopped corporate nengajō since 2020. The **phrases survive** in messages.

> **[Verify]** Nengajō volumes with Japan Post; the Risshū date (7 or 8 August) varies by year with the JMA's calendar.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Any time in January is fine for a New Year card

**Arriving on 1 January is the point**; post by 25 December. After 7 January, it becomes a **kanchū-mimai** with different wording.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Summer greetings are one thing

**Shochū-mimai until Risshū (about 7 August), zansho-mimai after.** Sending the wrong one is the kind of error a careful sender notices.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Send New Year cards to everyone you know

**Not to a household that has had a death that year** — they will have sent a mourning notice. Send a midwinter greeting in January instead.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The weather opening is filler

It is **the required first line of a formal letter**, chosen for the month from a conventional list, and its absence reads as brusque. Business email has its own fixed opener instead.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Paper greetings are dead

**More than half a billion cards is a lot of dead.** The volume is down roughly 87% from its peak — and still falling by a quarter a year — but the **calendar and the phrases have moved into messaging**; knowing them still matters.
