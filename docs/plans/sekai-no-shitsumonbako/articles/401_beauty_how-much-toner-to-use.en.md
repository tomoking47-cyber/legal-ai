# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how much toner to use?｜Japan Questions #401

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/how-much-toner-to-use` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Use plenty of toner" is advice that has been repeated in Japanese skincare for decades, and visitors often ask why Japanese people use so much of it. Japanese toner (化粧水, keshōsui) is a watery lotion applied straight after washing the face, before any milk or cream. Here we sort out the relationship between quantity and effect, using what cosmetics are legally allowed to claim and how the skin actually works.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the more toner you use, the more hydrated your skin becomes." Toner works only as far as the stratum corneum (the outermost layer of skin), and there is a ceiling on how much water that layer can hold. The right amount is what the product label says — usually a 500-yen-coin-sized pool or 2–3 pumps — and anything beyond that evaporates, sometimes leaving the skin drier. What matters is not the quantity but "straight after washing" and "sealing it in with a milk or cream afterwards."

## Points to consider

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Right amount | **Follow the product label**. Usually **a 500-yen-coin-sized pool (about 2–3 mL) or 2–3 pumps** |
| Water in the stratum corneum | **The stratum corneum (about 0.02 mm thick) can hold only a limited amount of water**; the rest **evaporates**. One common explanation is that **evaporation also draws out surrounding moisture** (over-drying) |
| "Penetration" | Cosmetics penetrate **only as far as the stratum corneum** (Japan's cosmetics advertising guidelines). **Using more does not make it go deeper** |
| Layering | **Applying in 2–3 thin layers** tends to give **more even coverage than one large dose**. **Even with more layers, the total stays within the recommended amount** |
| Timing | **Straight after washing (within a few minutes)**. The stratum corneum loses water quickly after washing |
| Order | **Toner → (serum) → milk or cream**. **Without a "lid", the water in the toner evaporates** |
| Cotton-pad mask | **About 3 minutes** at most (once the pad starts to dry it draws moisture back out; see our sheet-mask article) |

- The main ingredients of toner are **water (70–90%) and humectants (glycerin, BG, etc.)**. **The humectants help the stratum corneum hold water**; **the water itself does not get stored in the skin**.
- **The "use plenty" habit** is generally traced to toner's development as **the central item of Japanese skincare** and the spread of **large, low-priced bottles**, not to **dermatological evidence**.
- **If dryness worries you, the priorities are** not the amount of toner but **the oil content of your milk or cream** and **the temperature and frequency of face-washing** (see our bathing-and-skin article).

> **[Verify]** Check the description of stratum corneum thickness and water retention against standard dermatology texts, and the "penetration" wording against the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association's advertising guidelines.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The more you use, the more hydrated you are

**There is a ceiling on how much water the stratum corneum can hold**; **the surplus evaporates**. **The right amount plus a lid (milk)**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Expensive toner sparingly, cheap toner generously

**The amount is set by the product's design (the label), not by its price.** Rather than drowning your face in cheap toner, use **the right amount, daily, straight after washing**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜If you splash on enough toner you can skip the milk

**The water in toner evaporates unless you seal it in**. **Skipping the milk or cream leaves the skin drier** — this is the basic thinking of Japanese skincare (see our milk-lotion article).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Patting it in makes it absorb

**Slapping the skin can cause redness and pigmentation**; dermatologists recommend **pressing gently**. **It does not change how deep the toner goes**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Without toner your skin dries out

**Many countries have no toner habit at all**, and **plenty of people do fine with a single milk or cream**. **Toner is an "optional" item, not an "essential" one**.
