# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about neck and décolleté care?｜Japan Questions #403

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/neck-and-decollete-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"You look after your face and forget your neck" is true everywhere, and neck lines and a colour difference between face and neck are often said to give away your age. Here we look at what makes neck skin different, and at sunscreen, moisturising and the "smartphone neck" posture that is much discussed in Japan.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the neck needs special cosmetics that are different from the face." The neck and décolleté are a continuation of the face; for most people, simply carrying the same sunscreen and moisturiser "down to the neck" is enough. What affects neck lines is accumulated UV exposure, a downward-looking posture (smartphone neck) and pillow height — and no cosmetic can erase them.

## Points to consider

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Neck skin | **Thinner than the face, with fewer sebaceous glands, so it dries easily**. **It moves a lot**, so horizontal lines form easily |
| UV | **The neck and décolleté receive as much UV as the face, or more** (open collars). **Apply sunscreen to the neck, behind the ears and the décolleté** |
| Moisturising | **Take your face toner and milk down to the neck**. No dedicated product needed |
| Posture | **Time spent looking down (phone, PC)** is generally described as one factor in horizontal neck lines. **Keep the screen at eye level** (see our smartphone-neck article) |
| Pillow | **A pillow that is too high drops the chin and creases the neck** (see our pillow article) |
| How to apply | **Upwards, without rubbing**. **Neck skin is thin, and friction from massage can cause pigmentation** |
| What cosmetics may claim | Up to **"moisturises the skin" and "makes fine lines caused by dryness less noticeable (efficacy-tested)"**. **"Erases wrinkles" and "improves sagging" are not permitted claims** |
| Medical options | **Injections, lasers and similar for neck lines are cosmetic medicine (自由診療, jiyū shinryō — private, not covered by health insurance)**. **Ask the doctor about effects and risks** |

- **Neck sunburn** happens because **people put sunscreen on the face and forget the neck**, giving **a colour difference between face and neck** (a "dark neck"). **The Ministry of the Environment's manual lists "the back of the neck and the ears" among the spots people forget**.
- **"Neck care" products** are cosmetics, and **their ingredients are essentially the same as face products**. Most are simply **designed with a heavier texture (cream)**.
- **A UV-blocking scarf or a high collar** protects **without needing to reapply sunscreen** (see our UV-protective clothing article).

## Misunderstanding 1｜The neck needs its own cosmetics

**Taking your face sunscreen and moisturiser down to the neck** covers most of it. **Dedicated products differ mainly in texture**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Cosmetics can erase neck lines

**The most a cosmetic may claim is "makes fine lines caused by dryness less noticeable"**. **Deep lines and sagging belong to cosmetic medicine**; ask the doctor about effects and risks.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Massage improves lines and colour

**Neck skin is thin, and friction causes pigmentation**. **Smooth products in upwards, without rubbing**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Sunscreen on the face is enough

**The neck, décolleté and the area behind the ears receive as much UV as the face, or more**. This is the main cause of **the colour difference between face and neck**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Neck lines are just age, nothing to be done

**Accumulated UV and posture** play a large part, and **horizontal lines from smartphone posture are reported even in younger people**. **Sunscreen, screen height and pillow** are the prevention.
