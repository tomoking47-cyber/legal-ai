# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about hair-iron temperature and hair damage?｜Japan Questions #404

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/hair-iron-temperature` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Many people use a straightener or curling iron every day, and two questions keep coming up: "What temperature should I set?" and "Is damage just unavoidable?" Here we look at how the protein in hair behaves under heat, and at the burn accidents reported to Japan's consumer-safety bodies every year. There is also a note on using a hair iron bought abroad in Japan.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a higher temperature sets the style faster, so it is gentler on the hair." The protein in hair (keratin) starts to denature above about 150°C (302°F), and above 200°C (392°F) the surface is visibly damaged. The guide is "dry hair, 140–160°C, 2–3 seconds per section, one pass." Using an iron on wet hair damages the inside of the hair through what is described as a "steam explosion." Burn accidents, especially involving children, are reported every year.

## The numbers

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Heat tolerance of hair | **Keratin denaturation progresses above about 150°C**; **above 200°C the damage to the surface (cuticle) is marked** |
| Recommended settings | **Fine or damaged hair: 120–140°C**; **normal: 140–160°C**; **thick or coarse hair: 160–180°C**. **200°C and above is for brief salon techniques** |
| Contact time | **2–3 seconds per section; do not repeat the same spot**. **One quick pass** beats **one slow pass** |
| Wet hair | **Never**. The water inside turns to steam suddenly and **the "steam explosion" hollows out the inside of the hair**. **Dry completely first** |
| Protection | A thin layer of **hair oil or milk labelled heat-protective**. **Too much scorches under the heat** |
| Burns | **Plate temperature is around 200°C**. **Contact burns to the face, ears and neck**, and **children touching the device** (NITE, Consumer Affairs Agency). **Rest it on a heat-resistant mat after use; do not pull the cord** |
| Auto shut-off | **Many models switch off after a set time**. **Without it, forgetting to switch off is a fire cause** |

- **Hair-iron burns** are reported continuously in the Consumer Affairs Agency's accident data; many cases involve **children aged 5 and under touching the device after use**. **Do not leave it on the floor; let it cool out of reach**.
- **Daily use** advances **surface wear and dryness**. **Take a few days off each week**, **lower the temperature**, and **use a treatment** (see our conditioner article). **Damaged hair does not recover** (see our hair-oil article).
- Hair irons fall under **PSE (the Electrical Appliance and Material Safety Act, Japan's electrical-safety law)**. **If you use a device bought abroad in Japan, check the voltage (Japan is 100 V) and look for the PSE mark**.

> **[Verify]** Check the keratin denaturation temperature against standard hair-science texts, and the accident cases against NITE and Consumer Affairs Agency materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Do it in one go at high heat and it is gentler

**Denaturation progresses above 150°C and damage is marked at 200°C**. **One quick pass at 140–160°C** does less harm.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Slightly damp hair styles better

**Heat on wet hair hollows out the inside through a steam explosion** — the most damaging way to use an iron. **Dry completely first**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Load on hair oil before ironing

**A thin layer of oil labelled heat-protective**. **Too much scorches and turns greasy**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Daily use is fine if you look after it

**Damaged hair does not regenerate.** **Iron-free days and lower temperatures** are the only prevention.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Put it straight back on the shelf when done

**A 200°C plate stays hot for several minutes**. **Cool it on a heat-resistant mat, out of children's reach, and check whether it has auto shut-off**.
