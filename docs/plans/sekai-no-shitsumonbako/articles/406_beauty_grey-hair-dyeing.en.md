# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about how often to dye grey hair, and which type to choose?｜Japan Questions #406

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/grey-hair-dyeing` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan, many people from their 40s onwards dye their grey hair regularly, and the drugstore aisle (白髪染め, shiraga-zome) offers a bewildering range. The two common questions are "how often?" and "which type is kindest to hair and scalp?" Here we sort products by their legal category in Japan — quasi-drug (医薬部外品, a category between cosmetics and medicines) or cosmetic — and read this together with our patch-test article.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "all grey-hair dyes are the same, and frequency is a matter of taste." There are quasi-drug "oxidative hair dyes" (permanent colour: they dye thoroughly, with an allergy risk) and cosmetic "hair manicure" and "colour treatments" (they coat the surface, wash out sooner, and are gentler), and they differ in how often you use them and how hard they are on the scalp. A full-head colour every 1.5–2 months and a root touch-up every 3–4 weeks is typical, and the product label asks for a patch test every single time.

## Types and how to use them

| Type | Category | How it works | Lasts | Typical frequency | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oxidative hair dye (ヘアカラー / 白髪染め — permanent colour)** | **Quasi-drug** | Colour develops inside the hair. **Can also lighten** | **2–3 months** (regrowth at the roots shows) | **Full head: 1.5–2 months; roots: 3–4 weeks** | **PPD ("diamine") allergy**. **Patch test 48 hours before every use** (see our hair-colour article) |
| **Hair manicure (acid hair colourant)** | **Cosmetic** | Colour clings to the surface | **2–4 weeks** | 1–2 times a month | Cannot lighten. **Stains the scalp and will not come off**, so keep a few mm from the skin |
| **Colour treatment / colour shampoo** | **Cosmetic** | Builds up colour on the surface little by little | **A few days to a week** (with continued use) | **1–3 times a week** | Gentle colour. **Stains hands and towels** |
| **Henna (plant dye)** | Cosmetic (**unless it is pure henna, it may contain chemical dyes**) | Coats the surface (orange to reddish-brown) | A few weeks | Once a month | **Slow (an hour or more)**. **Allergy cases from "henna" containing PPD** |
| Temporary colourants (mascara, spray) | Cosmetic | Sits on the surface | **Washes out in one shampoo** | Each outing | Runs in sweat and rain |

- In its report on skin injuries from hair dye, **the Consumer Safety Investigation Commission (2015)** recommended that **anyone who has once reacted should stop using oxidative dyes and consider products in the cosmetic category**.
- **The more often you dye, the greater the burden on the scalp**. **Root touch-ups** are less taxing than a full head, and **bridging the gap with a colour treatment** is an increasingly common approach.
- **The causes of grey hair** are mainly **age and genetics**; **stress and nutrition** play only a partial role. **No cosmetic or quasi-drug may claim to "turn grey hair black again."**

> **[Verify]** Check the category definitions with the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, and the report with the Consumer Affairs Agency's Consumer Safety Investigation Commission (2015).

## Misunderstanding 1｜All grey-hair dyes are the same

**Oxidative dyes (quasi-drugs) and cosmetic colourants differ in mechanism, staying power and risk**. Choose by **whether you have an allergy, and how often you want to dye**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Frequency is up to you

**Oxidative dyes burden the scalp; the usual guide is full head every 1.5–2 months and roots every 3–4 weeks**. **Colour treatment in between** is the usual approach.

## Misunderstanding 3｜No reaction last time means no test needed

**PPD allergy can appear suddenly**. The product label asks for **a patch test 48 hours before every use** (see our hair-colour article).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Henna is natural, so it is safe

**Some products sold as "henna" contain PPD or other chemical dyes**, and allergy cases have been reported. **Check the full ingredient list**. **Some people react to henna itself**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜There are products that turn grey hair black again

**No such claim is permitted for cosmetics or quasi-drugs.** Claims like "reduces grey hair" or "grows black hair" **are problematic under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law) and the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations (Japan's advertising law)**. The choice is **dye it** or **leave it**.
