# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about the difference between dermatology and cosmetic dermatology in Japan?｜Japan Questions #408

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/dermatology-vs-cosmetic-dermatology` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Do I take acne to a dermatologist (皮膚科, hifuka) or a cosmetic dermatologist (美容皮膚科, biyō hifuka)?" and "Can dark spots be dealt with on health insurance?" are hard to answer unless you know how Japanese healthcare works. Here we draw the line between insured care and private (self-paid) care, and mark where cosmetics stop.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "cosmetic dermatology is an upgraded version of dermatology." A dermatology clinic deals with diseases (acne, eczema, athlete's foot, atopic dermatitis and so on) under Japan's national health insurance; a cosmetic dermatology clinic deals with non-disease concerns (dark spots, wrinkles, sagging and so on) as private, self-paid care. The same "spot" may be covered by insurance if judged a disease, or self-paid if the purpose is cosmetic, and the clinic sets the price. Acne is covered by insurance, so you do not have to keep struggling with it using cosmetics.

## Insured care versus private care

| Item | Dermatology (insured care) | Cosmetic dermatology (private care) |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | **Diseases**: acne, eczema, atopic dermatitis, athlete's foot, warts, hives, alopecia, **pathological pigmentation (some)**, **birthmarks (some)** | **Cosmetic concerns**: spots, dullness, wrinkles, sagging, pores, brightening, hair removal (medical laser), acne scars |
| Cost | **You pay 30%** (a first visit is a few thousand yen) | **Fully self-paid**. The clinic sets the price (a few thousand yen to several hundred thousand yen) |
| Care | **Approved medicines and procedures** | **May include unapproved medicines and equipment** (at the doctor's discretion) |
| Advertising | Medical Advertising Guidelines | **Medical Advertising Guidelines (tightened 2018)**: **definitive effect claims such as "absolutely safe", and before-and-after photos without conditions, are prohibited** |
| Complaints | — | **The National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan receives over 2,000 complaints a year about cosmetic medicine** (contracts, results, side effects) |

- **Acne (acne vulgaris) is covered by health insurance**; topical medicines (adapalene, benzoyl peroxide, etc.) are prescribed under the Japanese Dermatological Association's **2023 guideline**. **This is a different domain from the cosmetic claim "helps prevent acne" (a quasi-drug claim)**.
- **Dark spots**: **melasma and solar lentigines are mostly cosmetic (self-paid)**, while **some birthmarks such as naevus spilus and naevus of Ota are covered by insurance**. **The doctor decides**.
- **Cosmetic-medicine complaints** typically involve **large contracts signed on the day of the consultation, exaggerated claims of effect, and side effects**. **The amendment to the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions that took effect on 1 December 2017 brought some cosmetic medicine within the cooling-off rules** (contracts over 1 month and over ¥50,000).

> **[Verify]** Check the scope of insurance coverage against the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's fee schedule, the Medical Advertising Guidelines with the Ministry (2018), and the cooling-off rules with the Consumer Affairs Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cosmetic dermatology is dermatology, but better

**A different purpose (disease or cosmetic) and a different payment system (insurance or self-paid)**. **If you suspect a disease, start at a dermatology clinic**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Acne is something you fight with cosmetics

**Acne is covered by insurance, and there are guideline-based medicines**. If it **keeps coming back or is inflamed**, see a dermatologist. **Cosmetics go only as far as "helps prevent"**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜All dark spots are self-paid

**Some birthmarks are covered by insurance**. **Have a dermatologist confirm whether it is a disease** first; if the purpose is cosmetic, then choose self-paid care.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Signing on the day is normal in cosmetic medicine

**Do not decide on a large contract on the day of the consultation** is the Consumer Affairs Agency's advice. **Contracts over 1 month and over ¥50,000 fall under the cooling-off rules**. Consider **a second opinion** too.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Before-and-after photos in adverts can be trusted

**Under the Medical Advertising Guidelines (2018), before-and-after photos that do not meet the conditions (procedure, cost and risks stated alongside) are prohibited**. **Definitive claims of effect are also prohibited**. **Whether the risks are explained** is your yardstick.
