# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about keeping reusable shopping bags clean?｜Japan Questions #411

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/reusable-shopping-bag-hygiene` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Since Japan made plastic checkout bags chargeable in July 2020, reusable shopping bags (エコバッグ, eko baggu) have spread to almost every household. Many people, however, have never washed theirs. Because these bags carry fresh food, their hygiene is now flagged as a food-poisoning issue.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "a reusable bag is cloth, so it is clean and never needs washing." Use it again and again with meat or fish juices, soil from vegetables or spilled drink on it, and bacteria multiply and move to your food. The basics are: put meat and fish in their own thin plastic bags; keep one bag for food and another for household goods; wash about once a week (following the care label); dry the bag if it gets wet. For insulated cooler bags, wipe the inside and let it dry.

## Points to weigh

| Item | Detail | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| How dirt travels | **Drip from meat and fish, egg shells, soil on vegetables, leaked drinks** → inside of the bag → next time's food | MHLW "Six points" (keep bacteria off food) |
| Separate bags | **One bag for food, another for household goods (detergent, shoes, etc.)**. **Meat and fish always go in a thin plastic bag** (the free roll bags at the packing counter after the till) | As above |
| How often to wash | **About once a week, or straight away when soiled**. **Follow the care label** (Japan uses the international JIS L0001 symbols; nylon and polyester bags can usually go in the washing machine; insulated bags are hand-washed or wiped) | Makers' guidance |
| Drying | **Never fold it away wet**. **Turn it inside out to dry** | Mould and smell |
| Insulated bags | **Wipe the aluminium lining with a disinfectant wipe or diluted detergent, then dry**. Also wipe **condensation from ice packs** | — |
| When to replace | **Dark stains inside, smell, fraying seams** | — |

- Since **checkout bags became chargeable in July 2020**, **71.9% of people used no plastic checkout bag during the previous week's shopping** (Ministry of the Environment survey, November 2020; **51.9% said they "always carry a reusable bag"**). Meanwhile, private surveys find that **around half have "never washed" theirs**.
- A **US study (2010, University of Arizona and others; industry-funded, and its interpretation has been criticised)** reported that **bacteria were found in most reusable bags in use, and that washing removed almost all of them**. In Japan, **the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and others have issued hygiene advice** too.
- An insulated bag **left in a car boot gets very hot in summer, and bacteria multiply easily**. **Empty it and dry it as soon as you get home**.

> **[Verify]** Check the bring-your-own-bag rate against the Ministry of the Environment's survey on chargeable checkout bags, and the hygiene advice against materials from the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Bureau of Public Health and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is cloth, so it is clean

**Used repeatedly with meat juices, soil and spills on it, the bag becomes a carrier of bacteria**. **Wash it once a week**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Meat and fish are wrapped, so they can go straight in

**Drip leaking through gaps in the film on the tray** is normal. **Always put them in a plastic bag first**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜One bag does for everything

**Keep one bag for food and another for household goods**. Avoid putting **vegetables in a bag that has carried shoes or detergent**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜An insulated bag cannot be washed, so there is no need to wipe it

**Wipe the inside with a disinfectant wipe and leave it open to dry**. Pay special attention to **the base, where condensation collects**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Washing damages the bag

**If you follow the label, it can be washed** (nylon and polyester in the machine, in a laundry net). **Not washing is a bigger problem than wear**. **Owning several and rotating them** is the practical answer.
