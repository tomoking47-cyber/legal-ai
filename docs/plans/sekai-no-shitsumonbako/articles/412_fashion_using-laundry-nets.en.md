# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about using laundry nets (mesh washing bags)?｜Japan Questions #412

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/using-laundry-nets` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The laundry net (洗濯ネット, sentaku netto — a zipped mesh washing bag) is one of the most widely used laundry tools in Japan. Visitors often ask why the Japanese put everything in one. Here we set out what a net protects and what it gets in the way of, from how fibres and washing machines behave.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "put it in a net and anything is protected, and it still gets clean." A net is a tool for reducing friction, tangling, loss of shape, pilling and snagging; it actually lowers cleaning power. The key points are: one garment per net (do not cram); match the size to the garment; leave heavily soiled items out or pre-wash them; choose the mesh fineness for the job.

## Points to weigh

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| What a net does | **Reduces friction (pilling, colour loss), prevents tangling (sleeves, drawstrings), prevents stretching and loss of shape, protects decoration (beads, lace), stops zips and hooks catching other clothes** |
| Cleaning power | **Water flow is weaker inside the net, so dirt comes out less well**. **Do not net items heavy with sweat and sebum (underwear, socks), or pre-wash them** |
| Overfilling | **Fill to half to 70% of the net's capacity**. **One garment per net** (knits, blouses). Crammed items do not wash and come out creased |
| Size | **A size the folded garment fits snugly**. Too large and it moves around inside and rubs |
| Mesh fineness | **Fine mesh**: knits, stockings, lace, bras ／ **Coarse mesh**: T-shirts, shirts (keeps cleaning power) |
| Bras | **A shaped bra net** stops the cups deforming. Fasten the hooks |
| Tumble dryer | **Left in the net, clothes dry badly in the dryer**. **Take them out first** |
| The net itself | **Gets dirty and collects lint and pills**. Wash it now and then; replace it when torn (loose net threads tangle in clothes) |

- **The line under the wash-tub symbol on the care label (gentle process)** means "reduce mechanical action", and **a net helps you do that**. Japan uses the international JIS L0001 care symbols. Some products carry a **"use a net" note on the label**.
- Nets became common in Japan because **top-loading washing machines (which rub clothes hard against each other) are the norm** and **washing "delicates" at home became widespread**. **Front-loading drum machines wash by lifting and dropping, so friction differs and a net's effect changes slightly**.
- **Close zips, undo buttons** (buttons left fastened get pulled and come off) is the basic rule to pair with a net.

> **[Verify]** Check the meaning of the symbols in the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Care labelling" pages. The usage advice is general guidance from detergent and washing-machine makers.

## Misunderstanding 1｜In a net, anything is protected

**What it protects against is friction, tangling and loss of shape**. **Sweat and sebum come out less well**, so do not net soiled items.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Put everything together in one net

**Crammed items do not wash, come out creased and rub against each other**. **One garment per net, filled to half to 70%**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A bigger net is handier

**The garment moves around inside and rubs**. **Use a size the folded garment fits snugly**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The net itself never needs washing

**The net picks up lint, pills and dirt, and passes them back to your clothes**. **Wash it now and then; replace it when torn**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Leave it in the net for the tumble dryer too

**It dries badly and creases**. **Take it out of the net before the dryer, or hang it**.
