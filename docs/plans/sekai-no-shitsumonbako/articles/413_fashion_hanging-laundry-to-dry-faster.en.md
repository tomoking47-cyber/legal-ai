# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about hanging out laundry (drying faster, avoiding creases)?｜Japan Questions #413

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/hanging-laundry-to-dry-faster` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

How to hang laundry is one of the household tasks Japan has refined most, and visitors ask why the Japanese fuss so much over it. Laundry here is usually hung to dry, on a balcony or indoors, rather than tumble-dried. Here we set out what decides drying speed and finish, from how air moves and how fibres behave.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "how you hang it makes no difference; put it in the sun and it dries fast." What decides drying speed is wind (air exchange) more than sunlight, plus the space between garments and how much water is left in them (spin, towel thickness). The basics: leave 10–15 cm between items; alternate long and short items ("arch hanging"); thick items on the outside, thin in the middle; shake out creases before hanging; get air moving.

## What decides drying speed

| Factor | Detail | Trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Wind (air exchange)** | The faster moist air moves away from the garment, the faster it dries. **Wind works better than sun** | **A fan or air circulator** (see the article on drying indoors), **leave space** |
| **Spacing** | **10–15 cm** or more | Packed together, each item stays damp from its neighbour |
| **Arrangement** | **Arch hanging**: long and thick items on the outside, short and thin in the middle → an air channel underneath | A square peg hanger (角ハンガー, kaku hangā — the rectangular frame with many clips) |
| **Shape** | **Hang as a tube** (open T-shirts and trousers into a tube so air passes through); **jeans inside out, as a tube** | Pockets dry too |
| **Spin** | Spin thoroughly (**except items whose label calls for a gentle spin**) | Less water, faster drying |
| **Thickness** | **Spread towels out, do not overlap them**; **hoodies: lift the hood separately (hang upside down)** | Exposes the slow-drying parts |
| **Time of day** | **Hang in the morning, bring in in the afternoon**. **Before the evening damp sets in** | — |

## Preventing creases

| Trick | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Shake before hanging** | **Two or three big shakes to pull out creases**. **Pull along the seams to set the shape** |
| **Hang straight away** | Leaving washing sitting after the cycle ends causes creases and smell |
| **Hanger shape** | **A thick hanger matched to the shoulder width**. Wire hangers leave marks on the shoulders |
| **Pat flat** | **Pat lightly with the palm** to smooth creases |
| **Bring in before fully dry** | For shirts, **bring them in just before they are dry and iron** (see the article on creases) |

- **Sunlight's role** is **less about drying than about disinfecting, deodorising and bleaching** (whites). **Dry colours in the shade** (UV fades them).
- **The smell of laundry dried indoors** comes from **taking too long to dry** (see the towel article and the indoor-drying article). **"Dry within 5 hours"** is the rule of thumb usually quoted.

> **[Verify]** The effects of hanging methods are general guidance based on tests by makers (detergent, washing machines, drying racks).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Put it in the sun and it dries fast

**Wind works better.** **A cloudy day with a breeze dries; a sunny day with no wind is slow**. **Even indoors, a fan gets it done in a few hours**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Pack it tight and you can hang more at once

**With no gaps, each item stays damp from its neighbour, and that causes smell**. **10–15 cm apart, arch hanging**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Put colours in the sun to disinfect them too

**UV fades them**. **Colours in the shade or inside out; whites and towels in the sun**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The iron takes out creases, so hanging does not matter

**Shaking out, setting the shape and hanging straight away cut the ironing greatly**. **Bringing items in just before they are dry** helps further.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Hanging out at night is cool and efficient

**Evening damp and humidity slow drying and cause smell**. **Hang in the morning and bring in in the afternoon**; **at night, dry indoors with a fan**.
