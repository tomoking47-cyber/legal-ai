# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about stockings and tights?｜Japan Questions #415

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/stockings-and-tights` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"They laddered the first time I wore them" is a complaint the world over. In Japan, where stockings are worn widely for commuting and as part of uniforms, a good deal of know-how on making them last is shared. We set it out from the fibres (nylon and polyurethane) and from how they are washed and put on.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "stockings are disposable and laddering is luck." Most ladders come from nails, rings and rough heel skin when putting them on, from tangling in the washing machine, and from tumble-dryer heat — all avoidable. The basics: moisturise your hands and tidy your nails before putting them on; gather them up and start from the toes; hand-wash or use a laundry net; no tumble dryer; keep a spare pair.

## Points to weigh

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Fibres | **Nylon plus polyurethane (elastane)**. **Polyurethane degrades with heat, chlorine, UV and age** (2–3 years) |
| Causes of ladders | **Nails, rings, hangnails; rough skin on heels and elbows (see the heel article); seams inside shoes; tangling in the washing machine; the tumble dryer** |
| Putting them on | **Moisturise hands, tidy nails, take off rings**. **From the toes, gathering the leg and working up a little at a time**. **Set the heel in place before pulling up** |
| Denier | **The bigger the number, the thicker**: **20–30 denier** is sheer stockings, **40–80** light tights, **110 and above** thick tights |
| Washing | **Hand-wash (press gently) or a fine-mesh laundry net on a gentle cycle**. **Neutral detergent (中性洗剤, chūsei senzai)**. **Keep them away from hooks on other garments** |
| Drying | **Dry in the shade; no tumble dryer** (heat degrades and shrinks the polyurethane) |
| Storage | **Rolled, in a drawer**. **Out of direct sunlight** |
| Repair | **A dab of clear nail top coat or glue where a ladder starts** stops it spreading (emergency measure) |
| Lifespan | **Stockings a few wears to about 10; tights one season** as a guide |

- Under the **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act**, **fibre content (% nylon, % polyurethane) and care labelling** are compulsory. **"Ladder-resistant" finishes** are labelled, and **the knit (mixed-yarn knitting)** makes a difference.
- **Swollen legs** and **compression stockings**: **compression products sold in shops are general goods and may not claim any effect** (see the article on standing jobs). **Medical compression stockings are a separate category**.
- **Winter tights and static**: **nylon tends to charge positive**, and **worn with an acrylic skirt it builds static** (see the static article).

> **[Verify]** Check the fibre properties against general explanations from the fibre industry bodies, and the labelling duties against Consumer Affairs Agency materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Laddering is luck

**Nails, rings, rough skin, shoe seams and washing** are the main causes. **Preparing before you put them on** cuts ladders greatly.

## Misunderstanding 2｜They can go straight in the washing machine

**They tangle in hooks and zips on other garments and ladder**. **Fine-mesh net on a gentle cycle, or hand-wash**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The tumble dryer dries them quickly

**Heat degrades and shrinks the polyurethane, so they ladder more easily**. **Dry in the shade**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Expensive stockings do not ladder

**Knit and finish make some difference**, but **how you put them on and wash them matters more**. **A spare pair in your bag** is the practical answer.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Denier is a measure of quality

**Denier is the thickness of the yarn (how thick the fabric is)**, not quality. **Choose by season and purpose** (20–30 for sheer, 80 and above for warmth).
