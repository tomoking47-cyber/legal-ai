# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about looking after wallets and small leather goods?｜Japan Questions #417

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/wallet-and-leather-small-goods` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Wallets, business-card holders and key cases are the leather goods you touch every day, and the oil from your hands plus constant friction change them faster than anything else you own. In Japan this change is enjoyed as "ageing" (経年変化, keinen henka) — a culture of watching leather darken and take on a glow. Here we draw the line between ageing and plain wear, by type of leather and by how it is cared for.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "small leather goods are conditioned naturally by the oil from your hands, so they need nothing." Hand oil is uneven, and it also causes soiling, patchy colour and dark grime. The basics are: a monthly wipe with a dry cloth and a thin layer of cream; wipe immediately if wet and dry in the shade; do not overfill (it deforms the shape and strains the seams); and do not let metal (keys, coins) rest against the same spot for long. Good ageing is built on top of this care.

## Types of leather and how to care for them

| Leather | Character | Care | Ageing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nume leather (ヌメ革, vegetable-tanned, unfinished)** | **Darkens with sunlight and hand oil** to a caramel colour. **Marks easily with water** | **Monthly: dry wipe, then a thin layer of cream**. Some people **sun-tan it deliberately at the start** so the colour deepens evenly | Changes the most. **Wipe water off at once** |
| **Chrome-tanned, pigment-finished** | Stable colour, fairly water-resistant | **Dry wipe; for dirt, a little diluted neutral detergent on a cloth** | Little change |
| **Cordovan (horse rump)** | High gloss, sensitive to water | **Dry wipe, a small amount of the dedicated cream** | The gloss deepens |
| **Embossed, patent (enamel)** | Coated surface | Wipe only | Almost no change |
| **Synthetic leather (合皮, gōhi)** | Peels through hydrolysis (after a few years) | Wipe only | Only deterioration |

| Everyday care | Why |
|---|---|
| **Do not overfill** (cards, coins, receipts) | **Loss of shape, strain on the seams, zip failure** |
| **Do not sit on it in a back pocket** | Bends, deforms, splits the seams |
| **Do not leave coins or keys pressed against it for long** | Metal marks and dark grime |
| **If wet, press at once with a dry cloth and dry in the shade** | Water marks, mould |
| **Cream a few times a year, thinly** | Too much makes it sticky and feeds mould |
| **Store in a breathable bag with a desiccant** | Mould (see the leather-goods article) |

- **Ageing** is a change that happens to **vegetable-tanned leather**; **pigment-finished leather hardly changes at all**. **Not all leather "gains character" with age**.
- **Frayed seams and broken zips on small leather goods can be repaired** (see the article on guarantees and repairs). **A few thousand yen at the maker or a leather repair shop** is the usual guide.
- Under the **Household Goods Quality Labelling Act (Miscellaneous Manufactured Goods regulations)**, **the type of leather (cowhide, horsehide, etc.) and synthetic leather must be labelled**. **The label tells you whether it is "genuine leather" (本革, honkawa) or "synthetic leather" (合皮)**.

> **[Verify]** Check the scope of the labelling duty against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Miscellaneous Manufactured Goods Quality Labelling Regulations", and the care advice against Japan Leather and Leather Goods Industries Association materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Hand oil conditions it naturally

**Uneven oil causes soiling and patchy colour**. **A dry wipe and a thin layer of cream** keep it even.

## Misunderstanding 2｜All leather gains character with age

**Only vegetable-tanned leathers such as nume**. **Pigment-finished and chrome-tanned leather hardly change; synthetic leather only deteriorates**. **Read the label**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more cream, the longer it lasts

**Too much makes it sticky, feeds mould and dulls the colour**. **A rice-grain amount, spread thinly, a few times a year**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A wallet should hold as much as it can

The main cause of **loss of shape, split seams and zip failure**. **Carry only the cards you need and take receipts out daily**. **Do not sit on it in a back pocket**.

## Misunderstanding 5｜When it breaks, buy a new one

**Seams, zips and snap fasteners can usually be repaired**. **A few thousand yen at a repair shop**. **Repairing small leather goods and using them for ten years** is how people in Japan tend to use them.
