# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about when to brush your teeth and about flossing?｜Japan Questions #421

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/brushing-teeth-timing-and-floss` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Do not brush for 30 minutes after eating." "Japanese people hardly floss." Tooth-brushing habits differ from country to country, and opinions split on forums abroad too. Here is what the Japan Dental Association, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and Japan's "8020 Campaign" (keep 20 of your own teeth at age 80) say.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "brushing straight after a meal wears your teeth down, so avoid it." The Japan Dental Association (JDA) says you may brush straight after an ordinary meal. Only after acidic food or drink (fizzy drinks, citrus, vinegar) does it suggest rinsing with water and waiting a little. Floss and interdental brushes remove debris between the teeth where a toothbrush cannot reach. About 50% of people in Japan have used them (54.2% in the 2024 survey), and once a day is recommended to help prevent gum disease.

| Check-ups | **A dental check-up once or twice a year.** **The dental check-up rate is a target in Health Japan 21** (the national health-promotion plan) | MHLW |

- **The "do not brush for 30 minutes" idea** comes from research on **brushing enamel softened by acid (erosion)**. **The JDA's position is "no need to worry after an ordinary meal; take care only straight after acidic food or drink."**
- **Rinsing after fluoride toothpaste**: **rinsing many times with lots of water washes the fluoride away**, so the Japanese Society for Oral Health recommends **one rinse with a small amount (10–15 mL)**. **Children under 6 should use less toothpaste.**
- **Gum disease (periodontitis)** shows in **about half of Japanese adults** and is **the leading cause of tooth loss**. **Cleaning between the teeth** is the key to prevention.

> **[Verify]** Check brushing frequency and the 8020 achievement rate against the Survey of Dental Diseases (2022), and the fluoride recommendation against the Japanese Society for Oral Health's "Recommended Use of Fluoride Toothpaste" (2023).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Brushing straight after eating wears the teeth down

**You may brush straight after an ordinary meal.** **Only after acidic food or drink, rinse with water and wait a little.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜If you brush carefully, you do not need floss

**A toothbrush alone leaves much of the debris between the teeth.** **Floss or an interdental brush once a day** is the basis of gum-disease prevention.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Rinse thoroughly after brushing

**Rinse once with a little water, to leave the fluoride on.** **Not eating or drinking for 30 minutes afterwards** helps further.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Scrub hard with a stiff toothbrush

This causes **damaged gums and worn tooth necks (wedge-shaped defects at the gumline)**. **Use a soft to medium brush, light pressure and small strokes.** **Do not press an electric toothbrush against the teeth.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Go to the dentist when it hurts

**Gum disease progresses without pain; by the time you notice, the bone supporting the teeth has already receded.** **A check-up and cleaning once or twice a year** is the precondition for 8020.
