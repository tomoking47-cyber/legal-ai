# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about dry eye and eye drops?｜Japan Questions #423

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/dry-eye-and-eye-drops` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

With more screen time, an estimated 20 million people in Japan complain of dry or tired eyes. Here is how to choose over-the-counter (OTC) eye drops in Japan, and where ideas about "how often to use them", "contact lenses" and "cooling drops" go wrong, based on materials from the Japanese Ophthalmological Society and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW).

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "if your eyes feel dry, over-the-counter eye drops will solve it." Dry eye is a chronic condition in which the amount or quality of tears falls; OTC drops only top up moisture for a while. The basics are: blink more (blinking drops to a third when you stare at a screen), rest 10–15 minutes every hour, humidify the room, and use artificial tears with little or no preservative up to 5–6 times a day. If symptoms continue, have your tears tested at an eye clinic (眼科, ganka; covered by Japanese health insurance).

## Numbers and guidelines

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Estimated dry eye | **About 20 million people** (Japan) | Japanese Ophthalmological Society; Dry Eye Society |
| Blinking | **Normally about 20 times a minute → about 5–7 times when staring at a screen** | General ophthalmology explanation |
| Screen work | **No more than 1 hour at a stretch, then a 10–15 minute break**; **screen below eye level** (looking down narrows the eye opening, so the eye dries less) | MHLW guidelines |
| Types of OTC eye drops | **Artificial tears (人工涙液, jinkō ruieki; close to natural tears)**: for dryness ／ **Redness relievers (vasoconstrictors)**: rebound redness with repeated use ／ **Cooling (menthol)**: the sensation feels good, nothing more ／ **Antibacterial**: styes etc. | General pharmacist explanation |
| How often | **Artificial tears up to 5–6 times a day** (product label). **If you need more, use preservative-free single-use vials** | Product labels |
| Contact lenses | **Only drops labelled for use while wearing lenses.** **Hard lenses: yes; soft lenses: depends on the product** | Product labels |
| When to see a doctor | **Dryness, grittiness or blurring that continues; no improvement with OTC drops; pain or reduced vision** | Eye clinic |

- **Dry eye is diagnosed** at an eye clinic by checking **tear volume (Schirmer test), tear stability (break-up time, BUT) and damage to the cornea**. **Care is with prescription drops (hyaluronic acid, mucin secretagogues and others)**, which differ from OTC products.
- **The preservative in OTC drops (benzalkonium chloride)** is noted as **a burden on the cornea when used often**; the usual advice from eye doctors is **preservative-free single-use drops if you go beyond 5–6 times a day**.
- **Redness-relief drops** **constrict blood vessels to make the eye look white**; **repeated use is known to cause rebound redness (the eye ends up redder)**. **Avoid daily use "for tired eyes".**
- **Air-conditioning and humidity**: **40–60% humidity**, and **do not let the airflow hit your eyes directly** (see our article on air-conditioning).

## Misunderstanding 1｜OTC eye drops solve dry eye

**They top up moisture for a while.** **If symptoms continue, have your tears tested at an eye clinic.** **Prescription drops are different from OTC drops.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The more drops, the better

**Drops with preservative: up to 5–6 times a day.** **If you need more, go preservative-free.** **After a drop, press gently on the inner corner of the eye and do not blink repeatedly.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜A drop that feels icy is working

**The cool feeling is menthol irritation** and **has nothing to do with relieving dryness**. Some people **avoid strongly cooling drops**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Red eyes? Use redness-relief drops every day

**Repeated vasoconstrictor use causes rebound redness.** **Have the cause of the redness (dryness, allergy, inflammation) checked at an eye clinic.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Eye drops keep for a long time after opening

**About one month after opening** (product label). **Single-use vials: the same day.** **Do not let the tip touch your eye or lashes.**
