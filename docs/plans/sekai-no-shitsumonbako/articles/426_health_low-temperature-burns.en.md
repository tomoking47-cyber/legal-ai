# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about low-temperature burns (heat packs, hot-water bottles, kotatsu)?｜Japan Questions #426

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/low-temperature-burns` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Every winter, Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE (the National Institute of Technology and Evaluation) issue warnings about "low-temperature burns" from heating devices. They are tied to a distinctly Japanese way of keeping warm: disposable heat packs (カイロ, kairo), hot-water bottles (湯たんぽ, yutanpo), the kotatsu (a low table with a heater underneath and a quilt over it), electric blankets and heated carpets.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "you only get burned by touching something hot." At around 44°C (111°F), a few hours of contact damages the deeper layers of the skin: this is a "low-temperature burn", and it becomes deep while you feel little pain. The basics: do not hold a heating device against bare skin for long; take it off, switch it off or use a timer at bedtime; take extra care with older people, children, people with diabetes and after drinking. If redness or blisters appear, cool the area and see a doctor.

## Temperature and time (Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE materials)

| Contact temperature | Rough time to a low-temperature burn |
|---|---|
| **44°C (111°F)** | **About 3–4 hours** |
| **46°C (115°F)** | **About 30 minutes to 1 hour** |
| **50°C (122°F)** | **About 2–3 minutes** |

| Device | Surface temperature | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Disposable heat pack (kairo)** | **40–60°C (peak can exceed 60°C)** | **Never stick it on bare skin** (over clothing), **do not use it in bed**, **do not keep it on one spot for hours** |
| **Hot-water bottle (yutanpo)** | Surface 40–50°C for several hours | **Once the bedding is warm, take it out or move it away from your body.** **A cover is essential** |
| **Kotatsu** | Very hot near the heater | **Do not sleep inside it.** **Keep your feet away from the heater** |
| **Electric blanket, heated carpet** | 40–45°C | **Switch off or turn low at bedtime**; **do not lie on it for hours** |
| **Electric foot warmer (電気あんか, anka)** | Around 50°C | Do not hold it against your body |
| **Laptop, phone while charging** | Over 40°C | Take care with **long periods on your lap or under the covers** |

- Each winter the Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE publish **low-temperature burn cases (especially older people, infants, people with reduced sensation such as diabetes, after drinking, and during sleep)**. The hallmark is that they happen **at a temperature that "does not feel hot"**.
- **Depth**: low-temperature burns often **reach deep into the skin (second to third degree)** and are **worse than they look**. **Redness, blisters, pale patches and little pain** are signs of a deep burn.
- **What to do**: **cool under running water (15–30 minutes)**, **do not burst blisters**, **do not pull off clothing that sticks**, **go to a dermatology or plastic-surgery clinic**. **Folk fixes such as ointment, aloe or miso are not acceptable.**

> **[Verify]** Check the temperature and time guidelines and the accident cases against the Consumer Affairs Agency's "Beware of low-temperature burns" and NITE materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If it isn't hot, it can't burn you

**At 44°C, a few hours damages the deeper skin.** Low-temperature burns happen **at a "comfortable" temperature**.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A heat pack is warmer stuck straight on the skin

**Sticking it on bare skin is the classic low-temperature burn.** **Wear it over clothing, move it to a different spot, take it off before sleeping.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜You cuddle a hot-water bottle all night

**Once the bedding is warm, take it out or move it away from your feet.** **Especially for older people and children.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Sleeping in the kotatsu is a Japanese winter pleasure

**Low-temperature burns, dehydration and disrupted temperature control.** **If you are going to sleep, switch it off and go to bed.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜If it's only red, cooling is enough

**Low-temperature burns are often deeper than they look.** **Cool under running water and see a doctor.** **Folk fixes (aloe, miso, oil) cause infection.**
