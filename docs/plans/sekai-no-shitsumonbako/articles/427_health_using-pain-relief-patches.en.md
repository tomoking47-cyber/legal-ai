# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about using pain-relief patches (shippu)?｜Japan Questions #427

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/using-pain-relief-patches` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Medicated patches (湿布, shippu) are Japan's most familiar painkiller. Every household keeps some, and visitors ask "why do Japanese people use so many patches?" Here we cover the difference between cooling and warming types, how the active ingredients are classified, photosensitivity, and the misunderstanding about "where they work".

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "cooling patches cool the area and warming patches heat it." The temperature you feel comes from ingredients (menthol, capsaicin) acting on skin sensation; the actual skin temperature barely changes. What works is the anti-inflammatory painkiller (salicylates, diclofenac, loxoprofen and others). Choose by the stage of the pain (cooling is preferred for acute pain, warming for chronic stiffness) and by preference. Some ingredients, such as ketoprofen, carry a photosensitivity warning: the label requires you to keep the patched area out of sunlight.

## Types and how to choose

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Cooling vs warming** | **A difference in sensation** (menthol = cooling; capsaicin or nonivamide = warming). **Skin temperature barely changes.** The usual split is **cooling for acute pain (right after a knock or sprain), warming for chronic stiffness** (a matter of preference) |
| **Anti-inflammatory painkillers (first generation)** | **Methyl salicylate, glycol salicylate**: mild pain. Mostly **Class 3 OTC medicines** (Japan's lowest-risk over-the-counter category) |
| **Anti-inflammatory painkillers (second generation)** | **Diclofenac, loxoprofen, felbinac, indometacin, ketoprofen**: stronger. **Class 2 OTC medicines.** **Daily limit on the number of patches** (e.g. loxoprofen: once a day, up to 2 patches) |
| **Photosensitivity** | **Ketoprofen** (and some felbinac products etc.) can cause **a rash or dermatitis if the patched area is exposed to sunlight** (**take care for about 4 weeks after removal too**). **Follow the label and cover the area outdoors** |
| **How long to wear** | Per the product label (**8–12 hours, or once a day**). **Leaving a patch on too long causes dermatitis** |
| **Who and where not to use** | **Late pregnancy (NSAID-containing products), a history of asthma, skin with wounds or eczema, under 15 (some products)** — check the label |
| **Where it works** | **Absorbed through the skin at the patched site and acts locally.** **Does not work on internal or whole-body pain** |

- Patches are **medicines (Class 2 or Class 3 OTC)** with **dosage, directions and warnings** on the label. They **suppress pain and inflammation; they do not remove the cause of back pain**.
- The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and the PMDA (Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency) have repeatedly warned about **photosensitivity from ketoprofen**; the package insert says **"do not expose the patched area to sunlight while wearing it and for 4 weeks after removal."**
- **Dermatitis (contact dermatitis)** is the most common problem with patches. **Do not patch the same spot day after day, wash the skin after removal, stop if redness appears.**
- Also watch for **children swallowing patches or sticking them on by mistake** and **older people using too many (over the limit)**.

> **[Verify]** Check the classification of each ingredient, the daily patch limits and the photosensitivity warning against the product package inserts (PMDA).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cooling cools, warming warms

**A difference in sensation**; the temperature is nearly the same. **The painkilling ingredient does the work.** **Cooling for acute, warming for chronic** is a matter of preference.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It's external, so any number is safe

**Second-generation ingredients have a daily limit**; **many patches at once can cause the same side effects as tablets (stomach, kidneys)**. **Stick to the number on the label.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜It's fine to be in the sun with a patch on

**Ketoprofen and others cause photosensitivity.** **Cover the area with clothing; take care for about 4 weeks after removal too.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The longer you wear it, the better it works

**Beyond the labelled time (8–12 hours) you get dermatitis.** **Take it off and let the skin rest.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Patches get rid of the cause of back pain

**They suppress pain and inflammation**; **the cause stays the same**. Combine them with **movement, posture and warmth** (see our article on back stiffness), and **see a doctor for red-flag symptoms**.
