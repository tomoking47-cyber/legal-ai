# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about preventing falls and "locomotive syndrome"?｜Japan Questions #428

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/fall-prevention-and-locomotive-syndrome` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Among older people in Japan, falls are one of the main reasons for needing long-term care, and most of them happen at home. Here we explain why "stay still so you don't fall" is the worst possible strategy, and introduce "locomo" (locomotive syndrome), a concept proposed by the Japanese Orthopaedic Association.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "the safest way to avoid falls is not to move." Inactivity weakens muscles and balance and raises the risk of falling. The basics: exercises such as one-leg standing and squats ("Locotra"), fixing steps, lighting and handrails at home, reviewing shoes and slippers, and checking medicines and eyesight. Keeping up muscle strength and balance from your 40s helps prevent falls in old age.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Don't move, so you don't fall

**Muscle strength and balance decline, and falls increase.** **One-leg standing and squats (Locotra) every day.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Falls happen outdoors

**Most happen at home.** **Review steps, cords, lighting and slippers.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Slippers are safe

**Backless slippers slip off and trip you.** **Indoor shoes with a heel**, or **non-slip socks**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Locomo is about old people

**Muscle strength starts falling in your 40s; one "yes" on the Loco-check puts you in the at-risk group.** **Stairs, step counts and strength training** work better the earlier you start.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A fall and a fracture are just bad luck

**Bone-density testing and care for osteoporosis** lower fracture risk. The guideline for testing is **women after menopause, men from 70**. **Anyone who has fallen once has a higher risk of falling again, so review the causes.**
