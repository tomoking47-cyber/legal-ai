# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about cleaning the fridge?｜Japan Questions #431

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/cleaning-the-fridge` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The fridge is often assumed to be "clean because it is cold", and it is one of the least-cleaned appliances in any home. Japanese kitchens add their own habits: a dedicated vegetable drawer (野菜室, yasai-shitsu), a "chilled" compartment (チルド室) and small door pockets. Here we set out the "clean the fridge" advice from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) and the maintenance guidance from the Association for Electric Home Appliances.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "it is cold inside the fridge, so bacteria do not grow." Below 10°C (50°F) growth slows but does not stop, and some bacteria, such as Listeria, multiply in the cold. Spilt meat juices, vegetable scraps and out-of-date food are the source of both dirt and bacteria. The working rules are: "once a month, wipe shelves and walls with alcohol or diluted detergent", "do not forget the door seal and the bottom of the vegetable drawer", and "do not overfill (about 70%)".

## How often, and how

| Part | Frequency | Method | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shelves, walls, door pockets** | **Monthly** | **Take the food out, remove the shelves and wash them with detergent.** Wipe the walls **with diluted neutral detergent or food-safe alcohol spray, then dry-wipe** | Chlorine bleach damages plastic and metal (if you use it, dilute it and wipe it off thoroughly) |
| **Door seal (rubber gasket)** | Monthly | **Clean dirt and mould out of the grooves with a cotton bud or toothbrush** | A worn seal means poorer cooling and higher electricity bills |
| **Bottom of the vegetable drawer, chilled compartment** | **Immediately after a spill, and monthly** | Wipe up vegetable scraps, soil and meat drip | The dirtiest place in the fridge |
| **Ice maker, water tank** | Weekly | See the article on ice makers | — |
| **Freezer** | **Every 3–6 months** | Defrost (not needed with auto-defrost), sort out torn bags and expired items | — |
| **Back and underside (heat-exchange area)** | Once or twice a year | Vacuum off the dust | Poor heat release raises electricity use |
| **Odour control** | Ongoing | **Baking soda (in an open container) or activated charcoal.** **The source of smells is dirt and out-of-date food,** so clean first | — |

- MHLW's "Six points for preventing food poisoning at home" lists **not overfilling the fridge (about 70%), keeping meat and fish juices off other foods, and regular cleaning**.
- **Listeria** **multiplies even at 4°C (39°F)** and can be a problem in **natural cheeses, cured ham (生ハム) and smoked salmon**. **Pregnant women and older people should take particular care** (MHLW).
- **Overfilling** **blocks the flow of cold air, raises the temperature and increases electricity use.** **About 70%** full is the guide (Agency for Natural Resources and Energy).
- **Door opening**: **keep the door open for less time, and open it less often.**

## Misunderstanding 1｜Bacteria do not grow inside the fridge

**Growth only slows down.** **Listeria multiplies even at 4°C.** **Dirt and spills are the source.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜A deodoriser solves the smell

**The source of the smell is dirt, out-of-date food and meat or fish drip.** **Clean and sort first**; a deodoriser is only a back-up.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Cleaning is a year-end job

**Wipe the shelves monthly, and clean spills at once.** **The Japanese year-end "big clean" (大掃除, ōsōji) alone is not enough.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The fuller it is, the colder it stays

**Cold air cannot circulate, the temperature rises and electricity use goes up.** **Aim for 70%.** **The freezer is the opposite: a full freezer is more efficient** (the frozen items keep each other cold).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Chlorine bleach makes it safely sterile

It **damages plastic, metal and the door seal, and may leave residue on food.** **Wipe with food-safe alcohol or diluted neutral detergent, then dry-wipe.**
