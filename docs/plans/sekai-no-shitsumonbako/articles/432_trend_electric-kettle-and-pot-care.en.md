# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about caring for electric kettles and hot-water pots?｜Japan Questions #432

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/electric-kettle-and-pot-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Electric kettles have spread rapidly through Japanese homes and, alongside the traditional electric hot-water pot (電気ポット, denki potto — a large insulated dispenser that keeps water hot all day), they are the main way of boiling water. Here we look at the belief that "it only holds water, so it cannot get dirty", at the white crystals (limescale) that build up, and at scald accidents.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "an electric kettle only ever holds water, so it never needs cleaning." Minerals in tap water (mainly calcium) build up as white scale, which lowers heating efficiency and flakes off to float in your tea. The working rules are: "once a month, boil with citric acid (1 tablespoon per litre of water), leave for 1–2 hours and rinse", "wipe the outside and the spout" and "empty and dry after use". The most common accident is a kettle tipping over and scalding someone, especially a child.

## Care guide

| Part | Frequency | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale inside (white crystals)** | **Monthly** (every 2 weeks in hard-water areas or with heavy use) | **Dissolve 1 tablespoon (about 15 g) of citric acid (クエン酸, kuen-san, sold at drugstores and 100-yen shops) in 1 L of water, boil, leave for 1–2 hours, empty and rinse.** If the maker offers a **"citric acid cleaning" mode or its own cleaning product**, follow that |
| **Spout and filter** | Monthly | Remove and wash. Scale comes off with a cotton bud or toothbrush |
| **Outside, lid, handle** | Weekly | Wipe. **Never pour water over the electrical base** |
| **After use** | Every time | **Pour away leftover water and leave the lid open to dry** (prevents slime and smells) |
| **Electric hot-water pot (keep-warm type)** | Citric acid monthly; **change the water every day** (water kept hot for long periods can grow bacteria) | — |

- **Scale is crystallised calcium and magnesium** and **is not harmful**, but it **lowers heating efficiency, flakes off and floats, and can confuse the sensors.** **It dissolves in citric acid (an acid).** **Baking soda (an alkali) does not work on scale.**
- **Scald accidents**: the Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE (Japan's product-safety body) publish figures every year on **children scalded by kettles tipping over.** Choose a product with a **"spill-proof when tipped" (転倒お湯漏れ防止) design**, and **place it where the cord cannot be pulled and never at the edge of a table.**
- **Re-boiling**: **there is no safety problem in re-boiling water that has already been boiled**, though **the minerals become more concentrated and the taste may change.** **Boiling only what you need** saves energy.
- **Keep-warm mode on a hot-water pot**: **keeping water hot costs electricity** (about ¥10–20 a day). Switching to a **vacuum-insulated pot (keeps water hot without electricity)** is another energy-saving option.

> **[Verify]** Check the quantity and method for citric acid against the manual for your product, and the accident cases against Consumer Affairs Agency and NITE material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It only holds water, so no cleaning is needed

**Scale builds up over a matter of months, efficiency drops and flakes come off.** **Citric acid once a month.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The white crystals are dirt or something harmful

They are **minerals such as calcium** and are not harmful. **They dissolve in citric acid.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Baking soda will shift anything

**Scale is removed with an acid (citric acid).** **Baking soda is an alkali and does not work on scale** (see the article on baking soda and citric acid).

## Misunderstanding 4｜A kettle is safe wherever you put it

**The most common accident is a child scalded by a kettle tipping over.** **Choose a spill-proof design, keep the cord out of reach and stay away from the edge of the table.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Keeping the pot warm is economical

**It costs ¥10–20 a day in electricity.** **A vacuum-insulated pot, or boiling only what you need, is cheaper.** **Change water that has been kept hot every day.**
