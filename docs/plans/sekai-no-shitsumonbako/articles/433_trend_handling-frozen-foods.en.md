# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about handling frozen foods?｜Japan Questions #433

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/handling-frozen-foods` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Frozen food consumption keeps rising in Japanese homes, and the same questions come up again and again: "Can I refreeze something I have thawed?", "Do I throw it out if it has frost on it?" and "Can I eat it after the best-before date?" Here we look at how frozen food is defined in Japan and at the limits of a home freezer.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "frozen food keeps for years in the freezer without changing." At −18°C (0°F) or below bacteria do not grow, but drying out (freezer burn) and oxidation of fats continue, and a home freezer warms and cools every time the door opens. The working rules are: "use within the best-before date, and within about a month of opening", "never refreeze anything once thawed", "frost is a sign of lost quality, not a safety problem" and "if it says cook before eating, cook it".

## What to base your judgement on

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Definition of frozen food | **Pre-processed, quick-frozen, stored and distributed at −18°C or below**, and **packaged**. **The −18°C line is the Japan Frozen Food Association's and Codex's (the international standard) criterion; the Food Sanitation Act's storage standard is −15°C or below** | Japan Frozen Food Association (the Food Sanitation Act's storage standard is −15°C or below) |
| Home freezers | The target is **around −18°C**, but **the temperature swings with every opening** and **the door side is warmer** | Association for Electric Home Appliances |
| Best-before date (賞味期限, shōmi-kigen) | **Unopened and kept at −18°C or below: the date on the pack (usually 1 to 1.5 years).** **Once opened, use within about a month** (drying, oxidation) | Japan Frozen Food Association |
| Frost and freezer burn | **Frost inside the bag = the temperature rose and it refroze, or moisture got in when opened.** **White, dry patches = freezer burn.** **Safe to eat, but quality is lower** | Same |
| Refreezing | **Refreezing something once thawed risks lost quality and bacterial growth.** Most packs carry **"Do not refreeze" (再冷凍しないでください)** | Same |
| Cooking | **"Heat before eating" (加熱してお召し上がりください) means the food was not cooked before freezing, or cooking is assumed.** **"Thaw and eat" (解凍してそのまま) means it can be eaten without cooking** | Food labelling |
| Microwave | **Follow the wattage and time on the pack.** **A cold centre means it is under-heated** (a cause of food poisoning) | Same |
| Getting it home | **Buy frozen items last, use an insulated bag and put them straight in the freezer.** **In summer the surface thaws within 30 minutes** | Same |

- Japan's **domestic consumption of frozen food reached about 3.03 million tonnes in 2025, topping 3 million tonnes for the first time since records began, and about 24.6 kg per person**, an all-time high (Japan Frozen Food Association, preliminary figures released April 2026).
- **Food poisoning from frozen food** is mainly caused by **under-heating.** Cases have been reported where **products labelled "heat before eating" were eaten after thawing only.**
- **Home freezing** (batch-cooked meals, meat, vegetables) is **not quick-frozen, so quality declines faster: 2–3 weeks to a month** is the guide (see the article on freezing tips).

> **[Verify]** Check consumption figures against Japan Frozen Food Association statistics, and the meaning of the labels against the Food Labelling Standards.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It stays the same for years in the freezer

**Drying and oxidation continue, and a home freezer does not hold a steady temperature.** **Use within the date, and within a month of opening.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Frost means it is dangerous

**It is a sign of lost quality**, and **usually still safe to eat.** **Throw it out if there is heavy frost, a torn bag, discolouration or an off smell.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Thawed leftovers can go back in the freezer

**Lost quality and bacterial growth.** Many products are **designed so you take out only what you need** (portion packs).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Frozen food is pre-cooked, so it only needs warming

**"Heat before eating" means cooking is assumed.** **Check the label and heat until the centre is hot.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Buy it mid-shop and leave it in the car

**In a car in summer the surface thaws within 30 minutes, and refreezing lowers quality.** **Buy it last, use an insulated bag and freeze it at once.**
