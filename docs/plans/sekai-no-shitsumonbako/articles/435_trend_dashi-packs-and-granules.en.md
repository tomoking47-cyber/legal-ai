# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about dashi packs and granulated dashi?｜Japan Questions #435

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/dashi-packs-and-granules` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Our article on making dashi (Japanese soup stock) covered stock made from bonito flakes and kombu (dried kelp). In real kitchens, though, most dashi comes from dashi packs (だしパック, tea-bag-style sachets) or granules (顆粒だし, karyū dashi — the powder sold as "dashi no moto"). Here we compare the two, look at their salt content and explain how to read the label.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "granulated dashi and dashi packs are lumps of chemical seasoning and nothing like real dashi." A dashi pack is ground bonito flakes or kombu in a sachet, so it is almost pure ingredient. Granules (classified as "flavour seasoning", 風味調味料) are dashi extract with salt, sugars and umami seasoning added, and many products are 30–40% salt. The key to using them is salt: when you use granules, cut back on the miso and soy sauce.

## Comparison

| Item | Dashi pack | Granulated dashi (flavour seasoning) | Liquid dashi (concentrate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contents | **Ground bonito flakes, kombu, dried sardines (niboshi), shiitake and so on.** **Some are additive-free; others have salt and seasonings added** | **Dashi extract + salt + sugars + umami seasoning (monosodium glutamate, etc.)** | Dashi + soy sauce, salt, etc. (close to mentsuyu noodle sauce) |
| Label | Read the **ingredients list** (原材料名) for "salt" (食塩) and "seasoning (amino acids, etc.)" (調味料（アミノ酸等）) | Read the **salt equivalent** (食塩相当量) — **about 1–1.3 g per teaspoon (3 g)** | Same |
| Salt | **Near zero if additive-free.** Check the label on salted types | **Many products are 30–40% salt by weight** | High |
| How to use | **Put in cold water, bring to the boil and simmer 2–5 minutes** (as the pack says) | Just dissolve | Dilute |
| Storage | Room temperature unopened; **once opened, seal and refrigerate or freeze** (oxidation, insects) | Room temperature, sealed. **Cakes in humidity** | Refrigerate after opening |
| Price | Medium | Cheap | Medium |

- **"Flavour seasoning" (風味調味料)** is a **category under the Japanese Agricultural Standards (JAS)** and **the official classification of the granules known as "dashi no moto".** **Salt is the first ingredient listed** on many products; the **ingredients list is in descending order of weight**, so you can tell at a glance.
- **Umami seasoning (monosodium glutamate, MSG)** has been **assessed for safety as a food additive both in Japan and internationally**, and **no scientific evidence has been confirmed that it is "bad for you"** (Food Safety Commission of Japan; FAO/WHO). **Salt is the real issue.**
- **To cut salt**: **if you use granules, reduce the miso by 20–30%**, **switch to an additive-free dashi pack**, or **make dashi from bonito and kombu and keep it in the fridge (3 days) or freezer** (see the dashi article).

> **[Verify]** Check the flavour-seasoning standard against the MAFF JAS, salt equivalents against product labels, and the MSG assessment with the Food Safety Commission.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Granulated dashi is chemical seasoning and bad for you

**Its main components are dashi extract, salt and umami seasoning**, and **the safety of umami seasoning has been assessed.** **The issue is the salt (30–40%).**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Dashi packs and granules are the same thing

**A dashi pack is ground ingredient (some additive-free); granules contain salt and seasonings.** The **ingredients list** tells you.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Using dashi means you cut salt

**Umami can replace some saltiness, but granulated dashi itself contains salt.** The order for cutting salt is **additive-free dashi pack or home-made dashi, plus less miso.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜The longer you simmer a dashi pack, the stronger it gets

**Beyond the time on the pack (2–5 minutes) you get harsh, bitter notes.** **Take the pack out after simmering.** **Do not squeeze the bag.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Opened granules keep for years at room temperature

**They cake in humidity and lose flavour.** **Sealed, 6 months to a year.** **Dashi packs go in the fridge or freezer once opened** (oxidation, insects).
