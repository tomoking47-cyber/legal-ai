# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about washing vegetables (soil, pesticide residues, eating raw)?｜Japan Questions #437

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/washing-vegetables` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Should I wash vegetables with detergent?", "Does washing remove pesticide residues?" and "Do pre-cut vegetables need washing?" are asked in every country. Japan even sells dedicated "vegetable wash" products, and many Japanese dish detergents list "vegetables and fruit" among their uses. Here we explain how Japan's food-safety and pesticide-residue systems work.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "pesticide residues are frightening, so vegetables must be washed with detergent or a special product." Produce sold in Japan falls under the pesticide-residue limits of the Positive List system, and the guidance from the Consumer Affairs Agency (CAA), the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) and others is to wash carefully under running water. The purpose of washing is to remove soil, insects and micro-organisms (food-poisoning bacteria); detergent is not needed. Pre-cut vegetables are already washed, though many people give them a quick rinse before eating them raw. Follow the label.

## What to base your judgement on

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The residue system | **Positive List system (since May 2006)**: **food carrying pesticide residues above the limit may not be sold.** **Limits are set within the range of the Food Safety Commission's assessment (acceptable daily intake, ADI, and acute reference dose, ARfD)** | Consumer Affairs Agency; Food Safety Commission |
| Why we wash | **To remove soil, dust, insects and micro-organisms (O157, Salmonella, etc.).** **Pesticide residues are within the limits, so running water is enough** | MHLW; MAFF |
| Detergent | **Not needed.** **Food-grade detergent (labelled for vegetables and fruit) can be used, but rinse residue is a concern.** **Most Japanese dish detergents list "vegetables and fruit" as a use**, but MAFF's guidance is running water | Same |
| How to wash | **About 30 seconds under running water, rubbing.** **Leafy greens one leaf at a time; remove soil from the root end.** **Swish in a bowl of water, then rinse under the tap** | Same |
| Eating raw | **Vegetables eaten raw (salad, garnishes) need particular care.** **Herbs such as shiso (大葉) and mint too** (see the herbs article) | Same |
| Pre-cut vegetables | **Washed and sanitised** (sodium hypochlorite or similar, then rinsed). Most are labelled **"can be eaten without washing" (洗わずに食べられます).** **Eat soon after opening** | Food labelling |
| Skins | **Vegetables with nutritious skins (carrot, daikon, potato) can be washed and used unpeeled.** **Peeling is an option if residues worry you** | — |
| Blanching, boiling | **Some bitterness (in spinach, etc.) and some pesticide components decline with heating** | MAFF |

- **On food poisoning**: there have been **outbreaks of enterohaemorrhagic *E. coli* (O157) from raw vegetables** (the 2012 lightly pickled vegetable (浅漬け) outbreak, for example), so **washing off bacteria from soil and compost under running water** matters.
- **"Vegetable wash" detergents and "scallop-shell powder" (ホタテパウダー)** are sold as household goods or food additives, and **public evidence that they do more than running water is limited.** **Rinse residue** is also a concern.
- **Pesticide residues fall further with washing and cooking**, as MAFF material shows (**by a few tens of percent** with water, and further with boiling).

> **[Verify]** Check the Positive List system with MHLW, the reduction of residues by washing against MAFF material, and the sanitising process for pre-cut vegetables against MHLW's hygiene standards.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Without detergent, pesticides stay on

**Produce on sale is within the limits**, and the official guidance is that **running water is enough.** **Detergent raises the concern of rinse residue.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜The point of washing is to remove pesticides

**The main purpose is soil, insects and food-poisoning bacteria.** **Vegetables eaten raw deserve the most care.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Pre-cut vegetables are unhygienic

**They are washed and sanitised** and labelled **"can be eaten without washing".** **Eat soon after opening.** **The process can be more thorough than washing at home.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Peeling makes it safe

**Skins carry much of the nutrition, and MAFF's guidance is to wash and use unpeeled when residues are within limits.** **Peeling is a matter of preference.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Organic or pesticide-free produce needs no washing

**Soil, insects and bacteria have nothing to do with pesticide use.** **Organic produce in particular calls for care over compost-borne bacteria**: **wash under running water.**
