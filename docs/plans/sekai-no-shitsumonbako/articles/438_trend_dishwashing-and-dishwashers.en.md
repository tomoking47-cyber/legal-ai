# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about washing up and dishwashers?｜Japan Questions #438

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/dishwashing-and-dishwashers` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Washing up is a daily chore, and every country argues about whether hand-washing saves water, whether dishwashers leave things dirty, and how much pre-rinsing is needed. In Japan only about 30% of homes have a dishwasher, far below Europe and North America, and rented flats often have no space for one. Here we set out both the hand-washing know-how and the right way to use a dishwasher.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "hand-washing saves more water and energy than a dishwasher." By the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy's estimate, hand-washing in a basin uses about 40–80 L of water per session, while a dishwasher (for five people) uses about 10 L, and once hot-water costs are included the dishwasher usually works out cheaper. For hand-washing the key points are "wash in a basin, wear rubber gloves, do not overuse hot water"; for a dishwasher, "pre-rinse only to remove scraps", "use dishwasher detergent" and "load it properly".

## In numbers (Agency for Natural Resources and Energy estimates)

| Item | Hand-washing | Dishwasher |
|---|---|---|
| Water per session (5 people) | **About 40–80 L** (under a running tap) | **About 10–12 L** |
| Annual utility cost (incl. hot water) | **About ¥25,000** (running tap at 40°C (104°F)) | **About ¥17,000** (electricity + water) |
| Washing temperature | **Around 40°C** (as hot as hands can bear) | **60–80°C** (high heat helps with grease and bacteria) |
| Ownership rate (Japan) | — | **About 30%** (60–70% in Europe and North America) |

## Hand-washing: the key points

| Tip | Why |
|---|---|
| **Wash in a basin (洗い桶, arai-oke)** → **rinse everything together** | Less than half the water of a running tap |
| **Wipe grease off with paper first** | Keeps oil out of the drain and cuts detergent use |
| **Dilute the detergent with water on the sponge**, using the stated amount | Too much means more water to rinse |
| **Hot water only when needed** | Heating water is most of the utility cost |
| **Rubber gloves** | Prevents chapped hands (see the article on hand care) |
| **Order: glasses → chopsticks and plates → greasy items → pans** | Stops grease spreading |

## Dishwashers: the key points

| Tip | Why |
|---|---|
| **Pre-rinse only to remove scraps, bones and seeds.** **No scrubbing needed** | The dishwasher's sensor detects dirt and washes accordingly. Over-rinsing wastes water and time |
| **Dishwasher detergent only** (ordinary dish detergent foams and causes faults) | — |
| **Loading: do not block the spray, do not stack, openings facing down** | Poor loading is the main cause of dishes coming out dirty |
| **Do not load: wood, lacquerware, aluminium (blackens), crystal, gold-decorated china, non-heatproof plastic, iron pans** | Check for the dishwasher-safe mark (食洗機可) |
| **Clean the filter every cycle to weekly** | Smells and cleaning power |
| **Run it full, once or twice a day** | More efficient than several small loads |

> **[Verify]** Check the water and utility estimates against the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy's "Energy-saving performance catalogue" and "Complete guide to saving energy at home", and the ownership rate against the Cabinet Office Consumer Confidence Survey.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Hand-washing saves water

**A running tap uses 4–8 times the water of a dishwasher.** **If washing by hand, use a basin**; **if you have a dishwasher, run it full.**

## Misunderstanding 2｜Dishwashers leave things dirty

**The main causes are poor loading and too much or too little pre-rinsing.** **Do not block the spray, use dishwasher detergent and clean the filter.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Wash everything properly by hand before loading

**Removing scraps is enough.** **Washing twice wastes water and time**, and on some models the sensor then struggles to detect dirt.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Anything can go in the dishwasher

**Not wood, lacquer, aluminium, crystal, gold decoration or iron pans.** **Look for the "dishwasher-safe" (食洗機対応) mark.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cold water is fine for hand-washing

**Grease comes off more easily at around 40°C**, but **heating water is most of the utility cost.** **Wipe grease off with paper first, and use hot water only when needed.**
