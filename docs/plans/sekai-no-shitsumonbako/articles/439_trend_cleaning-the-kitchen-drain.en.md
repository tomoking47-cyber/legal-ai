# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about cleaning the kitchen drain?｜Japan Questions #439

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/cleaning-the-kitchen-drain` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The sink drain is the most disliked cleaning job in any kitchen, and slime, smells and blockages are a worldwide complaint. Japanese sinks have a particular design: a removable waste basket (ゴミ受け) sits in the drain opening, with a cup-shaped trap beneath it, and most homes line the basket with a disposable mesh bag. Here we explain the parts, the daily routine and how to use cleaning products safely.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "drain cleaning is a monthly job with a strong cleaner." Slime and smells form within days, so emptying the basket every day, drying it and scrubbing weekly with a brush works better than a strong cleaner once a month. Never use chlorine products (for slime and mould) and acidic or citric-acid products (for scale) at the same time, and never pour oil down the drain: that is the basis of preventing blockages.

## Parts and upkeep

| Part | What it is | Frequency | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Waste basket** | Catches food waste and vegetable scraps | **Daily**: empty and wash. **Leave to dry overnight** | Use a **mesh drain bag (水切り袋, mizukiri-bukuro, sold at 100-yen shops)** and bin it before bed |
| **Trap (cup or bowl)** | Holds a water seal that keeps sewer smells and insects out | **Weekly** | Remove and clean with **neutral detergent and a brush**; for slime, **diluted chlorine cleaner for a few minutes, then rinse thoroughly** |
| **Inside of the drain opening, cover** | Slime, black mould | Weekly | Same |
| **Drain pipe** | Oil and soap scum build up and block it | **About monthly** | **Pipe cleaner (パイプ用洗浄剤, as labelled)**, or **flush with hot water (50–60°C / 122–140°F)** (**boiling water damages the pipework**) |
| **Corner waste basket (三角コーナー)** | Temporary holder for food scraps | Daily | Fewer homes use one now (scraps go straight into the mesh bag) |

| Safety | Detail |
|---|---|
| **"Do not mix — danger" (まぜるな危険)** | **Never use chlorine products (mould, slime) and acidic products (citric acid, vinegar, some pipe cleaners) at the same time.** **They give off toxic chlorine gas.** **Use them on different days, after rinsing well** |
| **Ventilation** | Open a window and wear rubber gloves when using chlorine products |
| **Hot water** | **Ordinary PVC drain pipe (VP) is rated to about 60°C.** **Water above 60°C can deform the pipe and degrade the joints** (even heat-resistant HT PVC tops out at 90°C). **If you do pour, 50–60°C at most** |
| **Oil** | **Never pour it down the drain** (wipe it up or solidify it). **It blocks pipes and burdens the sewage system** (see the article on used frying oil) |

- **Slime is a bacterial biofilm** that needs **food waste and moisture.** **Emptying and drying every day** is the best prevention.
- **Fizzing baking soda with citric acid** **helps lift dirt a little**, but **once mixed they largely neutralise each other and the cleaning power is weak** (see the article on baking soda and citric acid). **Never mix with chlorine products.**
- **If a smell will not go**, the **trap's water seal may have dried out** (long absence, evaporation). **Running the tap restores it.**

> **[Verify]** Check the "do not mix" warning with the Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Public Health, and pipe heat tolerance against plumbing manufacturers' material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A strong cleaner once a month is enough

**Slime forms within days.** **Emptying and drying the basket daily and brushing weekly** works better.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Chlorine plus citric acid is extra powerful

**Toxic gas.** **Different days, after rinsing well.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Boiling water flushes away oil and bacteria in one go

**Ordinary PVC pipe (VP) is rated to about 60°C, and water above 60°C damages the pipes and seals.** **If you do pour, 50–60°C.** **Not pouring oil down in the first place** is the rule.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Fizzing baking soda and citric acid cleans completely

**The fizz is only a help, and after neutralising the cleaning power is weak.** **For slime, chlorine cleaner (on its own) or a brush**; **for scale, citric acid (on its own).**

## Misunderstanding 5｜The smell is nothing to do with the drain being dirty

**The main causes are a dried-out trap seal, leftovers in the basket and slime.** Check in this order: **run the tap, empty the basket, wash.**
