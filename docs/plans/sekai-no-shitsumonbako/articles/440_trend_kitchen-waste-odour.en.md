# Q. What are the common misunderstandings about dealing with kitchen-waste smells?｜Japan Questions #440

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/kitchen-waste-odour` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

The smell of food waste is the biggest summer headache in a Japanese kitchen. Burnable rubbish (可燃ごみ, kanen-gomi, which includes food waste) is usually collected only twice a week, so a great deal of household know-how has built up around the question "how do I keep it until the next collection day?" Here we cover the causes of the smell, and the answers from draining and freezing to waste-disposal appliances.

## A. The biggest misunderstanding is "you stop the smell with a deodoriser or a lidded bin." The smell comes from bacteria multiplying in the moisture and warmth of food waste. The order of action is: "drain off the water (the biggest factor)", "wrap it in newspaper to absorb moisture", "freeze meat and fish until collection day" and "keep the bin somewhere airy and wipe the inside"; a deodoriser is only a back-up. Council subsidies for food-waste disposal appliances, and composting, are further options.

## Causes and answers

| Cause | Detail | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Moisture** | **About 80% of food waste is water.** The wetter it is, the more bacteria grow, bringing smells and fruit flies | **Drain thoroughly in the corner basket or a mesh bag**, **wrap in newspaper or scrap paper** (absorbs water), **dry tea leaves and coffee grounds** |
| **Temperature** | Bacteria multiply fast in **a summer kitchen (25–35°C / 77–95°F)** | **Seal meat and fish trays, bones and skins in a bag and freeze**; put out on the morning of collection |
| **Air (aerobic) vs sealed (anaerobic)** | Sealed too tightly, **anaerobic bacteria give a much stronger smell** | **Close the bag loosely and keep the bin somewhere airy.** **If it has a lid, wipe the inside** |
| **Sour smell** | Rotting turns acidic | **Sprinkle baking soda** (alkaline, neutralises it) |
| **Fruit flies** | Food waste, fruit peel, the drain | **Seal fruit peel in a bag**, **clean the drain** (see the previous article) |
| **Rubbish bags** | Council-designated bags (指定袋) | **Double-bag**, **tie the neck** |

| Reduce or process | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Food-waste disposal appliance (drying or bio type)** | **Many councils subsidise 1/2 to 2/3 of the purchase price (capped at ¥20,000–30,000)** |
| **Composting (balcony or cardboard box)** | **Cardboard-box composting and sealed containers** are common even in cities. Councils run courses |
| **Sink disposal unit (ディスポーザー)** | **Installation needs council permission and a dedicated processing tank** (most councils do not allow a stand-alone unit) |
| **Reducing food loss** | **Buy less, use it all up** (see the food-loss article) is the root answer |

- **Household food waste is about 30–40% of burnable rubbish by weight**, and **80% of that is water.** **Draining alone lightens the load and fuel use at council incinerators** (Ministry of the Environment and council campaigns).
- **Freezing food waste**: many councils say that **although people balk at "food waste in the freezer", there is no hygiene problem if it is sealed**, and it is **standard summer practice.**
- **Wipe the inside of the bin weekly and let it dry.** **Deodorisers and odour-blocking bags** are a back-up.

> **[Verify]** Check the share and water content of food waste against Ministry of the Environment and council material, and appliance subsidies with your own council. Whether a sink disposal unit is permitted also varies by council.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A deodoriser and a lid stop the smell

**The cause is moisture and warmth.** **Drain, wrap in newspaper and freeze first**; a deodoriser is a back-up.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Seal it and it will not smell

**Sealed too tightly, anaerobic bacteria make a stronger smell.** **Close loosely, keep it somewhere airy, wipe the inside.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Putting food waste in the freezer is unhygienic

**Many councils say there is no hygiene problem if it is sealed**, and **freezing meat and fish waste in summer is standard practice.** **Set aside a dedicated bag and spot.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜A sink disposal unit solves everything

**Most councils do not allow a stand-alone unit** (the burden on sewage works). **Permission is needed, with a dedicated processing tank.**

## Misunderstanding 5｜Disposal appliances are expensive

**Council subsidies (1/2 to 2/3, capped at ¥20,000–30,000)** are common. **Cardboard-box composting** starts from a few hundred yen.
