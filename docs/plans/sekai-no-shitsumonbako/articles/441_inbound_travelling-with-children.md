# Q. What do people get wrong about travelling in Japan with children?｜Japan Questions #441

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/travelling-with-children` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan is one of the safest and most child-friendly countries to travel in, and one where the infrastructure for children — nursing rooms, kids' menus, changing tables in men's toilets — is easy to miss because it is not advertised in English. A few rules on trains and in restaurants are the exceptions.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japan is hard with small children because it is crowded and formal. Department stores and malls have nursing rooms with hot water and changing tables, children under 6 ride trains free, most restaurants have children's cutlery and a kids' plate, and convenience stores sell nappies and baby food. What needs planning: strollers on rush-hour trains, the lack of high chairs at counter restaurants, and pharmacies for children's medicine.

## The practical facts

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Train fares** | **Under 6: free** (up to two per fare-paying passenger; a third infant pays child fare); **6–11: half fare**; 12+: adult. Shinkansen: under 6 free without a seat, half fare if they occupy their own reserved seat |
| **Strollers** | Allowed on all trains; **fold or use the wheelchair space** in rush hour; **elevators at almost all stations** (marked on station maps); **priority space stickers** in some cars |
| **Nursing rooms (授乳室)** | **Department stores, malls, large stations, airports, some parks**; usually with **hot water, changing tables, microwaves, sinks**; apps (e.g. "Mamamap") list them |
| **Changing tables** | In **women's and many men's toilets**, and in multipurpose (accessible) toilets |
| **Restaurants** | **Kids' plates (お子様ランチ)** at family restaurants and department-store floors; **children's cutlery** on request almost everywhere; **high chairs** at family restaurants, **not at counters or izakaya** |
| **Supplies** | **Nappies, wipes, baby food, formula (powder and liquid since 2019), children's medicine** at drugstores and many convenience stores |
| **Medicine** | **Children's acetaminophen, cold medicine, ORS** OTC; **#8000** for night/weekend advice (Japanese-language; hours vary by prefecture, typically 19:00–08:00); **paediatric clinics** widespread |
| **Hotels** | **Children under 6 often free sharing a bed** (co-sleeping is normal); **family rooms and ryokan** for 3+; request a **cot (ベビーベッド)** early |
| **Discounts** | **Children under 6 free at most museums**, discounts to 12 or 15 |

- **Japan's child mortality and injury rates are among the lowest in the world**; the main travel risks are **heat in summer**, **stairs in older stations**, and **traffic on narrow streets without pavements**.
- **Etiquette**: children are welcomed, but **noise on trains and in quiet restaurants** is the one thing that draws looks (see the train etiquette article). **Family restaurants and food courts** are the low-stress option.
- **Tattoos, onsen and children**: **children can enter onsen**; some baths have **age limits for mixed-gender entry (often 6–7)**; nappy-wearing infants are excluded from most communal baths.

> **[Verify]** Fare rules with JR East and each operator; #8000 with MHLW; hotel child policies vary.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Japan is not set up for children

**Nursing rooms, changing tables, kids' plates and free travel under 6** are standard; they are just labelled in Japanese. Department stores are the reliable base.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Strollers are impractical on trains

**Every major station has elevators**; the rule is to **avoid 7:30–9:00 and fold in a packed car**. Off-peak is easy.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Children's medicine needs a doctor

**OTC children's formulations** are at every drugstore; **#8000 gives advice by phone** nights and weekends; **paediatric clinics** are numerous and cheap under the national system (visitors pay in full but fees are modest).

## Misunderstanding 4｜Restaurants will not want children

**Family restaurants, department-store floors, food courts and most ordinary restaurants** welcome them with kids' plates. **Counter sushi, high-end kaiseki and izakaya at night** are where children are out of place.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Hotels charge per child

**Under 6 sharing a bed is usually free** at business and city hotels; check the policy. **Ryokan charge per person including meals** — ask for a children's meal rate.
