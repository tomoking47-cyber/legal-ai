# Q. What do people get wrong about lost property in Japan?｜Japan Questions #443

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/lost-property` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan's reputation for returning lost wallets is deserved and it rests on a system, not only on honesty: a legal duty to hand in found items, a national police lost-property database, and station and store procedures that are followed. Visitors who know the system get their things back; those who assume the worst never ask.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a lost item is gone. In Japan, most lost items are handed in — Tokyo police receive about 4 million items a year and return the majority of wallets and phones — because the Lost Property Act obliges finders to hand items in and rewards them, and because stations, taxis and shops have procedures. The steps: retrace and ask at the venue the same day, then the police lost-property centre (with an online search in English for Tokyo), then your embassy for a passport.

## How the system works

| Step | Where | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Same day** | **The venue** (station office, shop, restaurant, taxi company) | Items are kept locally for **a few days to a week** before going to police; **JR East has an English lost-and-found phone line and online form** |
| **2. Within days** | **Police lost-property centre (遺失物センター)** or any **kōban** | File a report (遺失届); **Tokyo Metropolitan Police has an English online search** of found items; other prefectures by phone |
| **3. Passport** | Police report → **your embassy or consulate** for an emergency passport | Keep a photocopy separately |
| **4. Cards and phones** | Cancel cards; **phones are usually found** — use the tracking app, and note that finders often leave phones with the venue |
| Reward | Finder is legally entitled to **5–20% of the value**; **most decline** | Lost Property Act |
| Retention | Police hold items **3 months**; unclaimed valuables go to the finder or the prefecture |

- **Tokyo Metropolitan Police** received about **4.4 million lost items in 2023**; **cash handed in exceeded ¥4 billion**, of which about **three-quarters was returned to owners**.
- The **Lost Property Act (revised 2007)** requires finders to hand in items promptly, creates the police database, and allows quick disposal of perishables and low-value items; the finder's reward and the option to keep unclaimed items are incentives.
- **Trains**: items found on a train are taken off at the **terminal** and held at that station or the line's lost-property office; **know the train, car and time** to speed the search.
- **Taxis**: the receipt shows the **company and vehicle number**; call the company.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A lost wallet is gone

**Most are handed in.** Ask at the venue the same day, then the police centre. Cash is often returned intact.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Go straight to the police

**The venue first** — items stay at the station or shop for days before transfer. The police centre is the second stop, and the **online search** saves a trip.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Nobody will understand you at the lost-property counter

**Major stations, airports and the Tokyo police centre have English support**, and the JR East lost-and-found form is in English. A **photo of the item** on your phone helps enormously.

## Misunderstanding 4｜You should pay a big reward

**5–20% is the legal entitlement**; **most finders refuse**. A polite thank-you is expected; insisting on a reward can embarrass.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A lost phone means a new phone

**Phones are among the most returned items.** Track it, contact the venue, and wait a day — it is likely at a station office or kōban.
