# Q. What do people get wrong about withdrawing cash in Japan?｜Japan Questions #444

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/atms-and-cash-withdrawal` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan uses less cash than it did, but a visitor still needs some — for small restaurants, shrines, rural areas and some taxis. Which ATM works with a foreign card, what it costs, and when the machines close are the questions that cause airport panic.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that any bank ATM will take a foreign card. Most Japanese bank ATMs do not. The reliable ones are Seven Bank (at 7-Eleven, 27,000+ machines, 24 hours), Japan Post Bank (at post offices and some Lawson stores), Lawson Bank, and the international ATMs at airports and major stations. Fees are ¥110–220 per withdrawal plus your own bank's charge; a ¥100,000 limit per transaction is common.

- **Your bank's fee** (typically **1–3% foreign transaction fee plus a fixed withdrawal fee**) usually exceeds the ATM fee; **withdraw larger amounts less often**.
- **Decline the "dynamic currency conversion"** offer if presented — being charged in yen and letting your bank convert is almost always cheaper.
- **Cash need in 2025**: cashless payment is at **about 40% of consumer spending**; cards and IC work in cities, but **shrines, small restaurants, rural shops, some ryokan and coin lockers** still want cash. **¥20,000–30,000 in cash** at any time is a reasonable buffer.
- **Notes**: ¥1,000, ¥5,000, ¥10,000 (new designs since July 2024; old notes remain valid). **Coins ¥1–¥500**; the ¥500 is worth having for lockers and machines.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Any ATM takes foreign cards

**Most bank ATMs do not.** **7-Eleven (Seven Bank) and post offices** are the dependable ones, 24 hours in the former case.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Exchange cash at home before flying

**ATM withdrawal in Japan is usually cheaper** than airport exchange counters or pre-ordered cash, even with fees.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Japan is cash-only

**About 40% cashless and rising**; cards and IC cards handle most city spending. **Carry a cash buffer** for the exceptions (see the cash-and-cards article).

## Misunderstanding 4｜The ATM screen is in Japanese

**Seven Bank and Japan Post ATMs switch to English** (and several other languages) at the first screen.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Withdraw small amounts to be safe

**Each withdrawal carries fixed fees on both sides**; Japan is safe enough that **¥50,000–100,000 per withdrawal** is the economical choice for most visitors.
