# Q. What do people get wrong about buying electronics and cameras in Japan?｜Japan Questions #446

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/buying-electronics-and-cameras` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Akihabara and the big electronics chains are on many itineraries, and the assumption is that Japanese electronics are cheaper in Japan. Sometimes they are; often the advantages are range, tax-free and the overseas models — and the pitfalls are voltage, warranty and language.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that electronics are automatically cheaper in Japan. Prices for cameras and lenses are often competitive after tax-free (10% off), and Japan-only models exist, but many phones and laptops cost the same or more, and domestic models may have Japanese-only menus, keyboards and 100 V plugs. Check the model is an international version, confirm the warranty covers your country, and use the tax-free counter with your passport.

## What to check

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tax-free** | **10% consumption tax waived** on goods over ¥5,000 (until 31 October 2026); **from 1 November 2026 you pay the tax at the till and are refunded after customs checks the goods at departure**, and the general/consumable split and ¥500,000 cap are abolished (see the tax-free article). Large chains have dedicated counters |
| **Price** | **Cameras, lenses, some audio and gaming** are often good value; **phones, laptops, tablets** are usually **not cheaper** than in the US/EU. Compare online first |
| **Model version** | **Domestic (国内) models** may have **Japanese-only menus, Japanese keyboards (JIS layout), 100 V-only chargers, no English manual, and Japan-specific features** (e.g. shutter sound that cannot be muted on Japanese-market phones). Ask for the **"overseas model" (海外モデル)** or check menu language in the box |
| **Warranty** | **Manufacturer warranties are often Japan-only**; some brands offer **international warranty** (check the card). Retailer extended warranties are Japan-only |
| **Voltage / plugs** | **Japan is 100 V, Type A** (see the plugs article). Chargers rated 100–240 V are fine abroad; **hair dryers, kettles, some appliances are 100 V only** |
| **Frequency bands** | Phones: check **LTE/5G bands** for your country; Japanese-market phones may lack some |
| **Region locks** | **Blu-ray/DVD regions** (Japan is Region A / 2); game consoles are mostly region-free now but **software may be Japanese-only** |
| **Batteries in luggage** | **Lithium batteries in carry-on only**, spares protected from short circuit; **power banks over 160 Wh** not allowed, 100–160 Wh limited to two with airline approval; on flights to/from Japan since July 2025 power banks must stay with you (not in the overhead bin) |
| **Second-hand** | **Camera shops in Shinjuku/Ginza grade used gear rigorously** (A/B/C); prices firm, quality reliable; **Akihabara's used PC parts** likewise |

- Big-chain **points programmes (10% "points") are not usable by tourists** in most cases but tax-free is; **some stores stack a tourist discount coupon (5–7%) with tax-free** — ask.
- **Japanese shutter sound**: **domestic-market phones force the camera shutter sound** (a carrier convention); overseas models do not.
- **Duty at home**: your **country's import allowance** applies on return (see the customs article); a ¥300,000 camera may attract duty.

> **[Verify]** Tax-free procedure with the National Tax Agency; battery rules with your airline; model-specific details with the manufacturer.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Everything is cheaper in Akihabara

**Cameras and lenses often; phones and laptops rarely.** Compare online before you fly; **tax-free plus a tourist coupon** is where the saving lies.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Any model will work at home

**Domestic models may be Japanese-only in menus and keyboard, 100 V, or lack your LTE bands.** Ask for the overseas model or confirm language settings.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The warranty is worldwide

**Often Japan-only.** Check for an international warranty card; otherwise a fault means shipping it back to Japan.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Japanese phones are the same as international ones

**Domestic phones may have a forced shutter sound, carrier bloatware and different bands.** Buy the **SIM-free overseas model** or check the specifications.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Used electronics are risky

**Japanese used-camera and PC-parts shops grade and test rigorously** and offer short warranties; prices are firm but condition is reliable. It is one of the better reasons to shop in Japan.
