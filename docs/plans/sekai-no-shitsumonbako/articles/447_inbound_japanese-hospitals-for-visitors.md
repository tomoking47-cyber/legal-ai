# Q. What do people get wrong about seeing a doctor in Japan as a visitor?｜Japan Questions #447

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/japanese-hospitals-for-visitors` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Getting sick abroad is frightening, and Japan's system is unfamiliar: clinics rather than GPs, cash payment, a referral culture for big hospitals, and limited English. It is also fast, inexpensive by Western standards and high quality, if you know where to go.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a visitor should go to a large hospital's emergency department for anything. For non-emergencies, a neighbourhood clinic (naika = general internal medicine) sees walk-ins the same day, charges ¥5,000–15,000 without insurance, and can prescribe; large hospitals charge an extra ¥7,000+ without a referral. For emergencies, 119 is free and ambulances are free. The Japan National Tourism Organization runs a 24-hour multilingual medical helpline, and travel insurance is the thing to have arranged before you fly.

## Where to go

| Situation | Go to | Cost without insurance (approx.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold, stomach upset, rash, minor injury** | **Clinic (クリニック / 診療所)** — 内科 (internal medicine), 小児科 (paediatrics), 皮膚科 (dermatology), 整形外科 (orthopaedics) | **¥5,000–15,000** consultation + medicine | Walk-in; morning and late-afternoon sessions; closed Sunday and often Wednesday or Thursday afternoon |
| **Need English** | Clinics listed on **JNTO's medical institution search** or **your embassy's list**; international clinics in Tokyo/Osaka/Kyoto | Higher (¥10,000–30,000) | Some hotels have a doctor on call |
| **After hours** | **Municipal holiday/night clinic (休日夜間急患センター)**; **#7119** for advice (Tokyo and many regions); **#8000** children | — | See the clinic article |
| **Emergency (chest pain, stroke signs, severe injury, unconscious)** | **119** — ambulance is **free**; hospital treatment is charged | ER visit **¥20,000–100,000+** | Say "kyūkyū desu" (emergency); operators handle English via interpretation |
| **Large hospital without referral** | Possible but **¥7,000+ surcharge** and long waits | — | For serious or specialist needs |
| **Pharmacy** | **Prescription filled at a pharmacy (薬局)** near the clinic; OTC at drugstores | ¥1,000–5,000 | Bring the prescription slip |

- **JNTO's 24-hour "Japan Visitor Hotline" (050-3816-2787)** covers medical questions in English, Chinese and Korean and can locate a clinic.
- **Payment**: **cash or card at the counter after the visit**; **get an itemised receipt (領収書 / 診療明細書)** for your travel insurer. **Direct billing** to insurers exists only at some international clinics.
- **Prescriptions**: clinics prescribe **short courses (days, not months)**; **bring your usual medicines** (see the medicine article). Japanese doses may be **lower** than you are used to.
- **Cost benchmark**: without insurance you pay **100% of the national fee schedule** — a consultation is set nationally at about **¥2,900 first visit**, plus tests and medicine; **hospitals may charge visitors 200–300% of the schedule** since 2016 to cover interpretation and admin.

> **[Verify]** Hotline number and clinic search with JNTO; fee schedule and the surcharge for uninsured foreign patients with MHLW guidance; #7119 coverage with FDMA.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Go to the biggest hospital

**Clinics handle most illness the same day for a modest fee**; big hospitals add a referral surcharge and hours of waiting. Use hospitals for emergencies and referrals.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Nobody will speak English

**JNTO's hotline and clinic search, embassy lists and international clinics** exist; many doctors read English even if they do not speak it fluently; **translation apps work for symptoms**. Bring your **medication list in generic names**.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Ambulances are expensive

**Ambulances are free**; the hospital bill is not. Call 119 for real emergencies without hesitating over cost.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Travel insurance is optional for a safe country

**Safe, yes; free, no.** An ER visit or a night in hospital runs to **hundreds of thousands of yen**. Insurance with medical cover is the single most important pre-trip purchase.

## Misunderstanding 5｜You will get a long prescription

**Japanese clinics prescribe a few days at a time**; you may need to return. Bring enough of your own regular medication for the whole trip plus a margin.
