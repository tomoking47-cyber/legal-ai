# Q. What do people get wrong about tattoos at gyms, pools and beaches in Japan?｜Japan Questions #449

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/tattoos-gyms-and-pools` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The tattoo question is usually asked about onsen (see that article), but the same rules extend, unevenly, to gyms, public pools, water parks and some beaches. Coverage rules, sticker patches and the slow change since 2020 are what a tattooed visitor needs to know.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that tattoos are illegal or banned everywhere in Japan. They are legal; the bans are private-venue policies rooted in historic association with organised crime, and they are loosening. Most gyms, public pools and water parks still require tattoos to be covered (rash guards, patches), many sento and an increasing number of onsen admit them, beaches vary by municipality, and small tattoos covered with a patch pass almost everywhere.

## Where tattoos are an issue

| Venue | Typical policy (2025) | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| **Onsen resorts** | **Many still refuse visible tattoos**; growing number allow small covered ones or all | Private bath (kashikiri), tattoo-friendly lists (see the onsen article) |
| **Sento (public baths)** | **Mostly admitted** | — |
| **Gyms (chains)** | **Usually "cover them"**; some chains refuse | **Long sleeves, compression wear, tattoo cover patches (sold at drugstores, ¥500–1,000)** |
| **Public swimming pools** | **Often "cover them"** or refuse | **Rash guard / swim leggings** are normal swimwear in Japan anyway |
| **Water parks, hotel pools** | Varies; hotel pools often relaxed | Ask at check-in |
| **Beaches** | **Mostly unregulated**, but **some municipalities (e.g. Kanagawa's Zushi) have ordinances against exposing tattoos** at designated beaches | Rash guard |
| **Ryokan private baths** | No issue | — |

- Tattoos are **legal**; a 2020 Supreme Court decision confirmed **tattooing is not a medical act**, ending the prosecution of artists. **About 1.4 million Japanese adults have tattoos** (2014 estimate, likely higher now).
- The **Japan Tourism Agency (2016)** asked bathing facilities to consider **admitting tattooed visitors with covering stickers or private-bath options**; adoption is uneven but rising, especially in **Hokkaido, Kyushu and international resort areas**.
- **Cover patches** (タトゥーシール / カバーシール) are **skin-tone waterproof stickers** sold at drugstores; a **small tattoo under a patch** is accepted at most venues that require covering.
- **Rash guards and swim leggings** are **standard Japanese swimwear** for sun protection, so covering at a pool is unremarkable.

> **[Verify]** The 2020 Supreme Court ruling; JTA guidance (2016); venue policies change — check each.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Tattoos are illegal

**Legal.** The restrictions are private policies at specific venues.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Every gym and pool will refuse you

**Most require covering, not exclusion.** Compression wear and cover patches solve it in almost all cases; ask at the desk.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Beaches ban tattoos

**Most do not**; a **few municipalities have ordinances** for their managed beaches. A rash guard is normal beachwear regardless.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cover patches are a hack that will be challenged

**They are the venue-suggested solution**, sold in drugstores, and staff are used to them. A large piece cannot be patched; a private bath or tattoo-friendly venue is then the route.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Nothing has changed

**Sento are open, onsen are shifting, hotel pools relaxed, the law is settled.** The remaining bans are at traditional resort onsen and some gym chains — and they are the minority now.
