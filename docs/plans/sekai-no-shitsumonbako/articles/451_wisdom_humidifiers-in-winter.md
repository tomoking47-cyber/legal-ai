# Q. What do people get wrong about humidifiers in a Japanese winter?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #451

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/humidifiers-in-winter` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The dehumidifier runs from June to September; from December the humidifier takes over. Japanese homes swing from 80% humidity to 25% in a year, and the winter machine has its own rules — about what water to use, how to clean it, and the illness named after it.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a humidifier is a set-and-forget appliance. Heated Japanese rooms drop to 20–30% humidity, which dries skin and airways, and a humidifier brings them to the 40–60% target. But a humidifier tank is standing water in a warm room: cleaned less than weekly it grows bacteria and mould, and ultrasonic types spray whatever is in the tank into the air — the cause of "humidifier lung". Tap water, daily refills, weekly cleaning, a hygrometer to avoid overshooting.

## The types and the rules

| Type | How | Pros | Cons | Cleaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Steam (heated)** | Boils water | **Sterile mist**, fast, no minerals in the air | Higher power (**200–400 W**), hot water hazard for children | Descale with citric acid monthly |
| **Ultrasonic** | Vibrates water into droplets | Cheap, quiet, low power | **Sprays whatever is in the tank** — bacteria, minerals (white dust) | **Daily water change, weekly cleaning; never leave water standing** |
| **Evaporative (fan over a wet filter)** | Air over a wet wick | Cannot over-humidify much; low power | Filter grows mould; slower | Weekly filter clean, replace per manual |
| **Hybrid (heated evaporative)** | Warm air over a filter | Balance | Price | Weekly |
| No machine | **Wet towels hung up, laundry drying indoors, a bowl of water, the bathroom door open after a bath, plants** | Free | Slow, uncontrolled | — |

- **Target: 40–60% relative humidity**; below 40% dries mucous membranes and skin (and viruses survive longer in dry air), above 60% brings **condensation and mould** (see the condensation article). **A ¥1,000 hygrometer** is the essential accessory.
- **"Humidifier lung"** (加湿器肺) is a hypersensitivity pneumonitis from bacteria and mould in a dirty tank, reported in Japan mostly with **ultrasonic units cleaned rarely**; a **2017–18 outbreak at a care home in Ōita**, traced to an ultrasonic humidifier, infected three residents and killed one. **Use tap water** (chlorinated) not mineral water, **empty and dry daily, clean weekly**.
- **White dust** on furniture near an ultrasonic unit is **minerals from the water**; it is a sign the unit is spraying tank contents — and a reason many households moved to steam or hybrid types.
- **Placement**: away from walls and windows (condensation), not blowing on a person, at table height.

> **[Verify]** Humidity targets with the Building Standards for indoor environment (MHLW); humidifier-related pneumonitis and the 2018 Legionella incident with MHLW and press reports.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Fill it once and forget it

**Standing water in a warm room is a culture medium.** Empty and refill daily; clean weekly (citric acid for scale, then rinse and dry).

## Misunderstanding 2｜Ultrasonic is the modern, best type

**It is the cheapest and the riskiest**: it aerosolises whatever is in the tank. Steam or hybrid types are what Japanese households have moved toward after the pneumonitis reports.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Mineral water is cleaner for the tank

**Tap water's chlorine suppresses growth; mineral water has none.** Use tap water, as every manual says.

## Misunderstanding 4｜More humidity is better in winter

**Above 60% you get condensation on windows and mould behind furniture.** Run to a hygrometer; 50% is the comfortable target.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Humidifiers are only for comfort

**Dry airways are more vulnerable to respiratory infection**, and skin and eyes suffer (see the winter dryness article). The health case is real — provided the machine is clean.
