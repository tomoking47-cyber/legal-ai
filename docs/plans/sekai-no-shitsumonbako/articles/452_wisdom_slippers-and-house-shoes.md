# Q. What do people get wrong about slippers and house shoes in Japan?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #452

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/slippers-and-house-shoes` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

If shoes come off at the door, what goes on? In Japan the answer is a small system of indoor footwear — corridor slippers, toilet slippers, guest slippers, bare feet or socks on tatami — that visitors half-know and mostly get slightly wrong.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that slippers are optional comfort. In a Japanese home the floor is where people sit and sleep, so its cleanliness is protected by a hierarchy: outdoor shoes stop at the genkan; slippers cover wooden and vinyl floors; tatami is walked on in socks or bare feet only; the toilet has its own pair. Guests are handed slippers, and the two rules that matter are: never on tatami, never out of the toilet.

## The hierarchy

| Zone | Footwear | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Genkan (entrance, lower level)** | Outdoor shoes | The outside |
| **Wooden or vinyl floors (corridor, kitchen, living room)** | **Slippers** (household or guest pairs) | Floors are cold and the slipper is the boundary layer |
| **Tatami rooms** | **Socks or bare feet — slippers off** | Slippers scuff and dirty the woven straw; tatami is sat and slept on |
| **Toilet** | **Toilet slippers** (a separate pair kept inside the door) | The toilet floor is treated as unclean; the slippers never leave |
| **Bathroom (wet room)** | **Bare feet** or bathroom sandals | Wet |
| **Balcony** | **Balcony sandals** kept by the door | Outdoors again |
| Sitting on the floor | Slippers **off** when sitting on a cushion or the floor | — |

- **Guest slippers** are kept in a rack at the genkan; **a host places them facing into the house**; guests step out of shoes directly into them. Leaving, you step from slippers into shoes and **turn the slippers to face inward** for the next person.
- **Cold floors** are the practical origin: Japanese homes lack central heating and have hard floors; **slippers are insulation** (see the layering article). Winter slippers are fleece-lined; summer ones are woven or open.
- **Hygiene**: slippers are washed or replaced; **disposable guest slippers** are sold for visitors, and **antibacterial vinyl slippers** for clinics.
- **Fall risk**: **backless slippers are a documented cause of falls among the elderly**; households with older members switch to **heel-strap house shoes** (see the fall prevention article).

> **[Verify]** Fall-risk guidance with the Consumer Affairs Agency; the rest is convention.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Slippers are optional

**They are the boundary between floor zones**, and offered to every guest. Refusing them is odd; wearing them everywhere is worse.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Slippers on tatami are fine

**Never.** Tatami is for socks or bare feet. Slippers stop at the edge of the mat.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Toilet slippers are just extra slippers

**They live inside the toilet door and never leave it.** Switching in and out is the one manoeuvre every guest must learn.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Bare feet are cleaner than slippers

**On tatami, yes; on a cold vinyl floor, the slipper is for you** as much as the floor. In summer bare feet are common; in winter nobody goes without.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Any slipper will do for grandparents

**Backless slippers cause falls**; households with elderly members use **house shoes with a heel** and non-slip soles. It is one of the standard fall-prevention items on municipal checklists.
