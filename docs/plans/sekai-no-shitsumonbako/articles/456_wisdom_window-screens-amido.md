# Q. What do people get wrong about window screens (amido) in Japanese homes?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #456

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/window-screens-amido` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Every Japanese window and balcony door has a sliding insect screen, and residents use them from May to October as a matter of course. Visitors from countries without screens do not notice them; visitors from countries with fixed screens do not know they slide, come off, and need cleaning.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the screen is a fixed part of the window. Japanese screens (amido) slide in their own track, lift out for cleaning, and are the reason windows can stay open in mosquito season. The habits: keep the screen closed whenever the window is open, align it with the correct half of the window so no gap opens at the frame, wash it in spring and autumn, and replace the mesh (a ¥1,000 DIY job) when it tears.

## How the screen works

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Position | **Slides in the outermost track**; it covers **one half** of the window opening at a time. **Open the sash on the side the screen covers**; open the other side and there is a gap at the centre frame |
| Removal | **Lift the screen and swing the bottom out** (some have a release tab at the top). Do it on a windless day; screens fall from balconies |
| Cleaning | **Spring and autumn**: hose or shower, soft brush, mild detergent, dry before refitting. In place: **two sponges (one each side) or a screen roller**; **vacuum with a brush head** for dust and pollen |
| Mesh | **Grey or black polypropylene, 18–24 mesh**; **black is easier to see through**, grey reflects more sun; **finer (24–30) blocks small insects and some pollen** |
| Replacement | **Mesh, rubber cord and a roller tool: about ¥1,000–1,500** at any home centre; 30 minutes; **renter-friendly** (no damage to the frame) |
| Pets | **Pet-proof mesh** (thicker) and **screen locks** exist; cats push screens out |
| Balcony door | **Magnetic screen curtain** if the door has no built-in screen |

- **Mosquito season** runs **May–October**; dengue transmission in Tokyo in 2014 (about 160 cases, Yoyogi Park) made screens and removal of standing water public-health advice (see the insect article).
- Screens also stop **the larger cockroaches and moths** that fly in summer, and they let households **ventilate without air conditioning** on mild nights (see the ventilation article).
- The **gap at the centre frame** — screen on the left, window opened on the right — is the most common reason a household "has screens but still gets mosquitoes".

> **[Verify]** The 2014 dengue outbreak with the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare; mesh specifications with manufacturers.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The screen is fixed; nothing to do

**It slides, lifts out and needs washing twice a year.** Pollen and city dust clog it by June.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Open whichever half of the window you like

**Open the half the screen covers.** Otherwise the frame between the sashes leaves a mosquito-sized gap — the classic error.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A torn screen means calling the landlord

**Re-meshing is a ¥1,000 DIY job** with a roller tool; landlords expect tenants to do it, and home centres sell the kit next to the mesh.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Grey mesh is standard, so it is best

**Black mesh is markedly easier to see through**; grey reflects heat slightly better. Choose for the room's use.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Screens are only about mosquitoes

**They are what lets Japanese homes ventilate through the humid months without insects** — the precondition for the ventilation and mould habits described elsewhere.
