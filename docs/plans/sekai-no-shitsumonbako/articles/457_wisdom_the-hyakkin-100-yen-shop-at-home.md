# Q. What do people get wrong about using 100-yen shops for the home?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #457

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-hyakkin-100-yen-shop-at-home` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The 100-yen shop is where much of Japan's household ingenuity is sold — the drawer dividers, the hooks, the food-storage gadgets. Visitors buy souvenirs there; residents furnish a flat's storage. Knowing what is good value and what is not is a skill.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that everything in a 100-yen shop is equal. The organisation aisle — clear boxes in fixed modular sizes, dividers, hooks, tension rods, cable ties — is where the shops shine, because the products are designed as systems and the price allows buying many identical units. Kitchen consumables and stationery are reliable. Electronics, load-bearing hooks, and anything that must last are where residents buy elsewhere.

## What is worth it and what is not

| Category | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Storage boxes, drawer dividers, standing files** | **Excellent** — buy many identical units | Modular sizes fit the oshiire and standard drawers; the system works because you can buy ten |
| **Hooks, tension rods (light), cable ties, S-hooks** | **Good for light loads** | Check the printed load rating; ¥100 adhesive hooks hold 1–2 kg |
| **Kitchen consumables** (freezer bags, wrap, sponges, scrubbers, paper) | **Good** | Same factories as branded goods in many cases |
| **Cooking gadgets** (single-purpose tools, egg timers, silicone lids) | **Mixed but fun** | See the single-purpose tools article |
| **Cleaning** (melamine sponges, microfibre, citric acid, baking soda) | **Good** | — |
| **Stationery, batteries (alkaline)** | **Good** | — |
| **Electronics** (cables, chargers, earphones) | **Cables fine; chargers and anything with a battery — buy branded** | PSE-marked but quality varies; NITE has reported charger incidents |
| **Load-bearing anything** (shelf brackets, heavy hooks, strong tension poles) | **No** | Home centre; the failure mode is a falling shelf |
| **Knives, scissors** | **Scissors fine; knives poor** | A ¥1,000 knife from a home centre lasts years |
| **Cosmetics** | **Legal and safe (same regulation); quality varies** | See the beauty articles |

- The three big chains have **about 9,000 stores** in Japan and **over ¥1 trillion in combined sales**; **¥100 items are about 70% of the range**, with ¥200–500 tiers for larger goods.
- The **organisation system logic**: measure the space, buy the exact number of identical containers, label them. The shops sell **rulers and label makers** next to the boxes for this reason.
- **Renter-friendly**: adhesive hooks, tension rods, magnetic strips and door-hanging racks — the 100-yen shop is the rental flat's hardware store.

> **[Verify]** Store and sales figures with company reports; PSE and product incidents with NITE.

## Misunderstanding 1｜It is all junk

**The storage and kitchen aisles are the origin of half the "Japanese home ideas" online.** The junk is in electronics and anything structural.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Buy one to try

**The system works when you buy ten identical units** for one drawer or shelf. One box is clutter; a set is organisation.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any hook will hold a coat

**Read the load rating**; ¥100 hooks are for keys and towels. Heavy items need home-centre hardware.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The cheap charger is fine

**Chargers, power banks and anything with a battery are where to spend more.** Cables from the 100-yen shop are usually fine.

## Misunderstanding 5｜100 yen means 100 yen

**Prices tier to ¥300–500 for larger items**, and **tax adds 10%**: ¥110 at the till. Still cheap; just not literally 100.
