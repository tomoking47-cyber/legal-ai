# Q. What do people get wrong about the bathroom ventilation fan and bathroom drying?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #458

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/the-japanese-bathroom-fan-and-drying` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The switch by the bathroom door in a Japanese home often has three settings — ventilate, dry, heat — and the fan runs for hours a day. It is not extravagance: the bathroom is the wettest room in a humid country, and in many homes it doubles as the laundry drying room.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the bathroom fan is for smells and should be switched off to save electricity. In Japan it is the mould-prevention system: run during and 1–2 hours after bathing (or 24 hours at low power in newer homes), it keeps the wet room below the mould threshold. Many units also dry laundry — hang a load in the bathroom, close the door, run "dry" for 2–3 hours — which is how rainy-season laundry gets done in flats.

## The unit and how it is used

| Function | What it does | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Ventilate (換気)** | Exhausts humid air | **During bathing and 1–2 h after**, or **24 h on low** (mandated in homes built since 2003) |
| **Dry (乾燥)** | Warm air plus exhaust | **Laundry drying: 2–3 h for a load** hung on the bathroom rail; also to dry the room fast in the rainy season |
| **Heat (暖房)** | Warms the room before bathing | **Winter, 10–15 min before a bath** — the anti-heat-shock measure for older people (see the bath safety article) |
| **Cool (涼風)** | Fan only | Summer bathing |
| Cost | Ventilate: **about ¥1 per hour**; dry: **¥30–60 per load** (heater type) | Cheaper than a tumble dryer for a small load; slower |

- **Mould** grows above **70% humidity at 20–30°C**; a bathroom after a bath is at 90%+. **Squeegee the walls, run the fan, leave the door closed** (an open door pushes the moisture into the hallway).
- **Laundry in the bathroom** is standard in flats without a balcony or in the rainy season: the **pole across the bath** is a fitting, not an afterthought. Space the clothes, run "dry"; a fan on the floor speeds it.
- **Filter**: the fan's intake filter clogs with lint and dust — **clean monthly**; a clogged fan drops airflow by half and is a fire risk in heater units (NITE reports).
- **24-hour ventilation** in post-2003 homes is designed to run continuously; **do not switch it off** except to clean.

> **[Verify]** Running costs vary by unit; the 2003 ventilation requirement with MLIT; NITE fan incidents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Switch the fan off to save power

**Ventilation costs about ¥1 an hour and prevents the mould that costs a weekend to remove.** Leave it running after baths; leave 24-hour units on.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Open the bathroom door to let steam out

**That moves the moisture into the hallway and bedrooms** — the source of condensation elsewhere. **Door closed, fan on.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜The bathroom is a strange place to dry laundry

**It is a sealed, ventilated, heated room with a pole** — the best drying room in a Japanese flat, and the standard rainy-season method.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The heater setting is a luxury

**Warming the bathroom before a winter bath is the main preventive measure** against the blood-pressure shock behind bath deaths (see that article). Ten minutes before an older person bathes.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The fan needs no maintenance

**Monthly filter cleaning**; a clogged fan is quieter, weaker and, in heater units, a documented fire cause.
