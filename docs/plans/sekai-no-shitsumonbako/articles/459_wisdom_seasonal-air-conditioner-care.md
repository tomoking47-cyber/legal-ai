# Q. What do people get wrong about cleaning and maintaining the air conditioner?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #459

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/seasonal-air-conditioner-care` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Almost every Japanese room has its own wall-mounted air conditioner, used for cooling in summer and heating in winter. It is the most important appliance in the home for comfort and the one most often neglected — until the smell in June tells you what has been growing inside.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the filter is the whole maintenance. The filter (cleaned every two weeks in season) is the easy part; the mould that causes the June smell grows on the cooling fins and the fan inside, fed by condensation. The household routine: clean the filter, run "internal clean" or fan-only mode after cooling to dry the interior, open the front and wipe the louvers, and book a professional wash every few years. Never spray cleaner into the unit yourself.

## The routine

| Task | Frequency | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Filter** | **Every 2 weeks in season**, monthly otherwise | Lift the front panel, slide out, vacuum the outside, wash with water if greasy, dry fully before refitting |
| **Louvers and panel** | Monthly | Wipe with a damp cloth |
| **Dry the interior** | **After every cooling session** | Run **"internal clean" (内部クリーン)** or **fan-only mode for 1–2 h**; many units do this automatically — do not switch off at the wall mid-cycle |
| **Check the drain hose** | Start of season | The outdoor drain hose (ドレンホース) should drip when cooling; if blocked (insects, dirt), water leaks indoors. A **drain-hose cap** stops insects entering |
| **Outdoor unit** | Season start | Clear leaves and objects; keep 20 cm+ clearance; **do not cover** |
| **Professional cleaning** | **Every 2–4 years**, more with pets or smokers | **¥10,000–15,000** per unit; they dismantle and wash the fins and fan |
| **Test run** | **Before the first hot day** (May) | Run cooling for 30 min with windows open; check smell and drip |

- The **smell** is **mould on the heat exchanger and fan**, fed by condensation that stays wet after cooling stops. **Drying the interior after use** is the single most effective preventive measure; "internal clean" modes exist for this.
- **Spray-can "air-conditioner cleaners"** are widely sold but **manufacturers advise against them**: residue reaches electrical parts and the fan, and **fires have been reported** (NITE). Leave the inside to professionals.
- **Filter neglect** raises electricity use by **about 4% in cooling and 6% in heating** — clean it every two weeks (Ministry of the Environment / Agency for Natural Resources and Energy).
- **Life**: **about 10–15 years**; the **design standard use period** label is 10 years (see the warranty article).

> **[Verify]** Energy figures with the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy; spray-cleaner incidents with NITE and manufacturer advisories.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Clean the filter and you are done

**The filter stops dust; the mould grows behind it** on wet fins. Dry the interior after use and get it washed professionally every few years.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The smell is just "old machine"

**It is mould**, and it is blowing into the room. Run fan mode after cooling; book a cleaning if the smell persists.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Spray cleaner into the vents

**Manufacturers say do not**; residue causes faults and fires. The inside is a professional job.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Switch it off at the wall to save power

**Interrupting the automatic internal-clean cycle leaves the fins wet.** Let it finish (the fan runs on for an hour or two).

## Misunderstanding 5｜Water dripping outside is a fault

**The drain hose is meant to drip when cooling.** Water dripping **inside** means the hose is blocked — check the outdoor end.
