# Q. What do people get wrong about Japanese utility bills and saving on them?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #460

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/reading-a-japanese-utility-bill` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Electricity, gas and water bills in Japan arrive as slips or app notifications with a structure that new residents rarely decode — a base charge, tiered usage, a fuel adjustment that swings monthly, and a renewable levy. Understanding the tiers is where the savings are.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the bill is simply usage times a price. Japanese electricity is billed as a basic charge (by contracted amperage) plus three tiers of usage priced progressively, plus a fuel-cost adjustment that changes every month, plus a renewable-energy surcharge. Households save by staying in the lower tiers and choosing the right amperage; gas is billed similarly with tiers; water and sewer come every two months and are metered.

## The electricity bill (a typical regional-utility contract)

| Line | What it is | Typical figure (Tokyo area, 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic charge (基本料金)** | By **contracted amperage** (10–60 A); higher amperage lets you run more at once | **30 A ≈ ¥900/month; 40 A ≈ ¥1,200** |
| **Tier 1 (第1段階)** | First **120 kWh** | ≈ **¥30/kWh** |
| **Tier 2** | **121–300 kWh** | ≈ **¥36/kWh** |
| **Tier 3** | **Over 300 kWh** | ≈ **¥40/kWh** |
| **Fuel cost adjustment (燃料費調整額)** | ± per kWh, **changes monthly** with fuel import prices; can be negative | ± ¥0–5/kWh (was much higher in 2022–23) |
| **Renewable levy (再エネ発電促進賦課金)** | Per kWh, set annually | **¥4.18/kWh (FY2026)**, up from ¥3.98 in FY2025 |
| Total for a 2-person flat | **250–350 kWh/month** | **¥8,000–12,000**; more in Jan–Feb and Aug |

- **Gas**: **city gas (都市ガス)** is metered in m³ with tiers and a fuel adjustment; **propane (LP gas)** in rural areas and some flats is **2–3× the price** and set by the supplier — a factor when choosing a rental.
- **Water and sewer**: **billed together every 2 months**, metered, with a basic charge; a 2-person household pays **¥5,000–8,000 per two months** in Tokyo.
- **Breaker trips** (the whole flat goes dark) mean you exceeded the contracted amperage — **kettle + microwave + air conditioner + hair dryer** is the classic. Raising from 30 A to 40 A costs ~¥300/month more.
- **Liberalisation (2016)**: you can choose any electricity retailer; **new entrants offer flat rates or discounts**, but the tiers and levies above are the regional-utility default many households still use.
- **Payment**: bank transfer, credit card, or **at a convenience store with the barcode slip** (see the convenience store article). **Unpaid bills → disconnection after about a month's notice**.

> **[Verify]** Tier prices and levies with TEPCO (or your regional utility) and the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy; the FY2025 renewable levy with METI.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The bill is usage times a rate

**Three tiers, a base charge, a monthly fuel adjustment and a levy.** The unit price for your last kilowatt-hour can be a third higher than for your first.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Higher amperage is safer

**It costs more every month and lets you draw more** — useful for a family, wasted for one person. 30 A suits most single or two-person flats if appliances are not all run at once.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The bill went up because you used more

**Check the fuel adjustment line** — in 2022–23 it added thousands of yen for the same usage. It falls too.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Propane and city gas cost the same

**LP gas is typically 2–3 times city gas** and is priced by the supplier. Ask which a rental has before signing.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Saving means suffering

**Staying under 300 kWh** avoids tier 3: **air conditioner at 28°C with a fan, kotatsu or floor heating instead of heating the whole room, LED bulbs, and a filter-cleaned air conditioner** (see those articles) are where the reductions are, not in sitting in the dark.
