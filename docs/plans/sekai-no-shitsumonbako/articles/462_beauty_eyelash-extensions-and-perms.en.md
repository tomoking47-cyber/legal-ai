# Q. Are eyelash extensions and lash perms bad for my eyes?｜Japan Questions #462

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/eyelash-extensions-and-perms` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Eyelash extensions and lash perms (matsu-ege pāma) are popular in Japan for making the eyes look bigger. What many visitors do not know is that in Japan the person applying extensions must hold a cosmetologist licence, and consumer bodies receive a steady number of complaints about eye problems. Here are the points to check before you book.

## A. Check three things before you book. First, does the salon use licensed cosmetologists (biyōshi — by law, eyelash extensions count as cosmetology work)? Second, will they patch-test the glue (adhesive) or perm lotion? Third, if your eyes become red, itchy or swollen afterwards, go to an ophthalmology clinic first, not back to the salon. You should also know that no perm lotion has been approved by the Japanese government for use on eyelashes.

## Figures and rules worth knowing

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Qualification** | Eyelash extensions count as **"cosmetology" under Japan's Cosmetologists Act**, so **a cosmetologist licence is required** (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare notice, 2008) |
| **Injury complaints** | The National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan recorded **599 reports of harm from eyelash extensions in five years** (fiscal 2010–2014, published 2015). Mostly eye pain, redness, swelling and itching |
| **Adhesive** | Most are **cyanoacrylate-based**. Fumes released as the glue sets can sting the eyes, and there are reports of eyelid rashes (contact dermatitis) |
| **Lash perm lotion** | **No quasi-drug permanent-wave product has been approved for any use other than head hair** (the approval standard is limited to head hair — Notice Yakusei-hatsu 0628 No. 10, 28 June 2021), so the lash-curling lotions on sale circulate as "cosmetics". Products containing cysteamine hydrochloride have been handled differently since the end of March 2024. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare has warned against salons using products meant for head hair |
| **How long it lasts** | Extensions: **3–4 weeks**, falling out as your own lashes are replaced. Perm: **1–2 months** |
| **Your own lash cycle** | Lashes are replaced roughly every **3–5 months**; average length about **6–8 mm** |

- **Ask for a patch test**: at a new salon or with a new adhesive, one method is to apply a small amount to the inside of the arm and watch it for **48 hours**. It is wise to avoid a salon that refuses.
- **Keep your eyes closed throughout**: if you open them, the glue fumes reach the cornea and cause pain and redness.
- **Many products say to keep the lashes dry for 24 hours** after application. **Oil cleansers dissolve the adhesive**, so use oil-free products around the eyes.
- **Do not apply or remove extensions yourself.** Removal should be left to a technician using a remover.
- **If something goes wrong**: if redness, pain, discharge or eyelid swelling persists, go to an **ophthalmology clinic** (眼科, ganka — covered by Japanese health insurance). For a rash on the eyelid skin, go to a **dermatology clinic**. See a doctor before you go back to the salon.

> **[Verify]** The number of harm reports (599 in five years) and the figures for lash cycle and length vary with the edition of the source document. Check against the latest statistics before publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜I can get this done at a beauty salon (esthetic salon) or someone's home salon

**The person applying them must hold a cosmetologist licence.** When something goes wrong at an unlicensed provider, the response is often inadequate, and local authorities have issued warnings about unlicensed operators. Look for the salon's registration as a beauty establishment and the cosmetologist licence on display.

## Misunderstanding 2｜"Low-irritation glue" is safe

**"Low-irritation" (低刺激, tei-shigeki) is not a term with a legal definition.** Cyanoacrylate adhesives all give off fumes to some degree, and some people react to them. A patch test is the only way to check.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A lash perm is fine because the lotion does not go in the eye

**No quasi-drug perm lotion approved for eyelashes exists**, and the lash-curling lotions on sale are handled as "cosmetics". The government has warned about the practice of using head-hair products instead. The eyelid skin is thin, and complaints of eyelid dermatitis and conjunctivitis from lotion contact have been reported.

## Misunderstanding 4｜I can pull off extensions that are coming loose

**Your own lash comes out with it.** Repeated damage to the lash root can make the lashes that grow back thinner and shorter. Have them removed with a remover.

## Misunderstanding 5｜My eyes are red, but it will settle in a few days, so I will wait

**If there is pain, discharge or blurred vision, see an ophthalmologist the same day.** A scratched cornea can get worse if left. "Wait and see" is only for mild itching on its own.
