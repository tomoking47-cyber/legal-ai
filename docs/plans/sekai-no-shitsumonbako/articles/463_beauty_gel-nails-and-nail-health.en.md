# Q. Do gel nails weaken your natural nails if you keep having them done?｜Japan Questions #463

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/gel-nails-and-nail-health` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Gel nails (jeru neiru) are hugely popular in Japan: they last 3–4 weeks and the colour stays bright. But a steady number of people report "my nails got thinner" or "I got a rash". This article looks at how the nail is built and how to give your nails a rest if you want to keep having gel done.

## A. The gel itself does not dissolve the nail. The main causes of thin nails are over-filing at each re-application and forcing the gel off. Choose a salon that keeps sanding (filing the nail surface) to a minimum, and never peel the gel off yourself. Every 3–4 months, give the nails a break of 2–4 weeks. If water gets under a gap and the nail turns green, stop and see a dermatologist. If you develop an allergy to the gel ingredients (acrylates), it is hard to go back to gel later, so do not brush off itching or swelling.

## Basic figures for nails and gel

| Item | Guide |
|---|---|
| **How fast fingernails grow** | **About 0.1 mm a day, about 3 mm a month.** Full replacement from root to tip takes **about 6 months** |
| **Thickness of a healthy nail** | **About 0.3–0.5 mm.** If over-filing makes it thinner, it bends and can hurt |
| **Interval between re-applications** | **3–4 weeks.** Left longer, water gets in through the gap at the base |
| **How to remove** | Soak in acetone (the ingredient in nail-polish remover) for **10–15 minutes**, then lift off gently. Forcing it off peels away layers of the nail plate (the hard surface of the nail) |
| **Lamp** | UV or LED lamp. Curing time is **30–60 seconds** depending on the product. Some people protect the back of the hand with sunscreen or gloves |
| **Rest period** | After 3–4 months of gel, a break of **2–4 weeks** is a realistic approach |

- **Green nails**: when a gap opens between gel and nail, water and *Pseudomonas* bacteria get in and the nail turns green. The National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan has received complaints about this. **If you see discolouration, remove the gel and go to a dermatology clinic** (皮膚科, hifuka — covered by Japanese health insurance). Never paint over it.
- **Acrylate allergy**: contact dermatitis from the main gel ingredients (such as HEMA) can appear not only on the fingertips but also on the **eyelids and neck**. Once sensitised, some people also react to dental materials and medical tape; the Japanese Dermatological Association has warned about this.
- **Doing gel at home**: if uncured gel is left on the skin when you switch the lamp on, the risk of sensitisation rises. The golden rule is to **wipe off any gel that spills onto the skin before curing**.
- **Signs of over-filing**: the nail turns white and bends, hurts when pressed, or the tip looks thin and see-through. If any of these apply, take a break.

> **[Verify]** The figures for nail growth rate, thickness and curing time are general guides. Check the year of publication and the number of cases in the National Consumer Affairs Center's gel-nail harm information and reflect them in the article.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Gel dissolves the nail and makes it thin

**The main causes of thinning are over-filing and forced removal.** Gel itself does not dissolve the nail. It helps to choose a salon that uses a "non-sanding" base gel or a technician who files as little as possible.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Once it starts lifting, I can pick it off with my nail

**The surface of the nail plate comes off with it.** Repeated, this visibly thins the nail. Remove with acetone, or at the salon.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Nails breathe, so they die if you never let them rest

**Nails do not breathe** (they are fed by the blood supply at the nail root). The point of a rest is to give the filed part time to grow out and to give you a chance to notice any problem with the skin or nail.

## Misunderstanding 4｜I reacted to one brand, so a different brand will be fine

**Acrylate ingredients are common to most gels**, so once an allergy has appeared, people often react to other makers' products too. Have a dermatologist identify the ingredient responsible first.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If it turns green I can hide it with another coat

**That seals in the bacteria and makes it worse.** For green nails, the first step is to take the gel off and keep the nail dry. Then see a dermatologist for medical care.
