# Q. How far can I rely on acne cosmetics and over-the-counter acne products in Japan?｜Japan Questions #464

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/acne-care-and-otc-limits` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Acne is a common skin problem: more than 90% of Japanese people are generally described as experiencing it at some point. In Japan, cosmetics, quasi-drugs, over-the-counter (OTC) medicines and the dermatology clinic each play a different role. If you mix them up, you can easily end up feeling that "nothing I use works".

## A. Cosmetics and quasi-drugs (a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines) can only go as far as "helping to prevent acne"; they cannot clear up acne that has already formed. OTC medicines are for short-term care of mild cases. If you have 10 or more red spots, marks are starting to remain, or it has lasted more than 3 months — any one of these — go to a dermatology clinic. The Japanese Dermatological Association's guidelines describe prescription topical medicines (adapalene, benzoyl peroxide and others) as the central options, and shop-bought products cannot replace them.

## The roles of the four options

| Category | What it can do (legal position) | Suited to |
|---|---|---|
| **Cosmetics** | Cleanse the skin and moisturise it | Prevention and daily care |
| **Quasi-drugs ("medicated", 薬用 yakuyō)** | Claims are limited to "helps prevent acne" and "helps prevent rough skin" (in line with the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's list of 56 permitted claims) | Prevention; daily care for people who get repeated spots |
| **OTC medicines** | Topical products containing ibuprofen piconol, sulphur, resorcinol and similar. Short-term care for mild spots | A few mild red spots |
| **Dermatology clinic** | Prescriptions such as adapalene, benzoyl peroxide and topical antibiotics. Highly recommended options in the guidelines | Many red spots, marks remaining, lasting more than 3 months |

- **The Japanese Dermatological Association's "Guidelines for the Management of Acne Vulgaris 2017"** [尋常性ざ瘡治療ガイドライン2017] recommend adapalene or benzoyl peroxide for comedones (whiteheads and blackheads), and combination with topical antibiotics for inflamed red spots. These ingredients **require a dermatologist's prescription** in Japan.
- **Wash your face twice a day** as a guide. More than that strips too much sebum and can make things worse.
- **Do not touch or squeeze.** Squeezing spreads inflammation deeper and raises the risk of permanent marks (scars).
- **Acne marks (redness, dark pigmentation, pitting)**: pitted scars do not recover with cosmetics; they are the domain of dermatology and cosmetic dermatology clinics.
- **The quasi-drug claim "helps prevent acne"** refers to prevention, not to any effect on spots that already exist. A cosmetics advert that claims to make acne go away breaks the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law).

> **[Verify]** The guideline recommendations are based on the 2017 edition. Check before publication whether a revised edition exists and the current approval status of the OTC active ingredients.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If I use acne cosmetics my acne will disappear

**Cosmetics and quasi-drugs can only "help prevent".** The law does not allow them to claim any effect on acne that has already formed. Some people do see improvement, but that comes from prevention and keeping the skin clean.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It is not serious enough for a doctor

**Acne is covered by Japanese national health insurance at dermatology clinics.** The topical medicines the guidelines recommend are not sold over the counter, and seeing a doctor early is the shortest route to avoiding marks.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The more thoroughly I wash, the better

**Twice a day is the guide; more than that tends to backfire.** Scrubbing also makes inflammation worse.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Squeezing out the core makes it settle faster

**This is the classic way to spread inflammation deeper and create a scar.** If a spot really bothers you, a dermatologist can perform comedo extraction (面皰圧出, menpō asshutsu) as a clinical procedure.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Chocolate and crisps give you acne

**No direct causal link with any specific food has been clearly shown.** There are, however, studies linking high-sugar diets with worsening. Sleep, washing and keeping your hands off your face bring more noticeable results than extreme dieting.
