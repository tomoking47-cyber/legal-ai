# Q. What is wrong with going to bed with wet hair?｜Japan Questions #466

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/sleeping-with-wet-hair` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

You get home late, take a bath, and drying your hair feels like too much effort — "it will dry on its own". In Japanese homes, "never go to bed with wet hair" has been repeated for generations, and the reasons concern both the hair and the scalp. Here is what happens, and a drying routine that takes 5–10 minutes.

## A. When hair is wet, the cuticle (the scale-like outer layer) opens and the hair is weak against friction. Rubbing against the pillow causes breakage, split ends and bed hair. A scalp left damp for hours grows more microbes, leading to odour and itching. Use the dryer from the roots, held about 20 cm away, and finish with cool air once the hair is 80% dry. Five to ten minutes of effort makes a visible difference to your hair the next morning.

## Wet hair versus dry hair

| Item | Wet hair | Dry hair |
|---|---|---|
| **Cuticle** | Swells and opens with water; easily lifted off | Closed, protecting the inside of the hair |
| **Strength** | Tensile strength drops when wet (generally described as **about 70–80%** of dry strength) | — |
| **Friction** | Rubbing against pillow and bedding lifts the cuticle, causing breakage and split ends | Withstands it comparatively well |
| **Scalp** | Left damp, resident microbes (such as *Malassezia*) multiply, causing odour, dandruff and itching | Microbes multiply less on a dry scalp |
| **Bed hair** | The hydrogen bonds in wet hair are broken, so it sets in whatever shape it dries in | The set shape holds |

- **Drying order**: **press** the water out with a towel (do not rub) → dry **the roots and scalp** first → ends last; **once 80% dry, switch to cool air** to settle the cuticle.
- **Dryer distance and heat**: hold the nozzle **about 20 cm** away and **never aim at one spot for more than 3 seconds**. Many household dryers blow air at **around 100°C**, and holding them too close damages the hair's protein.
- **Leave-in conditioners** (hair oil or hair milk, sold as 洗い流さないトリートメント) go on after towel-drying, **mainly on the ends**. Keep them off the scalp.
- **Tips to save time**: use two towels; wrap the hair in a highly absorbent towel turban for 10 minutes; choose a dryer with strong airflow (**1.5 m³/min or more** as a guide).
- **Pillowcases** pick up sebum and styling products; change them **once a week** as a guide.

> **[Verify]** The percentage drop in wet-hair strength, the dryer outlet temperature and the airflow guide are commonly quoted figures. Check them against the source documents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Air-drying is gentler because there is no heat

**The longer the hair stays wet, the longer the cuticle stays open and the more friction damages it.** Used correctly, a dryer puts less strain on the hair.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Rubbing hard with a towel dries it faster

**The cuticle lifts off most easily when the hair is wet and rubbed.** Pressing the water out is the basic method.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Start drying from the ends

**The roots and scalp are the slowest to dry.** If you start at the ends, they are over-dried before the roots are dry. Go roots → mid-lengths → ends.

## Misunderstanding 4｜It has to be bone-dry

**Switching to cool air at 80% dry** prevents the frizz that comes from over-drying. Hair that no longer feels damp to the touch is dry enough.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A smelly scalp means I am not shampooing enough

**Leftover shampoo** and **not drying properly** are more often the cause. Before using more shampoo, look at how you dry your hair.
