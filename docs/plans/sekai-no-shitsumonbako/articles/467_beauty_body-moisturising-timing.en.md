# Q. How soon after a bath should I put on body moisturiser?｜Japan Questions #467

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/body-moisturising-timing` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Come winter, shins and elbows turn flaky and itchy. Dry skin (kansō-hada) and xerosis (kanpishō) are very common complaints in Japan's dry winter air, and moisturising advice is the most basic part of a dermatologist's guidance here. With the same moisturiser, results change depending on when, where and how much you apply.

## A. Apply within 5–10 minutes of getting out of the bath. The skin holds the most water straight after bathing, and it evaporates quickly from there. For quantity, the Japanese Dermatological Association's guide is "1 FTU (from the tip of the index finger to the first joint, about 0.5 g) covers an area the size of two palms" — two to three times what most people think. On shins and arms, the right amount is when a tissue sticks to the skin afterwards. Bath water at 38–40°C (100–104°F) for about 10 minutes, and no scrubbing with a nylon towel, matter as much as the moisturiser itself.

## Basic figures for moisturising

| Item | Guide | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **When to apply** | **Within 5–10 minutes** after bathing | Lock in the water the stratum corneum (outer skin layer) took in during the bath before it evaporates |
| **Amount (FTU)** | **1 FTU (about 0.5 g) covers two palms' worth of skin.** About 1 FTU per shin; **about 6–8 FTU** for both arms and both legs | Guide given in the Japanese Dermatological Association and Japanese Society of Allergology "Clinical Practice Guidelines for Atopic Dermatitis 2024" |
| **How often** | **Once or twice a day.** Mornings too in very dry spells | Reapplying keeps water in the stratum corneum |
| **Bath temperature** | **38–40°C**, **around 10 minutes** | Above 42°C (108°F) or long soaks wash out sebum and moisturising components |
| **How to wash** | Gently, with hands or a cotton towel. **No scrubbing with a nylon towel** | Do not scrape the stratum corneum |
| **Indoor humidity** | **40–60%** (the Building Sanitation Management Standard gives 40–70%) | Below 30%, dryness and itching increase |

- **How to apply**: spread it on your palms and smooth it on gently **along the direction of the skin's lines**. Do not rub it in. If **a tissue sticks lightly** afterwards, the amount is right; if the skin shines and feels sticky, it is on the generous side.
- **Choosing the texture**: lotion (light, good for large areas) < cream < ointment (most protective; for elbows, heels and severe dryness). In winter, cream or heavier suits many people.
- **Where skin dries most**: **shins, outer thighs, lower back, elbows, heels** — areas with few sebaceous glands.
- **If itching is severe, the skin turns red, or it cracks and hurts**, this is beyond "dry skin". Go to a **dermatology clinic** (皮膚科, hifuka — covered by Japanese health insurance) for an examination and a prescription moisturiser (such as heparinoid).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Wait until the body is completely dry before applying

**The few minutes after bathing are when the skin holds the most water.** Ideally, towel off and apply straight away in the changing area. The longer you wait, the drier the stratum corneum becomes.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A thin layer is enough

**Most people are generally described as applying less than half the recommended amount.** Measured in FTU, the right amount feels like "this much?".

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is unhygienic not to scrub with a nylon towel

**Most dirt on the body comes off with hot water alone.** Scrubbing with a nylon towel scrapes the stratum corneum and is a direct cause of dryness and itching. Hands or a cotton towel are enough.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The hotter and longer the bath, the more moisturised the skin

**Bathing at 42°C or above for 15 minutes or more washes out sebum and the moisturising components of the stratum corneum.** The skin may feel plump straight after, but the dryness afterwards is worse.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The itching is just dryness, so moisturiser is all I need

**Redness, eczema or cracking may mean dermatitis.** If there is no improvement after about two weeks of moisturising, go to a dermatology clinic.
