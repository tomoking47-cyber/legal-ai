# Q. How often should I wash or replace my foundation puff or sponge?｜Japan Questions #468

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/makeup-sponges-and-puffs` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Many people wash their brushes but only deal with puffs and sponges "when they look dirty". In fact, a puff coated in layers of sebum and foundation is one of the tools most likely to cause skin problems. This article sets out how often to wash and replace them, and how to wash them with what you can find in any Japanese drugstore or 100-yen shop.

## A. For puffs and sponges that touch the skin directly, a realistic line is to wash them about once a week and replace them every 1–3 months. Sponges used damp grow microbes especially fast, so wash them after every use and dry them completely. To wash: work up a lather of neutral detergent or a dedicated cleaner in lukewarm water, press-wash, rinse, press out the water with a towel, and dry out of direct sun in a well-ventilated place. Do not dry a puff inside the foundation case.

## Guide for each tool

| Tool | How often to wash | When to replace | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Powder puff** | **Once a week** | **1–3 months**, or when the pile goes flat | Using both sides in turn makes it last longer |
| **Liquid foundation sponge (used dry)** | **Once a week**, or whenever it looks dirty | **1–2 months** | Replace if it shrinks or tears after washing |
| **Sponge used damp** | **After every use** | **1–3 months** | Never leave it damp. Complete drying is essential |
| **Cushion foundation puff** | **Once a week** | **About 1 month** (many makers recommend this) | Sealed in the case with liquid soaked in, so it soils quickly |
| **Brushes** | Once every 2–4 weeks | When bristles shed or splay | See separate article |

- **Why they get dirty**: **sebum, the oils in foundation and dead skin cells** build up in layers on a puff. A British university study (2019) reported that **bacteria were found on about 80–90% of the make-up tools examined**, and that **more than 90% of sponges used damp** were contaminated.
- **How to wash**: (1) wet lightly in lukewarm water; (2) apply neutral detergent (中性洗剤, chūsei senzai — washing-up liquid is fine) or a dedicated cleaner and **press-wash** (no rubbing or kneading); (3) **rinse** thoroughly so no detergent remains; (4) **press** out the water with a towel; (5) dry completely **out of direct sun** (half a day to a day).
- **Where to dry**: not left in a damp bathroom but somewhere with good airflow. Put it back in the case **only when completely dry**.
- **If you are prone to skin problems** (acne, sensitive skin), options include **disposable puffs**, or keeping several and rotating them.

> **[Verify]** The British study is understood to be from Aston University (Bashir & Lambert, 2019, Journal of Applied Microbiology). Check the figures and year of publication against the original paper.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If it does not look dirty, it is fine

**Sebum and microbes are invisible.** A sponge that touches the skin and gets damp becomes unhygienic well before it looks it. Managing by frequency is the reliable way.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Washing wears puffs out, so it is better not to

**An unwashed puff is harder on the skin.** Press-washed and dried out of the sun, a puff lasts several months. Replace it once it wears out.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Wiping with alcohol is as good as washing

**Alcohol does not lift the layer of oils well**, and it can damage the material. Washing with neutral detergent is the basic method.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A hair dryer speeds up drying

**Heat warps and degrades the sponge.** It takes longer, but dry it in the shade.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cushion foundation is hygienic because the case is sealed

**Because it is sealed, it never dries, and microbes multiply easily.** Replace the puff about once a month as a guide; keeping spare puffs on hand is convenient.
