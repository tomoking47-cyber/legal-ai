# Q. Does "white teeth" toothpaste actually make my teeth whiter?｜Japan Questions #469

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/whitening-toothpaste-and-oral-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Toothpastes promising whiter teeth (sold as ホワイトニング, howaitoningu, or 美白, bihaku) stand out on the shelves of Japanese drugstores (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.). But a toothpaste can only shift "surface staining". Changing the colour of the tooth itself is dental work, and the two need to be kept apart — especially if you are used to stronger products sold over the counter in your home country.

## A. What toothpaste can do is lift surface stains from coffee, tea and tobacco and return teeth to their original colour. Bleaching that makes the tooth itself lighter (hydrogen peroxide and similar) is a procedure done at dental clinics, and these ingredients may not be put in toothpaste sold in Japanese shops. Brushing hard with a highly abrasive product damages the tooth surface (enamel) and the gums. If what you want is "whiter than my natural colour", the shortest route is to ask a dentist.

## Ways of making teeth whiter, by category

| Method | Legal position | What it can do | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Toothpaste (cosmetic or quasi-drug — a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines)** | "Makes teeth white" is a permitted claim **only in the sense of removing surface stains** | Lifts stains and **returns teeth to their original colour** | Overuse of abrasives damages enamel and gums |
| **Professional cleaning at a dentist (PMTC)** | Dental care | Removal of tartar and staining | Health-insurance coverage depends on conditions |
| **Dental bleaching** | Dental care (hydrogen peroxide, carbamide peroxide) | **Breaks down pigment inside the tooth, making it whiter than its natural colour** | Not covered by insurance (private fee). Can cause tooth sensitivity |
| **Overseas bleaching products sold over the counter** | May contain ingredients that in Japan **may only be used or sold by dentists** | — | Consultations reported about gum inflammation from personally imported products |

- **Fluoride concentration**: toothpaste sold in Japan may contain up to **1,500 ppm** (the ceiling was raised in 2017). For preventing tooth decay, the 2023 recommendation from four academic societies including the Japanese Society for Oral Health is **1,000 ppm in a rice-grain to pea-sized amount for children under 6, and 1,500 ppm in a 1.5–2 cm strip for age 6 and over**.
- **How to brush**: **twice a day for 2 minutes**; **put the bristle tips at the line between tooth and gum and move in small strokes**. Pressure of **150–200 g** (the bristles should not splay) is the guide. Rinsing once with a small amount of water leaves fluoride in the mouth.
- **Preventing stains**: **rinse with water** after coffee, tea or red wine; cut down on tobacco. **Interdental brush or floss once a day.**
- **"Howaitoningu" on toothpaste has no legal definition.** Within the cosmetics category, the only permitted claim is "makes teeth white" in the sense of removing stains. Adverts saying "bleaches teeth" or "whiter than your natural colour" are a problem under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law).

> **[Verify]** Check the fluoride recommendations (joint statement of four societies, 2023) and the brushing-pressure figure against the source documents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Keep using this toothpaste and I will have teeth like a celebrity

**Toothpaste can only bring teeth back to their original colour.** Going whiter than that is the domain of dental bleaching.

## Misunderstanding 2｜More abrasive means whiter

**Brushing hard with a highly abrasive product wears away enamel, so the yellowish dentine underneath shows through and teeth can look more yellow.** The gums recede too.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Rinse thoroughly several times after brushing

**To keep fluoride on the teeth, one rinse with a small amount of water is recommended.** Rinsing repeatedly washes the fluoride away.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Buying a strong overseas bleaching kit online is cheaper

**Some products contain hydrogen peroxide at concentrations that only dentists may handle in Japan**, and consumer bodies have received complaints of gum inflammation and tooth sensitivity. In Japan, have it done at a dental clinic.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Less fluoride is safer for children

**The four societies' 2023 statement recommends 1,000 ppm in a small amount even for children under 6.** Keeping to the amount (rice grain to pea size) matters more.
