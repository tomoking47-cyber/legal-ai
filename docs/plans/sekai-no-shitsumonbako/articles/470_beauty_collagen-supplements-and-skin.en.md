# Q. Is it true that drinking collagen is good for your skin?｜Japan Questions #470

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/collagen-supplements-and-skin` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Collagen drinks, powders and jellies are everyday "beauty foods" in Japan — you can buy them at any convenience store (konbini). Some people say "it is digested, so there is no point"; others say "my skin got firmer". This article looks at which is right, from the side of both Japan's food-labelling rules and the research.

## A. Collagen taken by mouth is broken down into amino acids and small peptides, so "the collagen you drink does not become your skin" as such. However, some peptides do enter the bloodstream, and there are clinical trials and meta-analyses reporting improvement in measures of skin hydration and elasticity, so it cannot be dismissed as "pointless" either. In Japan, some products are registered as Foods with Function Claims with wording such as "helps maintain skin elasticity", and that is the ceiling of what the current system allows. Claims like "spots disappear" or "rejuvenates" are not permitted for foods.

## The rules and the research today

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Legal position** | Collagen is a **food**. Drug-like wording — that it makes a condition go away or "improves" it — is not allowed |
| **Foods with Function Claims (機能性表示食品)** | Some products are **notified to the Consumer Affairs Agency**, on the maker's own responsibility, with claims such as "helps maintain skin elasticity" or "helps keep skin hydrated". **The government has not reviewed or approved their effects** |
| **State of the research** | Meta-analyses pooling clinical trials of oral collagen peptides (for example a 2023 analysis of 26 trials and 1,721 participants, with newer reports in 2025) report **improvement in skin hydration and elasticity**. Limitations noted: small trials, and many funded by manufacturers |
| **Amounts commonly used in studies** | **2.5–10 g a day** for **8–12 weeks** |
| **Nutritional value as protein** | Collagen lacks one of the essential amino acids (tryptophan), so it is **an incomplete protein source**. It does not replace protein from ordinary meals (recommended intake for women: 50 g a day) |
| **Safety** | Generally regarded as safe, but **allergy to fish- or pork-derived products** and, rarely, stomach upset have been reported |

- **The National Institute of Health and Nutrition's "Health Food" safety and efficacy database** lists, side by side, an entry stating that "sufficient reliable data on efficacy in humans could not be found" for oral collagen, and reports of individual trials. The position of the public body is that **it is not yet possible to say for certain whether it "works" or "does not work"**.
- **How to tell products apart**: if the package says 機能性表示食品 (Foods with Function Claims) and shows a notification number (届出番号), you can look up the claim and its supporting papers in the Consumer Affairs Agency's database. A product without a function claim may not make any claim about skin.
- **Cost**: one collagen drink at **¥150–300** a day comes to **¥4,500–9,000 a month**. Spending the same budget on sun protection, moisturising and sleep rests on firmer evidence for the skin.
- **From food**: chicken wings and cartilage, fish skin and gelatine are rich in collagen. **Vitamin C** is a nutrient needed for collagen synthesis, and the evidence for that is clear.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The collagen I drink becomes collagen in my skin

**Digestion breaks it down into amino acids and peptides.** What the research suggests is "the possibility that some peptides act on skin cells", not that it "becomes skin" as such.

## Misunderstanding 2｜So there is no point drinking it

**Clinical trials and meta-analyses reporting improvement in skin hydration and elasticity measures do exist.** The quality of the research has limits, but there is also no basis for dismissing it as "pointless".

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is a Food with Function Claims, so the government has recognised the effect

**Foods with Function Claims are a notification system under the maker's own responsibility**, not a system in which the government reviews and approves effects. (Government review applies to "Foods for Specified Health Uses", FOSHU or tokuho.)

## Misunderstanding 4｜The more I drink, the more it works

**The amounts used in studies were about 2.5–10 g a day**, and there is no evidence that more than that increases the effect. Some products also mean too many calories and too much sugar.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Drinking collagen makes spots and wrinkles disappear

**Such claims are not permitted for foods, and there is no evidence for them.** Spots and wrinkles are the domain of sun protection and the dermatology clinic.
