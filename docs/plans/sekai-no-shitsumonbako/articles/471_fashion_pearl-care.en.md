# Q. How should I care for and store a pearl necklace?｜Japan Questions #471

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/pearl-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan a pearl necklace is the standard piece for weddings, funerals and formal ceremonies (冠婚葬祭, kankonsōsai), and it is often passed from mother to daughter. Yet pearls are among the most delicate of all gems. Store them the wrong way and the lustre fades within a few years.

## A. On the day you wear them, wipe off sweat and cosmetics with a soft, dry cloth, then put them away separately so they touch no other jewellery. That alone makes a big difference to how long the lustre lasts. Pearls are mainly calcium carbonate and are soft (Mohs hardness 2.5–4.5); they are damaged by acid, sweat, perfume and hairspray. Never use an ultrasonic cleaner. Have the string replaced every 1–2 years if you wear them often, or once every few years if only occasionally. Avoid places that are too dry, such as next to a desiccant inside a safe.

## Where pearls are weak, and how to care for them

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Composition and hardness** | Layers of calcium carbonate (aragonite) and protein (conchiolin). **Mohs hardness 2.5–4.5** (diamond 10, quartz 7, a fingernail about 2.5) |
| **Weak against** | **Acid (sweat, fruit juice, vinegar), perfume, hairspray, cosmetics, chlorine (pools, bleach), high heat, extreme dryness** |
| **After wearing** | **Wipe with a soft, dry cloth** (a glasses cloth or a jewellery cloth). For heavier soiling, wipe with a **cloth lightly dampened in lukewarm water**, then dry immediately with a dry cloth |
| **Never** | **Ultrasonic cleaners, detergent, alcohol, acidic cleaning fluids, steam** |
| **Restringing** | Worn daily to weekly: **every 1–2 years**. Ceremonies only: **every 3–5 years**. Time to restring when you see **gaps between the pearls**, the string has stretched and sags, or it has discoloured |
| **Storage** | **Apart from other jewellery**, in a soft cloth or a dedicated case. Avoid **long periods next to a desiccant or in a sealed container** (a cause of cracking). Avoid **direct sunlight and heat** |

- **Order of dressing**: **put pearls on last, and take them off first when you get home.** Putting them on after perfume, hairspray and make-up reduces what settles on the pearls.
- **On a day you have sweated**, do not only dry-wipe: **lift the sweat with a damp cloth, then wipe dry.** The salt and acid in sweat dull the lustre.
- **Type of string**: silk thread stretches, and if it snaps the pearls scatter. A necklace strung "all-knotted" (a knot between every pearl) is much less likely to scatter. Restringing is generally described as costing around **¥3,000–8,000** (varies with length and knotting).
- **Discolouration and lost lustre are hard to reverse**, so **prevention is almost everything.**
- **In long-term storage**, taking the pearls out now and then (once every few months) to **let them breathe and wipe them with a cloth** is said by the trade to help prevent cracking from dryness.

> **[Verify]** The Mohs hardness, the going rate for restringing and the explanation of cracking caused by dryness follow industry-body explanations. Check them against the sources.

## Misunderstanding 1｜They are gems, so they are tough and can be stored with other accessories

**Pearls are soft enough to be scratched by a fingernail.** Contact with diamonds or metal leaves scratches and dulls the lustre. Store them on their own.

## Misunderstanding 2｜I rarely wear them, so the string does not need replacing

**String deteriorates with age even when unworn.** Check every few years for gaps, sagging and discolouration, and have it restrung. When a string breaks, pearls are the piece that suffers most.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sweat can simply be rinsed off with water

**Soaking for long in water damages the string.** Wipe with a damp cloth, then dry immediately. Never leave them wet.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A safe or sealed case with a desiccant is the safest place

**Extreme dryness is described as causing the pearl layers to crack**, and jewellers commonly advise against keeping pearls next to desiccants.

## Misunderstanding 5｜An ultrasonic cleaner will make them look new

**This is a classic way to damage the pearls, the string and the glued parts.** Never use one on pearls. If the dirt will not come off, take them to the shop you bought them from or to a jeweller.
