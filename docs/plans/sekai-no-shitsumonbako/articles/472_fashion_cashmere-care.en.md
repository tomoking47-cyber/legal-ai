# Q. Can I wash a cashmere sweater or scarf at home? How do I make it last?｜Japan Questions #472

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/cashmere-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Cashmere is finer, lighter and warmer than ordinary wool. Because it is expensive, it also raises the most questions: "Should I send it to the cleaner?", "It has pilled", "It went fuzzy after one season". Here is a guide to care and storage, based on how the fibre behaves, with the Japanese care label and shop terms you will need.

## A. If the care label (Japan uses the international JIS L0001 symbols) shows the hand-wash symbol, you can wash it at home. Dissolve a neutral "delicates" detergent (おしゃれ着用洗剤, oshareki-yō senzai) in lukewarm water of 30°C (86°F) or below, press-wash gently for 1–2 minutes, rinse twice, spin in the machine for about 30 seconds and dry flat. Cashmere fibres are very fine (14–16 μm) and are damaged by friction and heat, so no rubbing, no hot water, no hairdryer. Three habits make it last: do not wear it on consecutive days (rest it for a day), brush it after wearing, and wash it before storing it off-season with a moth repellent.

## Cashmere basics and care guide

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Fibre fineness** | **14–16 μm** (wool is generally 20 μm or more). Finer means softer, but the fibres break under friction and become pills and fuzz |
| **Yield** | One cashmere goat gives **150–200 g of down a year**. One sweater is described as needing **3–4 goats' worth**, which is why it costs so much |
| **Washing (if the label allows hand-washing)** | Lukewarm water at **30°C or below**, a **neutral delicates detergent**, **press-wash for 1–2 minutes**. Do not knead or rub |
| **Rinsing and spinning** | Rinse **twice**; machine spin for **about 30 seconds**, or press out water in a towel |
| **Drying** | **Dry flat** (a hanger stretches it). In the shade, somewhere airy. **No hairdryer, no tumble dryer** |
| **Label says "do not wash at home"** | Take it to a cleaner. Check the label for whether **dry cleaning** or **wet cleaning** is permitted |
| **Resting** | **Do not wear on consecutive days.** After a day's wear, rest it **1–2 days** so the fibres recover |
| **Pilling** | **Cut pills off at the base with scissors**, or use a fabric shaver lightly. Do not pull |

- **Brushing after wearing**: use a **clothes brush** (洋服ブラシ) with boar or horse bristles, following the nap from top to bottom. It lifts dust, untangles knotted fibres and helps prevent pilling.
- **Storage**: off-season, **always wash first** (skin oils and food traces attract insects). Store **folded**, with a **moth repellent** (防虫剤, bōchūzai, sold at drugstores and 100-yen shops). Hanging stretches the shoulders. **Cashmere is one of the materials most often attacked by clothes moths and carpet beetles.**
- **Fibre-content labels**: under Japan's Household Goods Quality Labelling Act, cashmere is shown as "毛（カシミヤ）" (wool (cashmere)). **Where the content is under 5%, use of the word "cashmere" on the label is restricted.** The National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan has in the past published cases where products labelled "Cashmere 100" (all-cashmere) were found to fall short of that content.
- **Yellowing of white cashmere** is oxidised skin oil and sweat. **Wash soon after wearing** to prevent it.

> **[Verify]** Check the fibre diameter, the yield per goat and the fibre-content labelling rules against the latest industry documents and legislation.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cashmere cannot be washed at home

**If the label shows the hand-wash symbol, it can.** Storing it unwashed for months is in fact a bigger cause of insect damage and yellowing. If the label forbids washing, use a cleaner.

## Misunderstanding 2｜With a delicates detergent I can use the normal machine cycle

**Cashmere is weak against friction; a normal cycle causes pilling, shrinkage and felting.** Hand-wash, or use the machine's "hand-wash" or "dry" cycle in a laundry net with a short spin.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Pull the pills off

**That pulls out the surrounding fibres too and thins the fabric.** Snip them at the base with small scissors, or use a fabric shaver lightly.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Drying on a hanger keeps the shape

**A wet knit stretches at the shoulders and hem under its own weight on a hanger.** Use a flat drying net, or shape it on a bath towel.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Expensive cashmere is tough, so I can wear it all season long

**The finer the fibre, the weaker it is against friction, and daily wear gives it no time to recover.** Resting it and brushing it changes how good it looks for years.
