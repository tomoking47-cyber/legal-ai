# Q. How do I wash and dry linen clothes without shrinkage and creases?｜Japan Questions #473

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/linen-clothing-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Linen shirts and dresses have become a summer staple in Japan's humid heat. They are cool and quick-drying, but people often complain: "It shrank in the wash", "The creases will not come out", "The colour faded in the sun". Once you know how flax fibre behaves, you make peace with the creases.

## A. Linen gets softer the more you wash it, so washing itself is no problem. Three habits limit shrinkage and creasing: use a laundry net on a gentle cycle, spin for no more than 1 minute, and while the garment is still wet, pat the creases out by hand, shape it and hang it to dry. Shrinkage of around 2–5% in the first wash is normal, so allow for it when buying. Ironing works best while the fabric is damp. Dark colours fade in sunlight, so dry them in the shade.

## How linen behaves and how to wash it

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **The fibre** | From the flax plant. **Absorbs and releases moisture fast**, and gets stronger when wet. **Creases easily**, but softens with every wash |
| **"麻 (asa)" on the label** | Under Japan's Household Goods Quality Labelling Act, only **flax (linen) and ramie** may be labelled 麻. Jute and hemp are labelled as "unlisted fibres" (指定外繊維) |
| **Shrinkage** | **Around 2–5% in the first wash** is normal. Hot water, a hard spin and a tumble dryer increase it |
| **Water temperature** | **30–40°C (86–104°F) or below** (follow the label). Ordinary laundry detergent is fine; for dark colours a neutral detergent limits colour loss |
| **Cycle** | **In a laundry net on a gentle cycle (hand-wash or delicates)**. Spin **30 seconds to 1 minute** |
| **Drying** | Take it out as soon as the spin ends. **While still wet, pat the creases out sharply with your hands**, pull the seams straight, and **hang on a thick hanger**. Dark colours **in the shade** |
| **Ironing** | **High heat (linen setting, 180–210°C), with the fabric dampened** or misted with a spray bottle. Creases are hard to remove when dry |
| **Tumble dryer** | Causes shrinkage and creasing. **Safest not to use it** |

- **Wearing the creases as part of the look** is how linen is meant to be enjoyed; the harder you try to remove them all, the more work it becomes. **Iron only the collar, front placket and cuffs** and the whole garment looks neat.
- **Colour loss**: dark navy, black and coloured linen **bleed easily in water**, so wash them alone the first time. They also **fade in sunlight**, so dry them inside out in the shade.
- **Yellowing on white linen** is skin oil. The guide is **30 minutes in oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) at about 40°C.** Chlorine bleach damages the fabric.
- **Storage**: linen copes well with humidity, but **long-term storage on a hanger leaves marks on the shoulders.** Fold it off-season, or use a thick hanger.
- **Linen fluff**: white fibres may appear on dark clothes; this settles down with washing.

> **[Verify]** The shrinkage rate and ironing temperatures are general figures used in the textile trade. Check the definition of 麻 in the labelling regulations against the Consumer Affairs Agency's documents.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Linen shrinks, so it should go to the cleaner rather than the wash

**Linen is a strong fibre well suited to washing in water.** Accept a little shrinkage the first time; most pieces are fine to wash at home. Follow the label.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Creases come out if I iron once it is dry

**Linen creases are hard to remove dry; the rule is damp fabric and high heat.** Even just smoothing it by hand straight after the spin makes a big difference.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A hard spin means faster drying

**A hard spin sets the creases in.** Linen dries fast anyway, so a 30-second to 1-minute spin is enough.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The sunniest spot is the best place to dry it

**Dark linen fades in sunlight.** An airy, shaded spot, garment inside out, is the safe choice.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If the label says 麻, it is all the same

**Under Japan's labelling rules only linen and ramie may be written as 麻**; hemp and jute are labelled differently. Even within 麻, ramie is crisper and stiffer than linen.
