# Q. Can I wash a pillow? How often should I wash the cover and the pillow itself?｜Japan Questions #474

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-pillows` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Your face and scalp rest on a pillow for 6–8 hours every night, so it collects skin oil, sweat and dandruff. Even so, many people have never washed the pillow itself. Whether it can be washed depends on the filling, so here is how to read the label and how often to wash. Pillows sold in Japan carry a wide range of fillings, some of which will be unfamiliar.

## A. Wash the pillowcase once a week, and the pillow itself once every 2–3 months if the filling allows. Polyester fibre, plastic pipe and bead fillings can mostly be washed, but memory foam (low-resilience urethane), feather and down, and buckwheat hulls (そばがら, sobagara) either cannot be washed or have limits, so always check the care label. For pillows that cannot be washed, air them in the sun or in the shade to drive out moisture, and put a "pillow pad" (枕パッド, makura paddo) under the cover to keep dirt off. Once a pillow has flattened and no longer springs back, it is time to replace it whatever the filling (1–3 years).

## Washing by filling type

| Filling | Wash at home | Drying | Replace after |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Polyester fibre** | **Yes** if the label allows (net, gentle cycle) | In the sun, reshaping part-way through. Takes **2–3 days** to dry | **1–3 years** |
| **Plastic pipe (polyethylene)** | **Yes.** In a net in the machine, or in the basin | Drains fast; **half a day to a day** | **3–5 years** (when crushed) |
| **Micro-beads** | **Mostly no.** Escaped beads block the drain | Wash the cover only | **1–2 years** |
| **Memory foam (low-resilience urethane)** | **No** (falls apart when wet and will not dry) | **Shade only.** Direct sun speeds deterioration | **2–3 years** (when it stops springing back) |
| **Feather and down** | Depends on the label. Most are **cleaner-recommended** | Follow the label | **3–5 years** |
| **Buckwheat hulls (sobagara)** | **No** (causes mould and insects). Air in the sun | Air in the sun several times a year; replace the hulls | **1–2 years** |

- **Why it gets dirty**: a person is generally described as sweating **about a glassful (roughly 200 ml) a night**, and sweat, oil and dandruff from the head all collect in the pillow. **House dust mites** thrive on dandruff and moisture, and a pillow is an ideal place for them to breed.
- **The cover**: **once a week**; if it is cotton, washing at **40–60°C (104–140°F)** removes both mites and skin oil more easily. Putting a **pillow pad** under the cover and changing that **1–2 times a week** also works well.
- **Dust mites**: mites die **at 50°C (122°F) in 20–30 minutes, and quickly at 60°C.** For pillows that cannot be washed, a **tumble dryer's hot cycle (if the label allows)**, or **airing in the sun followed by vacuuming** to remove dead mites and droppings, is effective. **Sun-airing alone does not kill mites** (the inside does not get hot enough).
- **A pillow left damp after washing goes mouldy and smells.** Dry it right through, and pick a run of sunny days.
- Replace it if **the smell will not go, the yellowing is widespread, or it has flattened so the height is wrong.** **A pillow of the wrong height strains the neck and shoulders.**

> **[Verify]** The overnight sweat volume and the mite-kill temperatures are commonly quoted figures. Check them against municipal or specialist sources.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Airing in the sun kills the mites

**The inside of a pillow does not reach 50°C.** Sun-airing is good for driving out moisture, but for mites you need a hot tumble-dryer cycle, or washing combined with vacuuming.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The pillow itself stays clean because I use a cover

**Sweat and skin oil pass through the cover.** Take the cover off and you will usually find yellowing on the pillow. A pillow pad on top reduces it.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Memory-foam pillows can go in the machine too

**Urethane soaks up water, breaks apart and never dries.** It becomes a breeding ground for mould. Air it in the shade, wash the cover, and replace it when it deteriorates.

## Misunderstanding 4｜If I wash it, it will last for years

**Fillings flatten.** A change in height changes the load on your neck and spoils your sleep. Replace according to the guide for each filling.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A buckwheat-hull pillow is natural, so it is hygienic

**Buckwheat hulls are vulnerable to moisture, insects and mould.** They need frequent sun-airing and periodic replacement of the hulls, and are unsuited to a damp bedroom.
