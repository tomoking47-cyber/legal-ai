# Q. Soy sauce, oil or blood on my clothes — what should I do on the spot?｜Japan Questions #476

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/stain-first-aid` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Soy sauce splashed at a restaurant, dressing on a sleeve, a nosebleed on a shirt. Think "I'll wash it later" and the stain sets before you get the chance. The outcome depends on two things: what kind of stain it is, and what you do in the first five minutes. Here is the first aid passed down in Japanese households, sorted by type of stain, with the shop names for the products you will need.

## A. Sort stains into three kinds — water-based, oil-based and protein — and change your first move accordingly. Soy sauce, coffee and juice (water-based): dab with a handkerchief dampened with water. Dressing, lipstick and meat sauce (oil-based): blot the oil with dry paper, then at home work neat washing-up liquid (neutral) into the spot. Blood, egg and milk (protein): hot water sets them for good, so always use cold water. The three rules for every stain: do not rub, do not rinse in hot water, do not leave it. If it will not come out, take it to a cleaner soon and tell them what the stain is.

## Types of stain and first aid

| Type | Examples | On the spot | At home | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Water-based** | Soy sauce, coffee, tea, juice, wine | Blot with dry paper, then **dab with a handkerchief dampened with water** (hold a tissue against the back) | Apply laundry detergent directly, wait a few minutes, wash | Rubbing, hot water |
| **Oil-based** | Dressing, chilli oil, meat sauce, lipstick, foundation | **Blot the oil with dry paper.** No water (it spreads) | Work in neat **washing-up liquid (neutral)**, rinse in water at **about 40°C (104°F)**, then wash | Dabbing with water (spreads it) |
| **Protein** | Blood, egg, milk, sweat | Dampen with **cold water** and dab | Wash in **cold water with detergent.** For dried blood, soak in **oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) in cold water** | **Hot water** (sets the protein) |
| **Mixed** | Curry, meat sauce, cosmetics | Blot the oil, then dab with water | Neutral detergent, then oxygen bleach (safe for colours), then wash. The yellow of curry (turmeric) **fades in sunlight** | Chlorine bleach (on colours or prints) |
| **Mud** | Splashes | **Let it dry** (do not rub while wet) | Once dry, brush off and spot-wash with a bar of soap | Rubbing while wet |
| **Ink** | Ballpoint pen | Do not touch | Hold rubbing alcohol (消毒用エタノール, sold at drugstores) against the back and dab (test for colour loss first) | Water (it bleeds) |

- **Tools for every case**: tissues, a handkerchief and **two clean cloths** (one underneath, and dab from above so the stain transfers into the lower cloth). **"Dab" does not mean "rub"** — rubbing drives the stain deep into the fibres and roughens the surface.
- **A race against time**: within **24 hours** there is a good chance of removing it at home; after **a week or more** it oxidises and sets, and even a cleaner will struggle.
- **Which bleach**: **oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate powder, or the liquid type) can be used on colours and prints**; **chlorine bleach (塩素系, enso-kei) is for whites only.** **Mixing chlorine bleach with an acidic cleaner (citric acid and the like) gives off toxic gas**, so never use them together.
- **Colour-loss test**: put detergent on a hidden spot on the inside, press with a white cloth and check that no colour comes off before you start.
- **At the cleaner's**: tell them **when it happened and what the stain is.** It changes how they handle it and raises the chance of success. **If you have tried bleach at home**, say so too.

> **[Verify]** Each method follows general explanations from cleaning-industry bodies and the soap and detergent association. Silk, rayon and leather are handled differently; consider a warning note at publication.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Just rub it with the wet towel for now

**Rubbing pushes the stain deep into the fibres and spreads it.** Blot with dry paper, then "dab" with a water-dampened cloth.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Blood comes out in hot water

**Heat sets protein and bonds it to the fibres.** For blood, egg and milk, cold water is the iron rule.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Oil comes out eventually if I rinse it in water

**Oil repels water, and dabbing with water only spreads it.** Break the oil down with washing-up liquid first.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Bleach gets anything out

**Chlorine bleach strips the colour from prints and damages the fabric.** Use oxygen bleach on colours. And never mix it with other cleaners.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If I could not get it out, the cleaner cannot either

**Tell them what the stain is and the cleaner often can.** But take it in early, before repeated attempts at home set the stain.
