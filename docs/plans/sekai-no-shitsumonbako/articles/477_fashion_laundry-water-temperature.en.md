# Q. Is cold water fine for laundry? What changes if I use hot water?｜Japan Questions #477

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/laundry-water-temperature` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

If you come from Europe or North America, washing at 40–60°C is probably normal for you. Japanese household machines, though, almost all wash in cold tap water, and most have no heater. Newcomers worry that a Japanese wash does not get clothes clean; Japanese readers worry that hot water ruins clothes. Here is how it works, along with what the numbers on a care label mean.

## A. The number inside the wash-tub symbol on a care label (30, 40, 60 and so on) is an upper limit, not an instruction to wash at that temperature. Japanese tap water drops to around 10°C (50°F) in winter, and skin-oil soiling comes out more easily at 30–40°C (86–104°F), so for yellowed or smelly items the realistic option is to use leftover bath water (around 40°C) for the wash cycle only. Rinse with tap water. Water above 40°C causes shrinkage in wool and colour loss in dyed items, and 60°C (140°F) is best kept for bedding and towels where you want to deal with mites and bacteria.

## What each water temperature does

| Water temperature | Cleaning | Suits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Around 10°C (winter tap water)** | Detergent may not dissolve; skin oil solidifies and is hard to remove | — | Use liquid detergent, or dissolve powder in warm water first |
| **20–25°C (summer tap water)** | Enough for everyday soiling | Everyday clothes in general | — |
| **30–40°C (leftover bath water, warm water)** | **Skin oil and sweat come out more easily.** Oxygen bleach also works better | Yellowed shirts, towels, underwear, socks | Wool, silk and colours: **check the upper limit on the label** |
| **50–60°C** | **Effective against mites (50°C for 20–30 minutes) and many bacteria** | Bedding and towels (cotton, label 60 or above) | Shrinkage, colour loss, creasing in synthetics. **Check the machine's heat limit** (many are rated to 50°C) |

- **Reading the label**: the **number in the tub symbol is the maximum temperature** (JIS L0001). "40" means you can wash in water or warm water up to 40°C. One bar underneath means a gentler cycle, two bars gentler still. **Anything at or below the number is fine.**
- **Using leftover bath water** (残り湯, nokoriyu — many Japanese machines have a pump hose for this): **bath water for the wash cycle only; tap water for the rinse.** Bath water contains skin oil and bacteria, and using it for the rinse too **leaves the laundry smelling.** If the bath contained a bath additive, check its packaging ("can be used for laundry", etc.).
- **Feeding hot water into the machine**: most fully automatic machines have an **inlet-water limit of about 50°C.** Ignore the manual and you risk degraded hoses and seals, or a breakdown.
- **Undissolved detergent**: in winter, powder detergent **dissolves poorly at 5–10°C** and may leave white residue. **Switch to liquid**, or **dissolve the powder in warm water before adding it.**
- **If you use 60°C against mites**, keep it to **cotton sheets and towels whose label says 60 or above.** Note, though, that **domestic washing machines generally accept water only up to about 50°C**, and pouring in 60°C water can damage the machine. It is more practical to soak the items **in the bath or a wash basin** rather than in the drum, or to use **a tumble dryer's hot cycle or a launderette's high-heat dryer.**

> **[Verify]** Seasonal tap-water temperatures, mite-kill temperatures and machine inlet limits vary by region and model. Check the sources and the manuals of common models.

## Misunderstanding 1｜"40" on the label means wash at 40°C

**It is a maximum.** Anything up to 40 is fine. Washing a "30" garment in 10°C winter water is no problem.

## Misunderstanding 2｜A Japanese cold-water wash does not get clothes clean

**Everyday soiling comes out perfectly well in water at around 20°C with detergent.** Only items with skin-oil yellowing or smells need warm water, and only for the wash cycle.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Using bath water for the wash and the rinse saves the most

**Bath water in the rinse leaves skin oil and bacteria on the laundry.** Rinse with tap water.

## Misunderstanding 4｜With hot water I do not need detergent

**Hot water only helps the detergent work**; it is no substitute for the surfactants that break down skin oil.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Hotter is cleaner, so 60°C every time

**That leads to shrinkage, colour loss, creased synthetics and a broken machine.** Keep 60°C for bedding, towels and other items that need it.
