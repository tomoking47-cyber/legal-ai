# Q. Vacuum storage bags for futons and winter clothes: is there anything I should not put in them?｜Japan Questions #478

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/vacuum-storage-bags` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Vacuum storage bags (圧縮袋, asshuku-bukuro), which you flatten with a vacuum cleaner or by rolling, have long been a fixture in Japan's small homes because they free up space in the oshiire (押し入れ, the deep built-in closet). But they also cause plenty of grief: "My down futon never fluffed back up", "My down jacket is flat", "Six months later: creases and mould". What can go in depends on the material and how long it stays there.

## A. Vacuum bags suit polyester-fibre futons and blankets and cotton clothing. Down futons, down jackets and wool knits should stay out, or at most have a little air squeezed out, because the fibres break and do not recover. For every material the rules are: wash and dry completely first, and take it out at least once within 6 months. Volume drops to about a third, but overfill the bag and it splits, which defeats the purpose.

## What can and cannot be compressed

| Item | Compress? | Reason and notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Polyester-fibre futons and blankets** | **Yes** | Recover well. Take out and air **within 6 months** |
| **Cotton clothing (T-shirts, sweatshirts)** | **Yes** | Will crease. Air or iron after taking out |
| **Down futons** | **Avoid, or squeeze out only a little air** | The barbs of the down break and **the loft (warmth) does not return.** The Japan Down Products Cooperative Association also **advises against heavy compression** |
| **Down jackets** | **Avoid** | As above. Store in a **roomy non-woven cover** instead |
| **Wool and cashmere knits** | **Avoid, or short-term only** | Broken fibres and creases; **moth repellents work differently** in a sealed bag |
| **Leather and synthetic leather** | **No** | Fold marks, cracking, surface damage |
| **Memory foam and urethane products** | **No** | Do not recover; the material breaks down |
| **Anything damp or unwashed** | **No** | **Seals in mould and smells and lets them grow** |

- **How far to compress**: down to **about one third of the volume.** Sucking it "flat as a pancake" causes severe fibre breakage and creasing. Fill the bag **no more than 80%**; overfilling is the main cause of splits.
- **Before it goes in**: **wash, then dry completely.** Leftover skin oil and moisture breed mould and insects. **If you put a moth repellent in a vacuum bag**, use one **labelled for vacuum bags (圧縮袋対応)**. Most repellents work by evaporating; in a sealed bag they may not work as designed, or the active ingredient may build up on the fabric.
- **Duration**: **take things out and air them once every six months.** Most vacuum bags are **not a perfect seal and let in some air within a few months.** Leaving items for over a year is the main cause of creases, mould and smells.
- **After taking out**: **half a day to a day in the sun or in the shade** to let the air back in. Polyester fibre recovers with a light beating. Down does not recover easily.
- **Alternatives**: put down, feathers and knits in **non-woven cases** or **breathable storage bags**, filled **no more than 80%.** Some people say the real way to save space is not compression but owning less.

> **[Verify]** Check the Japan Down Products Cooperative Association's position on compressing down products. The "suitable for vacuum bags" labelling of moth repellents follows the individual makers' explanations.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A down futon can be flattened in a vacuum bag with no harm

**The barbs break and the loft (warmth) does not return.** Do not compress a down futon, or squeeze out only a little air, and take it out within 6 months.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Vacuum bags are sealed, so no need to worry about mould or insects

**Trap moisture inside and mould grows in there.** Everything must be completely dry before it goes in. The seal is not perfect either: air gets in within months.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A moth repellent in the bag makes it safe

**Most repellents do not work as designed in a sealed space.** Use a product labelled for vacuum bags and follow its instructions.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The more air you remove, the more efficient the storage

**Fibres break, creases set hard and the bag splits more easily.** Stop at about one third.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once packed, it can stay shut until next winter

**Take it out and air it once every six months.** Creases, smells and mould all scale with how long it is left.
