# Q. Mould on clothes I had put away. Can I get it off, and how do I stop it in the closet?｜Japan Questions #480

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/clothing-mould-and-mildew` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

You open the closet after the rainy season (tsuyu, June to mid-July) and find white specks on a black jacket, or green fuzz on a leather bag. Japan's hot, humid summer is ideal for mould on clothing, and every year it strikes the clothes people "had not taken out for a while". Here is how to remove it, and separately, how to stop it at the root.

## A. White mould (a powdery layer on the surface) usually comes off if you brush it away outdoors, wipe with rubbing alcohol and then wash. Black mould (which roots inside the fibres) needs a 30-minute to 1-hour soak in oxygen bleach dissolved in water at 40–50°C (104–122°F), after a colour-loss test on anything coloured or printed. Leather, wool and silk are easily damaged by home methods, so take them to a specialist. Prevention means keeping closet humidity below 60%: dehumidifier packs, air movement from a fan, filling the closet no more than 80%, and never putting worn clothes away unwashed.

## Types of mould and how to remove them

| Type | What it looks like | Method | Chance of success |
|---|---|---|---|
| **White mould** | White powder or fuzz, sitting on the surface | 1. Brush or wipe off outdoors with a dry cloth 2. Dab with **rubbing alcohol** (消毒用エタノール, shōdoku-yō etanōru, from any drugstore) 3. Normal wash 4. Dry in the sun | **High** |
| **Black mould (black specks)** | Black to green specks; the hyphae have grown into the fibres | 1. Dissolve **oxygen bleach** (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) in water at **40–50°C** and **soak for 30 minutes to 1 hour** (check the label and test for colour loss first) 2. Wash 3. Dry in the sun | **Medium.** Marks may remain |
| **Mould on leather or synthetic leather** | White to green fuzz, or black specks | Wipe off with a dry cloth, then wipe with **leather cleaner** or **diluted alcohol**, dry in the shade, then condition with leather cream | Surface mould only. Deep mould: specialist |
| **Wool, silk, down** | Mostly white mould | Brush off and take to a **cleaner** (home bleaching damages them) | Ask the shop |

- **Conditions for mould**: **humidity above 60%, temperature 20–30°C, and food (skin oil, dust, food traces).** A Japanese oshiire (built-in closet) between June and September can reach 70–80% humidity. Of the three conditions, **cutting off humidity and food** is the core of prevention.
- **Handling with care**: **do the brushing outdoors and wear a mask, so you do not breathe in the spores.** Brushing indoors sends spores into the air and onto other clothes. **Chlorine bleach is for whites only** — never on colours, prints, wool or silk — and **mixed with an acidic cleaner it gives off toxic gas.**
- **Keeping closet humidity below 60%**:
  - **Dehumidifier packs** (除湿剤, joshitsuzai; place the calcium-chloride type low down and replace when the water has collected)
  - **A fan or air circulator**: open the doors and **blow air through for 30 minutes a few times a month**
  - **Fill no more than 80%**: a finger's width between garments
  - Keep things **at least 10 cm off the floor**; moisture collects near the floor
  - Lay a **slatted board** (すのこ, sunoko, sold at home centres) underneath and keep **5 cm from the wall**
- **Never put worn clothes away unwashed**: skin oil and moisture are exactly the food and water mould needs. **Remove the plastic from dry-cleaned clothes too** before storing (it traps moisture).
- **If the musty smell will not go**: the mould may be gone but **the smell remains.** Sun-drying, a soak in baking-soda water (重曹, jūsō) or steaming can improve it. If not, take it to a cleaner and tell them it is mould.

> **[Verify]** The conditions for mould growth (humidity, temperature) are general figures based on municipal public-health documents. The warning against mixing bleaches follows NITE and makers' labels.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Drying in the sun kills the mould

**UV reduces the mould on the surface, but the hyphae inside the fibres remain.** White mould needs brushing and wiping; black mould needs a bleach soak. Sun-drying is the finishing step.

## Misunderstanding 2｜I can just brush it off indoors

**The spores fly and spread to other clothes and the room.** Do it outside or on the balcony, wearing a mask.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Chlorine bleach will shift it in one go

**It strips colour from prints, and dissolves wool and silk.** Use oxygen bleach for black mould on clothes. Chlorine is for white cotton only, and never mixed with other cleaners.

## Misunderstanding 4｜With a dehumidifier pack in the closet, mould cannot grow

**A dehumidifier pack is only a helper in a small space**; it cannot keep up with overfilling, no airflow and unwashed clothes. Use all four measures together.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Leaving the dry-cleaner's plastic on keeps clothes clean in storage

**The plastic traps moisture and causes mould and discolouration.** Take it off when you get home and switch to a non-woven cover.
