# Q. For constipation, what should I change first? What to do before turning to laxatives｜Japan Questions #484

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/constipation-and-daily-habits` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

In Japan's national health survey, constipation ranks near the top of self-reported symptoms among women, and laxatives are a year-round staple of drugstore (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.) shelves. Many people believe "if I do not go every day, I am constipated", or take a stimulant laxative every day. There is a lot here worth looking at again.

## A. Medically, constipation is judged not by "how often" but by the state of things: hard stools, difficulty passing them, a feeling that some remains. Change things in this order: ① eat breakfast to set off the bowel reflex, ② keep 5 minutes after breakfast to sit on the toilet, ③ increase dietary fibre (target 21 g a day for men, 18 g for women) and fluids, ④ on the toilet, use a footstool so your knees are higher than your hips, ⑤ walk. OTC stimulant laxatives (senna, bisacodyl and the like) are for "occasional" use as a rule; taken daily, they become less effective. Blood in the stool, weight loss, or constipation that starts suddenly after 50: see a doctor before changing your habits.

## The basics of constipation and daily guides

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Definition (clinical practice guideline)** | "A state in which stool that should be passed cannot be passed in sufficient quantity and comfortably." **Frequency is not part of the definition** |
| **How many people** | In the Comprehensive Survey of Living Conditions, **about 3–4% of the population** report constipation; **about twice as many women as men**; **a sharp rise over 70** |
| **Dietary fibre target** | **Adult men 21 g or more, women 18 g or more per day** (Dietary Reference Intakes for Japanese, 2025 edition). Actual average intake is **about 14–15 g**, so short |
| **Fluids** | **Around 1.2 L** besides meals. **A glass of water right after waking** can start the bowel moving |
| **Bowel reflex** | Most likely **within 30 minutes after breakfast**. Making a habit of sitting on the toilet at this time is the first move |
| **Sitting position** | **Knees higher than the hips, leaning forward** (use a footstool): the rectum straightens and stool passes more easily |
| **Exercise** | Reports that **20–30 minutes of walking a day** improves bowel movement |

## Types of OTC laxative and how to use them

| Type | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Stimulant laxatives** | Senna, sennoside, bisacodyl, daiō (rhubarb root, common in Japanese herbal laxatives) | **Occasional use (up to 2–3 times a week).** Daily use makes them less effective, with abdominal pain and dependence problems. **The most widely taken type, and the one needing most care** |
| **Osmotic (saline) laxatives** | Magnesium oxide (酸化マグネシウム, sanka-maguneshiumu) | Softens the stool. **People with reduced kidney function and older people should watch for high blood magnesium**; for long-term use, ask a doctor |
| **Bulk-forming and gut-flora products** | Fibre preparations, lactic acid bacteria, bifidobacteria (整腸剤, seichōzai) | Gentle. **Take with fluids** |
| **Suppositories and enemas** | Glycerin enemas and the like | For emergencies **when you just need to go**. Not for regular use |

- **There are two kinds of fibre**: **soluble** (seaweed, oats, fruit, barley) softens the stool; **insoluble** (vegetables, beans, brown rice) adds bulk. **Some people get harder stools if they only add insoluble fibre**, so take both.
- **Fermented foods** (yoghurt, nattō, miso, nukazuke rice-bran pickles) and **oligosaccharides** (onion, banana, soya) feed the gut bacteria.
- **Do not hold it in**: repeatedly ignoring the urge dulls the rectum, and you feel the urge less.
- **Symptoms where seeing a doctor comes first**: **blood in the stool or black stool, sudden weight loss, severe abdominal pain or vomiting, persistently thin stools, constipation that newly starts after 50, colorectal cancer in the family**. Colorectal cancer screening (faecal occult blood test) is recommended **once a year from age 40**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If I do not go every day, I am constipated

**Once every 2–3 days is not constipation if it passes comfortably and without discomfort.** Conversely, going every day but with hard stools, a remaining feeling or straining is constipation.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Just eat lots of vegetables and it is solved

**Some people get harder stools when they only add insoluble fibre.** Add soluble fibre, fluids and some fat as a set.

## Misunderstanding 3｜OTC laxatives are fine to take every day

**Daily use of stimulant laxatives leads to reduced effect, abdominal pain and a sluggish bowel.** Occasional use is the rule. If you need one every day, ask a doctor.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Mornings are busy, so I will sit on the toilet at night

**The bowel reflex is most likely after breakfast.** Getting up 5 minutes earlier to sit works better than 30 minutes at night.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is just age, nothing to be done

**Constipation in older age often has fluids, exercise or medicine side effects (painkillers, anticholinergics and so on) behind it**, and improves when these are reviewed. Constipation that starts suddenly may also be a sign of illness, so ask a doctor.
