# Q. Is 16-hour fasting (time-restricted eating) good for you? Is it safe to keep up?｜Japan Questions #486

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/time-restricted-eating` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Go 16 hours without eating and you lose weight." "It switches on autophagy." This way of eating has spread in Japan too, through books and videos. Research is growing, but the results are not as simple as the marketing. Here is what is known, what is not, and who should avoid it.

## A. Restricting your eating to an 8–10 hour window (time-restricted eating) makes most people "eat fewer calories as a result", and studies report a weight loss of 2–4% over a few months. However, compared with restricting the same calories without a time window, no added benefit has been shown (a 12-month trial in 2022), and there are reports that lean mass (muscle) is more likely to be lost. The real effect comes from "drawing a line around eating hours", which cuts snacks and late-night eating. Avoid it if you take diabetes medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, are still growing, or have a history of an eating disorder.

## Main studies and results

| Study | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Liu et al. (NEJM, 2022)** | 139 adults with obesity, **12 months**. Compared "8-hour time-restricted eating plus calorie restriction" with "calorie restriction alone" | Weight loss **about 8 kg vs about 6 kg, no significant difference**. Blood pressure, blood glucose and lipids improved equally in both groups |
| **Lowe et al. (JAMA Intern Med, 2020)** | 116 adults with overweight, **12 weeks**. Compared 16-hour fasting with normal eating | Weight loss **about 0.9 kg vs about 0.7 kg, no significant difference**. **Loss of lean mass** stood out in the fasting group |
| **Several meta-analyses (2020–2023)** | Pooled short-term trials of time-restricted eating | **Weight falls by about 1–3 kg on average.** The effect comes mainly from **reduced calorie intake**. Long-term health effects **not established** |
| **Early vs late window** | Placing the eating window "morning to afternoon" tends to give better blood-glucose markers than "midday to night" | For a Japanese eating pattern, **an earlier dinner** is the realistic direction |

- **The heart of the effect**: **"drawing a line around eating hours" reduces the occasions for snacks, late-night food and alcohol, so calories fall.** Other methods (stopping snacks, eating dinner earlier) give the same result.
- **"Autophagy"** is the cell's self-cleaning process, and in animal experiments fasting activates it. **It has not been shown that a 16-hour fast leads to health benefits in humans.** The word is popular in marketing, but the evidence is at animal level.
- **Muscle loss**: with fewer meals, protein easily falls short, and **loss of lean mass** has been reported. If you do it, **make sure you get enough protein (1.0–1.2 g per kg of body weight) within the eating window** and combine it with strength training.
- **Who should avoid it**: **people with diabetes on insulin or SU drugs (hypoglycaemia)**, **pregnant or breastfeeding women**, **growing children and teenagers**, **anyone with a history of an eating disorder**, **underweight people (BMI under 18.5)**, **older people with a small appetite (frailty)**. **If you have a chronic condition, ask your doctor.**
- **A realistic form if you try it**: even **"finish dinner by 20:00, breakfast after 8:00" — about 12 hours** — gives the benefit of less late-night eating and drinking. **Skipping breakfast has repeatedly been linked in Japanese studies to weight gain and worse blood glucose**, so if you drop something, bringing dinner forward suits better.

> **[Verify]** Check participant numbers and weight-loss figures against the original papers (Liu D et al. NEJM 2022;386:1495-1504; Lowe DA et al. JAMA Intern Med 2020;180:1491-1499).

## Misunderstanding 1｜Fast for 16 hours and you lose weight whatever you eat

**What works is the fall in calorie intake.** Pack high-calorie food into the 8 hours and you will not lose weight.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It works better than calorie restriction

**In a 12-month comparison there was no difference from calorie restriction alone.** It is one option, chosen for "whether you can keep it up".

## Misunderstanding 3｜Autophagy makes your cells younger

**No health benefit has been shown in humans.** Applying animal results straight to people is a leap.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Skip breakfast and you easily reach 16 hours

**Skipping breakfast is linked in Japanese data to weight gain and worse blood glucose.** An earlier dinner with breakfast kept suits better.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is a health method, so anyone can do it

**People on diabetes medication risk hypoglycaemia; pregnant, growing and underweight people risk nutritional shortfall.** If you have a chronic condition, ask your doctor.
