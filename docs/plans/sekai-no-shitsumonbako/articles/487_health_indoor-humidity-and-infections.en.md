# Q. In winter, does raising indoor humidity help you avoid colds? What percentage should I aim for?｜Japan Questions #487

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/indoor-humidity-and-infections` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Come winter, you will hear "a humidifier keeps viruses away". Japanese rooms in winter, with the heating on (usually the air conditioner's heat mode), drop to 20–30% humidity: the season of sore throats, dry skin and static shocks. Here is what is known about humidity and infections, and the downsides of overdoing it.

## A. Aim for 40–60% humidity. This range fits both the indoor standard set by the Building Sanitation Act (建築物衛生法, Japan's law on indoor environments in offices and commercial buildings), 40–70%, and the "40% or above" the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare gave in its winter infection guidance. Influenza viruses linger longer in the air when it is cold and dry, and the membranes of the nose and throat lose their defensive power when they dry out, so there is a basis for not falling below 40%. But above 60%, condensation, mould and dust mites increase and bring different health problems, and a neglected humidifier can spread bacteria (Legionella and others) from its tank. "40–60% on a hygrometer" and "change the humidifier water daily, wash it weekly" go together.

## Humidity guides and the basis

| Humidity | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Below 30%** | The membranes of the nose and throat dry out, and the cilia (which sweep foreign matter out) slow down. Dry skin, static. **Droplets containing viruses dry out, shrink, and float in the air for longer** |
| **40–60%** | **Recommended range.** Virus survival tends to fall, the membranes' defences work better, and mould and dust mites do not spread easily |
| **Above 60%** | **Condensation** forms readily; **mould (from 60%) and dust mites (active at 60–80%)** increase. A factor in worsening asthma and allergies |

- **Influenza virus survival**: in a classic experiment (Harper, 1961), **at 20–24°C (68–75°F) and 50% humidity or more, survival after 6 hours fell to a few per cent**, while **at around 20% humidity about 60% survived**. The government also explains **cold, dry conditions as the setting for epidemics**.
- **MHLW winter ventilation guide (2020)**: **keep ventilating, with room temperature 18°C (64°F) or above and humidity 40% or above**. The idea is "make up with a humidifier for the humidity that ventilation takes out".
- **Building Sanitation Act standard**: indoor **relative humidity of 40–70%** is the management standard for offices, commercial facilities and the like. If your office air conditioning is drying, you can cite this to ask for an improvement.
- **Ways to humidify, and cautions**:
  - **Humidifier**: **change the tank water every day and wash the tank and filter once a week.** In particular, **ultrasonic humidifiers (超音波式) turn bacteria in the water straight into mist**, and neglected units have caused Legionella pneumonia (with deaths in Japan). **Steam humidifiers (加熱式/スチーム式) have fewer bacteria problems, but watch the electricity cost and the burn risk.**
  - **Without a humidifier**: a wet towel, drying laundry indoors, leaving the bathroom door open, houseplants, hot-pot (nabe) cooking.
- **Hygrometer**: place it **out of the air conditioner's airflow, 1–1.5 m above the floor**. Near the humidifier's outlet, it reads higher than the room really is.
- **For a dry throat**: besides humidity, **frequent sips of water and a mask (while sleeping)** also help.

> **[Verify]** Check the Harper (1961) survival figures against the original (J Hyg (Lond) 1961;59:479-486). The MHLW "humidity 40% or above" wording is based on the winter 2020 ventilation leaflet.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The higher the humidity, the fewer colds

**Above 60%, mould, dust mites and condensation increase and bring other health problems.** The goal is to stay within 40–60%.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Leave the humidifier on and you are protected from infection

**Humidifying only "sets the conditions"; it is a supplement.** It does not replace hand-washing, masks, ventilation or vaccination.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Just top up the humidifier water as it goes down

**Bacteria multiply in the tank water within a day.** Replace it daily, wash weekly. A neglected humidifier can cause pneumonia.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Heating dries the air, and nothing can be done

**Heating lowers "relative humidity" because warm air can hold more water, and a humidifier makes up for this.** Room temperature 18°C and humidity 40% or above is the winter guide.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If the air conditioner's humidity display says 50%, all is well

**The readings on air conditioners and humidifiers are for the air right around the unit.** Check with a hygrometer in the middle of the room, at bed height.
