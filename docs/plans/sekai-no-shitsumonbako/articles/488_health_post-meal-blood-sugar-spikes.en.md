# Q. What is a "blood sugar spike"? Should I worry even if my health check is normal?｜Japan Questions #488

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/post-meal-blood-sugar-spikes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Your fasting blood glucose at the annual health check (健康診断, kenkō shindan — the check most employees in Japan receive) is normal, but after meals your blood glucose shoots up. Japanese television made the phrase "blood sugar spike" (血糖値スパイク, kettōchi supaiku) widely known. The term is informal, but the condition behind it, "post-meal hyperglycaemia", is taken seriously in medicine too. Here is what it means and who should pay attention.

## A. "Blood sugar spike" is not a medical term. It is an informal name for blood glucose that rises sharply above 140 mg/dL after a meal and then falls sharply. Some people have hidden post-meal hyperglycaemia even with normal fasting glucose, and it is a marker for pre-diabetes and for strain on the blood vessels. But healthy people also have some rise and fall after meals, and "a spike does not equal disease". The four counter-measures are: eat vegetables and protein first, cut refined carbohydrates, walk for 15 minutes after meals, and do not skip breakfast. If you have diabetes in the family, an HbA1c of 5.6% or above at your health check, obesity, or strong sleepiness after meals, it is worth asking a doctor about a post-meal glucose test (such as the 75 g oral glucose tolerance test).

## Post-meal blood glucose in numbers

Japan reports blood glucose in mg/dL (140 mg/dL is about 7.8 mmol/L).

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| **Normal post-meal glucose** | **Below 140 mg/dL** 2 hours after a meal |
| **Borderline (impaired glucose tolerance)** | 2-hour value in the 75 g oral glucose tolerance test of **140–199 mg/dL** |
| **Diabetic range** | 2-hour value **200 mg/dL or above**, or fasting **126 mg/dL or above**, or HbA1c **6.5% or above** |
| **HbA1c (a marker of average glucose over the past 1–2 months)** | **5.6% or above** qualifies for health guidance under the Specific Health Checkup (特定健診, the metabolic-syndrome check for ages 40–74); **6.0–6.4%** means diabetes cannot be ruled out |
| **Diabetes in Japan** | **About 10 million** people "strongly suspected of having diabetes" and **about 10 million** "who cannot be ruled out" (National Health and Nutrition Survey 2016) |
| **The health-check blind spot** | Specific Health Checkups usually measure **only fasting glucose and HbA1c**, so people with post-meal hyperglycaemia alone **can be missed** |

## Ways to soften the post-meal rise (those with evidence)

| Method | Content | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Eating order** | **Vegetables, mushrooms, seaweed → meat, fish, soya → rice, bread.** Start on the vegetables about 5 minutes ahead | Several Japanese clinical studies confirm a smaller post-meal rise |
| **Light exercise after meals** | **Walk for 10–15 minutes, 15–30 minutes after eating** | Many reports of a smaller post-meal rise |
| **Cut refined carbohydrates** | White rice → brown rice or mixed grains; fewer sweet pastries and soft drinks | Dietary fibre slows the absorption of sugar |
| **Do not skip breakfast** | Skipping breakfast **makes the rise after lunch larger** | Studies in Japanese subjects |
| **Chew well, eat slowly** | **20 minutes or more per meal** | Fast eating has been linked to diabetes risk |
| **Sleep** | **Less than 6 hours** of sleep worsens the action of insulin the next day | Experimental studies |

- **Consumer glucose meters and continuous glucose monitors (CGM)**: more and more healthy people are wearing these on their own and worrying that "I had a spike". **Rises and falls after meals in a healthy person are a normal response.** Interpret the numbers with a doctor.
- **Strong sleepiness or heaviness after meals** is generally described as one sign of post-meal hyperglycaemia, but it also happens for other reasons (lack of sleep, overeating).
- **When to ask about a test**: **HbA1c 5.6% or above**, **diabetes in a parent or sibling**, **BMI 25 or above**, **a history of gestational diabetes**, **over 40 with no exercise habit**.

> **[Verify]** Check the diagnostic thresholds against the Japan Diabetes Society's *Clinical Practice Guideline for Diabetes 2024* and its *Guide to Diabetes Care* [糖尿病治療ガイド]. Update the diabetes population estimate to the latest National Health and Nutrition Survey.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If my health-check glucose is normal, I need not worry about diabetes

**Fasting glucose and HbA1c alone can miss the stage where only post-meal glucose is high.** If your risk is high, ask a doctor about a glucose tolerance test.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Blood sugar spike is the name of a disease

**It is an informal term.** Medically it is "post-meal hyperglycaemia", which is not a disease in itself but a **marker** for diabetes and strain on the blood vessels.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Cut carbohydrates completely and there are no spikes

**The long-term safety of extreme carbohydrate restriction is not established, and the Japan Diabetes Society does not recommend it.** Changing the amount, the quality (refinement, fibre) and the order is the realistic route.

## Misunderstanding 4｜As long as I avoid sweets I am fine

**White rice, bread, noodles, fruit and soft drinks also raise blood glucose.** What matters is not sweetness but the amount of carbohydrate and how fast it is absorbed.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Checking my own "spikes" with a glucose meter gives peace of mind

**Even healthy people have normal rises and falls after meals, and more people are becoming anxious over the numbers alone.** If you measure, interpret the results together with a doctor.
