# Q. On your back, on your side or face down — which sleeping position is best for the body?｜Japan Questions #489

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/sleeping-position` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Sleeping on your back is correct." "Your side is better for snoring." There is plenty of talk about sleeping positions. But people turn over 20–30 times a night, so "staying in one position" is not realistic. Even so, we do know that the position you fall asleep in, combined with your bedding, changes symptoms such as back pain, snoring and acid reflux.

## A. There is no "correct position" for everyone; the realistic approach is to choose the position you fall asleep in according to your symptoms. If you tend to snore or have sleep apnoea, sleep on your side (the tongue does not fall back). For reflux oesophagitis, lie on your left side with the upper body slightly raised. For lower-back pain, lie on your back with a cushion under the knees, or on your side with a pillow between the knees. Sleeping face down keeps the neck twisted, and most orthopaedic doctors advise against it. In late pregnancy, lying on the left side is recommended. Whatever the position, the preconditions are "a mattress firm enough not to block turning over" and "a pillow height that keeps the neck straight".

## Advantages and cautions by position

| Position | Advantages | Cautions | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **On the back** | The spine easily keeps its natural curve. No pressure on the face or neck | **The tongue falls back into the throat, worsening snoring and apnoea.** Back pain for people whose lower back arches | Lower-back pain (cushion under the knees), people without neck or shoulder problems |
| **On the side** | **Snoring and apnoea ease.** **Lying on the left reduces acid reflux.** Recommended for pregnant women | Pressure on the lower shoulder and hip. A low pillow bends the neck | Snoring, reflux oesophagitis (left side), pregnancy (left side), lower-back pain (pillow between the knees) |
| **Face down** | Snoring eases in some people | **Neck twisted for hours**, lower back arches, chest compressed. **Suffocation risk for infants (babies go on their backs, as a rule, to prevent SIDS)** | Basically not recommended |

- **Turning over 20–30 times a night** is normal. It is a physiological movement that avoids prolonged pressure on one spot and trapped body heat. **A mattress that is too soft, a narrow bed or a duvet that is too heavy** all get in the way of turning.
- **Pillow height**: the height at which **there is no gap behind the neck on your back, and the neck bones line up with the spine on your side**. People who mostly sleep on their side need **a higher pillow than for back-sleeping** (to allow for shoulder width) (see our separate article on pillow height).
- **Lower-back pain**: **on your back, put a cushion under the knees** to reduce the arch; **on your side, a pillow between the knees** stops the pelvis twisting. The Japanese Orthopaedic Association recommends bedding that is "neither too hard nor too soft, and easy to turn over on".
- **Reflux oesophagitis**: several studies show **lying on the left side** reduces reflux because of the shape of the stomach, and **raising the upper body by 10–15 cm** (by raising the head end of the mattress, not by stacking pillows) is effective. **Finishing dinner at least 3 hours before bed** is also basic.
- **Snoring and apnoea**: many people improve on their side, and a **body pillow** or **a cushion behind the back** can stop you rolling onto your back. But if you have **strong daytime sleepiness, or family members notice you stop breathing**, get tested for sleep apnoea syndrome (see our separate article on snoring).
- **Late pregnancy**: **lying on the left side** avoids the uterus pressing on the vena cava and keeps blood flowing to the baby, so obstetricians recommend it.

> **[Verify]** The number of turns per night and the link between left-side lying and reflux are general explanations from the sleep and gastroenterology fields. Check against Japanese Society of Sleep Research and Japanese Society of Gastroenterology material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜On your back is the correct position

**For people who snore or have apnoea, the back can be the worst position.** Choosing by your symptoms is the right answer.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Turning over a lot means shallow sleep

**Turning over is a normal and necessary movement.** If anything, turning less (bedding that is too soft) leads to morning back pain and stiffness.

## Misunderstanding 3｜If I sleep well face down, there is no problem

**It is a position that loads the neck and lower back for hours.** If you wake with neck or back pain, it is worth moving to your side. **Infants risk suffocation, and go on their backs as a rule.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜For reflux, just use a higher pillow

**Raising only the pillow bends the neck and compresses the abdomen, which can make things worse.** Raise the head end of the whole mattress, or use a wedge cushion.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A high pillow and a hard mattress are good for the body

**The best choice changes with build and position.** A height that keeps the neck straight, and a firmness that lets you turn over, are the only criteria.
