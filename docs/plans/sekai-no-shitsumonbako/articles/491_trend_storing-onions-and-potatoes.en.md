# Q. Where should I keep potatoes and onions so they last? Can I eat a potato that has sprouted?｜Japan Questions #491

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-onions-and-potatoes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Potatoes and onions are staples of the Japanese kitchen, but the same failures come up again and again: "it had sprouted before I noticed", "it had turned green", "the onion had gone soft from the inside". The sprouts and green parts of a potato contain a natural toxin, and mass food poisoning has occurred in Japanese school gardens. Here we sort out where to store each vegetable and where the line between "edible" and "throw it away" lies.

## A. Keep potatoes in a cool (around 10°C), dark, well-ventilated place, and not in the fridge (cold increases the sugars, so they burn more easily when fried and produce more acrylamide). Gouge out sprouts deeply, down to the base, and peel green skin thickly. Discard potatoes that have many sprouts, are green all over, or are wrinkled and soft. Onions hate moisture, so keep them at room temperature with good airflow, for example hung in a net. New-season onions (shin-tamanegi, 新玉ねぎ), however, are high in water, so refrigerate them and use them quickly. Keeping potatoes and onions in the same container speeds up spoilage in both, because of the moisture and the gases they give off.

## Storage guide

| Vegetable | Where | Rough keeping time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Potatoes** | **Dark, cool place (around 10°C)**, in a paper bag or cardboard box to **keep out light**. In summer, wrap in newspaper and use the fridge's vegetable drawer | Room temperature (spring, autumn) **1–2 months**; fridge vegetable drawer **2–3 weeks** | **Light turns them green and raises solanine levels**. One apple in the box slows sprouting (ethylene) |
| **Onions** | **Room temperature with good airflow**. **Hang** them in a net or an old pair of tights, or lay them out so they do not touch | **1–2 months** | **Moisture makes them rot**. Never leave them in the plastic bag. Wrap cut onion in cling film and refrigerate, **3–4 days** |
| **New-season onions (shin-tamanegi)** | **Fridge vegetable drawer**, wrapped in newspaper | **1 week** | High in water; spoil within days at room temperature |
| **Sweet potatoes** | **13–15°C**, room temperature. **Refrigeration causes chilling injury** | 1 month | Sensitive to cold |

## The potato's natural toxins (solanine and chaconine)

- **Where they are found**: **in the sprouts and their bases, in skin that has turned green, and in small, immature potatoes**. **Exposure to light** turns the skin green and increases the toxin. **Damaged parts** also contain more.
- **Symptoms of poisoning**: **nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, diarrhoea and headache**, **a few minutes to a few hours** after eating. Most cases are mild, but **mass poisonings from potatoes grown and cooked in school gardens** happen repeatedly in Japan (often from eating **small, immature potatoes with the skin on**).
- **Cooking does not reduce it**: solanine-type toxins **break down very little when boiled or fried**. "It is fine once cooked" does not apply.
- **What to do**: 1) **gouge out sprouts deeply, down to the base**; 2) **peel green skin thickly**; 3) **do not eat small, immature potatoes or strongly green ones**; 4) **discard potatoes that have sprouted all over and gone soft**.
- **If it tastes bitter or acrid, do not eat it.** Bitterness is linked to the amount of toxin.

## Why potatoes do not belong in the fridge

- The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) explains that **at low temperatures (5°C or below) sugars (reducing sugars) increase**, so potatoes **burn more easily when fried or roasted, and more acrylamide (a harmful substance formed by heating) is produced**. Bringing them back to room temperature for **about a week** reduces the sugars.
- In summer (room temperature above 25°C) the vegetable drawer is unavoidable. In that case, **wrap in newspaper and loosely close a plastic bag**, and use the potatoes for **simmered or steamed dishes**.

> **[Verify]** Check the number of solanine-poisoning cases and the symptoms against MAFF and Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) materials, and the acrylamide explanation against the relevant MAFF page.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A green potato is fine once the sprouts are removed

**The green skin also contains a lot of toxin and needs to be peeled thickly.** The safe choice is to discard potatoes that are green all over, taste bitter, or are small and immature.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Cooking destroys the toxin

**Solanine-type toxins break down very little with heat.** Removing them is the only countermeasure.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Potatoes keep longest in the fridge

**Cold increases the sugars, so fried and stir-fried potatoes burn more and produce more acrylamide.** In spring and autumn, a dark place at room temperature; only in summer, the vegetable drawer, wrapped in newspaper.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Onions can stay in the bag they came in

**The moisture inside a plastic bag makes them rot.** Take them out and hang them in a net or lay them in a basket. One rotten onion spreads to its neighbours.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is handy to keep potatoes and onions in one box

**The moisture from the onions and the ethylene from the potatoes make both spoil faster.** Keep them in separate containers.
