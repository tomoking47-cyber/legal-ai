# Q. If food has gone mouldy, can I cut off the mouldy part and eat the rest?｜Japan Questions #492

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/mouldy-food-what-to-discard` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Green dots on the edge of a loaf, white fluff on a mikan (Japanese mandarin), blue spots on a cheese. It is natural to think "what a waste, I'll just cut that bit off", but mould spreads its roots (hyphae) far beyond what you can see. Some kinds also produce "mycotoxins" that survive cooking, so "cut off what you can see and it is safe" does not hold. Here we go food by food.

## A. The rule is: **soft or moist foods go in the bin entirely at the first sign of mould.** That covers bread, cooked rice, fruit (strawberries, peaches, mikan), jam, yoghurt, tofu, ham, nuts and cooked side dishes. Mould hyphae reach into places you cannot see, and the more moisture there is, the faster they spread. The exceptions are hard cheese, hard vegetables (carrots, the outer leaves of a cabbage) and dried goods: according to the US Department of Agriculture and others, these can be eaten if you cut away a generous margin of at least 2.5 cm around the mould. Mould on nuts, grains and spices can produce aflatoxin, a carcinogen that heat does not destroy, so these must always be discarded.

## Food by food

| Food | If mould appears | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Bread, cooked rice, mochi, noodles** | **Discard all of it** (including the other slices in the bag) | Porous, so hyphae spread inside. Spores have already scattered inside the bag |
| **Soft fruit: strawberries, peaches, grapes, mikan** | **Discard that piece and check the ones touching it**. Discard any that are damaged | High in water, so mould spreads fast. One mikan can spread through a whole box |
| **Hard fruit and vegetables: carrots, cabbage, peppers, apples** | **Fine if you cut away at least 2.5 cm around the mould** | Dense, so hyphae penetrate less |
| **Jam, sweet spreads other than honey, sauces** | **Discard all of it** (scooping out the mould with a spoon is not enough) | Toxins may spread through the liquid |
| **Hard cheese (Parmesan, Cheddar)** | **Fine if you cut away at least 2.5 cm around the mould**. Do not let the knife touch the mould | Hard, so hyphae penetrate less |
| **Soft cheese (cottage, cream cheese, sliced)** | **Discard all of it** | High in water |
| **Blue cheese, Camembert** | **The intended blue or white mould is edible**. Discard if other colours appear (black, pink, green fluff) | Anything other than the intended culture is a different organism |
| **Ham, bacon, sausages, cooked side dishes** | **Discard all of it** | High in water and nutrients; dangerous |
| **Nuts, grains, beans, spices, dried fruit** | **Discard all of it** | Prone to moulds that produce **aflatoxin** (strongly carcinogenic, **not destroyed by heat**) |
| **White film on the surface of miso or soy sauce** | **A white film (film yeast) can be removed and the rest used**. Discard if the mould is blue, black or fluffy | Film yeast is harmless. If you cannot tell the difference, discard |

- **Mycotoxins**: harmful substances produced by moulds; **more than 300 kinds** are known. The main ones are **aflatoxin** (nuts, grains), **patulin** (juice from damaged apples) and **deoxynivalenol** (wheat and barley). **Ordinary cooking does not break them down**, so "it is fine once toasted" does not hold.
- **Mould grows in the fridge too** (slowly, at low temperature). **In the freezer (−18°C) it does not grow**, but it does not die either.
- **Prevention**: **freeze bread if you will not finish it within 3 days**; **remove damaged fruit before storing the rest**; **take jam and other opened jars out with a clean spoon and keep them refrigerated**; **do not overfill the fridge, and wipe the inside once a month**.
- **Do not sniff mould**: inhaling spores can trigger allergic symptoms. Seal it in the bag and bin it.
- **If you have already eaten some**: a small amount rarely causes problems in a healthy person. See a doctor if you get **nausea, diarrhoea or allergic symptoms**. **Infants, older people and people with weakened immunity** should take extra care.

> **[Verify]** The "2.5 cm (1 inch)" guide is based on US Department of Agriculture (USDA FSIS) material. Check the types of mycotoxin and their heat stability against the MAFF page "Mycotoxins in food".

## Misunderstanding 1｜Cutting off the mouldy part is enough

**In soft foods the hyphae have spread into parts you cannot see.** Apart from hard cheese and hard vegetables, the rule is to discard the whole thing.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Cooking destroys mycotoxins too

**The main mycotoxins are not broken down by heat.** Mould on nuts and grains is especially dangerous; cooking does not make them edible.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Food in the fridge does not go mouldy

**It still grows, slowly, in the fridge.** For longer storage, freeze.

## Misunderstanding 4｜If something white is floating on the miso, throw it away

**A white film on the surface is film yeast, and once removed the miso is fine.** If you cannot tell it apart from blue, black or fluffy mould, discard.

## Misunderstanding 5｜I eat blue cheese, so other moulds will not hurt me

**Edible moulds are specific, selected strains.** Mould that grows on its own is a completely different thing and includes toxin-producing species.
