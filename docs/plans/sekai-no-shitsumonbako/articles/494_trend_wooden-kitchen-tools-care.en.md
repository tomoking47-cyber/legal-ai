# Q. Wooden chopping boards, cooking chopsticks and rice paddles: can I still use them once they darken? How do I care for them?｜Japan Questions #494

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/wooden-kitchen-tools-care` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Wooden chopping boards, long cooking chopsticks (saibashi, 菜箸) and lacquered bowls are fixtures of the Japanese kitchen, and so are the complaints: "it has gone dark", "it went mouldy", "it warped". Wood absorbs water, so it needs a different approach to washing and drying from plastic.

## A. Most dark staining is either mould or a reaction between compounds in the wood, iron and water. Surface staining can sometimes be removed by scrubbing with coarse salt or a scouring powder, pouring boiling water over it and standing it up to dry, but deep black mould means re-planing (by a professional, or with sandpaper) or replacement. Prevention: "wash straight after use"; "after washing, stand it upright in a well-ventilated place until completely dry"; "no chlorine bleach (it damages the wood and the smell lingers)"; "direct sunlight and the dishwasher cause warping and cracking". Dark tips on cooking chopsticks are usually mould that has soaked into the wood; if it cannot be sanded off, replace them. For lacquerware, the basics are a mild detergent and a soft sponge, wipe dry straight away, and beware of letting it dry out too much.

## Care by tool

| Tool | Washing | Drying | Never | When to replace or repair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wooden chopping board** | **Straight after use**, scrubbing brush and water (detergent after raw meat or fish). **Pour boiling water over it** (proteins set in heat, so wash with detergent first) | **Stand upright** in a well-ventilated, shaded place. **Dry both sides** | Chlorine bleach, dishwasher, direct sunlight, leaving it wet | **Re-plane when black mould is deep or knife scars are deep** (once every 2–3 years); replace if warped or cracked |
| **Cooking chopsticks, wooden spatulas** | Wash with detergent, wipe off water | Stand upright to dry | Heating with them left in the pan, dishwasher | Replace when **dark tips will not come clean, or splinters appear** (**around 1 year**) |
| **Rice paddle (shamoji), wooden pestle (surikogi)** | Rinse straight after use, before rice grains dry on | Stand upright | Resting it wet on the rice-cooker lid | Replace if stained dark or cracked |
| **Lacquerware (bowls, chopsticks)** | **Mild detergent and a soft sponge**, lukewarm water. **Do not soak** | **Wipe dry at once with a soft cloth**. Air-drying leaves water marks | Dishwasher, microwave, direct sunlight, scrubbing brushes, abrasives, long dry spells (cracks) | Re-lacquering (**by a lacquer craftsman, from a few thousand yen**) keeps it going for years |
| **Magewappa (bentwood lunch box)** | Wash in lukewarm water straight after use (unfinished wood: no detergent, or well diluted), wipe at once | **Remove the lid, stand upright, dry for at least one night**. If used daily, alternate two boxes | Microwave, dishwasher, leaving it in water | Dark stains may fade in sunlight. If it cracks, take it to a repair shop |

- **What the dark staining is**: 1) **mould** (left damp); 2) **a reaction between tannins in the wood and iron** (from the knife, or iron in tap water); 3) **pigments from food**. If it is mould, **planing the surface removes it**; if it is deep, replace.
- **Sanitising a wooden board**: **wash with detergent → pour hot water (80°C / 176°F or above) over the whole surface → stand to dry**. **Chlorine bleach stays inside the wood and causes smell and discolouration.** If you must, use **diluted oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) briefly** and rinse well.
- **Why boards warp and crack**: **one side gets wet or dries while the other does not**; **direct sunlight or heat from the hob**; **the high temperature of a dishwasher**. After washing, **wet both sides** and stand upright to dry, and it is less likely to warp.
- **Re-planing**: DIY stores, knife shops and online services re-plane boards for **about ¥1,000–3,000**; a board can be planed several times **until it is thinner than 2 cm**.
- **Oil**: a thin coat of **edible drying oil (linseed or perilla oil; olive oil oxidises and goes sticky)** on unfinished wooden boards and utensils repels water and helps prevent dark staining. **About once a month.**
- **Wood or plastic — which is more hygienic?** **Bacteria get into scratches on both.** Wood loses bacteria as it dries, but takes time to dry; plastic can be bleached, but scratches deepen more easily. **Separating boards by use (raw meat and fish versus vegetables) and drying them completely** matters more than the choice of material.

> **[Verify]** The re-planing costs and wood-sanitising methods are general trade explanations. Check them against municipal food-hygiene materials on chopping-board hygiene.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A darkened board can be whitened again with chlorine bleach

**Wood soaks up bleach, and the smell and discolouration stay.** For surface staining, plane, boiling water or coarse salt. If deep, re-plane or replace.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wooden boards are unhygienic, so switch to plastic

**Bacteria get into the scratches on both.** Wood loses bacteria as it dries; separated by use and dried completely, it can be handled much like plastic.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Boiling water gets everything off

**Pouring boiling water first after raw meat or fish sets the proteins and makes them harder to remove.** Detergent first, then boiling water.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Lacquerware is expensive, so keep it on display and not in use

**Lacquerware keeps its lustre by being used, washed and wiped; if it dries out too much it cracks.** Everyday use is the care.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cooking chopsticks with dark tips are fine once washed

**Dark tips are usually mould that has gone right into the wood, and washing does not remove it.** Realistically they are a consumable, replaced roughly once a year.
