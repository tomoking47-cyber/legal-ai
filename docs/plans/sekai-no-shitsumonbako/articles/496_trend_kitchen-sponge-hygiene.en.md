# Q. How often should I replace my kitchen sponge, and how do I sanitise it?｜Japan Questions #496

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/kitchen-sponge-hygiene` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

A washing-up sponge combines skin oils, food scraps and moisture, making it one of the kitchen items where bacteria multiply most readily. A German study reported tens of billions of bacteria per cubic centimetre. Since you use it every day, it is easier to fix numbers for how to wash it, how to dry it and when to replace it.

## A. The guide is: "wash with detergent after use, wring out hard and stand upright to dry" every day; "sanitise with boiling water or diluted chlorine bleach" once or twice a week; "replace" every 2–4 weeks. If the smell will not go, it is falling apart, or the sliminess keeps coming back, replace it regardless of the calendar. Some studies find that heating a sponge in the microwave is effective, but heating one that contains metal or is dry carries a fire risk, so we (VV) do not recommend it. A sponge used on raw meat or fish should be kept separate from the one for dishes, or sanitised the same day.

## Sponge hygiene guide

| How often | What | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **Every time (after use)** | **Wash with detergent, rinse, wring out hard, stand upright** | Rinse off food scraps under running water, knead in detergent → rinse → wring → **stand it where it drains well**. Do not leave it on the bottom of the sink |
| **Once or twice a week** | **Sanitise** | 1) **Pour hot water (80°C / 176°F or above) over it, or boil for 1 minute** (check it is heat-resistant); 2) **soak 5 minutes in diluted chlorine bleach (about 2 caps per litre of water; follow the product label)**, then rinse well; 3) **soak 30 minutes in a 40°C oxygen-bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) solution** |
| **Every 2–4 weeks** | **Replace** | Japanese hygiene bodies and manufacturers recommend **2 weeks to 1 month**. Replace at once if the smell will not go, it is falling apart, or it stays slimy |
| **Always** | **Separate by use** | At least 2–3 sponges: **dishes / chopping boards used for raw meat and fish and the sink / hob trivets and drain** |

- **Why bacteria multiply**: **moisture, nutrients (food scraps, oil) and room temperature** are all present, and **the internal foam structure is slow to dry**. A German study (2017) detected **up to about 54 billion bacteria per cm³** in used sponges, reported in the press as **more than a hospital toilet seat**. However, **most of the bacteria found were environmental species harmless to healthy people**, so this does not translate directly into "danger".
- **What does matter**: **Salmonella and Campylobacter from raw meat and fish**, **slime (biofilm)** and **the bacteria behind bad smells**. All of these are managed by **separating sponges by use and sanitising**.
- **On microwave sanitising**: studies show that **heating a wet sponge for 1–2 minutes** greatly reduces bacteria, but **dry sponges, sponges with metal parts (scouring layers, mesh) and non-heat-resistant sponges** have **caught fire or melted**. The National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan has issued warnings, and **we (VV) recommend the hot-water and bleach methods** instead.
- **If you have a dishwasher**: you can also **run the sponge through the top rack** (check it is heat-resistant).
- **Drying is the key**: **left lying flat and wet**, bacteria keep multiplying. **Stand it up, hang it, or use a sponge holder that lifts the base.** If you can, **alternate two sponges so one dries completely.**
- **Alternatives**: **silicone sponges** (dry fast, harbour fewer bacteria, but do not foam well), **cotton or linen cloths (can be laundered and boiled daily)**, **disposable non-woven cloths**.

> **[Verify]** Check the bacterial counts in Cardinale et al. (Scientific Reports, 2017) and the microwave fire cases (National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan) against the original documents. For chlorine-bleach dilution, follow the product label.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If it looks clean, it is still usable

**Bacteria and smell build up before anything is visible.** Replace every 2–4 weeks, or at once if smell or slime will not go.

## Misunderstanding 2｜There is detergent on it, so the bacteria die

**Washing-up liquid is a cleaning agent; unless a product specifically claims to sanitise, it does not kill bacteria.** Sanitise separately with hot water or bleach.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The microwave is an easy way to sanitise it

**Fires and melting have been reported.** Metal parts and dry sponges are dangerous. We (VV) recommend hot water or bleach.

## Misunderstanding 4｜One sponge for dishes, pans and the sink

**Dirt from raw meat and fish, and slime from the sink, transfer to your dishes.** At the very least, separate the dish sponge from everything else.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Soak it in bleach overnight and it is perfect

**Long soaking in chlorine bleach degrades the sponge and leaves residue that is hard to rinse out.** About 5 minutes is enough; then rinse well.
