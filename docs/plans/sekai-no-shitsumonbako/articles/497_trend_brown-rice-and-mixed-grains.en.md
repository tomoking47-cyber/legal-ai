# Q. Are brown rice and mixed grains better for you than white rice? How to cook them, and what to watch for｜Japan Questions #497

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/brown-rice-and-mixed-grains` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Convenience-store onigiri now come in "mochi-mugi" (barley) and "zakkoku" (mixed-grain) versions, and brown rice (genmai, 玄米) is an established part of Japanese eating. The image of being "healthier than white rice" is broadly right, but there are things to know: it is cooked differently, it is harder to digest, and it contains more arsenic.

## A. Brown rice and mixed grains contain more dietary fibre, B vitamins and minerals than white rice, and raise blood sugar more gently after a meal (cooked brown rice has about 4–5 times the fibre of cooked white rice). There are four conditions, though: 1) soak brown rice for at least 6 hours and cook it on the rice cooker's brown-rice setting or in a pressure cooker; 2) chew well (it is harder to digest, so start small if your stomach is sensitive); 3) brown rice contains more arsenic than white rice, so reduce it by soaking and changing the rinsing water, and do not eat it to the exclusion of everything else; 4) the oil in the germ oxidises, so refrigerate brown rice and use it up quickly. Rather than switching entirely to brown rice at once, mixing 10–20% grains or barley into white rice is generally described as the easier habit to keep up in Japan.

## Cooking and cautions

| Type | Soaking | Water | Cooking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brown rice (genmai)** | **At least 6 hours (in the fridge in summer)**. Soaking **starts the germination process and makes the rice easier to digest**, and **some arsenic leaches out** → **discard the soaking water and cook in fresh water** | **1.2–1.5 times** the amount for white rice | **Brown-rice setting** or **pressure cooker**. On the normal setting it comes out hard |
| **Partially milled rice (bu-zuki-mai: 3, 5 or 7 bu)** | 1–2 hours | Slightly more than for white rice | Normal setting works. A realistic **entry point to brown rice** |
| **Mochi-mugi, pressed barley (oshi-mugi)** | Not needed | Add **twice the volume of the barley in water** | Mix **10–30% into white rice**, normal setting |
| **Mixed-grain blends (zakkoku mix)** | Not needed (follow the label) | Add as the label says | Mix **10–20%** into white rice, normal setting |

- **On arsenic**: rice readily takes up arsenic from the soil, and **because it concentrates in the bran layer, brown rice contains about 1.5–2 times as much as white rice**. The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) states that "**there is no health concern in the normal Japanese diet**", while explaining that **rinsing thoroughly, soaking and cooking in plenty of water** reduce it. Avoid the extreme of **brown rice only, in large amounts, at every meal**; for **small children, white or partially milled rice** is the safer choice.
- **Digestion**: the **outer bran layer of brown rice is hard to digest**, and people with sensitive stomachs, older people and small children may get **diarrhoea or bloating**. **Chew well (30 times a mouthful)**, **start small**, and if it does not agree with you, move to **partially milled rice or a grain mix**.
- **Storage**: the **oil in the germ oxidises** easily, so in some respects brown rice **deteriorates faster than milled white rice**. **Airtight container in the fridge**, **within 1 month**. Cooked brown rice **freezes** well.
- **Allergen labelling**: some zakkoku blends contain **wheat, barley or buckwheat**; **if you have allergies, check the ingredients**.
- **Beta-glucan in mochi-mugi**: a soluble fibre; several studies find it **improves markers of post-meal blood sugar and cholesterol**. In Japan some products are registered as **"Foods with Function Claims" (kinōsei hyōji shokuhin, a notified health-claim category)**.

> **[Verify]** Check the nutrient figures against the relevant entries in the "Standard Tables of Food Composition in Japan 2020 (Eighth Revised Edition)", and the arsenic statements against the MAFF page "Arsenic in food".

## Misunderstanding 1｜Switch to brown rice and you will be far healthier than on white

**You get more fibre and vitamins, but if the way you eat it does not suit you, it burdens the digestive system.** Mixing it into white rice gives a worthwhile benefit too.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Brown rice cooks just like white rice

**It needs at least 6 hours' soaking and the brown-rice setting or a pressure cooker.** Cooked the ordinary way it is hard and harder to digest.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Brown rice cleanses the body of toxins

**There is no basis for this claim.** If anything, brown rice contains more arsenic than white rice, so what matters is soaking, rinsing and not eating it to the exclusion of everything else.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Mixed-grain rice is safe for everyone

**Some products contain wheat, barley or buckwheat, so anyone with allergies must check the ingredients.** Small children and people with sensitive stomachs should start small.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Brown rice keeps well

**Because the oil in the germ oxidises, in some respects it deteriorates faster than white rice.** Refrigerate, airtight, within 1 month.
