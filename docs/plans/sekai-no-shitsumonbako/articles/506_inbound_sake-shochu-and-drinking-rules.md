# Q. What should visitors know about drinking in Japan? Sake, shōchū, the legal age and where you can drink｜Japan Questions #506

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/sake-shochu-and-drinking-rules` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan is relaxed about alcohol in some ways — cans in the park, beer from vending machines, salarymen asleep on trains — and strict in others: the legal age is 20 with no exceptions, driving after a single drink is a crime, and public drinking is being restricted in tourist districts. The drinks themselves also confuse: sake is not a spirit, shōchū is, and "sake" in Japanese just means alcohol.

## A. The legal drinking age is 20 and it is enforced with ID checks in convenience stores and bars (a passport works). Drinking in public is legal but increasingly restricted — Shibuya and Shinjuku ban street drinking around Halloween and New Year, and year-round from 2024 in Shibuya's centre. Driving with any detectable alcohol is a crime for the driver and for passengers who let it happen, and there is no "one beer" allowance. On the drinks: nihonshu (sake) is a brewed rice drink at 14–16%, drunk cold, warm or hot depending on type; shōchū is a distilled spirit at 20–25% drunk with water, hot water or soda; both are inexpensive and rarely cause hangovers unless volume does.

## The law

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Legal age** | **20** for drinking and buying (Act on Prohibition of Drinking by Persons Under 20, renamed in 2022 when the age of majority fell to 18 — the drinking age did not change). **ID checks are routine**: convenience-store tills require pressing an "I am over 20" button; bars and clubs may ask for a passport |
| **Public drinking** | **Legal nationally**. Local bans: **Shibuya centre (year-round from October 2024, 18:00–05:00)**, **Shinjuku Station area (Kabukichō / Shinjuku 3-chōme) on 31 Oct–1 Nov and any other period the ward designates**, some parks during cherry-blossom season. There are no fines or penalties in either ordinance; enforcement is by patrol staff asking people to stop or move on |
| **Drink-driving** | **Zero-tolerance in practice**: **0.15 mg/L breath alcohol** is the offence threshold, roughly one small drink. **Passengers and the person who served the driver are also liable**. Penalties up to **3 years' prison or ¥500,000** for the driver (up to 5 years or ¥1,000,000 for 'drunk driving' when visibly impaired); passengers and the person who served the drinks face up to 2–3 years or ¥300,000–500,000. **Bicycles count** (see the cycling article) |
| **Public drunkenness** | Being drunk is not itself an offence, but rowdy or nuisance behaviour while drunk in public is a minor offence under the 1961 Act on Prevention of Drunken Nuisance, and police may take a heavily drunk person into protective custody; the city sees many sleeping drinkers and handles them gently |
| **Vending machines** | Alcohol vending machines exist but are **few** since the 1990s age-verification rules; most are in hotels or with ID card readers |
| **Tax-free** | Alcohol can be bought tax-free as a consumable (sealed bag, ¥5,000–500,000 per shop per day) until 31 Oct 2026; from 1 Nov 2026 Japan switches to a refund-at-departure system and the consumable/sealed-bag rules disappear — see the tax-free article; **sake and whisky are the popular souvenirs** — check your home customs allowance |

## The drinks

| Drink | What it is | ABV | How to drink |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nihonshu (sake)** | **Brewed** rice drink, not a spirit. Grades: **junmai** (rice only), **honjōzō**, **ginjō / daiginjō** (more polished rice, fruitier) | **14–16%** | Ginjō chilled (10–15°C); junmai/honjōzō **warm (40–50°C, atsukan)** in winter. Served in a small cup; **pour for others, not yourself** |
| **Shōchū** | **Distilled** from sweet potato (imo), barley (mugi), rice (kome) or brown sugar (kokutō) | **20–25%** (up to 45% for genshu) | **With hot water (oyuwari)**, ice, or soda; Kyushu's home drink |
| **Awamori** | Okinawan rice spirit | 25–43% | With water or ice |
| **Umeshu** | Plum liqueur | 10–15% | On ice or with soda |
| **Chūhai / sours** | Shōchū or vodka + soda + fruit, in cans (**3–9%**) | Cheap; **the 9% "Strong" cans are the ones that catch visitors out** |
| **Highball** | Whisky and soda — the default izakaya drink | 7–9% | — |
| **Beer** | Asahi, Kirin, Sapporo, Suntory; **happōshu and "third beer"** are cheaper low-malt versions | 5% | — |

- **Izakaya conventions**: **otōshi** — a small compulsory appetiser (¥300–500) that acts as a cover charge; **nomihōdai** — all-you-can-drink for 90–120 minutes (¥1,500–3,000); **the first round is often beer ("toriaezu bīru")**; **kanpai** before the first sip; **pour for others** and let them pour for you.
- **Hangover culture**: convenience stores sell **Hepalyse, Ukon no Chikara (turmeric)** drinks; evidence for them is thin. Water and food work better.
- **Refusing**: "I don't drink" (**nomemasen**) is accepted without pressure in most settings today; **the pressured drinking of older office culture is fading and is now called "alcohol harassment"**.
