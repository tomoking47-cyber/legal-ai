# Q. What do people get wrong about the plastic-bag charge and Japan's "my bag" habit?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #519

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/plastic-bag-charge-and-my-bag` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Since 1 July 2020 every shop in Japan must charge for plastic carrier bags, and the phrase "my bag" (a reusable shopping bag) entered daily life along with a new checkout question: "Do you need a bag?" Foreign visitors are surprised at both the small charge and the elaborate folding bags people carry; residents argue about whether it changed anything. The data is clearer than the argument.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that the charge is a tax or is optional for shops. It is a mandatory national rule — shops must charge, at a price they set (usually ¥3–5), with exemptions only for bags that are at least 25% bio-based plastic, marine-biodegradable, or thick enough to be reusable. Convenience-store bag refusal went from about 25% before the charge to 75% after, and Japan's carrier-bag consumption is estimated to have halved. Carry a folding bag, and for food learn the "my bag" etiquette: say no to the bag before the cashier packs, pack cold and hot separately, and never put raw meat unwrapped into a bag you will reuse.

## The rule and its effect

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Law** | Amendment to the **Containers and Packaging Recycling Act** ordinance; **mandatory from 1 July 2020** for all retailers, including convenience stores, kiosks and takeaways |
| **Price** | Set by the shop, **minimum ¥1**; typical **¥3 (small) to ¥5 (large)**; supermarkets ¥3–5, department stores often ¥10–20 for paper |
| **Exempt bags** | **≥ 25% biomass plastic** (many convenience stores switched and still charge), **marine-biodegradable plastic**, **thick reusable bags (≥ 50 μm)**; **paper bags and bags for wet items** (fish, meat) are not covered |
| **Effect** | Convenience-store **bag-refusal rate ~25% → ~75%** (industry data); national plastic carrier-bag use estimated **down by about half**; carrier bags were only **~2% of Japan's plastic waste** by weight, so the measure is more about habit than tonnage |
| **Compliance** | Retailers report charges; **no fines on consumers** |

## The "my bag" etiquette

- **Say it early**: "**Fukuro wa irimasen**" (no bag) or shake your head when asked "**Fukuro go-riyō desu ka?**" — before the cashier scans, because bags are added at the register.
- **Packing**: at supermarkets you **pack yourself at the sakkodai (packing counter)** after paying; **cold things together**, **eggs and bread on top**, **meat and fish in the free small clear bags** from the roll by the counter so juices don't contaminate the bag.
- **Wash the bag**: **Japanese consumer research found bacteria in reused bags**, especially where raw meat had leaked; **fabric bags weekly**, **nylon bags wiped**; **keep a separate bag for meat/fish**.
- **Convenience-store honesty**: with no bag, **put items in your bag only after paying** — shop staff are alert to bags being filled before the till; keep the receipt visible.
- **The folding bag culture**: **Shupatto-style one-pull folding bags**, **furoshiki** (see that article), **brand eco-bags** from department stores — a **market of hundreds of millions of yen**; a bag that folds to pocket size is the reason compliance is high.
- **Reusable bags and the environment**: a cotton tote must be reused **50–150 times** to beat a plastic bag on climate impact (UK and Danish life-cycle studies) — **use the one you have, don't collect them**.
- **Other packaging**: the charge did not touch **individual wrapping** (the famous single-wrapped biscuits); from **April 2022 the Plastic Resource Circulation Act** asks shops to reduce **spoons, straws, cutlery, hotel amenities** — convenience stores now ask before adding a spoon.
