# Q. Should women shave facial hair? Effects on the skin, and how to do it｜Japan Questions #523

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/facial-shaving-for-women` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Fine hair around the eyebrows, above the lip, on the cheeks. In Japan, women have shaved their faces with a razor for generations, and "ladies' shaving" at barber shops is an established service — something that surprises many visitors. You will hear all sorts of claims: "shaving makes hair grow back thicker", "make-up sits better afterwards". Here we look at what it does to the skin and, if you do shave, how to do it.

## A. There is no medical problem with shaving facial hair, and shaving does not make hair grow back thicker (the cut end is flat, so it only looks thicker). But facial skin is thin, and a razor also removes part of the outer skin layer (stratum corneum). The conditions for not damaging the skin are: shave no more than once every 2–3 weeks, always glide the blade over soap or shaving foam, move in one direction along the hair growth, and moisturise afterwards. Do not shave over acne, rashes or freshly sunburnt skin. Freshly shaved skin is vulnerable to UV, so do not forget sunscreen.

## Effects and method

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Does hair grow back thicker?** | **No.** Thickness and number of hairs are decided at the root, and shaving does not change them. After shaving, **the cut end is flat, so the hair looks thicker**, and **the tip is no longer rounded, so it feels prickly** — that is all |
| **Effect on the skin** | A razor **removes part of the stratum corneum (dead skin cells) along with the hair.** Over-shaving or shaving too often causes **dryness, redness and stinging.** At a sensible frequency, some people feel that "the old dead cells come off and make-up sits better" |
| **Frequency** | **Once every 2–3 weeks** as a guide. **Weekly or more** is too hard on the stratum corneum |
| **Tools** | **A guarded face razor (L-shaped, 顔用カミソリ, sold at any drugstore such as Matsumoto Kiyoshi or Welcia)** or **an electric shaver for the face.** **Body T-razors have sharp blades and are unsuitable.** Replace the blade **after 3–5 uses** |
| **Steps** | ① Wash your face so it is clean ② Apply **shaving foam or soap lather** (never shave dry skin) ③ **One direction, along the hair growth**, light pressure ④ Rinse with lukewarm water ⑤ **Moisturise well with toner (化粧水) and lotion (乳液)** ⑥ **Sunscreen during the day** |
| **Skin to avoid** | **Acne, eczema, fresh sunburn, just after a peel.** Do not shave over **moles or warts** |
| **Time of day** | **Evening** is safest (freshly shaved skin is vulnerable to UV and cosmetics sting more easily) |

- **Eyebrows**: shape them with **brow scissors and tweezers**; use the razor only on the fine hair around the brows. **Shaving inside the brow can stop hair growing back** (disrupted growth cycle), so pluck and shave as little as possible.
- **Barber-shop shaving**: a **face shave (顔そり, kaosori) by a licensed barber (理容師, riyōshi)** is a distinctly Japanese service, costing **¥3,000–6,000**. The razor handling is professional, and many people find it **gentler on the skin than shaving at home.** It is a classic **pre-wedding** ritual.
- **Hair-removal creams**: **many are not for use on the face** (check the label). They are alkaline and irritating.
- **Light and laser hair removal**: fine facial hair contains little melanin, so **light-based (IPL) removal works poorly** on it. Medical laser removal can deal with it but needs several sessions.
- **Why it "feels thicker"**: besides the flat cut end, **hairs in their growth phase come through at the same time**, so density looks higher for a while. It returns to normal in **2–3 weeks**.

> **[Verify]** "Shaving does not make hair thicker" is a standard dermatological explanation; check the relevant section of the Japanese Dermatological Association Q&A. Barber-shop shaving prices are a guide only.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Shaving makes hair grow thicker

**Hair thickness is decided at the root and shaving does not change it.** The flat cut end only looks thicker, and it returns to normal as it grows.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Make-up sits better, so shave every week

**Weekly or more removes too much of the stratum corneum and causes dryness and redness.** Once every 2–3 weeks is enough.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A body razor is fine for the face too

**Body T-razors have sharp blades and are unsuitable for thin facial skin.** Use a guarded face razor or an electric shaver.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A quick dry shave saves time

**A blade on dry skin scrapes off the stratum corneum and causes stinging.** Always use lather.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Putting nothing on afterwards is kinder to the skin

**Freshly shaved skin has a weakened barrier and needs moisturiser and sunscreen.** They prevent dryness, redness and dark spots.
