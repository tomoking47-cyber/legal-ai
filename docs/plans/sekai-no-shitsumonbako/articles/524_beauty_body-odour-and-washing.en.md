# Q. Body odour and "ageing odour": how should I wash, and can over-washing backfire?｜Japan Questions #524

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/body-odour-and-washing` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Kareishū" (加齢臭, ageing odour) is a term that spread in Japan around 2000, after a sebum component called nonenal, which increases from the 40s onwards, was identified as the substance responsible. You will see it on drugstore shelves and hear it in conversation. Sweat odour, armpit odour (wakiga), ageing odour, "middle-age greasy odour" — each has a different cause, and the way to wash is different too.

## A. Most body odour comes not from sebum or sweat itself but from substances produced when the skin's resident bacteria break them down. The four basics are: ① wash the places where odour forms most (head, behind the ears, neck, chest, centre of the back, armpits) with soap; ② do not scrub with a nylon wash towel — it strips the outer skin layer and increases sebum, so it backfires; use your hands or a cotton towel; ③ dry off after washing; ④ change your pillowcase and underwear every day. For ageing odour there are washes marketed with antioxidant ingredients or "anti-nonenal" claims, but the root is a lifestyle that limits sebum oxidation (too much fat and alcohol, too little sleep). Wakiga is a matter of constitution; if antiperspirant is not enough, it is a job for a dermatology or plastic-surgery clinic.

## Types of odour and what causes them

| Type | Typical age | Substance | Where it forms | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sweat odour** | All ages | **Eccrine sweat** (almost all water) broken down by skin bacteria into **short-chain fatty acids** and so on | Whole body, feet | Wipe often, change clothes, breathable fabrics |
| **Wakiga (axillary osmidrosis)** | Puberty onwards | **Apocrine sweat** (containing fats and proteins) broken down by bacteria. **Hereditary constitution** (**about 10–15%** of Japanese people) | Armpits, around the nipples, groin | Antiperspirant (aluminium chloride etc.), removing armpit hair, dermatology or plastic-surgery clinic |
| **"Middle-age greasy odour" (ミドル脂臭)** | **Mid-30s to 50s** | **Diacetyl** (lactic acid in sweat broken down by bacteria) plus sebum. **Smells like old cooking oil** | **Back of the head, nape** | Wash the scalp, change the pillowcase |
| **Ageing odour (加齢臭)** | **50s onwards** | **Nonenal** (fatty acids in sebum oxidised and broken down). **Smells like old books or candles** | **Chest, centre of the back, behind the ears, neck** | Limit sebum oxidation (lifestyle), wash those areas |
| **Fatigue odour (疲労臭)** | All ages | **Ammonia** (the liver cannot keep up) | Whole body | Sleep, rest, less alcohol |

- **How to wash**: **lather soap or body wash and use your hands or a cotton towel.** **Scrubbing with a nylon towel (ナイロンタオル, the rough wash towels sold in every Japanese supermarket) strips the stratum corneum, sebum is over-produced, and the odour can get worse.** **Left-over soap** (behind the ears, back of the neck, back) causes odour. **Once a day** is enough; after sweating, a rinse in the shower is fine.
- **Head**: for both middle-age odour and ageing odour, the **scalp** is a common source. **Rinse first → wash the scalp with your fingertips → rinse well → dry straight away** (sleeping with wet hair lets bacteria multiply).
- **Clothes and bedding**: **much of the odour is oxidised sebum stuck to clothing.** **Underwear and pillowcases daily**; **soak yellowed collars and underarms in oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) at 40°C (104°F).** The **musty smell of laundry dried indoors** is a different bacterium (Moraxella) and needs a clothing-side fix (see our separate article).
- **Diet and lifestyle**: **too much fat and animal protein, alcohol, smoking, lack of sleep and stress** push sebum oxidation and ammonia odour up. **Vegetables and fruit (antioxidants)** and **moderate exercise (keeps sweat glands working)** push the other way.
- **Antiperspirants and deodorants**: a combination of **an antiperspirant (sweat-suppressing) ingredient and an antibacterial ingredient.** Apply to **clean, dry skin** (see our separate article on antiperspirants).
- **Hard to judge on yourself**: you get used to your own smell, so people **ask family** or **put a worn undershirt in a bag and smell it a few hours later.**
- **When to see a doctor**: **wakiga that interferes with daily life** (surgery is available under health insurance); **a sudden strong ammonia or sweet smell** (possible liver disease, diabetes or other illness).

> **[Verify]** The proportion of Japanese people with wakiga and the explanations of nonenal and diacetyl are general descriptions based on research announcements by cosmetics makers. Check against material from the Japanese Dermatological Association and related societies.

## Misunderstanding 1｜I worry about smell, so I scrub hard with a nylon towel

**It strips the stratum corneum, increases sebum, and can make things worse.** Lather and hands, gently, and rinse thoroughly.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Everyone gets ageing odour equally as they get older

**How much sebum oxidises differs with lifestyle.** Fat, alcohol, sleep and exercise leave room for change.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sweat itself smells

**Sweat is almost odourless; the smell appears when skin bacteria break it down.** Wiping, washing and drying to limit the bacteria is the answer.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Perfume or a body spray is enough to mask it

**If you do not deal with the source (sebum, bacteria, clothing), the smells can mix into something more unpleasant.** Washing and clothing come first.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Wakiga means someone is unclean

**Wakiga is a constitution — the number of apocrine sweat glands — and has nothing to do with cleanliness.** If antiperspirant is not enough, you can consult a dermatology or plastic-surgery clinic (some procedures are covered by health insurance).
