# Q. Red face and flushed cheeks: can cosmetics help, and when should I see a dermatologist?｜Japan Questions #528

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/facial-redness` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Redness on the cheeks and nose that will not go away; a face that turns crimson the moment it gets a little warm. "Akaragao" (赤ら顔, red face) is a complaint you hear both at Japanese cosmetics counters and in dermatology clinics. The causes range from thin skin with visible blood vessels to rosacea and dermatitis, and what cosmetics can do about it is limited. Here is how to tell them apart, and when a clinic visit is the shorter route.

## A. Redness has four broad causes: ① a constitution in which the skin is thin, vessels show through and dilate easily with temperature changes; ② sensitive or dry skin with a weakened barrier; ③ irritation from face washing, scrubs, peels and the like; ④ skin conditions such as rosacea, seborrhoeic dermatitis or contact dermatitis. ①, ② and ③ usually ease with "less irritation (no over-washing, no rubbing, no hot water) plus moisturiser plus UV protection", and that is as far as cosmetics go. If you have persistent flushing, bumps or visible vessels, peeling or itching, ④ is possible, and a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance) is a shorter route than switching cosmetics. Japan now has clinical guidelines for rosacea (2023), and medicines can be prescribed.

## Causes of redness and what to do

| Cause | Features | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Constitution — vessels show through, temperature changes** | Goes red at once with heat, cold or nerves. **Fades when you cool down.** Common in people with thin or fair skin | Avoid temperature swings (loosen your scarf before walking into a heated room, for example), low-irritation skincare. **Green-tinted primer** to cancel the colour |
| **Dryness, weakened barrier (sensitive skin)** | Flakiness, stinging, toner stings | **Wash no more than twice a day, lukewarm water, no rubbing**, **moisturiser (ceramides etc.)**, **sunscreen.** Do not add new products |
| **Irritation from skincare** | Started after a scrub, peel, high-strength vitamin C, retinol or a high-alcohol toner | **Stop that product and watch for 2–4 weeks.** If it does not settle, see a clinic |
| **Rosacea (酒さ, shusa)** | **Persistent redness in the centre of the cheeks, nose, chin and forehead**, **flushing**, **visible fine vessels**, **acne-like bumps** (pustules). **Common in women in their 30s to 50s.** Worsened by **sun, alcohol, spicy food, hot baths and stress** | **Dermatology clinic.** Japan published the **"Guidelines for the Treatment of Acne Vulgaris and Rosacea 2023"** in 2023 (it includes a chapter on rosacea); **topical drugs (metronidazole gel, ivermectin cream)** and others are used. Cosmetics do not settle it |
| **Seborrhoeic dermatitis** | Redness and **flaking** at the **sides of the nose, between the brows and along the hairline**, itching | Dermatology clinic (antifungal or topical steroid) |
| **Contact dermatitis (allergic reaction)** | Redness, itching and small bumps matching exactly where a product was used | Stop the product; patch test at a dermatology clinic |
| **Long-term topical steroid use** | Redness and dilated vessels after prolonged steroid use on the face | **Do not stop on your own.** Talk to the prescribing doctor |

- **What cosmetics can do**: **add no irritation** (no over-washing, no rubbing, avoid alcohol, fragrance and scrubs), **support the barrier with moisturiser**, **avoid UV** (both rosacea and sensitive skin worsen in sunlight), **cover redness with a complementary colour** (green primer or colour corrector). **No cosmetic is permitted the claim "improves redness".**
- **What not to do**: **washing more, peeling or splashing cold water to "tighten" in an attempt to get rid of redness** — all of these irritate and make it worse. **Hot-water washing, long baths and saunas** also dilate the vessels.
- **When to see a dermatologist**: **redness lasting several weeks or more**, **frequent flushing**, **bumps or pus**, **fine vessels appearing**, **peeling or itching**, **no change after switching cosmetics.** With rosacea, **the earlier therapy starts, the less the vessel changes are described as progressing.**
- **Lifestyle advice for rosacea (as listed in the guidelines)**: **sunscreen (physical-filter types are gentler)**, **limit alcohol, spicy food and hot food and drink**, **avoid sudden temperature changes**, **low-irritation skincare.**

> **[Verify]** Check the publication year of the rosacea clinical guidelines (2023), the recommended drugs and the typical age of onset against Japanese Dermatological Association material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A red face can be fixed with cosmetics

**Cosmetics can reduce irritation, moisturise, protect from UV and cover — and no more.** Persistent redness, bumps and visible vessels belong to the dermatologist.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Redness is poor circulation, so massage it away

**Most redness is dilated blood vessels, and the friction of massage makes it worse.** Do not touch, do not rub.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Washing with cold water makes redness go down

**The vessels shrink briefly, then rebound and dilate more easily, and the cold itself is a stress on the skin.** Use lukewarm water.

## Misunderstanding 4｜I assumed it was acne and I'm using an over-the-counter acne product

**Bumps with flushing, centred on the cheeks and nose, may be rosacea**, and acne products will not improve it. Get a diagnosis at a dermatology clinic.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A red face is just my constitution, so I have to live with it

**Even when it is constitutional, managing irritation, temperature swings and UV changes how it looks.** Rosacea has prescription medicines, and for visible vessels, laser therapy at a cosmetic dermatology clinic is another option.
