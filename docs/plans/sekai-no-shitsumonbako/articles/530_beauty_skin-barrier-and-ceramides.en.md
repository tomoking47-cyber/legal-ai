# Q. What is the "skin barrier", and do ceramide cosmetics work?｜Japan Questions #530

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/skin-barrier-and-ceramides` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Not a day goes by in Japan without a cosmetics advert saying "supports the barrier function" or "contains ceramides" — the words appear on packaging in every drugstore. What do they refer to, and is there any point in applying ceramides? We separate the dermatologist's explanation from what a cosmetic is legally allowed to claim in Japan.

## A. The skin barrier is the work done by the outermost layer of the skin, the stratum corneum (角層, about 0.02 mm thick): it limits water loss and keeps out irritants, microbes and allergens. About half of the "intercellular lipids" that fill the gaps between its cells are ceramides, and people with dry skin or atopic dermatitis are known to have less ceramide. Ceramides in cosmetics are meant to fill those gaps for a while and reduce water loss, which makes them a reasonable choice of moisturiser. But as a cosmetic their standing is "moisturises"; the claims "repairs the barrier" and "improves atopic dermatitis" are not permitted. The first step in protecting the barrier is not "apply ceramide" but "do not over-wash, do not rub".

## What the barrier is made of

| Element | Role | What damages it | How to protect it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stratum corneum** (**about 0.02 mm** thick, **about 15–20 layers** of cells) | Limits water loss, blocks external irritants | **Over-washing, rubbing, hot water, too much peeling** | Wash no more than twice a day, lukewarm water, no rubbing |
| **Intercellular lipids** (**about 50% ceramides**, cholesterol, free fatty acids) | Fill the gaps between cells and hold water in between | Washed out by cleansing agents, age (**ceramide falls with age**), dry environments | Gentle face wash, moisturiser (ceramides, petroleum jelly etc.) |
| **Natural moisturising factor (NMF)** | Holds water inside the cells (amino acids, urea, lactic acid etc.) | Over-washing, UV | As above, plus UV protection |
| **Sebum film** | Oily film on the surface; slows water loss | Over-washing, alcohol | Do not over-wash |
| **Mildly acidic (pH 4.5–6)** | Keeps the resident bacteria balanced and the enzymes working | Strongly alkaline soap | Mildly acidic to neutral face wash |

- **When the barrier breaks down**: **transepidermal water loss (TEWL) rises and the skin dries out**, and **irritants and allergens get in more easily, causing itching and redness.** A vicious circle of **dry → scratch → more damage.**
- **Types of ceramide**: **human-type ceramides (listed as Ceramide NP, AP, EOP and so on)** have the same structure as the skin's own and are **the most studied for moisturising.** **Plant-derived and pseudo-ceramides** have a different structure but work as moisturisers. There is no obligation to declare the amount; **the earlier an ingredient appears on the list, the more there is** (ingredients at 1% or below may be in any order).
- **What cosmetics can do**: **add water and lipids to the stratum corneum and reduce water loss** (that is, moisturise). **"Improves or repairs the barrier function" and "works on atopic dermatitis" are not permitted claims for cosmetics**, and an advert that says so breaches the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law). Even a quasi-drug (a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines) may only go as far as "prevents rough skin".
- **How to use a moisturiser**: **within 5–10 minutes of bathing**, **a generous amount** (see the FTU guide in our separate article on moisturising the body), **press in, do not rub.** **A cream or lotion containing ceramides** is a reasonable choice for dry skin. **Petroleum jelly (ワセリン, waserin — sold cheaply at any drugstore)** adds no ingredients but strongly limits water loss, at low cost.
- **If you have atopic dermatitis**: **skincare (moisturising) is written into the Japanese Dermatological Association guidelines as one of the pillars of care.** But that care is **a combination of doctor-prescribed drugs (topical steroids, tacrolimus etc.) and moisturiser**; ceramide cosmetics alone cannot stand in for it.
- **A barrier-friendly lifestyle**: **indoor humidity 40–60%**, **UV protection**, **sleep**, **no nylon wash towel**, **do not add several new cosmetics at once.**

> **[Verify]** The thickness and layer count of the stratum corneum and the ceramide share of the intercellular lipids are standard figures from dermatology. Check the skincare section of the 2021 Atopic Dermatitis Clinical Guidelines.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Apply ceramide and a broken barrier goes back to normal

**Ceramide in a cosmetic is a moisturiser that fills the gaps in the stratum corneum for a while and reduces water loss.** "Repair" is not a permitted cosmetic claim, and unless you stop over-washing and rubbing, what you add washes out again.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The barrier is something skincare builds

**The barrier is a structure the skin builds itself; the job of cosmetics is "do not damage it, top it up".** The biggest measure is not over-washing and not rubbing.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Any product with ceramide is the same

**The type (human-type, plant-derived, pseudo) and the amount differ, and there is no obligation to declare the amount.** Judge by the position on the ingredient list and by how your skin responds.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Atopic dermatitis can be fixed with ceramide cosmetics

**Care for atopic dermatitis is a combination of dermatologist-prescribed drugs and moisturiser.** Moisturising matters, but a cosmetic alone is not medical care.

## Misunderstanding 5｜To strengthen the barrier, strip dead skin and speed up turnover

**The stratum corneum is the barrier itself; removing it with peels or scrubs weakens the barrier.** The drier or more sensitive your skin, the more you should hold back on "stripping" care.
