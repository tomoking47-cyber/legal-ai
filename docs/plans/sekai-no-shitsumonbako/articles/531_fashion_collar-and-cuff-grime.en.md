# Q. Why won't the dark grime on shirt collars and cuffs come out in a normal wash?｜Japan Questions #531

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/collar-and-cuff-grime` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Only the collar and cuffs of your work shirt or school uniform turn dark, and the washing machine does not shift them. In Japan this is such a standard complaint that "collar-and-cuff pre-wash" (襟袖用部分洗い, eri-sode-yō bubun-arai) is its own detergent category in every drugstore (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.). Here is why the grime stays, and the pre-wash routine Japanese households use.

## A. Collar and cuff grime is sebum, dust and dead skin that has oxidised and hardened deep in the fibres. A short machine cycle in weak detergent solution cannot break it down. You need a **pre-wash step**: ① apply liquid detergent or a collar-and-cuff detergent neat, ② leave 5–10 minutes, ③ rub gently with your fingers or a toothbrush, ④ put the shirt straight into the machine. For yellowing that has oxidised, dissolve oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) in water at 40–50°C (104–122°F) and soak for 30 minutes to an hour. Prevention is "wash after every wear" plus sweat-absorbing collar tape or a collar protection spray. Grime left for months has set inside the fibres, and there is a limit to what a household can do.

## What the grime is, and how to remove it

| Stage | Condition | How to remove |
|---|---|---|
| **After one wear (fresh sebum)** | Invisible, but sebum is there | **A normal machine wash removes it.** Skip the wash and it moves to the next stage |
| **Worn several times, or left a few days (oxidising)** | Faint yellow or grey | **Apply neat liquid detergent, leave 5–10 minutes** → rub → wash |
| **Grime has set** | A clear dark band | **Pre-wash with a collar-and-cuff detergent or a bar of laundry soap** → **soak 30 minutes to 1 hour in oxygen bleach at 40–50°C** → wash |
| **Left for months** | Hardened deep in the fibres | Repeat the above 2–3 times. **If it does not come out, take it to a dry cleaner and say "collar grime" (襟の黒ずみ, eri no kurozumi).** It may never fully recover |

- **Why the machine cannot do it**: the detergent in a machine wash is a weak solution of **around 0.1%**, and the wash lasts **about 10 minutes**. Collar sebum has **oxidised and hardened inside the fibres**, and those conditions cannot break it down. **Applying neat detergent and giving it time** lets the surfactants and enzymes reach the dirt.
- **Pre-wash tools**: **neat liquid detergent**; **a collar-and-cuff detergent** (¥200–400 at a drugstore); **a bar of laundry soap** (洗濯石けん, sentaku sekken — wet it and rub it on; an old method that works well); **washing-up liquid** (sebum is oil, so it works). **Do not use chlorine bleach** (塩素系漂白剤, enso-kei hyōhakuzai): it yellows and damages fabric and cannot be used on colours.
- **Temperature**: **sebum loosens most easily at 30–40°C (86–104°F)**, so soak and pre-wash in lukewarm water. **Oxygen bleach works best at 40–50°C.**
- **Rubbing**: **keep it gentle to protect the fabric.** Do not scrub. **Pinch and knead with your fingers**, or **tap with a soft toothbrush**.
- **Prevention**: **wash after one wear** (before the sebum oxidises); **sweat-absorbing tape inside the collar** (¥300–500); **a collar-and-cuff spray that stops sebum sticking**; and the old trick of **dusting baby powder inside the collar before wearing**. The main culprits are **sebum from the back of the neck and hair product**, so **wash your neck and keep styling products off the collar**.
- **School uniforms**: worn **every day but washed only at the weekend**, so grime sets easily. **Pre-wash just the collar on a weekday and let it dry**, or **rotate two uniforms**.
- **Yellowing vs. dark grime**: **yellowing = oxidised sebum**; **dark grime = sebum with dust and exhaust particles stuck to it**. Both sit on a sebum base, so the method is the same.

> **[Verify]** The detergent concentration in a wash, the temperature at which sebum loosens and the ideal temperature for oxygen bleach are general figures given by the detergent industry. Check them against the Japan Soap and Detergent Association's materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Add more detergent and the machine will get it out

**More detergent only makes the whole wash slightly stronger; it never reaches the hardened sebum on the collar.** Neat detergent applied directly and left to work is what does it.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Give it a proper bleaching with chlorine bleach

**Chlorine bleach does little against sebum itself, damages fibres, strips colour and can cause yellowing.** Oxygen bleach is the one for sebum yellowing.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Grime is dirt, so scrubbing hard gets it out

**Hard scrubbing roughens the fabric and can push dirt deeper into the fibres.** Neat detergent, time, and a gentle rub.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Wearing a shirt several times before washing is kinder to the fabric

**Sebum starts to oxidise from the day you wear it, and gets harder to remove the longer it sits.** Washing after every wear is what makes the shirt last.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Collar-and-cuff detergents cost more because of special ingredients

**They are concentrated surfactants and enzymes; neat liquid detergent or a bar of soap does the same job.** The difference is convenience.
