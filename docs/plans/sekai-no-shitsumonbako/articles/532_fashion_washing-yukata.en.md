# Q. Can I wash a yukata at home? How do I fold and store it?｜Japan Questions #532

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-yukata` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

A yukata is the light cotton kimono worn to summer festivals and fireworks, and handed out as nightwear at ryokan (Japanese inns). Because most people wear one only a few times a year, a common mistake is to put it away still full of sweat, without knowing how to wash it. Most cotton yukata can be washed at home, and once you learn the folding, care is simple.

## A. A cotton or cotton-linen yukata can be washed at home if the care label (Japan uses the international JIS L0001 symbols) shows a wash symbol. Fold the sleeves onto the body ("sode-datami"), put it in a laundry net, and wash with a neutral delicates detergent on the gentle (hand-wash / "dry") course. Spin for only 30 seconds to 1 minute, smooth the creases by hand while still wet, and dry in the shade on a kimono hanger (or thread the sleeves onto a laundry pole). Iron while still slightly damp, with a pressing cloth. Shibori (tie-dye), pure silk and anything labelled "do not wash" go to a dry cleaner. Shijira-ori and other textured cotton weaves can be washed at home once you have checked the care label, but they shrink easily, so keep the spin short. A yukata stored with sweat in it develops yellowing, mould and moth damage, so always wash it at the end of the season and store it wrapped in tatōshi (kimono storage paper).

## Yukata fabrics and how to wash them

| Fabric | Home washing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cotton (combed cotton, fancy weaves)** | **Yes** (check the label) | **Indigo and dark colours bleed; wash alone the first time** |
| **Cotton-linen** | **Yes** | Linen shrinks and creases. Short spin, shape it while wet |
| **Shibori tie-dye (e.g. Arimatsu-Narumi shibori)** | **Dry cleaner recommended** | Washing flattens the puckered texture. If you wash at home, **never stretch it; dry in the shade still crinkled** |
| **Polyester** | **Yes.** Dries fast and resists creasing | Heat-sensitive. Iron on low |
| **Pure silk / high-end pieces** | **Dry cleaner** (a kimono specialist) | Risk of colour bleeding and shrinking at home |

## How to wash a cotton yukata

1. **Check**: the care label, and do a **colour-bleed test** (wet a hidden spot and press with a white cloth). **The obi (sash) is separate**; most cannot be washed.
2. **Fold**: use **sode-datami** (sleeves folded onto the body to make a rectangle) and put it in a **laundry net**. Loose in the drum, it loses shape and tangles.
3. **Wash**: **neutral delicates detergent** (おしゃれ着用洗剤, oshareki-yō senzai — Emal, Acron, etc.), **water at 30°C (86°F) or below**, **hand-wash / "dry" course (gentle flow)**. For sweat yellowing, **apply neat detergent and wait 5 minutes** (collar, cuffs, back).
4. **Spin**: **30 seconds to 1 minute.** Longer sets the creases.
5. **Dry**: **while still wet, slap the creases out by hand** and **pull along the seams to shape it**. Use a **kimono hanger** (¥500–1,500), or **thread both sleeves onto a laundry pole so the shoulder line follows the pole**. **Dry in the shade** (sun fades the colour). Takes **half a day to a day**.
6. **Iron**: **while slightly damp, with a pressing cloth, on medium**. If it has dried fully, mist it with a spray bottle. Iron **along the seams and folds** to keep the shape.

- **Folding for storage (hon-datami)**: ① fold in half along the centre-back seam, ② match the side seams and fold the body over, ③ fold the sleeves back onto the body, ④ fold the length in half or in thirds. **The rule is that folds follow the seams**; get this right and the yukata comes out of storage almost crease-free. Videos and the diagrams in ryokan rooms help.
- **The obi**: **many hanhaba obi (narrow sashes) in cotton or synthetics can be washed**, but **stiffened or silk obi go to the cleaner**. After wearing, **stretch it out fully and dry in the shade**, then **store rolled** (folding leaves marks).
- **Storage**: **wash at the end of the season and dry completely** → **wrap in tatōshi (kimono paper)** or a breathable cloth → **store with one kind of moth repellent only**, **somewhere dry**. **Once or twice a year, take it out on a low-humidity day and air it** (see our article on doyō-boshi, the summer airing of clothes).
- **Left with sweat in it until next year**, a yukata develops **yellowing, mould and moth holes** that a household usually cannot reverse.
- **Ryokan yukata** are nightwear and **cut differently from a kimono-style yukata** (the obi and length work differently too). The inn washes them, so there is no need to take one home to wash.

> **[Verify]** The washing method follows general guidance from the kimono and dry-cleaning trades. Check the handling of shibori yukata with the producing region's association.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A yukata is a kimono, so only a dry cleaner can wash it

**Cotton and cotton-linen yukata can be washed at home if the label allows.** The ones that cannot are shibori, pure silk and anything labelled "do not wash".

## Misunderstanding 2｜I only wore it a few times, so I can put it away unwashed

**Sweat is absorbed even if you cannot see it, and turns into yellowing, mould and moth holes by next year.** Always wash and dry fully at the end of the season.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Fold it like any other garment and put it in the machine

**Loose in the drum it loses shape and tangles, and the fabric suffers.** Sode-datami, then the net.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A thorough spin means faster drying

**A long spin sets the creases.** 30 seconds to 1 minute, then smoothing by hand while wet, also saves ironing.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Iron only once it is completely dry

**Cotton yukata press better while still slightly damp.** If it has dried, mist it and use a pressing cloth.
