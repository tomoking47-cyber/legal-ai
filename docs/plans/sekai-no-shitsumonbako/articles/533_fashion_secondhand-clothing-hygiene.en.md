# Q. Should I wash secondhand clothes before wearing them? What about smells and mites?｜Japan Questions #533

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/secondhand-clothing-hygiene` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Buying clothes on Mercari (Japan's biggest flea-market app), in reuse chains or in vintage shops is now completely normal in Japan. Some people worry that anything not new is unhygienic; others wear it straight from the bag. Here are the real risks, and how Japanese households deal with a secondhand purchase.

## A. The rule for secondhand clothes is "wash once before wearing". The things worth worrying about are the previous owner's sebum and sweat, dust and musty or mothball smells from storage, and — rarely — mite or bedbug (トコジラミ, tokojirami) eggs. Wash anything washable according to the care label, ideally at 40°C (104°F) or above or with a hot tumble-dry. Steam or use a deodorising spray and shade-dry what cannot be washed; wipe and dry leather. If bedbugs are a concern, 30 minutes on a hot tumble-dry (60°C / 140°F or above) is the method usually recommended. Storage smells mostly come out with a bicarbonate-of-soda soak, oxygen bleach and fresh air. The infection risk from normal use is low, and washing lowers it further.

## Care after buying

| Item | Care | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Cotton or synthetic tops and bottoms** | **Wash according to the care label.** If possible **40°C or above**, or a **hot tumble-dry**. For strong smells, **soak 1 hour in bicarbonate of soda (1 tablespoon per litre of water) or oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) at 40–50°C (104–122°F)** before washing | Removes sebum, dust and odour; deals with mites |
| **Knitwear and wool** | **Delicates wash (30°C / 86°F or below, neutral detergent)** or **dry cleaner**. **Check for moth holes** | Any moth eggs come out in the wash |
| **Jackets and coats (not washable)** | **Dry cleaner**, or **steam all over (iron or garment steamer) and dry in the shade**, plus **deodorising spray** | Odour and surface dust |
| **Leather and suede** | **Wipe with a dry cloth, leather cleaner, then conditioning cream.** **Dry in the shade** | Removes mould and odour |
| **Shoes** | **Remove and replace the insoles**, **wipe the inside with alcohol**, **antibacterial spray**, dry in the sun | Removes foot bacteria and odour |
| **Bags** | **Empty out debris and wipe the inside.** Wash fabric bags according to the label | — |
| **Hats and scarves** | Wash according to the label, or **steam and shade-dry** | The scalp produces a lot of sebum |

- **Bedbugs (トコジラミ, tokojirami)**: in recent years Japan has seen outbreaks via hotels and secondhand goods. **Eggs, nymphs and adults all die at 60°C or above**, so use **30 minutes on a hot tumble-dry**, or **run an iron or steamer along the seams**. **Freezing (−18°C / 0°F for 4 days or more)** also works. For peace of mind, **bring the clothes home still in their plastic bag and deal with them before spreading them around the house**.
- **Mites**: the mites in fabric are mostly house-dust mites, which **do not bite**, but their droppings and remains are allergens. **They wash out**, and **die at 50°C (122°F) or above**.
- **Musty and storage smells** (the typical vintage-shop odour): **residual mothball smell (naphthalene)** and **trapped damp**. **Two or three days in a well-ventilated shady spot**, **a bicarbonate-of-soda soak**, **oxygen bleach** or **steam** removes most of it. If not, take it to a dry cleaner and say "smell" (におい, nioi).
- **Infection**: **the infection risk from ordinary secondhand clothes is generally described as low**, and washing lowers it further. **Athlete's foot (a fungal infection) can be passed on via shoes and socks**, so clean the inside of shoes and replace the insoles.
- **What to check when buying**: **yellowing at the underarms and collar** (hard to remove), **tiny moth holes** (knitwear), **spots of mould**, **a strong mothball smell** (wool and cashmere are often long-stored), **loose seams**, **sticky leather (hydrolysis of synthetic leather = end of its life)**.
- **If you are the seller**: **wash and dry before listing** is the basic etiquette of Japan's flea-market culture. **Writing "washed" (洗濯済み, sentaku-zumi)** improves your ratings.

> **[Verify]** The kill temperature and freezing conditions for bedbugs, and the kill temperature for mites, follow general pest-control materials. Check with municipal or Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare sources.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If it is labelled "dry-cleaned", I need not wash it

**Since being cleaned it has been tried on and stored on the shop floor, picking up smells and dust.** Wash it once at home, or steam it and dry in the shade.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Secondhand clothes are unhygienic and dangerous

**The infection risk from normal use is low, and washing lowers it further.** What matters is sebum, odour and insect eggs, and household methods handle all three.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Sunlight takes the smell out

**Sunlight fades colour; mothball and musty smells come out with ventilation and washing.** Shade-dry plus a soak.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Bedbugs are a problem abroad, not in Japan

**Reports inside Japan have risen in recent years.** 30 minutes on a hot tumble-dry, or freezing, deals with them reliably.

## Misunderstanding 5｜A synthetic-leather bag or shoe can be polished back to life

**Synthetic leather (polyurethane) starts to hydrolyse a few years after manufacture; stickiness and peeling cannot be reversed.** If it feels sticky in the shop, leave it.
