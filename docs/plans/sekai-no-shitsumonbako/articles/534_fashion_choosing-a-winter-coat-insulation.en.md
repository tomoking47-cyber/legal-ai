# Q. Down or synthetic fill — which is warmer? How to choose a winter coat in Japan｜Japan Questions #534

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/choosing-a-winter-coat-insulation` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Winter coat labels in Japan carry numbers that are hard to compare: "Down 90% / Feather 10%", "Filling: polyester", "Fill power 700". Japan's winters also vary enormously by region, so the right coat for a Tokyo commute is not the right coat for outdoor Hokkaido. Here is what the numbers mean and how to choose by use.

## A. Weight for weight, down is warmer and lighter than synthetic fill and compresses smaller. But it loses much of its warmth when wet and is more work to wash and care for. Synthetic fill (polyester) keeps most of its warmth when wet, washes easily at home and costs less, but is heavier and bulkier for the same warmth. For commuting or walking around the Tokyo area (0–10°C / 32–50°F), synthetic fill or about 150 g of down is enough. For the snow country or long hours outdoors, choose high-fill-power down; where rain and wet snow are common, choose synthetic or water-repellent down. "Fill power" is how much the down lofts (quality); "down weight" is how much is in the coat (quantity). Warmth depends on both.

## Down vs. synthetic fill

| Item | Down (feathers) | Synthetic fill (polyester) |
|---|---|---|
| **Warmth for weight** | **Warmest for the same weight.** Light | **About 60–80% of down.** Heavier for the same warmth |
| **When wet** | **Loses much of its warmth** (the down collapses). Slow to dry | **Keeps most of its warmth when wet.** Dries fast |
| **Packing** | **Compresses small** | Bulky |
| **Washing** | Hand wash, special detergent, and **tumble-dry to break up the clumps** (see separate article). Dry cleaning ¥2,000–4,000 | **Many can go in the washing machine** |
| **Lifespan** | **10 years or more** with care. Down shifts and leaks | Flattens in **5–10 years** (loses its recovery after compression) |
| **Price** | High (**from ¥20,000** as a guide; huge variation by brand) | Low (**from ¥5,000**) |
| **Animal welfare / labels** | Certifications such as RDS (Responsible Down Standard) | Not animal-derived |
| **Best for** | **Cold regions, dry cold, long hours outdoors, travel (light)** | **Rain and wet snow, commuting (hot trains), frequent washing, children** |

## Reading the label

| Label | Meaning | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Down 90% / Feather 10%** | **The share of down (the fluffy clusters)** in the filling. More down = lighter and warmer | **80/20 or better** is normal quality. 50/50 is mostly feather and heavy |
| **Fill power (FP)** | **The volume one ounce (about 28 g) of down lofts to**, in cubic inches. A measure of **quality** | **600+ is good, 700–800 high quality, 900 the top grade.** Not a mandatory label in Japan; brands show it voluntarily |
| **Down weight (fill weight)** | **The weight of down in the coat** (g) | **City 100–150 g, cold regions 200–300 g.** **High FP with little down is not warm** |
| **Shell water resistance** | "Water-repellent finish", "waterproof rating X mm" | For rain, **5,000 mm or more**, or choose synthetic |
| **Household Goods Quality Labelling** | **Type and blend of filling** (down as "Down X% / Feather X%"), **shell and lining fabric**, **care label** | Mandatory on clothing sold in Japan |

- **Tokyo winter (lows of 0–5°C / 32–41°F, short spells outdoors, heated trains and shops)**: **100–150 g of down** or **mid-weight synthetic** is enough. The real problems are **taking it off easily, weight and overheating on trains**; **a heavy down coat becomes a burden to carry**.
- **Hokkaido, Tohoku and the Sea of Japan coast (−5°C / 23°F or below, wind and snow)**: **FP 700+ and 200 g+ of down**, or **thick synthetic with a windproof shell**. **A hood** and **a sealed neck** change how warm you feel far more than you expect.
- **Regions with rain and wet snow**: down is weak when wet, so choose **synthetic fill** or **water-repellent down (the down itself given a water-repellent finish)**.
- **Children**: frequent washing, dirt and fast growth make **synthetic fill** the realistic choice.
- **Care**: **air down coats once or twice during the season in a well-ventilated shady spot** (damp collapses the down). **Wash at the end of the season, then store loosely, never compressed** (see our article on vacuum storage bags).
- **Layering**: **the air trapped inside — base layer plus a thin fleece or sweater — decides how warm you feel more than the outer coat does.** **A thin outer plus a mid-layer** is easier to adjust than one thick coat.

> **[Verify]** The warmth ratio for synthetic fill, the fill-power bands and the price ranges are general industry figures. Check the down-labelling rules under the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act with the Consumer Affairs Agency.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Anything with down is warm

**Warmth = fill power (quality) × down weight (quantity).** High FP with only 100 g inside is a light city jacket.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Synthetic fill is cheap and cold

**In wet conditions, with frequent washing or on a train commute, synthetic is often the more sensible choice.** It only loses to down on weight and packed size for the same warmth.

## Misunderstanding 3｜One thick coat gets you through winter

**In Japan the gap between outdoors and indoors is often 20°C (36°F) or more**, and a thick coat is a burden to carry indoors. A thin outer plus a mid-layer is more comfortable.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Down cannot be washed, so it has to go to the cleaner every year

**If the label shows a hand-wash symbol, you can wash it at home** (method in a separate article). Storing it unwashed does more harm: sweat and sebum degrade the down.

## Misunderstanding 5｜"Down 90%" means high quality

**The down ratio describes the make-up of the filling; without fill power and fill weight you cannot tell how warm it is.** Only with all three can you compare.
