# Q. How strictly do I need to follow Japanese dress codes for weddings and funerals?｜Japan Questions #536

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/dress-codes-for-weddings-and-funerals` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese ceremonial dress has many rules that are written down nowhere yet followed by everyone. Foreign guests often trip on "black is fine for anything" or "the invitation said heifuku (平服, 'ordinary dress'), so I wore casual clothes" — and feel awkward on the day. Here are the points people most often get wrong, by type of occasion.

## A. For a wedding guest: women wear a knee-covering dress or matching set in any colour except white or off-white, cover bare shoulders with a wrap, carry a small bag and wear closed-toe pumps. Men wear a dark suit with a white shirt and a white, silver or pale tie — never black. For a funeral or farewell ceremony (告別式, kokubetsushiki), wear mofuku (喪服, "black formal"): a matt black suit or dress, black stockings, black fabric or leather shoes, no jewellery beyond a single strand of pearls; men wear a plain black tie, black shoes and black socks. At a wake (通夜, tsuya), subdued ordinary clothes were traditionally accepted as a sign that you "rushed straight over", but today most people wear mofuku. "Heifuku" on an invitation does not mean everyday clothes; it means semi-formal — a dark suit or a smart dress.

## Wedding (as a guest)

| Item | Women | Men |
|---|---|---|
| **Basics** | **Knee-covering** dress, matching set or trouser-dress | **Dark suit (black, navy, charcoal)** + **white shirt**. With a black suit, brighten it with accessories |
| **Colours to avoid** | **White, off-white, pale beige** (the bride's colours). **All black** suggests mourning, so add colour with accessories | **Black tie** (funerals). **White tie** is the classic, but **silver, pale colours and small patterns** are now the norm |
| **Fabrics and patterns to avoid** | **Animal prints, fur and leather, which suggest killing** (avoid fur on bags and shoes too). **Cover bare shoulders, chest and back** with a wrap | Loud patterned shirts, casual button-down collars |
| **Shoes and bag** | **Closed-toe pumps (3–7 cm heel).** **A small party bag**; check large luggage into the cloakroom | **Black leather shoes (closed-lacing cap-toe Oxfords are the most formal)**, black socks |
| **Accessories** | **Pearls are the standard.** Keep **dangling earrings and watches** low-key | Pocket square (white, or matching the tie) |
| **Kimono** | Unmarried women: furisode (long-sleeved); married women: hōmongi or iro-tomesode (formal coloured kimono). **Black tomesode is for family only** | Few non-family men wear kimono |
| **Gift money (goshūgi)** | Carry the gift envelope wrapped in a **fukusa (袱紗, a cloth wrapper)** | Same |

## Funeral and farewell ceremony (as a general mourner)

| Item | Women | Men |
|---|---|---|
| **Clothes** | **Black formal** (matt black dress, ensemble or suit). **No bare skin**, **hem below the knee** | **Black suit** (**reifuku, 礼服** — a deeper black than a business black suit) + **plain white shirt** + **plain black tie** (**no dimple in the knot**) |
| **Feet** | **Black stockings (30 denier or less, so the skin just shows through)**, **black pumps (no decoration, no shine, 3–5 cm heel)** | **Black leather shoes (closed-lacing cap-toe or plain-toe)**, **black socks** |
| **Accessories** | **Wedding ring, one strand of pearls and single-stone earrings at most.** **Avoid two strands — "sorrow doubled"** | **Take off your watch, or wear a plain one.** No tie pin |
| **Bag** | **Black fabric, small, with no conspicuous metal.** Some avoid leather because it suggests killing, but it is now accepted | — |
| **Make-up and hair** | **Subdued (kata-keshō, "half make-up")**, **no glitter or red lipstick**, hair **tied low**. **Remove nail polish or wear black gloves** | Tidy facial hair |
| **Children** | **School uniform is formal dress.** Otherwise plain black, navy or grey | Same |
| **To bring** | **Juzu (Buddhist prayer beads; the informal type works for any sect)**, **condolence money (kōden) in a fukusa**, **a plain white or black handkerchief** | Same |

- **The wake (tsuya)**: the old etiquette was **subdued ordinary clothes (a dark suit, a plain dress)** to show you "hurried over on hearing the news". **Today more people attend only the wake, and mofuku is the majority.** Subdued ordinary clothes are still not wrong.
- **"Please come in heifuku"** (memorial services, farewell gatherings, wedding after-parties): **it means semi-formal, not everyday clothes**. **A dark suit or a dress in a quiet colour.** No T-shirts, jeans or trainers.
- **Memorial services (hōji, 法事 — the first and third anniversaries)**: mofuku up to the first anniversary; after that follow the host's guidance and move to **subdued ordinary clothes**.
- **Winter coats**: **black, navy or grey; no fur, leather or fur trim.** **Take it off before entering the venue**; at a farewell ceremony **remove it before reaching the reception desk**.
- **Rainy days**: **a black or plain umbrella.** A clear plastic umbrella is usually accepted.
- **Sudden news and no mofuku**: **hire one (¥5,000–10,000)**, **buy a black suit at a chain store near the station**, or **attend the wake in subdued clothes and the farewell ceremony in mofuku**. In Japan **owning one set of mofuku is considered basic adulthood**; many people **buy it in their twenties**.

> **[Verify]** Dress customs differ by region, Buddhist sect and generation. Check current general practice against materials from the ceremonial-services trade bodies and the Funeral Director Skills Examination Association.

## Misunderstanding 1｜A black outfit works for weddings and funerals alike

**All black at a wedding suggests mourning; a business black suit at a funeral looks paler than "reifuku black" and stands out.** Japanese custom keeps the two separate.

## Misunderstanding 2｜"Heifuku" means I can wear everyday clothes

**Heifuku = semi-formal (a dark suit or a smart dress).** In casual clothes you will be the only one.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Wearing mofuku to a wake is rude because it looks as if you expected the death

**That is the old view; today most people attend only the wake and most wear mofuku.** Either is acceptable.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Whitish is off-limits at a wedding, but beige is safe

**Pale beige and off-white read as white in photographs, so it is safer to avoid them.** Add colour with a wrap or bag.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Funeral stockings can be thick as long as they are black

**Sheer, 30 denier or less, is generally described as correct**; thick tights look casual. Thicker is increasingly tolerated in deep winter, but choose sheer if you can.
