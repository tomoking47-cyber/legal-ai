# Q. Do "non-iron" shirts really need no ironing? How are they different from ordinary shirts?｜Japan Questions #537

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/non-iron-shirts` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"Non-iron", "shape-retaining" (形態安定, keitai antei) and "easy care": in Japanese shirt departments these labels are now on most of the stock. Do they truly mean no ironing, why do they resist creasing, and does the way you wash them matter? Here is what we know.

## A. A shape-retaining shirt is cotton whose fibres have been fixed by cross-linking them with resin (combined with a liquid-ammonia process, or a VP finish - the method differs by maker) so that creases do not return after washing. To get close to "no ironing", the washing matters: spin for under 1 minute, pull the collar, front placket and cuffs straight by hand as soon as the cycle ends, and hang-dry on a broad hanger. No tumble-drying — it damages the finish. Follow this and most shirts can be worn without ironing, though a light press on the collar and placket makes them look sharper. The finish weakens over a few dozen washes; expect 2–3 years. Polyester-blend shirts resist creases better but breathe and absorb less than cotton.

## Types of crease-resistant finish

| Type | How it works | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Resin-finished cotton (shape-retaining / non-iron)** | The cellulose molecules in the cotton are **cross-linked with resin** so the fabric springs back after washing. Names differ by maker: **liquid-ammonia finish**, **VP finish**, etc. | Keeps the cotton feel while resisting creases. **Weakens after about 50–100 washes** |
| **Cotton-polyester blend** | Polyester (**30–50%**) holds the shape; cotton gives the hand-feel | **Most crease-resistant.** **Breathes and absorbs less than cotton; sweaty in summer.** Cheap |
| **All-polyester** | Synthetic only | Almost no creasing, but **does not absorb sweat and traps odour** |
| **Unfinished cotton (no resin finish)** | — | **Kindest to the skin and most breathable**, but must be ironed |

## Washing so you can skip the iron

1. **Undo all the buttons**, or **fasten only the top one** (keeps the collar shape).
2. **Fold and put in a laundry net** (stops tangling and creasing).
3. **Gentle flow / delicates course**; ordinary detergent is fine. **Some makers advise that fabric softener weakens the finish.**
4. **Spin 30 seconds to 1 minute.** A long spin is the biggest cause of creases.
5. **Take it out straight away and pull the collar, placket, cuffs and side seams straight by hand.** Button up to set the shape.
6. **Hang on a broad hanger that matches the shoulder width.** **The shirt's own weight pulls out the creases.** Direct sun causes yellowing and fading.
7. **No tumble-dryer** (damages the finish and the fabric).
8. **If it bothers you, iron only the collar and placket** (medium heat, follow the label).

- **Reading the labels**: **there is no single official standard for "shape-retaining" or "non-iron"**; the trade commonly uses **JIS L1905 (appearance retention after washing) and a "wash-and-wear (W&W) rating of grade 3.2 or better"** as the benchmark. **The higher the grade, the less it creases.**
- **Lifespan of the finish**: **2–3 years (50–100 washes)** before it weakens. **Once it has faded, the shirt is still wearable with ironing.**
- **Formaldehyde**: the substance that caused problems in older resin finishes. **Japan's household-goods law (the Act on Control of Household Products Containing Harmful Substances) sets "not detectable" for infant wear and 75 ppm or less for adult wear**, and today's shirts are within the limits. **Washing a new shirt once before wearing** is the safe habit (see separate article).
- **Summer**: cotton-poly and synthetics **do not absorb sweat and get clammy**, so choose **all-cotton shape-retaining or a shirt labelled sweat-absorbing / quick-dry**.
- **Yellowing**: collar sebum is no different on a shape-retaining shirt (see our article on collar and cuff grime). **Wash after wearing.**
- **Made-to-measure and luxury shirts**: mostly unfinished cotton, meant for ironing or the cleaner. **A dry cleaner's "non-iron finish"** is a pressing service, not the same as the fabric finish.

> **[Verify]** Check the durability (number of washes) of shape-retaining finishes, the W&W grade benchmark and the formaldehyde limits against industry and statutory materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Shape-retaining means no ironing however I wash it

**A long spin, leaving it wet in the drum or a thin wire hanger will all give you creases.** Spin under 1 minute, straighten at once, broad hanger — those are the conditions.

## Misunderstanding 2｜The tumble-dryer takes the creases out, so it is easier

**Dryer heat damages the resin finish and the fabric, and causes shrinking.** Shape-retaining shirts are hang-dried.

## Misunderstanding 3｜A polyester blend is the higher-performance option

**It resists creases, but breathes and absorbs less than cotton and gets sweaty in summer.** Choose by use and season.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The shape-retaining effect lasts for ever

**It fades gradually over about 50–100 washes.** Expect it to become "a shirt that needs ironing" after 2–3 years.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Resin finishes are bad for the skin

**Today's products are made within the legal limits for formaldehyde.** Wash a new shirt once before wearing and it is not normally a problem.
