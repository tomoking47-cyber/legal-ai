# Q. Washing my child's uwabaki every week is a chore. Is there an easier way to get them white?｜Japan Questions #538

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-school-shoes-uwabaki` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Uwabaki (上履き) are the white canvas-and-rubber indoor shoes every Japanese schoolchild wears in the building. At most primary schools the routine is: bring them home on Friday, wash them, take them back on Monday. Rubber and canvas soil quickly, and every weekend brings the same complaints — "the dark marks won't scrub off", "they won't dry", "they've gone yellow". Here is a routine that cuts the effort.

## A. Uwabaki are washed in five steps — soak → brush → rinse → spin → dry — and almost none of it is hard scrubbing. Dissolve oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) or laundry detergent in water at about 40°C (104°F) and soak for 30 minutes to an hour; most of the dirt lifts on its own. Then brush lightly, rinse well, wrap in a towel and spin 30 seconds in the washing machine (or press out the water with a towel), and dry heel-up in a well-ventilated shady spot. To prevent yellowing from sunlight, dry in the shade and rinse until no detergent remains. In winter, when drying takes longer, wash on Friday and dry on Saturday, or rotate two pairs.

## Steps and timing

| Step | How | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Knock off the loose dirt** | Bang out sand and grit from the soles outside; remove the insoles if they come out | 1 min |
| **2. Soak** | Dissolve **oxygen bleach (1–2 tablespoons per 2 litres)** or **laundry detergent** in **water at about 40°C**; soak **30 minutes to 1 hour** (2 hours for heavy dirt) | Leave it |
| **3. Brush** | With a **shoe brush or an old toothbrush**, lightly scrub the dark marks on the rubber toe and canvas, and the inside | 3–5 min |
| **4. Rinse** | **Leftover detergent causes yellowing.** Change the water and rinse **2–3 times** | 3 min |
| **5. Spin** | **Wrap in a towel and spin 30 seconds in the machine** (protects the drum), or **press with a towel to soak up the water** | 1 min |
| **6. Dry** | **Heel up, toe down, in a well-ventilated shady spot.** A **shoe hanger** (¥100 at a 100-yen shop) helps | Spring/summer half a day to 1 day; winter 1–2 days |

- **Dark marks on the rubber**: a **melamine sponge** (メラミンスポンジ, sold at 100-yen shops) wetted and rubbed on shifts them (not on the canvas). **An eraser** works on light marks.
- **Dark marks on the canvas**: if the soak does not lift them, **wet a bar of laundry soap, rub it directly on**, then brush.
- **Yellowing**: caused by **detergent or bleach residue plus sunlight**. **Rinse well and dry in the shade.** If they have already yellowed, **a soak in oxygen bleach** lightens it. **Do not use chlorine bleach** (塩素系漂白剤): it yellows the canvas and degrades the rubber.
- **Smell**: mainly because **they never dried fully**. **Take out the insoles and dry them separately**; **sprinkle bicarbonate of soda inside, leave overnight, then knock it out**.
- **Saving time**: **machine-washable uwabaki (check the label)** can go **in the machine in a laundry net**. **Rotating two pairs** gives you slack on drying. **Uwabaki detergent and shoe-washing nets** cost ¥100–300.
- **When to replace**: **thin canvas at the toe, rubber peeling, or a tight fit** (children's feet grow **0.5–1 cm every six months**). **Tight shoes affect foot development**, so check the size before you wash.
- **Won't dry in winter**: **stuff with balled-up newspaper** (change it after an hour; see our article on wet shoes), **use the bathroom dryer**, or **point a fan at them**. **In front of a heater or under a hairdryer the rubber warps.**

> **[Verify]** The oxygen-bleach quantity and the rate of children's foot growth are general guide figures. Check against the product label and specialist material on children's footwear.

## Misunderstanding 1｜The harder I scrub, the cleaner they get

**Most of the dirt lifts in the soak.** A few minutes of brushing to finish is enough; hard scrubbing roughens the canvas.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Drying in the sun makes them whiter

**Detergent residue plus sunlight turns them yellow.** Rinse thoroughly and dry in the shade.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Chlorine bleach gets them white in one go

**It degrades the rubber and causes yellowing.** Oxygen bleach for uwabaki.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Weekly washing is a nuisance; once a month will do

**Foot sweat and sebum build up, cause smell and mould, and become hard to remove.** A weekly soak is the least work in the end.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If they wash up white, they are still fine to wear

**Size and sole wear matter more.** Children's feet grow 0.5–1 cm every six months, so check the fit each time you wash.
