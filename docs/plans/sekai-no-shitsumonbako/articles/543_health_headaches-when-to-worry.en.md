# Q. How long can I wait out a headache? How to spot a dangerous one｜Japan Questions #543

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/headaches-when-to-worry` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Headaches are generally described as a chronic problem for one in three or four people in Japan, and over-the-counter painkillers are a mainstay of every drugstore (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, etc.). Most are "primary headaches" (migraine and tension-type headache) that do not threaten life, but occasionally a headache caused by a brain condition is mixed in. Here is how to tell them apart, and what to watch when using painkillers.

## A. Most headaches are either migraine (throbbing, light and sound hurt, worse with movement, 4–72 hours) or tension-type headache (a tight, squeezing feeling, comes with stiff shoulders, not worse with movement), and can be handled with rest and over-the-counter medicines. But a sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had before (peaking within seconds to a minute); fever with a stiff neck; numbness in the limbs, trouble getting words out or clouded consciousness; a first-ever headache after age 50; a headache after a blow to the head; a headache that worsens day by day — these are danger signs and need emergency care. The other trap is "medication-overuse headache": take painkillers on 10 or more days a month for three months and the medicine itself becomes the cause of the headache. If your over-the-counter use passes 10 days a month, go to a headache clinic (頭痛外来, zutsū gairai).

## Telling common headaches apart

| Type | Features | Duration | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tension-type headache** | **A squeezing feeling from the back of the head to the whole head**. Comes with **stiff shoulders and neck**. **Not worse with movement**. **The most common type** | 30 minutes to several days | Warmth, stretching, posture, sleep. Over-the-counter painkillers |
| **Migraine** | **Throbbing on one or both sides**. **Light, sound and smells hurt**, **nausea**, **worse with movement**. Some people get an **aura (zigzag lights)**. **Women are affected 3–4 times as often as men**; triggers include periods, air pressure, lack of sleep, hunger, cheese and wine | **4–72 hours** | Rest in a dark, quiet place; cool the head. If over-the-counter medicines do not work, **prescription drugs (triptans, and since 2021 preventive injections)** |
| **Cluster headache** | **Excruciating pain as if the back of one eye is being gouged out**, **tears, runny nose, red eye**. **Every day at the same time for 1–2 months**. More common in men | 15 minutes to 3 hours | **Headache clinic** (oxygen inhalation, prescription drugs) |
| **Medication-overuse headache** | Taking **painkillers on 10 or more days a month (15 or more for simple analgesics) for 3 months or more**. **A heavy or aching head almost every day** | Chronic | **Adjust the medicines at a headache clinic**. Stopping suddenly on your own can make it worse |

## Danger signs (see a doctor at once / emergency)

| Sign | What it may indicate |
|---|---|
| **Sudden, "worst of my life" pain peaking within seconds to a minute** (thunderclap headache) | **Subarachnoid haemorrhage** |
| **Fever + a neck too stiff to bend + headache** | **Meningitis** |
| **Numbness or weakness in the limbs, trouble getting words out, slurred speech, loss of part of the visual field, clouded consciousness** | **Stroke, brain tumour** |
| **Headache after a blow to the head** (even hours to weeks later) | **Epidural or subdural haematoma** (in older people, chronic subdural haematoma weeks later) |
| **A first-ever headache after age 50; a headache that worsens day by day** | **Temporal arteritis, tumour, etc.** |
| **Headache brought on by coughing, straining or exercise** | A structural problem in the brain |
| **Severe headache during pregnancy or after childbirth** | Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy |

- **Choosing an over-the-counter medicine**: **acetaminophen (paracetamol)** (gentle on the stomach, easier for children and pregnant women), **ibuprofen and loxoprofen** (reduce inflammation, harder on the stomach). **Products with added caffeine** feel more effective but **are more likely to cause overuse headache**. The basic rule is **"one dose early, as soon as the headache starts"**; **if you hold out until it is severe, it works less well**.
- **How often**: **no more than 10 days a month**. Beyond that, ask a **headache clinic, neurologist (神経内科・脳神経内科) or neurosurgeon (脳神経外科)** about preventive medicines.
- **Headache diary**: recording **date, time, pain level, triggers (sleep, period, weather, food) and what you took** speeds up diagnosis at your appointment. There are many apps.
- **Living with migraine**: **regular sleep (oversleeping is also a trigger)**, **avoid hunger**, **keep caffeine intake steady**, **anticipate changes in air pressure (apps)**, **manage light with sunglasses and screen brightness**.
- **Children's headaches**: **children get migraines too, and stomach ache or nausea may be the main symptom**. If **morning headaches with vomiting continue**, see a paediatrician.
- **Where to go**: a **headache clinic** (the Japanese Headache Society publishes a list of certified specialists), **neurology (神経内科・脳神経内科)**, **neurosurgery (脳神経外科)**. **Danger signs: call an ambulance (119).**

## Misunderstanding 1｜My headaches are routine, not worth a doctor's visit

**Usually true, but "sudden severe pain", "fever with a stiff neck", "numbness or speech problems" and "a first headache after 50" are the exceptions.** The danger signs alone are worth memorising.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Over-the-counter painkillers are safe, so daily use is fine

**10 or more days a month for 3 months, and the medicine itself becomes the cause of the headache (medication-overuse headache).** If your use is rising, go to a headache clinic.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Painkillers work better if you wait until the pain is strong

**For migraine, taking one early, at the start, works better.** Once the pain has built up it works less well.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Migraine is just "being a headache person" and nothing can be done

**Prescription triptans and, since 2021, preventive injections (anti-CGRP antibodies)** can greatly reduce the impact on daily life. Seeing a doctor is a shorter route than going on with over-the-counter medicines.

## Misunderstanding 5｜If nothing happens right after a bang on the head, you are fine

**In older people, a chronic subdural haematoma can cause headache, unsteadiness and forgetfulness weeks later.** See a doctor about any change after a fall.
