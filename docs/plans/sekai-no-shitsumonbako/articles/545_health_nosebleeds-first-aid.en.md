# Q. Nosebleed: tilt your head back? Stuff in a tissue? The right way to stop it｜Japan Questions #545

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/nosebleeds-first-aid` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

A child's nosebleed, or a nosebleed on a dry winter morning. "Tilt your head back", "tap the back of the neck", "stuff a tissue in" — many of the responses handed down in Japanese households are in fact not recommended. Here is the method ENT doctors recommend, and when to see one.

## A. The right method is: sit down, lean slightly forward, pinch the soft part of the nose (below the bone) firmly from both sides with thumb and index finger, and keep pressing for 10–15 minutes. Tilting your head back sends blood down your throat; you swallow it and it causes nausea. Stuffing in a tissue makes re-bleeding likely when you pull it out, and tapping the back of the neck does nothing. Nine in ten nosebleeds come from near the entrance of the nose (Kiesselbach's area) and stop with this method. Not stopped after 20–30 minutes of pressure, heavy bleeding, frequent repeats, taking blood-thinning medicines, bleeding after an injury — these mean an ENT clinic (耳鼻咽喉科, jibi-inkōka) or emergency care.

## How to stop it, step by step

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Sit** | **Sit and lean slightly forward**. Do not lie down or tilt back | Keeps blood out of the throat. Swallowing it causes nausea and vomiting |
| **2. Pinch** | **Pinch the soft part of the nose (below the nasal bone) firmly from both sides with thumb and index finger** | Direct pressure on **Kiesselbach's area** (1–2 cm inside the nostril), where 90% of bleeding points are |
| **3. Keep pressing** | **10–15 minutes, without letting go to check** | Letting go breaks up the clot that is forming |
| **4. Cool** | A cold towel on the bridge of the nose and forehead (supplementary) | Constricts the blood vessels |
| **5. Breathe through the mouth** | Breathe quietly through the mouth. Spit out any blood that reaches it | — |
| **6. Once it stops** | **For several hours, do not blow or rub the nose, do not strain hard, avoid a hot bath** | Prevents re-bleeding |

## What not to do

| Action | Problem |
|---|---|
| **Tilting back or lying down** | Blood runs down the throat and is **swallowed, causing nausea and vomiting**. You also lose track of how much you are losing |
| **Stuffing in a tissue** | **The scab tears off when you remove it and bleeding restarts**. Fibres are left behind. If you must pack, use **clean gauze, shallowly** — but pinching is better |
| **Tapping or cooling the back of the neck** | **Has no effect on the bleeding** |
| **Pressing the hard part of the nose (nasal bone)** | Does not press on the bleeding point |
| **Letting go after a few minutes to check** | Breaks up the clot that is forming |

## When to see a doctor

- **Still bleeding after pinching for 20–30 minutes**, or **heavy bleeding** (more than half a cup, dizziness, pale face) → **emergency care**.
- **Several times a week**, **repeatedly on one side only** (possible tumour or structural problem), **easy bleeding elsewhere too (gums, under the skin)** → **ENT or internal medicine (内科, naika)**.
- **Taking antiplatelet or anticoagulant medicines (blood thinners)**, **high blood pressure with a flushed, hot face** → harder to stop. See a doctor early.
- **Nosebleed after a blow to the face** (possible fracture), **after a blow to the head** (an emergency if clear fluid is mixed in).
- **A child who may have put something up the nose** (one side only, foul-smelling discharge).

## Prevention

- **Winter dryness**: **indoor humidity 40–60%**, **a thin layer of petroleum jelly (Vaseline) at the nostril entrance with a cotton bud** (before bed). **Nose-picking and hard blowing** are the biggest triggers (in children, almost always the cause).
- **Allergic rhinitis**: itching → rubbing → bleeding. **Controlling the inflammation with nasal sprays or tablets** comes before stopping bleeds.
- **Flushing**: bleeds are more likely **right after a hot bath, after hard exercise and after drinking**.
- **Children with frequent nosebleeds**: an ENT clinic can **cauterise the bleeding point (electrically or chemically)**.

> **[Verify]** The share of bleeds from Kiesselbach's area and the guide for compression time are standard ENT guidance. Check against materials from the Japan Society of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Tilt your head back so the blood does not drip

**Blood runs down the throat, is swallowed and causes nausea.** Sit and lean forward.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Stuffing in a tissue is the best method

**Bleeding restarts when you pull it out, and fibres are left behind.** Pinching the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes is more reliable.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Tap the back of the neck and it stops

**It has no effect on the bleeding.** An old folk belief.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Frequent nosebleeds are a sign of anaemia or leukaemia

**Most frequent nosebleeds in children come from dryness, nose-picking and allergy.** But if there is easy bleeding elsewhere too, or more bruising, get a blood test.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once it stops you can blow your nose as normal

**For several hours the scab is weak, and blowing, rubbing or a hot bath restart the bleeding.** Leave the nose alone for the rest of the day.
