# Q. Leg cramps at night (komura-gaeri): is there a way to prevent them?｜Japan Questions #547

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/night-leg-cramps` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Waking at night with a violent pain in the calf — komura-gaeri in Japanese. More than half of middle-aged and older people experience it, and it is common in pregnant women and after exercise. Many causes are suggested — "not enough water", "lack of minerals", "cold feet" — but much about it is still unclear. Here is what is known, and what to do in the moment.

## A. A cramp is the calf muscle contracting hard against your will. In the moment, "slowly stretch the cramped muscle": pull your toes towards you (towards the shin), straighten the knee and hold for 20–30 seconds. Preventive measures with some evidence: (1) stretch the calves and hamstrings before bed; (2) avoid dehydration during the day (alcohol and diuretics play a part too); (3) keep your feet warm (leg warmers, a hot-water bottle); (4) avoid a heavy duvet that pushes your toes into a fully pointed position. Cramps several times a week, cramps in the daytime too, swelling or numbness, or more cramps since starting a medicine — in these cases see an internal medicine doctor (内科, naika) to check for back problems, blood-vessel problems, thyroid disease or drug side effects. The kampo (traditional herbal) formula shakuyaku-kanzō-tō is often prescribed as fast-acting, but continuous use needs care.

## What to do in the moment

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| **1. Stretch** | **Straighten the knee and pull the toes towards the shin** (with your hand, or by pressing the sole against a wall). **20–30 seconds**, slowly |
| **2. Stand** | If you can, **stand and put your weight on the cramped leg** (stretches the Achilles tendon) |
| **3. Warm and massage** | Once the contraction has passed, **massage gently and warm**. Hard massage can damage the muscle |
| **4. Afterwards** | **Muscle soreness may last until the next day**. If bad, cool it → warm it the next day |

## Suggested causes and what to do about them

| Factor | Details | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Muscle fatigue** | Standing work or exercise during the day; weak muscles from lack of exercise | **Stretch before bed** (calves and hamstrings, 30 seconds × 2–3 each). **Light exercise several times a week** |
| **Dehydration and electrolytes** | Sweat, alcohol, diuretics, diarrhoea. Low **magnesium, potassium or calcium** may play a part (**evidence is limited**) | **Fluids during the day**, **cut back on evening alcohol**, a balanced diet (**studies on supplements disagree**) |
| **Cold** | Cold feet at night make the muscles tense | **Leg warmers and socks**, **a hot-water bottle**, **warm up in the bath before bed** |
| **Sleeping position** | **Toes pushed into a fully pointed position (plantar flexion) by the weight of the duvet** makes the calf prone to contracting | **A light duvet**, **space at the foot of the bed**, **sleep on your side** |
| **Pregnancy** | Common in late pregnancy (**about 30–50%** experience it). Changes in blood flow and minerals | Stretch, keep warm, ask your obstetrician (magnesium preparations are sometimes prescribed) |
| **Medicines** | Can increase with **diuretics, statins (cholesterol drugs), some asthma medicines** | **If cramps increased after starting a medicine, ask the prescribing doctor** (do not stop on your own) |
| **Illness** | **Lumbar spinal stenosis, herniated disc** (with numbness), **varicose veins, peripheral arterial disease** (swelling, pain on walking), **diabetes, thyroid, kidney or liver disease** | **Frequent (several times a week or more), in the daytime too, with numbness, swelling or pain on walking** → internal medicine or an orthopaedic clinic (整形外科, seikei-geka) |

- **Kampo: shakuyaku-kanzō-tō (芍薬甘草湯)**: generally described as **working within minutes when taken during an attack**, and often prescribed by orthopaedic and internal medicine doctors. But it **contains liquorice (kanzō), and continuous use can cause low potassium, swelling and raised blood pressure (pseudoaldosteronism)**, so the rule is **to take it as needed, not every day as prevention**. Older people need particular care.
- **Magnesium**: outside pregnancy, **studies showing an effect and studies showing none are mixed**. **Seaweed, nuts, beans and brown rice** in the diet are enough. People with reduced kidney function should avoid supplements.
- **The bedtime stretch**: **hands on a wall, one leg back with the heel on the floor, stretch the calf for 30 seconds**. **Sit with your legs straight, loop a towel around the sole and pull it towards you.** 2–3 times each side. Doing it **right before bed** has been reported to reduce night-time cramps.
- **Age**: **about half of people aged 60 and over** experience it at least once a month. Muscle mass and tendon flexibility decline with age, so **stretching and light strength training** are the core of prevention.

> **[Verify]** Check the rates for older people and pregnant women, and the side-effect statements for shakuyaku-kanzō-tō, against the relevant societies and the package insert. Check the state of research on magnesium against the latest reviews.

## Misunderstanding 1｜When it cramps, massage it hard

**Hard massage during the contraction can damage the muscle.** First "stretch slowly"; massage gently and warm once it has passed.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Drink water and you can prevent it

**Dehydration is one factor, but not the only one.** Drinking a lot right before bed only means more trips to the toilet at night. Combine daytime fluids with stretching and keeping warm.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Magnesium supplements prevent it

**Results are not consistent, and outside pregnancy the effect is limited.** Diet is enough; avoid supplements if kidney function is reduced.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Take shakuyaku-kanzō-tō every night to be safe

**The liquorice side effects (low potassium, swelling, raised blood pressure) make this a medicine to avoid taking continuously.** As needed during an attack is the rule.

## Misunderstanding 5｜It is just age, nothing to be done

**Several times a week, in the daytime too, or with numbness or swelling can point to back, blood-vessel or metabolic disease, or a drug side effect.** Ask an internal medicine doctor at least once.
