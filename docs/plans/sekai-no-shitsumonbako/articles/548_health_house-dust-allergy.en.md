# Q. Sneezing and a runny nose indoors that will not stop. What actually works against house dust?｜Japan Questions #548

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/house-dust-allergy` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

It is not pollen season, yet you sneeze when you get home and wake with a blocked nose. In Japan, the second most common cause of allergic rhinitis after pollen is "house dust", and most of that is the droppings and dead bodies of house dust mites (hyōhi-dani). Before you buy an air purifier, here is the order in which the measures work.

## A. The main cause of house dust allergy is mite droppings and dead bodies, and the place with the most is bedding. In order of impact: (1) bedding — vacuum futons and pillows at least once a week (20 seconds per square metre), wash sheets and pillowcases weekly, use anti-mite covers (high-density weave); (2) floors — remove or reduce carpets and rugs, and wipe wooden floors; (3) humidity — keep it at 60% or below (mites multiply at 60–80%); (4) reduce fabric sofas, soft toys and heavy curtains. An air purifier has some effect on mite allergen "while it is airborne", but most allergen has settled on floors and bedding, so on its own its effect is limited. If symptoms persist, have the cause tested at an ENT (耳鼻咽喉科) or allergy clinic and ask about medicines or sublingual immunotherapy (covered by health insurance for mites since 2015).

## The measures, their effect and their priority

| Priority | Measure | Details | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Bedding** | **Vacuum futons, mattresses and pillows at least once a week (20 seconds per m², slowly)**. **Wash sheets and covers weekly (at 55°C (131°F) or above the mites die too; an ordinary wash still rinses away the allergen)**. **Anti-mite covers** (a high-density weave that mites cannot pass) | **Large** |
| **2** | **Floors** | **Remove carpets and rugs, or switch to washable ones**. **Wipe tatami and wooden floors** (vacuuming alone stirs dust up). **Use a vacuum with a HEPA filter and weak exhaust** | **Large** |
| **3** | **Humidity** | **Indoor humidity 50–60% or below** (mites multiply most at **60–80% and 20–30°C**). **Dehumidifier, ventilation, the air-conditioner's dry mode**. **Closets and oshiire (built-in futon cupboards) too** | **Medium to large** |
| **4** | **Fewer fabrics** | **Fabric sofa → leather or synthetic leather**, **only soft toys that can be washed** (wash and tumble-dry, or bag them and freeze for a day → wash), **heavy curtains → washable ones** | **Medium** |
| **5** | **Air purifier** | **Reduces airborne allergen**. Guide: **HEPA filter, rated floor area twice the size of the room** | **Small to medium** (not enough on its own) |
| **6** | **Mite traps and anti-mite sprays** | Supplementary. Reduce live mites, but not the allergen (droppings and bodies) | **Small** |

- **Mite basics**: **house dust mites (chiri-dani)** are **0.3 mm** long, invisible to the eye, and **do not bite**. They **feed on human skin flakes and dander**, and **a single futon can hold tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands**. **The allergen is the droppings and dead bodies**, which **remain after the mites die** — so the focus is on "removing and washing away" rather than "killing".
- **Sun-drying alone is not enough**: the inside of a futon rarely reaches 50°C, and **droppings and bodies remain after airing**. **Vacuum after airing** — the two go together.
- **Cleaning order and timing**: **in the morning before people move about**, or **right after coming home**. **High places → low places**, **wipe → vacuum** (vacuuming first stirs dust up). **Open the windows while cleaning**; people with allergies should **wear a mask**.
- **Pets**: **dog and cat dander is also a major allergen**. Keep them out of the bedroom; brush once a week (outdoors).
- **Medicines and therapy**: **antihistamines (over-the-counter and prescription)**, **steroid nasal sprays** (prescription; highly effective, with little effect on the rest of the body). **Sublingual immunotherapy** (**covered by health insurance for mites since 2015, age 5 and over**) involves placing a tablet under the tongue every day; **many people find their symptoms much milder after 3–5 years**. Few people know that **an option addressing the root cause exists**.
- **Testing**: **a blood test (specific IgE)** can **check the major allergens — mites, pollen, pets, mould — all together** (covered by health insurance). Knowing "what you react to" cuts wasted effort.
- **Children**: **mite control in infancy is generally described as relevant to preventing the onset of asthma**, and bedding measures are given particular weight.

> **[Verify]** Check the breeding conditions for mites, mite numbers in bedding, and the start year and minimum age for health-insurance coverage of sublingual immunotherapy against Japanese Society of Allergology and MHLW materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Buy an air purifier and the problem is solved

**Most allergen has settled on floors and bedding; an air purifier alone has limited effect.** Bedding and floors come first.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sun-dry the futon and the mites die

**The inside rarely reaches 50°C, and even when mites die, the droppings and bodies remain as allergen.** Vacuum after airing; wash the covers.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Kill the mites with traps or sprays and the symptoms go

**Fewer live mites, but the allergen (droppings and bodies) remains.** Removing and washing is the core.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The only option for house dust allergy is to keep it down with medicines

**Sublingual immunotherapy (covered by health insurance) aims to change the underlying reaction.** It takes 3–5 years, but many people find their symptoms much milder.

## Misunderstanding 5｜The more you vacuum the better

**The exhaust stirs dust up, so wipe first, vacuum slowly, and pair it with ventilation and a mask.** Method matters more than frequency.
