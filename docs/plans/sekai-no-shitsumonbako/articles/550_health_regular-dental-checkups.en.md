# Q. Should I see a dentist even without toothache? How often to have a check-up, and what happens｜Japan Questions #550

- Shelf: `health` ／ URL: `/en/health/regular-dental-checkups` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Many people in Japan still see the dentist as "somewhere you go when it hurts", and only about half of adults have regular dental check-ups. Meanwhile, in countries where check-ups are established, such as Sweden, people are known to keep more teeth at 80 than in Japan. Here is what a check-up involves, how often to go and what it costs.

## A. Even without pain, a check-up every 3–6 months is the most cost-effective way to keep your teeth for life. Both decay and gum disease are symptom-free early on; by the time it hurts, it is well advanced. A check-up covers: (1) checking for decay and gum disease (measuring the depth of the gum pockets); (2) removing tartar and cleaning (which you cannot do yourself); (3) advice on the spots you miss when brushing; (4) X-rays and fluoride application where needed. It is covered by health insurance, at about ¥3,000–4,000 per visit (30% co-payment). More than half of people in Japan now keep 20 or more teeth at 80, but the gap between those who have regular check-ups and those who do not is known to be wide.

## What a check-up involves, and how often

| Item | Details | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Check for decay** | Visual inspection; X-rays if needed (early decay and decay between the teeth cannot be seen) | Every check-up. X-rays about **once every 1–2 years** |
| **Check for gum disease** | Measuring the **depth of the gum pockets** (**3 mm or less is healthy, 4 mm or more is gum disease**), bleeding, loose teeth | Every check-up |
| **Tartar removal (scaling)** | Removes with instruments the **tartar (calcified plaque)** that a toothbrush cannot. **Tartar starts to form in about 2 weeks** | **Every 3–6 months** |
| **Cleaning (PMTC)** | Professional mechanical cleaning, stain removal | As above |
| **Brushing advice** | Dye to show missed spots; tools that suit you (interdental brush size, floss) | Every check-up |
| **Fluoride application** | High-concentration fluoride strengthens the teeth. **Children every 3–6 months**; adults too if at high risk of decay | Mainly children |
| **Oral cancer check** | Visual inspection of the lining of the mouth | Every check-up (a benefit few people notice) |

## Dental health in numbers

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| **People with 20 or more teeth at 80 ("8020" achievers)** | **51.6%** (2022 Survey of Dental Diseases). **About 7%** when the campaign began in 1989 |
| **Check-up rate** | **About 58%** of adults had a dental check-up in the past year (2022). Wide differences by age and region |
| **Why teeth are lost** | **Gum disease about 37%, decay about 29%, fracture about 18%** (8020 Promotion Foundation survey) |
| **Prevalence of gum disease** | **About half of adults** have gum pockets of 4 mm or more; **a majority of those aged 45 and over** |
| **Cost** | A check-up (basic periodontal examination + scaling): **about ¥3,000–4,000 per visit at the 30% co-payment**. **Filling one decayed tooth: ¥3,000–10,000; an implant after extraction: ¥300,000–500,000, not covered by insurance** |
| **National policy** | In 2022 the government announced that it would consider **"universal dental check-ups"**; **from fiscal 2024 (Reiwa 6), ages 20 and 30 were added to the target ages for gum-disease check-ups** (ages 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 and 70) |

- **Why "before it hurts" matters**: **early decay (enamel only) can sometimes be halted by remineralisation**, with no drilling. **Gum disease advances with almost no pain**, and **by the time teeth are loose it is often too late**. **Links between gum disease and diabetes, cardiovascular disease, aspiration pneumonia and premature birth** have also been reported.
- **The interval**: **3 months** (existing gum disease, lots of missed spots, smokers), **6 months** (stable), **1 year** (the minimum). **Your dentist will suggest an interval for you**; the rule is to follow it.
- **Children**: on top of the **municipal check-ups at 18 months and 3 years** and **school dental check-ups**, **fluoride application and a check-up every 3–6 months at your regular dentist**. **Decay in baby teeth affects the permanent teeth too.**
- **Pregnancy**: hormonal changes make gum inflammation more likely, and **many municipalities offer a free dental check-up for pregnant women**. Have it in the stable period (second trimester).
- **Municipal check-ups**: **gum-disease check-ups** (under the Health Promotion Act; free to a few hundred yen depending on the municipality). The previous ages of **40, 50, 60 and 70** have been widened to include **20 and 30** as well. If a notice arrives, go. **Some company health insurance societies (kenpo) subsidise dental check-ups.**
- **Choosing a regular dentist**: **puts effort into prevention and maintenance**, **has dental hygienists on staff**, **measures your gum pockets and explains the results**, **shows you X-rays and photos of your teeth**.

## Misunderstanding 1｜No pain, no need for a dentist

**Both decay and gum disease are symptom-free early on; by the time it hurts, it is well advanced.** A check-up every 3–6 months ends up the cheapest option.

## Misunderstanding 2｜I brush thoroughly every day, so I do not get tartar

**Tartar is missed plaque that has calcified over about 2 weeks, and a toothbrush cannot remove it.** Everyone gets it, so regular removal is needed.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Gum disease is an old person's disease

**Some people already have gum pockets in their 30s, and by 45 and over it is more than half. It advances quietly.** Check-ups from a young age make the difference.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Teeth are lost to decay

**The biggest cause is gum disease (about 37%).** It advances without pain, so early detection at check-ups is the key.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Check-ups are expensive

**¥3,000–4,000 per visit at the 30% co-payment; under ¥10,000 a year even at twice a year.** Compared with filling one tooth, or an implant after losing one (¥300,000–500,000, not covered by insurance), it is clearly the cheaper investment.
