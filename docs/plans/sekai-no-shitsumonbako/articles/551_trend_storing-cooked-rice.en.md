# Q. Cooked rice: keep it warm or freeze it? How many days does it last?｜Japan Questions #551

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/storing-cooked-rice` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Some households in Japan leave the rice cooker on "keep warm" (保温, hoon) overnight; others freeze the rice the moment it is cooked. Rice is the base of most Japanese meals, and how you store it changes both the taste and the safety. Here are the limits of keep-warm, fridge and freezer, laid out in hours and days.

## A. For cooked rice, "portion it and freeze it as soon as it is cooked" gives the best taste and the best safety. The rice cooker's keep-warm function is fine for up to 5–6 hours as a guide; after that the rice yellows and starts to smell, and beyond 12 hours the flavour drops sharply. Do not leave rice at room temperature: in summer, bacteria (Bacillus cereus and others) begin to multiply within about 2 hours. In the fridge the starch hardens and the taste suffers, so keep it only until the next day and reheat in the microwave. To freeze, wrap single portions thin and flat in cling film or put them in containers while still hot, let the steam heat pass, then freeze; eat within 1 month. To thaw, heat in one go in the microwave (2–3 minutes at 600W) and the rice comes back close to freshly cooked.

## Guide by storage method

| Method | Guide | Taste | Safety |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rice cooker keep-warm** | **Up to 5–6 hours**. **Avoid 12 hours or more** | **Yellowing, hardening and off-smells increase** with time | At keep-warm temperature (**60–70°C / 140–158°F**) bacteria barely grow, but **they start growing the moment keep-warm is switched off** |
| **Left at room temperature** | **Summer: eat within 2 hours; winter: within a few hours**, or refrigerate/freeze | — | **Bacillus cereus** (a soil bacterium, common on rice) **multiplies at 10–50°C** and produces **a toxin that heat does not destroy**. A well-known cause of food poisoning from onigiri (rice balls) and fried rice |
| **Fridge** | **Until the next day (within 24 hours)** | **The starch retrogrades (hardens) and the rice goes dry and crumbly**. Microwaving helps, but it is worse than frozen rice | At fridge temperatures (10°C or below) the growth of Bacillus cereus is largely held back, but other bacteria and loss of quality mean long storage is not possible |
| **Freezer** | **Within 1 month** (flavour guide). **2–3 weeks** is best | **Chilling freshly cooked rice quickly keeps the taste best** | Bacteria do not grow |

## How to freeze rice

1. **Freshly cooked and still hot**: wrap single portions (**150–200 g**) **thin and flat in cling film** (no more than 2 cm thick), or put them in **freezer containers**. **Wrapping the steam in with the rice** keeps it moist.
2. **Let the initial heat pass**: **placing the wrapped rice on a metal tray** cools it faster. **Putting it in the freezer while hot damages other foods**, so wait until it is **cool enough to touch**.
3. **Into the freezer**: **lay flat, then stand upright once frozen**. **Write the date**.
4. **Thawing**: **microwave in the wrap at 600W for 2–3 minutes** (do not thaw at room temperature; it goes sticky). **Break it up halfway through** to reduce uneven heating. **Moving it to a bowl under loosely laid cling film** also works.

- **Keep-warm tips (if you must use it)**: **loosen the rice as soon as it is cooked to release excess steam**; **do not leave the rice paddle (shamoji) in the cooker** (bacteria); **a small amount dries out quickly, so gather it in the centre**. More rice cookers now come with a **"no keep-warm" setting**.
- **Bacillus cereus**: **it forms spores that survive heating to 100°C**. It multiplies and produces toxin during the **slow cooling time at room temperature** after cooking. The **vomiting type** causes nausea and vomiting **30 minutes to 6 hours** after eating. **"Chill freshly cooked rice quickly"** is the only countermeasure; reheating does not destroy the toxin.
- **Onigiri (rice balls)**: **shape them in cling film, not with bare hands**; **use salty or acidic fillings**; **carry them with an ice pack**; **in summer, eat within 4 hours at room temperature**.
- **Taste of frozen rice**: it depends on **freezing freshly cooked rice**. **Rice dried out by keep-warm does not recover when frozen.** A household trick is to **cook with a little extra water** to reduce dryness.
- **The inner pot**: **yellowing and smells after keep-warm are partly due to a dirty inner pot**. **Wash the inner lid and steam vent every time**.

> **[Verify]** Check the keep-warm guide times against the general figures published by rice cooker makers, and the growth temperature and symptoms of Bacillus cereus against Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜On keep-warm it stays safe and tasty all day

**Bacteria grow little during keep-warm, but the taste starts to drop after 5–6 hours, and yellowing and smells increase beyond 12 hours.** Freeze whatever you cannot eat as soon as it is cooked.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Rice keeps for several days in the fridge

**In the fridge it goes hard and dry, and bacteria still grow slowly.** Keep it only until the next day; freeze anything beyond that.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is better to let rice cool before freezing

**Wrapping it while hot, letting the first heat pass and freezing straight away** is the tastiest and safest method. The longer it sits at room temperature, the more bacteria grow and the more the taste drops.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Frozen rice can be thawed at room temperature

**Thawing at room temperature makes it sticky, and the time at room temperature is itself a bacterial risk.** Heat it in one go in the microwave.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Any rice is safe once reheated

**The Bacillus cereus toxin is not destroyed by heat.** Rice left at room temperature can cause food poisoning even after reheating if the toxin is already there. "Chill it quickly" is the countermeasure.
