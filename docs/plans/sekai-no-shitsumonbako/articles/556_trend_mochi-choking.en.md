# Q. Why do New Year mochi accidents happen every year? How older people and children should eat it｜Japan Questions #556

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/mochi-choking` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Every January in Japan, a run of people are taken to hospital after choking on mochi (sticky rice cake), and some die. In the Tokyo Fire Department's area alone, around 100 people a year are taken by ambulance, and 90% of them are 65 or older. Many people assume "chew well and you are fine", but for anyone whose chewing and swallowing have weakened, mochi is one of the foods most likely to cause choking. If you spend New Year with a Japanese family, this is worth knowing.

## A. Mochi is very sticky, forms a lump in the mouth and hardens at body temperature, so it carries a high choking risk for older people with weaker chewing and swallowing, and for infants who cannot bite through it. Prevention: (1) cut it small (about 1 cm cubes); (2) one piece at a time, chew well, swallow before the next; (3) moisten the mouth with tea or soup before eating; (4) use shiratama dumplings, porridge-style mochi or shop-bought "soft mochi" instead; (5) never let someone eat alone. If a piece gets stuck and the person cannot speak, is struggling or changing colour, call 119 (Japan's ambulance number) at once and give hard back blows or abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich manoeuvre). Sucking it out with a vacuum cleaner is not recommended.

## Mochi accidents in numbers

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Ambulance cases** | **Around 90–130 people a year** taken to hospital for choking on mochi in the Tokyo Fire Department's area. **More than half in January, concentrated on 1–3 January** |
| **Age** | **About 90% are 65 or older**. **People in their 80s are the largest group** |
| **Deaths** | Nationally, **choking deaths from food run at about 3,500–4,000 a year**, and **mochi is among the most common foods involved**. Consumer Affairs Agency analysis found **mochi deaths concentrated in January: more than 40% of the year's total** |
| **Properties of mochi** | **It hardens and becomes stickier at mouth temperature (around 37°C / 99°F)**. **Saliva does not dissolve it**. **It is easy to swallow whole without biting through** |
| **Children** | **Under-threes are generally not given mochi**. **The foods behind choking in children aged 5 and under are nuts, grapes, cherry tomatoes and mochi** |

## Prevention (households with older people or children)

| Measure | Details |
|---|---|
| **Cut** | **About 1 cm cubes, or thin slices**. Cut the mochi in ozōni (New Year soup) too |
| **Mouthful size** | **One piece at a time; swallow before the next**. **Do not eat while talking or laughing** |
| **Moisten the mouth** | **Have tea or soup before eating** to wet the mouth and throat. **Washing mochi down with tea mid-mouthful is counter-productive** (the mochi shifts and blocks the airway) |
| **Posture** | **Sit up, leaning slightly forward**. Do not eat lying down or straight after waking |
| **Alternatives** | **Shiratama dumplings (cut small), rice porridge with mochi, shop-bought "soft mochi" (やわらかい餅) or "melting mochi" (とろける餅)**, **bread porridge or suiton (flour dumplings) instead of mochi** |
| **Supervision** | **Never let someone eat alone**. Avoid mochi if dentures are not fitting well |
| **Time of day** | **Swallowing is weaker at breakfast and just after waking**. Some advice says midday or later is safer |

## What to do if it gets stuck

1. **Check "can you make a sound?"**: if the person **can cough or speak**, **let them keep coughing** (they may clear it themselves). **Do not hit their back** (the mochi may move and block the airway completely).
2. **No sound, face turning blue, hands clutching the throat** (signs of choking): **call 119 at once** (ask someone nearby), and at the same time:
   - **Back blows**: **bend the person forward, head low**, and **strike hard several times between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand**.
   - **Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre)**: from behind, **place a fist above the navel and thrust sharply inwards and upwards**. **Not for pregnant women or infants** (use chest thrusts).
   - Alternate the two until the object comes out or the person loses consciousness.
3. **If they lose consciousness**: **start CPR (chest compressions)**. Remove anything visible in the mouth (do not probe with fingers for anything you cannot see).
4. **Vacuum cleaner**: **not recommended** by the fire service or doctors (it injures the mouth and wastes time). Do the above rather than searching for equipment.
5. **See a doctor even after it comes out**: possible airway injury or remaining fragments.

- **Infants**: **back blows (face down, head low, strike the back) and chest thrusts**. **Do not use abdominal thrusts on infants; they damage internal organs**.
- **Dentures**: **ill-fitting dentures weaken the bite**. Have them adjusted at the dentist before the year end.
- **Other high-risk foods for older people**: **konnyaku (yam jelly), boiled eggs, bread, meat, dango dumplings, cherry tomatoes, grapes**. The same measures apply.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Chew well and mochi is safe

**For older people with weaker chewing and swallowing, mochi is the most dangerous food precisely because it forms a lump even when chewed.** Cutting, small amounts and supervision go together.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Wash it down with tea and you are fine

**Washing it down with liquid mid-mouthful can shift the mochi and block the airway.** Moisten the mouth before eating, not during.

## Misunderstanding 3｜If it sticks, hit the back first

**If the person can cough or speak, do not hit them; let them keep coughing** (a blow can cause complete blockage). **If they cannot make a sound, give back blows and abdominal thrusts and call 119**.

## Misunderstanding 4｜A vacuum cleaner will suck it out

**The fire service and medical bodies do not recommend it.** The time spent finding the machine makes things worse. Back blows and abdominal thrusts instead.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Cut it small and a child of any age can have it

**Under-threes cannot bite through it, and the general practice is to avoid mochi.** Shiratama dumplings have the same properties, so take care with those too.
