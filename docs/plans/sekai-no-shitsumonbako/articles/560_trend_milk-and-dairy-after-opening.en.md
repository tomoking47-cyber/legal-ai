# Q. How many days does milk keep once opened? What about yoghurt and cheese?｜Japan Questions #560

- Shelf: `trend` ／ URL: `/en/trend/milk-and-dairy-after-opening` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"I opened the milk two days before its best-before date; how long can I drink it?" "What about yoghurt opened a week ago?" Dairy is used every day, yet many people do not know how long it keeps after opening. The date on an unopened pack and the number of days after opening are two different things, and Japanese milk has a few particularities of its own. Here is the sorting.

## A. Once milk is opened, finish it within 2–3 days regardless of the date on the pack (the printed date is a guarantee for the unopened state only). This applies especially to the ultra-high-temperature (UHT, 120–150°C for 2–3 seconds) milk that dominates the Japanese market: unopened it keeps a long time, but once opened, airborne bacteria get in and it is like any other milk. Plain yoghurt keeps 3–4 days after opening; serve it with a clean spoon. Processed cheese keeps 2–3 weeks after opening; natural cheese a few days to 4 weeks depending on type (mozzarella and camembert a few days to 1 week; hard cheeses such as parmesan 2–4 weeks). Butter keeps about a month in the fridge after opening. Three rules apply to all of them: "refrigerate at 10°C or below", "in the body of the fridge, not the door pocket", and "never drink straight from the carton".

## Guide after opening

| Dairy product | After opening (fridge, 10°C or below) | Storage tips | Signs it has gone off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Milk (UHT, carton)** | **2–3 days** | **The door pocket swings in temperature, so store at the back of the fridge**. **Do not drink from the carton**. Close the spout | **Sour smell, separation or lumps, curdles when heated** |
| **Low-temperature pasteurised milk (63–65°C for 30 minutes)** | **1–2 days after opening**; a short date even unopened | As above | As above |
| **Long-life milk (shelf-stable)** | **Refrigerate after opening, 2–3 days** | Room temperature until opened | As above |
| **Plain yoghurt (large tub)** | **3–4 days** | **Serve with a clean spoon; do not eat from the tub**. **Liquid on top (whey) is normal** | **Mould, pink or yellow discolouration, alcohol smell, strong sourness** |
| **Drinking yoghurt, single-serve yoghurt** | **Same day** once opened | — | — |
| **Processed cheese (slices, 6P wedges)** | **2–3 weeks** (individually wrapped pieces count as unopened) | Wrap tightly in cling film | Mould, dried and hardened |
| **Natural cheese (mozzarella, camembert, brie)** | **A few days to 1 week**. Mozzarella in water **2–3 days** | Cling film plus a sealed container | Mould other than the natural white rind; strong ammonia smell |
| **Hard cheese (parmesan, cheddar)** | **2–4 weeks** | Cling film plus a sealed container. **Mould can be cut away with a 2.5 cm margin** (see our separate article) | — |
| **Butter** | **About 1 month after opening** (fridge). **3–6 months frozen** | **Keep in the foil wrapper, away from strong-smelling foods** | Yellowing surface, rancid smell |
| **Fresh cream** | **2–3 days after opening** | Close the spout and store upright | Separation, sourness |
| **Condensed milk** | **2–3 weeks** | Clean spoon | Discolouration, crystallisation |
| **Infant formula (powder)** | **1 month after opening** (tin). **Once made up, use within 2 hours; discard leftovers** | Dry place; extra hygiene for infant formula | — |

- **Best-before vs use-by**: most milk carries a **"best-before" date (賞味期限, shōmi kigen: the date for best quality; unopened milk is often still drinkable a little past it)**, while **low-temperature pasteurised milk and the like carry a "use-by" date (消費期限, shōhi kigen: do not drink after it)**. **Both assume an unopened pack**; once opened, the table above takes precedence.
- **Why not the door pocket**: **the temperature rises every time the door opens, and can be 3–5°C higher than the body of the fridge**. **Milk and yoghurt go at the back**; use the door for condiments.
- **Pasteurisation methods**: **UHT (ultra-high temperature, 120–150°C for 2–3 seconds)** accounts for more than 90% of Japanese milk. **LTLT (low temperature, 63–65°C for 30 minutes)** tastes closer to fresh milk but keeps for less time. **"Whole" (成分無調整), "low-fat" (低脂肪) and "processed milk" (加工乳)** are separate categories unrelated to pasteurisation.
- **Leftover milk you have drunk from**: **bacteria from the mouth get in and it can turn sour by the next day**. Pour into a glass.
- **Freezing**: **milk separates when frozen and is not suitable for drinking after thawing** (fine for cooking). **Butter, hard cheese and pizza cheese freeze well**.
- **Children and pregnant women**: **some natural cheeses (unpasteurised imported soft cheeses) carry a Listeria risk**, so **pregnant women should check for a "heat-pasteurised" label**. Japanese processed cheese and most Japanese natural cheese are made from heat-pasteurised milk.
- **Dairy food poisoning**: the main causes are **long storage after opening, leaving dairy at room temperature (lunch boxes, in the car) and drinking from the container**. There are also warnings about **school-lunch milk left at room temperature in summer**.

> **[Verify]** The after-opening figures are general guides from makers and the industry body (Japan Dairy Industry Association). Check the pasteurisation-method shares and the Listeria warning against MHLW and Dairy Association material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Within the best-before date, it is fine even after opening

**The best-before date is a guarantee for the unopened pack; once opened, 2–3 days is the guide.** Finish it soon, whatever the date.

## Misunderstanding 2｜UHT milk keeps longer even after opening

**Only the unopened shelf life is longer; once opened, airborne bacteria get in and it is the same as any other milk.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜Yoghurt is fermented, so it cannot go off

**Bacteria other than the lactic-acid bacteria, and moulds, do grow.** 3–4 days after opening, with a clean spoon. Discard if there is mould, discolouration or an alcohol smell.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Milk belongs upright in the fridge door pocket

**The door pocket swings in temperature and is unsuitable for milk and yoghurt.** Put them at the back of the fridge.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Liquid on top of the yoghurt means it has gone off

**The liquid on top (whey) is normal separation, and it is nutritious too.** Stir it in. The signs of spoilage are mould, discolouration and smell.
