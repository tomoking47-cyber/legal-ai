# Q. Non-silicone, sulphate-free, amino-acid based… how do you read the words on a shampoo label?｜Japan Questions #561

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/shampoo-ingredients` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Japanese shampoo shelves are covered in appealing words: "ノンシリコン" (non-silicone), "サルフェートフリー" (sulphate-free), "アミノ酸系" (amino-acid based), "ボタニカル" (botanical). Most of the labelling is in Japanese only, so many people end up choosing by price and scent. This article explains what the words mean and how to read the ingredient list (全成分表示), which every product must carry.

## A. A shampoo's character is set by the **cleansing agents (surfactants)** listed right after "水" (water) in the ingredient list, and they fall into three main families. ① Sulphate-type (sodium laureth sulfate, sodium lauryl sulfate — the "sulphates"): strong cleansing, cheap, good lather, suited to oily scalps and heavy styling-product users, sometimes too strong for dry or sensitive scalps. ② Amino-acid type (sodium cocoyl glutamate, sodium lauroyl methylalanine, etc.): gentle cleansing, kind to the scalp, suited to dry scalps and coloured hair, higher price. ③ Betaine type (cocamidopropyl betaine): the mildest, common in children's shampoo. "Non-silicone" only tells you whether coating agents are present; it is a matter of preference depending on your hair, and there is no evidence that silicone harms the scalp.

## The three families of cleansing agents

| Family | Ingredient names to look for | Cleansing power | Suits | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sulphate type (higher-alcohol sulphates)** | **Sodium laureth sulfate (ラウレス硫酸Na), sodium lauryl sulfate (ラウリル硫酸Na), sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate (オレフィン(C14-16)スルホン酸Na)** — the last is not a sulphate but cleans just as strongly | **Strong** | **Oily scalp, heavy sweat or styling products, people who want a thorough wash** | **Over-strips** dry or sensitive skin; colour fades faster. **Not a safety problem** |
| **Amino-acid type** | **Sodium/TEA cocoyl glutamate (ココイルグルタミン酸Na／TEA), sodium lauroyl methylalanine (ラウロイルメチルアラニンNa), sodium cocoyl methyl taurate (ココイルメチルタウリンNa)** | **Gentle to medium** | **Dry or sensitive scalp, coloured or permed hair, daily washers** | Lather feels thin, price is high. **Styling products may not wash out fully** |
| **Betaine type (amphoteric)** | **Cocamidopropyl betaine (コカミドプロピルベタイン), lauramidopropyl betaine (ラウラミドプロピルベタイン)** | **Mildest** | **Children, sensitive skin.** Also blended with other agents to soften their sting | Not enough cleansing power on its own |
| **Soap type** | **Potassium soap base (カリ石ケン素地), potassium fatty acid salts (脂肪酸カリウム)** | Medium to strong (alkaline) | Soap devotees | **Alkalinity opens the cuticle and hair squeaks** → needs an acidic rinse |

## What the label words mean

| Label | Meaning | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| **ノンシリコン (non-silicone)** | **Contains no silicone (dimethicone, cyclopentasiloxane, etc.)** | Silicone coats the hair and improves feel. **There is no evidence it clogs scalp pores**, and the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association and dermatologists see no safety problem. **Fine hair or people wanting volume suit non-silicone; damaged or thick hair sits better with silicone** — a matter of preference |
| **サルフェートフリー (sulphate-free)** | No sulphate cleansing agents | Main agent is **amino-acid or betaine type**. Gentler, but "free" does not mean "safe" |
| **アミノ酸系 (amino-acid based)** | Amino-acid agents make up part of the cleansing base | **Can be claimed even if only a small part**, so judge by **what follows "水" (water) in the ingredient list** |
| **ボタニカル・オーガニック (botanical, organic)** | Contains plant-derived ingredients | **No legal definition in Japan.** The cleansing agents are usually synthetic. **Plant extracts can themselves cause contact rashes** |
| **弱酸性 (mildly acidic)** | pH around 5–6 | Same pH as scalp and hair. **Most shampoos are mildly acidic**, so it hardly sets a product apart |
| **薬用 (medicated) — quasi-drug (医薬部外品, a Japanese category between cosmetics and medicines)** | Contains an approved active ingredient (piroctone olamine, dipotassium glycyrrhizate, etc.) | Approved claim is **"prevents dandruff and itching"**. **Worth trying if you have dandruff or itch**, but seborrhoeic dermatitis and the like belong at a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance) |
| **ヘマチン・ケラチン配合 (with hematin, keratin)** | Suggests hair "repair" | **As a cosmetic, the permitted claim stops at "makes hair smooth".** "Repair" wording is restricted under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (Japan's cosmetics and drugs law) |

- **How to choose, in order**: ① **Scalp type** (oily → sulphate type is fine; dry or itchy → amino-acid or betaine type) ② **Hair concern** (lack of volume → non-silicone; dryness and frizz → silicone or amino-acid type) ③ **Check the two or three ingredients listed after "水" (water)** ④ **Use one bottle and judge by your scalp** (switch if itching or dryness appears).
- **How you wash matters more**: **pre-rinse for 1–2 minutes**, **lather in your hands before it touches the scalp**, **move the scalp with your fingertips (not nails)**, **rinse for twice as long as you washed**, **dry straight away**. These five points change dandruff, itch and smell more than the brand does.
- **Price**: amino-acid agents are generally described as costing **several times to ten times more than sulphates as raw materials**, which is why a bottle costs ¥1,500–3,000. **Sulphates are not bad; if your scalp is happy, they are the cheaper choice.**
- **Children**: **low-irritation betaine or amino-acid type** that does not sting the eyes. **Adult sulphate shampoos can strip too much.**
- **Persistent itch or dandruff**: if switching shampoo brings no improvement in 2–4 weeks, see **a dermatology clinic** (possible seborrhoeic dermatitis or contact dermatitis).

> **[Verify]** The classification of cleansing agents and the comparison of cleansing power are the general framework of cosmetic chemistry. Check the position on silicone safety against Japan Cosmetic Industry Association material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Silicone clogs scalp pores and is bad for hair

**There is no evidence for this, and the Japan Cosmetic Industry Association and dermatologists see no safety problem.** Choose non-silicone or not by whether you want volume or smoothness.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Sulphate-free is gentle for everyone

**Some people (oily scalp, heavy styling products) are not suited to gentle cleansing.** It means "mild cleansing power", not "safe".

## Misunderstanding 3｜If it says "amino-acid based", the main agent is amino-acid type

**The claim can be made even when only a small part is amino-acid type.** Judge by what comes after "水" (water) in the ingredient list.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Botanical or organic means kind to skin

**There is no legal definition, and plant extracts can cause contact rashes.** Looking at the cleansing agents is more reliable.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Switching to an expensive shampoo will settle dandruff and itching

**How you wash (pre-rinse, rinse, dry) has more effect, and persistent symptoms are a dermatologist's territory.** Fix the washing before the brand.
