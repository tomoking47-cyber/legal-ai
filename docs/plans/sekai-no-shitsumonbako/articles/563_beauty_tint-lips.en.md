# Q. Are lip tints bad for your lips? How the colour stays on, and what to watch for｜Japan Questions #563

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/tint-lips` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Lip tints (ティントリップ) spread through Japan during the mask years as "the lipstick that does not come off". The colour lasts for hours, but many users also complain of dry lips, colour that will not wash off, and peeling. This article explains how tints work and how to use them.

## A. A tint works by letting a dye soak into the outermost layer of the lip (the stratum corneum). Unlike ordinary lipstick, where pigment sits on the surface, the colour survives meals and masks. The weak points: ① tints are high in dyes and volatile ingredients (alcohol types) and low in oil, so lips dry easily; ② because the surface layer itself is dyed, any patch that peels loses its colour and the lips look blotchy; ③ people tend to rub hard when removing them. If you use one, moisturise with a lip balm first and apply thinly; to remove, hold a cotton pad soaked in point make-up remover on the lips for a few seconds and slide it off. Do not use a tint on cracked lips, and if soreness persists see a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance) for cheilitis. In products distributed through official channels in Japan, the colourants used are approved within the range permitted for cosmetics (products bought from overseas mail-order sites have been found to contain unapproved colourants).

## Tint versus lipstick

| Item | Tint | Ordinary lipstick or gloss |
|---|---|---|
| **How the colour works** | **Dye soaks into the outer skin layer** (a stain) | **Pigment sits on the surface** |
| **Wear** | **Several hours to half a day**; survives meals and masks | Comes off with a meal |
| **Texture** | Water types (light and thin), oil types, velvet types | Creamy, glossy |
| **Dryness** | **Dries easily** (volatile ingredients, little oil) | Protected by oils and waxes |
| **Removal** | **Often needs a dedicated remover** | Comes off with cleanser |
| **Blotchiness** | **Peeling patches lose colour and the outline bleeds** | Fades evenly |
| **Ingredient safety** | **Colourants permitted in cosmetics are legally listed colours (so-called tar colours, 法定色素) and other approved substances.** Some overseas products have used colourants not approved in Japan | Same |

- **How to apply**: ① **remove loose flakes first with a lip scrub or a warm damp towel** (without over-rubbing) ② **moisturise with lip balm and let it settle** (too much balm stops the tint taking, so keep it thin) ③ **apply thinly from the centre outwards and blur the outline** (against bleeding) ④ **wait a few seconds for it to set before pressing your lips together**.
- **How to remove**: **soak a cotton pad in oil-based point make-up remover, hold it on the lips for 5–10 seconds, then slide without rubbing.** **Use a cotton bud for the outline.** **If it will not come off, leave it until tomorrow** — forcing it rubs away the outer layer and causes peeling and darkening. **Always remove before bed** (dye left on for long periods is generally described as one cause of dullness).
- **Against dryness**: **layer balm over the tint** (shine and protection), **at night apply petroleum jelly (ワセリン, Vaseline) or lip balm generously**, **drink water**. **Water tints are the driest**, so oil tints and balm tints suit dry lips better.
- **Peeling and sore lips**: **stop licking, picking and rubbing**, **protect with petroleum jelly**, **use sun protection (an SPF lip balm)**. **Redness, cracks or scabs lasting more than 2 weeks** may be **cheilitis (contact dermatitis, atopic eczema, candida, etc.)** — see a dermatology clinic.
- **Darkening of the lips**: **the usual causes are friction, UV and dryness**; there is little solid evidence that tint dye itself stays in the skin. However, **rubbing during removal is friction**, so use a remover.
- **Tints bought from overseas online shops**: products containing **colourants or ingredients not approved in Japan** have come up in complaints to the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan. Choose products **with Japanese labelling (full ingredient list and the name of the marketing authorisation holder)**.
- **Children and teenagers**: the lip's outer layer is thin, so **dryness and soreness come easily**. If they use one, an oil tint applied thinly with balm on top.

> **[Verify]** The account of how tints colour and dry the lips is the general explanation of cosmetic science. Check colourant regulation (legally listed colours) against the MHLW ordinance "Ministerial Ordinance on tar colours permitted for use in pharmaceuticals etc."

## Misunderstanding 1｜A tint dyes your lips, so it must be bad for you

**Only approved colourants may be used, and what is dyed is the outer layer, which renews itself within days.** The real issues are dryness and friction during removal.

## Misunderstanding 2｜It does not come off, so there is no need to remove it before bed

**Dye left on for long periods, plus dryness, is one cause of dullness and soreness.** Remove with a remover and protect with petroleum jelly.

## Misunderstanding 3｜It is stubborn, so scrub it off

**Friction strips the outer layer and causes peeling and darkening.** Hold the remover on for 5–10 seconds and slide. If it will not come off, leave it until tomorrow.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Sore lips can be hidden with a tint

**Peeling patches lose colour and look blotchy, and the soreness gets worse.** When lips are sore, use balm only; if it persists, see a dermatologist.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Tints popular abroad are better than Japanese ones

**There have been complaints about products containing ingredients not approved in Japan.** Choose products with a full ingredient list in Japanese.
