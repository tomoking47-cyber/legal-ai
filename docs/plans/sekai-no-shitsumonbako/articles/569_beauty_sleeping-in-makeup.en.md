# Q. What actually happens if you sleep in your make-up? And how do you recover the next morning?｜Japan Questions #569

- Shelf: `beauty` ／ URL: `/en/beauty/sleeping-in-makeup` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

You get home exhausted and fall asleep in your make-up. In Japan you often hear that "one night in make-up ages your skin by X days". This article looks calmly at what really happens, how to recover the next morning, and the bare minimum for nights when you know you might not make it to the bathroom.

## A. If you sleep in your make-up, a layer of sebum, sweat, dust and make-up sits on the skin for a whole night (6–8 hours) and can cause clogged pores, spots, dullness and dryness. Eye make-up in particular can lead to darkening around the eyes and inflammation at the lash roots (blocked meibomian glands, styes). There is no scientific basis for "ages you by X days"; a one-off, followed by careful cleansing and moisturising the next morning, leaves no lasting harm. Make a habit of it and the difference shows. On nights you might fall asleep, the minimum is: cleanse the moment you get home (before changing clothes), or if you cannot manage that, use a cleansing wipe on at least the eyes and mouth.

## What happens when you sleep in make-up

| Area | What happens | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Whole face** | **A layer of sebum, sweat, dust and make-up** stays on the skin for 6–8 hours. **Interferes with skin renewal (most active at night)** | **Clogged pores, spots, dullness, dryness (the oil blocks water loss on one hand, while cleansing agents and alcohol in the make-up dry the skin on the other)** |
| **Eyes** | **Eyeliner and mascara block the oil glands at the lash roots (meibomian glands).** **Rubbing against bedding causes darkening** | **Styes, eyelid inflammation, dry eye, brown under-eye circles** |
| **Lips** | Lipstick or tint dries them out | Peeling |
| **Pillow and bedding** | Make-up transfers, **sebum and microbes multiply**, and you sleep on it again the next night | An indirect cause of facial spots and irritation |
| **"Ages you by X days"** | **No evidence.** One night causes no permanent change | **As a habit, differences in pores and skin condition do appear** |

## Recovering the next morning

1. **Cleanse carefully**: **oil or balm on dry skin, no rubbing, worked in for 40 seconds to 1 minute until it emulsifies** → rinse with lukewarm water. For eye make-up, **hold a cotton pad with point remover on the eye for 5–10 seconds first**.
2. **Face wash**: gentle foam. **Scrubbing, hot water or washing twice in a row** do harm.
3. **Moisturise**: **lotion (化粧水), then a slightly generous layer of milky emulsion (乳液) or cream.** A **sheet mask** is fine if the skin is dry, but if there is redness or stinging it will irritate — moisture only.
4. **That day's make-up**: **keep it light**, or **sunscreen plus point make-up only**, to rest the skin.
5. **Eyes**: if there is **redness, itching or a gritty feeling**, do not touch; if it persists, see an ophthalmology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance). **Wipe the eyelid margin gently with a cotton bud and lukewarm water** (lid hygiene).
6. **Change the pillowcase.**

## The bare minimum on nights you might fall asleep

| Situation | Minimum action |
|---|---|
| **Just home, still able to move** | **Go straight to the bathroom and cleanse before changing clothes.** "I'll do it after I change" never happens |
| **Collapsed on the sofa** | **One cleansing wipe each for eyes, mouth and the rest of the face** (see our separate article), **apply emulsion and sleep** |
| **Had a few drinks** | **Drink water and wipe at least the eyes.** Cleanse properly in the morning |
| **A night out is planned** | **Wear "easy to remove" make-up from the morning** (skip waterproof, choose lipstick over tint). **Carry wipes and a mini emulsion in your bag** |

- **When to take make-up off**: **as soon as you get home** is ideal, **before dinner** is fine. Dermatologists' rule of thumb **"make-up on for no more than 8–10 hours"** exists because beyond that the mixing of sebum and make-up progresses.
- **Washing again after cleansing**: one face wash after the cleanser is enough.
- **Remove sunscreen even on no-make-up days**: **sunscreen counts as make-up**; products that a face wash does not shift need a cleanser.
- **Unlike a child dozing off**, adult skin **takes longer to recover**, so an occasional lapse is fine but not a habit.

> **[Verify]** The link between blocked meibomian glands and eye make-up is the general explanation given in ophthalmology. Check against Japanese Ophthalmological Society and Japanese Dermatological Association material.

## Misunderstanding 1｜One night in make-up ages your skin by X days

**There is no evidence for the phrase.** But it is true that the layer of sebum and make-up causes clogged pores, spots and dullness, and as a habit the difference shows.

## Misunderstanding 2｜An occasional lapse needs no attention at all

**Careful cleansing and moisturising the next morning, plus a fresh pillowcase, and no real harm remains.** Do not touch red eyes.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Scrub hard the next morning to make up for it

**Friction and over-washing strain the skin more than the leftover make-up does.** Oil or balm without rubbing, then one foam wash.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Eye make-up does not get into the eyes, so leaving it is fine

**It blocks the oil glands at the lash roots and causes styes, eyelid inflammation and dry eye.** The eyes come first when you remove anything.

## Misunderstanding 5｜I'll take it off after I change

**Change, sit down, fall asleep — that is the classic sequence.** Straight home, bathroom before wardrobe.
