# Q. Which clothes pick up less pollen, and how do I keep pollen out of the house?｜Japan Questions #572

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/pollen-on-clothes` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

If you have hay fever (花粉症, kafunshō — in Japan mainly from cedar and cypress pollen in late winter and spring), the clothes you come home in are the pollen's transport. "Brush off at the door" and "avoid wool" are common sense in the Japanese pollen season. This article puts numbers on how much sticks to which fabric, and explains what to do about laundry and drying indoors.

## A. Pollen sticks to fabric through static electricity and surface texture. Wool, fleece and brushed fabrics pick it up easily; polyester and nylon with a smooth surface (slick, shiny outerwear) pick up far less. A Ministry of the Environment survey found that smooth-surfaced polyester collected a fraction of the pollen that cotton or wool did. The steps: (1) when you go out, wear a smooth-surfaced jacket as your outermost layer; (2) when you get home, brush your coat lightly outside the front door, take it off in the entrance hall (genkan) and do not bring it into the rooms — put a coat hook in the genkan; (3) dry laundry indoors, or if you dry outside, take it in at low-pollen times (early morning or night; take care the day after rain, when counts are high) and brush it first; (4) use anti-static spray or fabric softener to cut static; (5) do the same for hair and face.

## How much pollen each fabric picks up

| Fabric / surface | How much sticks | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wool, fleece, brushed fabrics (knits, corduroy, faux-fur "boa")** | **Very easily** | Pollen catches in the fibre texture; static builds easily | Do not wear as the outermost layer in pollen season. **Wear as an inner layer under a slick outer** |
| **Cotton (denim, twill)** | **Easily** | Textured surface | Brush off when you get home; wash often |
| **Polyester, nylon (smooth weave)** | **Not easily** (in a Ministry of the Environment test, around **1/5–1/10** of cotton) | Smooth, easy to brush off | **The standard pollen-season outer** (nylon coat, trench coat, mountain parka) |
| **Leather, synthetic leather, vinyl** | **Not easily** | Flat surface | Can be wiped |
| **Pollen-repellent finishes (fluorine, anti-static)** | **Not easily** | Anti-static plus smoothing | The effect fades with washing; top up with spray |

## Keeping pollen out of the house

| Place | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Outside the front door** | **Brush coat, hat and bag lightly by hand** (beating makes pollen fly up); **brush your hair lightly**. Keep a **sticky lint roller or pollen brush** by the door |
| **Inside the genkan (entrance hall)** | **Take your coat off in the genkan and hang it on a hook**. Do not bring it into the rooms. **Take off and bin your mask here too** |
| **Washbasin** | **Wash hands, gargle, wash face** (pollen on the face); if possible **rinse your hair in the shower** (more effective straight after coming home than at night) |
| **Changing** | **Keep outdoor clothes and indoor clothes separate**. Wash outdoor clothes the same day, or leave them by the genkan |
| **Bedroom** | **Never bring outdoor clothes into the bedroom**. **Do not air bedding outdoors** (if you do, brush it and wash the covers) |

- **Drying laundry**: **during the season, drying indoors is the rule**. **If you dry outside**, hang it **early morning to mid-morning when counts are low and bring it in before noon** (peaks are **around midday and in the evening**); **brush before bringing it in**; use **a tumble dryer, bathroom dryer or dehumidifier**. **Fabric softener and anti-static spray** reduce sticking.
- **High-count days**: **sunny and warm, windy, rain the day before** (Ministry of the Environment and Japan Weather Association forecasts). **Rainy days are low**, but **the next day it all flies at once**.
- **Does washing get pollen out?**: **most comes out in a normal water wash**. **Pollen allergens (proteins) are water-soluble**, so ordinary laundering rinses them away. But **pollen can transfer to wool from other items in the load**, so wash wool separately.
- **Car**: **brush off before getting in**; **set the air-con to recirculate and fit a pollen filter**.
- **Children**: **school blazers (wool blends)** — brush off in the genkan and hang on the hook. **Hats** too.
- **Pets**: **wipe them down after walks** (pollen sticks to fur).
- **Face and hair**: **hat, glasses and mask** reduce sticking. **Hair wax and spray attract pollen**, so cut down or tie hair up during the season. **Washing face and hair straight after coming home** works better than waiting until night.

> **[Verify]** The ratio of pollen picked up by each fabric is based on test results in the Ministry of the Environment's "Hay Fever Environmental Health Manual"; check peak dispersal times against Ministry of the Environment and Japan Weather Association materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Pollen sticks to clothes whatever the fabric

**Wool and fleece pick up several to 10 times as much as smooth polyester.** Make your outermost layer a slick fabric during the season.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Beating your coat hard at the door knocks the pollen off

**Beating sends pollen into the air and you breathe it in.** Brush lightly by hand, or use a lint roller or brush.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Laundry has to go in the sun or pollen stays on it

**Most pollen comes out in a water wash, and drying outside puts it back on.** Dry indoors during the season, or hang early and bring in before noon.

## Misunderstanding 4｜The day after rain has less pollen

**Pollen washed down by the rain all flies the next day, so counts are higher.** Watch out for sunny, warm, windy days and the day after rain.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Once I'm indoors pollen doesn't matter

**Pollen carried in on clothes, hair and bedding causes night-time symptoms.** Stop it at the genkan; keep outdoor clothes out of the bedroom.
