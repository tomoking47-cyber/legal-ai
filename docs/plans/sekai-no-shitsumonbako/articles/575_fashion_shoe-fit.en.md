# Q. How do I choose shoes that won't hurt my feet? Tips for trying them on｜Japan Questions #575

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/shoe-fit` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

"The size is right, but they hurt." "My shoes feel tight by evening." Feet differ not only in length but in width, instep height and toe shape. Japanese shoes are sized in centimetres of foot length, and many also carry a width code — "E, 2E, 3E" — that you may not have seen elsewhere. Here are tips for trying shoes on, and fixes for pain by cause.

## A. Choose shoes on four points: length, width (ウィズ, "width"), heel hold and toe room. Try them on in the evening when feet are swollen, in the socks you will wear, on both feet, and walk around the shop. Check that the heel does not lift, that there is about 1 cm of space beyond your toes (捨て寸, sute-zun, the "allowance"), that the width is not squeezed from the sides, and that the instep does not hurt. Japanese feet are often described as wide with a high instep, but in reality individual variation is large. If the width is wrong and you "go up a size", the shoe becomes too long, the heel lifts and you get a different pain. For width problems, look for brands that offer the same length in different widths (2E, 3E, 4E), or laced designs you can adjust. If your feet swell, assume they change by around 0.5 cm between morning and evening.

## How to try shoes on

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Time of day** | **Evening or later** (some people's feet swell by **0.5 to nearly 1 cm**) | Fitted in the morning, tight by evening |
| **2. Socks** | **The socks you will wear with that shoe** (thick if thick) | Sock thickness changes the fit by **0.5 cm** |
| **3. Both feet** | **Left and right differ** (by a few mm in most people), so **fit the larger foot** | Trying one shoe is a recipe for failure |
| **4. Heel** | **Push your heel back, then fasten laces or straps**. **Walk; the heel should not lift** | Heel lifting = too long, or too wide |
| **5. Toes** | **0.5–1 cm of room (sute-zun) beyond the longest toe**. **Toes can move** | The foot slides forward about **1 cm** when walking |
| **6. Width** | **The base of the little toe and big toe are not pressed from the sides**. **The instep is not squeezed** | The wrong width causes **bunions and calluses** |
| **7. Walk** | **Walk around the shop for a few minutes**; **squat, try stairs** (if there are any) | Standing still tells you nothing |
| **8. Measure your feet** | **Once a year, on the shop's foot gauge** (**adult feet change with age, weight and pregnancy**) | "I've always been 24 cm" is not reliable |

## Width codes

| Code | Meaning | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **JIS (Japan)** | **A to E, 2E (EE), 3E, 4E, F, G**, in increasing width. **E–2E is standard**; **3E and above is wide** | Set by foot girth (around the base of the big and little toes) and foot length |
| **Overseas** | **B (women's standard), D (men's standard), W or EE (wide)** etc. | Correspondence with Japanese codes is approximate |
| **No code** | Most fashion shoes and trainers have no width code | Check by trying on. **Each brand uses a different last** |

## Causes of pain and what to do

| Symptom | Cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Heel rubs, blisters** | Heel lifts (too long or too wide); hard material | **Heel pads**; **plasters or anti-blister tape beforehand**; **break leather in gradually (short wears first)** |
| **Pain at the base of the little toe or big toe** | **Too narrow** | A wider width size; **shoe stretcher** (leather only); **do not go up a size** (it only gets longer) |
| **Toes hit the front** | Too short; **not enough sute-zun**; **downhill walking or swelling** | 0.5 cm larger; **lace firmly to hold the foot back** |
| **Instep hurts** | Low-cut instep; laces too tight | A higher-instep model; **change the lacing (skip the eyelets over the instep)** |
| **Sole or heel of the foot hurts** | Too little cushioning; **too little arch support**; worn too long | **Insoles** (see separate article); check sole wear |
| **Tight in the evening** | Swelling | Try on in the evening; shoes with adjustable laces or straps; **rest with your feet raised** |
| **Calluses, corns** | **Repeated** wear of shoes that are wrong in width or shape | Rethink your shoes; have them pared at a dermatology clinic (covered by Japanese health insurance) |

- **Children's shoes**: **check the size every 3–6 months** (feet grow **1–2 cm a year**). **Buying big** causes trips and foot deformity. **Sute-zun 0.5–1 cm**; **firm heel, wide toe box, flexes at the ball of the foot**.
- **Heels**: **3–5 cm** is the usual guide for less strain on the foot. If your toes hurt from **sliding forward**, use **a toe cushion**.
- **Buying online**: **try on unless you already know the same brand and model**. Use **shops that accept returns** (see separate article).
- **Ongoing foot problems**: **bunions, flat feet and splayed feet** may not be solved by shoe choice alone. See **an orthopaedic clinic (整形外科, foot surgery)**, and get measured at **a specialist shoe shop with a certified shoe fitter**.

> **[Verify]** Check the swelling range, the sute-zun guide and the JIS foot-girth width classes against the shoe-fitter association (FHA) materials and JIS S5037.

## Misunderstanding 1｜If the size (cm) matches, the shoe fits

**Width, instep and heel hold must fit too, or it hurts.** Check all four points when trying on.

## Misunderstanding 2｜If the width is tight, go up a size

**The shoe gets longer, the heel lifts, and you get a different pain.** Try a wider width (2E, 3E) or another brand.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Trying on in the morning is more accurate

**Feet swell by evening, so an evening fitting is closer to real life.**

## Misunderstanding 4｜Leather stretches, so buy them tight

**Width stretches a little; length does not.** Without sute-zun your toes will keep hitting the front even after the leather gives.

## Misunderstanding 5｜My foot size never changes

**Age, weight, pregnancy and loss of muscle change foot length and width.** Measure once a year.
