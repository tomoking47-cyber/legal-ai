# Q. Can I wash curtains? How often, and how do I stop them shrinking?｜Japan Questions #576

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/washing-curtains` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Curtains are one of the household items people rarely think of as washable, and many homes go years without washing them. Yet a curtain is a large piece of cloth that soaks up dust, pollen, exhaust fumes, mould from window condensation and cigarette smoke. In Japan the curtains in a rented flat are usually yours, not the landlord's, so their care is yours too. Here is how, and how often, to wash them.

## A. Most curtains can be washed at home. Check the care label; if it allows washing, remove the hooks (or fold the hook tape inward), fold the curtain into a large laundry net, wash on a gentle or delicates cycle, spin briefly (30 seconds to 1 minute) and hang it straight back on the curtain rail while still wet to dry. This is the best way to avoid shrinkage and creases. Frequency: lace (sheer) curtains 2–3 times a year, heavy (drape) curtains once a year. Cotton, linen and rayon shrink; polyester hardly does. Mould comes out with oxygen bleach, and blackout, flame-retardant and shape-memory finishes may weaken with washing. Curtains labelled do-not-wash, silk, velvet and heavy lined curtains go to the dry cleaner.

## How often, and how

| Curtain | Frequency | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Lace (sheer) curtains** | **2–3 times a year** (after pollen season, after the rainy season, at year end) | Nearest the window, so they catch the most **pollen, exhaust fumes and condensation mould** |
| **Heavy (drape) curtains** | **Once a year** (year end or spring) | Dust and odour build-up |
| **Kitchen, smokers' rooms** | **More often** | Grease and smoke odours |
| **Mould has appeared** | Straight away | Left alone it roots into the fibres |

### Steps

1. **Check the care label**. **Do-not-wash, silk, velvet and lined curtains** go to the cleaner.
2. **Remove the hooks** (or, if that is too much trouble, **fold the hook tape inward and hold it with rubber bands**; removing is safer). **Shake off the dust** (a light shake outside, or the vacuum brush).
3. **Fold into a large laundry net** (**accordion folds** reduce uneven washing). **One panel at a time**.
4. **Gentle or delicates ("dry") cycle**, **neutral delicates detergent**. **Water below 30°C (86°F)**.
5. **Spin for 30 seconds to 1 minute**.
6. **Hang back on the original rail while wet to dry**. **The weight pulls out the creases, and you avoid the shrinkage a dryer or the sun would cause**. **Open the window for air**. Put **towels or newspaper under heavy curtains** (drips).
7. **Leave until fully dry (half a day to a day)**.

## Notes by fabric and finish

| Fabric / finish | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Polyester** | **Hardly shrinks; easy to wash**. Most curtains are polyester |
| **Cotton, linen** | **Shrinks (2–5%)**. **If the length has no spare, dry clean** or **adjust the hem for shrinkage before washing** |
| **Rayon, acetate** | **Weak in water; shrinks and creases**. Dry cleaning recommended |
| **Blackout, heat-insulating, flame-retardant finishes** | Some products **lose their effect gradually with washing**. Check for "effect retained after washing" on the label. **Flame-retardant labels** may state a limit on the number of washes |
| **Shape-memory (wave) finish** | **The waves may weaken with washing**. **Hanging without spinning** helps preserve them |
| **Embroidery, fine lace** | Net essential; hand-wash cycle |
| **Mould** | **Dissolve oxygen bleach (酸素系漂白剤, sanso-kei hyōhakuzai) in 40°C (104°F) water and soak for 30 minutes to 1 hour** (**after a colour test**) → wash. **Chlorine bleach yellows polyester and strips colour**, so use it only on white lace, and briefly |

- **Hooks**: remove **adjuster hooks** and **wash them in detergent solution** to shift the dust. **Watch for rust on metal hooks**.
- **Clean the rail**: while the curtains are down, **wipe the condensation mould off the rail and window frame**.
- **Tobacco and cooking smells**: **soak for 30 minutes in baking-soda (重曹, jūsō) water** → wash, to reduce them.
- **Curtain lifespan**: **5–10 years**. **The back deteriorates in the sun and tears**, and **blackout weakens** — signs to replace. **UV damages the window-facing side**, so lace curtains especially.
- **When replacing, buy for washability**: **a "washable" label, all-polyester fabric, hooks that come off easily**, and **2–3 cm of spare length** (for shrinkage).
- **Renting**: curtains belong to the tenant, and you **take them with you when you move out** (the rail is a fixture).

> **[Verify]** Follow the maker's label for shrinkage rates and the wash durability of flame-retardant and blackout finishes. Check how to read care labels against Consumer Affairs Agency materials.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Curtains don't need washing

**They are large cloths that have soaked up pollen, exhaust fumes, mould and odour.** Lace 2–3 times a year, heavy once a year.

## Misunderstanding 2｜They'll shrink, so they can't be washed

**Polyester hardly shrinks, and most curtains can be washed at home.** For cotton, linen and rayon, judge by the label and the spare length.

## Misunderstanding 3｜The tumble dryer is quicker

**It causes shrinkage, creases and damage to finishes.** Hanging back on the rail wet is the easiest way and gives the best finish.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Blast the mould with chlorine bleach

**It yellows polyester and strips colour.** Soak in oxygen bleach instead.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Blackout curtains work just as well however often you wash them

**Some finishes weaken gradually with washing.** Check the label for "effect after washing" and wash less often.
