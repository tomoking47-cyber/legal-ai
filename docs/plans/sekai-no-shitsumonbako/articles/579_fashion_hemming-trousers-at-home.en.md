# Q. Can I hem trousers myself? What do shops charge?｜Japan Questions #579

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/hemming-trousers-at-home` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

When you buy trousers in Japan, most shops will hem them for you before you take them home. Online purchases and second-hand clothes leave you to deal with the length yourself, and many people have no sewing machine and would rather use hemming tape. Here are the methods step by step, and what alteration shops charge.

## A. There are four options: (1) ask a shop — free to ¥1,000 at the shop you bought from, ¥1,000–3,000 at an alterations shop (お直し, onaoshi), ¥2,000–3,500 for a chain-stitch hem on jeans; (2) iron-on hemming tape (about ¥300, 30 minutes, may peel in the wash); (3) hand-sew a blind hem (まつり縫い, matsuri-nui) or slip stitch (30 minutes to 1 hour per pair, needing only a needle and thread); (4) a sewing machine. Decide the length standing in your shoes: for suit trousers, the hem rests lightly on the top of the shoe with a small break ("half cushion"); for jeans, to taste — slightly long or at the ankle. Before cutting, always pin or clip and try on, and leave a 3–4 cm hem allowance. Jeans can also be turned up (rolled) instead of cut.

## Deciding the length

| Garment | Guide |
|---|---|
| **Suit trousers (business)** | **Standing in your shoes, the hem lightly touches the top of the shoe with one small break at the front ("half cushion")**. At the back, **between the top and the middle of the heel counter**. **No break** for slim or fashion-forward cuts |
| **Jeans** | **To taste**. The classic is **resting on the shoe opening, or with a 1–2 cm fold**. **Slim cuts can be ankle-length (cropped)** |
| **Chinos, casual** | Between suit trousers and jeans |
| **Children** | **Leave growing room in the allowance** (3–5 cm, a "let-down hem"). **School uniforms: a generous allowance for a double hem** |
| **Wide, flared** | Longer (**1–2 cm** off the floor); **decided by the heel height of the shoes you wear with them** |

## Hand-sewing steps (blind hem)

1. **Try on, decide the length, and mark with tailor's chalk or pins**. **Always in shoes**.
2. **Leave a 3–4 cm allowance and cut with fabric shears** (over-cutting cannot be undone; leave it long). **Zigzag-stitch or use fray-stop liquid** on the raw edge.
3. **Fold the hem inside and press the crease** (3–4 cm).
4. **From the inside, blind-hem just inside the fold**: **pick up only 1–2 threads of the outer fabric**, and 2–3 mm on the allowance side. **Thread in the same colour as the fabric**. **Stitches 1 cm apart**.
5. **Press and you are done**. The aim is **almost no visible stitching on the outside**.

- **Hemming jeans**: **chain stitch** (matches the original stitching; washing brings out a "roping" fade) **needs a special machine**, so use an alterations shop. **A single (ordinary straight) stitch** is fine too. **Reattaching the original hem ("original hem finish", 元裾仕様)** costs +¥1,000–2,000.
- **Keep the cut-off hem**: **you cannot restore the length**, so keep the spare fabric for repairs.
- **Decide after washing**: **cotton and denim shrink 1–3 cm in the first wash**. **Wash new trousers once before hemming**.
- **Removing hemming tape**: **warm with the iron and peel → remove leftover adhesive with ethanol**. **A mark usually remains**.
- **When buying**: **shops with free hemming**; **shops that stock different lengths (S, M, L length)**; **online shops with a hemming service**.

> **[Verify]** The prices are guidelines based on typical price lists of alterations shops and mass retailers, and vary by shop.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Hemming tape lasts once it's on

**It peels in the wash, stiffens and leaves a mark.** It suits urgent, short-term use. Sew if you will wear them for long.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Measure the length sitting down

**Decide standing, in your shoes.** Hems rise when you sit.

## Misunderstanding 3｜Cut jeans first, wash later

**They shrink 1–3 cm in the first wash.** Wash once, then hem.

## Misunderstanding 4｜If I cut too much I can let out the allowance

**Cut fabric does not come back.** Leave 3–4 cm, and more for children.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Hand-sewing looks bad

**A blind hem picks up only 1–2 threads on the outside, so it is almost invisible.** 30 minutes to an hour per pair, and with a little practice the finish is perfectly neat.
