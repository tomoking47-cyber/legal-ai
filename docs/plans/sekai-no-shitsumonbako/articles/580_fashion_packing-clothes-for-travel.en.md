# Q. How do I pack clothes for a trip so they take less space and arrive uncreased?｜Japan Questions #580

- Shelf: `fashion` ／ URL: `/en/fashion/packing-clothes-for-travel` ／ Status: **Draft — English edition, sources unverified**

Whether it is a weekend away or a business trip, clothes are bulky and arrive creased. Japanese travellers have settled on a set of tricks — rolling, compression bags, the hotel-shower steam method — that cut volume and creases. Here they are, with a guide to how much to pack by number of nights.

## A. To cut creases and volume: (1) choose crease-resistant fabrics (polyester blends, wool, knits; avoid all-cotton or linen shirts, or take just one); (2) plan to mix and match — keep to 2–3 colours, take bottoms for half the days, tops for every day, and underwear and socks for every day or 3 days' worth if you will wash; (3) fold by type — roll T-shirts, knits and underwear, and lay shirts and jackets flat on top; (4) sort into packing cubes or compression bags (over-compressing causes creases); (5) unpack and hang as soon as you arrive; creases come out overnight with shower steam, or a mist of water plus a hanger. Up to 3 nights, a carry-on-size bag (around 40 L) is usually enough.

## How much to pack, by nights

| Nights | Tops | Bottoms | Underwear, socks | Outer layer | Shoes | Bag size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2 nights** | 2–3 | 1 (the pair you wear + 0–1) | One per day | 1 (worn) | The pair you wear | **20–30 L** (backpack, holdall) |
| **3–4 nights** | 3–4 | 2 | One per day, or 3 days' worth if washing | 1 | 1–2 pairs | **35–45 L** (carry-on size) |
| **5–7 nights** | 4–5 (washing assumed) | 2–3 | 3–4 days' worth + washing | 1 | 2 pairs | **50–65 L** |
| **Longer** | A week's worth, washing assumed | 3 | 3–4 days' worth | 1–2 | 2 pairs | 65 L or more, or wash locally |

## Folding and packing to avoid creases

| Garment | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **T-shirts, knits, underwear, socks, pyjamas** | **Roll** (fold the sleeves in, then roll from the bottom) | No fold lines; fits into gaps |
| **Shirts, blouses** | **Button up and fold flat**; **put rolled socks or underwear inside the collar to hold its shape**; **lay flat on top** | Rolling crushes the collar |
| **Jackets** | **Turn inside out, match the shoulders and fold in half (shoulders inside)**, or **wear it while travelling**. Use a **garment case** if you have one | Protects the outer fabric and keeps the shoulder shape |
| **Trousers, skirts** | **Fold in half along the seams, or in thirds, at the bottom of the case** | On the flat base |
| **Dresses, suits** | **Fold inside a dry-cleaner's polythene cover or a garment bag** (the plastic slides, so creases form less easily) | **A hanging garment bag** is the surest |
| **Shoes** | **Wrap in a shoe bag or shower cap, at the edge of the base**. **Stuff with socks or chargers** | Keeps dirt off; uses the space |

## Packing order and kit

1. **Bottom (wheel side): heavy items** — shoes, toiletries, trousers (rolled).
2. **Middle**: rolled clothes, packing cubes.
3. **Top**: shirts, jackets and dresses laid flat. **Take them out as soon as you arrive**.
4. **Gaps**: rolled socks and underwear, chargers.
5. **Liquids in a sealed bag near the top** (where a leak is less likely to reach clothes, or in the gaps inside shoes).

- **Packing cubes**: sort into **tops / bottoms / underwear / worn**, and at the hotel **drop each cube straight into a drawer**. **Mesh** lets you see the contents.
- **Compression bags**: **good for underwear, T-shirts and knits**; **not for shirts or jackets, because compression sets the creases**. Take one **for dirty laundry on the way home**.
- **De-creasing on the spot**: **run a hot shower for a few minutes to fill the bathroom with steam, then hang the garment there for 30 minutes to overnight**. **Mist with water (a spray bottle from the 100-yen shop, or an empty toner-spray bottle), smooth by hand and hang**. **The hotel iron** (often lent out at the front desk). **Travel-size crease-release spray**.
- **Fabric choice**: **wool (recovers), polyester blends (crease-resistant), knits, jersey**. **All-cotton and all-linen shirts hold creases**. **"Shape-retaining" (形態安定, keitai antei) shirts** (see separate article) are the business traveller's friend.
- **Colours**: **2 base colours (black, navy, grey, beige) + 1 accent**, so that everything goes with everything.
- **Washing on the trip**: **for 3 nights or more, wash locally** (hotel laundry, coin laundry, or hand-wash underwear in the basin — quick-dry fabrics rolled in a bath towel dry overnight). Pack **travel detergent (single-dose sachets), a washing line and pegs** in a small pouch.
- **Going home**: **dirty clothes in the compression bag**; **leave 10–20% of the space free for souvenirs on the way out**.
- **Weight**: guide figures are **20 kg for checked baggage on domestic flights (15 kg or less on low-cost carriers, and charged for)** and **10 kg with total dimensions of 115 cm for carry-on** (varies by airline).

> **[Verify]** Check the bag-capacity guides and the airlines' weight and size limits against each airline's current rules.

## Misunderstanding 1｜Compress clothes as small as possible

**Compression sets creases into shirts and jackets.** Compress only underwear, T-shirts and knits.

## Misunderstanding 2｜Without a hotel iron, creases can't be fixed

**Bathroom steam, or a mist of water plus a hanger overnight, takes out most creases.**

## Misunderstanding 3｜You need bottoms and shoes for every day

**Bottoms for half the days and no more than 2 pairs of shoes will mix and match.** Keeping to 2–3 colours is the condition.

## Misunderstanding 4｜Cotton shirts are the easiest

**All-cotton and all-linen fabrics hold creases.** For travel, polyester blends, wool and knits are easier.

## Misunderstanding 5｜Leave the suitcase as it is when you arrive

**Just taking out your shirts and jackets and hanging them on arrival cuts next-morning creases a great deal.**
