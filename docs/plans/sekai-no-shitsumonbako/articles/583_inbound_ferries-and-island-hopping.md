# Q. How do ferries work in Japan? Island-hopping, car ferries and the long-distance routes｜Japan Questions #583

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/ferries-and-island-hopping` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan is 14,000 islands, and ferries are how people reach most of the 400 inhabited ones — from 5-minute hops in the Seto Inland Sea to 40-hour voyages to Okinawa. Visitors overlook them, then discover the ferries are cheap, punctual and often the most beautiful part of the trip.

## A. Ferries range from small municipal boats (¥200–1,500, buy at the pier, no reservation) to overnight long-distance liners with cabins, baths and restaurants (Tokyo–Tokushima–Kitakyushu, Osaka–Beppu, Maizuru–Otaru, Kagoshima–Naha) costing ¥10,000–25,000 in a shared room. Reservations matter for cars and cabins, rarely for foot passengers on short hops; timetables are seasonal and cancellations happen for typhoons and rough seas, so build slack. The Setouchi art islands (Naoshima, Teshima, Shodoshima) are the best-known visitor circuit; the Japan Rail Pass covers only the JR Miyajima ferry. Take motion-sickness pills for the open-sea crossings.

- **Cabin classes on overnight ferries**: **2nd class carpet (shared open room, blanket, ¥8,000–12,000)**, **2nd class bunk (curtained berth)**, **1st class (2–4 berth cabin)**, **special class (private with bath)**. **Carpet class is the traditional budget option and fine for one night.**
- **Cars and bicycles**: **car ferries need reservations**; **bicycles cost ¥500–2,000** and are why the **Shimanami Kaidō** cyclists use island ferries for shortcuts.
- **Cancellations**: **typhoons (July–October), winter gales on the Japan Sea, spring fog**. Operators post **運航状況 (operating status)** on their sites by 06:00; **jetfoils cancel at lower wave heights than conventional ferries**. **Never book the last boat off an island the day before a flight.**
- **Motion sickness**: **Kagoshima–Yakushima, Tokyo–Izu islands, Sado in winter** are notoriously rough. **Take medication 30–60 minutes before departure** (see the motion-sickness article), sit low and central, go outside for air.
- **JR Pass**: covers **JR Miyajima ferry** only. **Setouchi and Kyushu passes** sometimes include specific ferries.
- **Setouchi Triennale islands**: **Naoshima, Teshima, Inujima, Shodoshima** from **Takamatsu and Uno**; buy the **Setouchi Islands ferry pass** during the triennale; **last boats are early and museums close Mondays**.
- **Food and facilities**: long-distance ferries have **restaurants (¥1,000–2,000 a meal), large baths with sea views, coin laundry, Wi-Fi (patchy at sea)**. Short ferries: bring your own.
- **Etiquette**: **shoes off in carpet class**, **quiet after 22:00**, **no smoking except designated decks**, **don't feed the gulls at Miyajima** (they take food from hands).
- **Safety**: Japan's ferry safety record is strong; after the 2022 Shiretoko sightseeing-boat sinking, **small tour boats** were re-regulated — book licensed operators and check life-jacket provision on small craft.
