# Q. Capsule hotels, manga cafés and other places to sleep cheap in Japan: what should visitors know?｜Japan Questions #584

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/capsule-hotels-and-manga-cafes` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A capsule hotel bed in central Tokyo for ¥3,000–5,000, a reclining seat in a 24-hour manga café for ¥2,000, a business hotel single for ¥9,000–13,000: Japan has a layered market of cheap, clean places to sleep that visitors either romanticise or fear. Here is how each actually works and who it suits.

## A. Capsule hotels are safe, clean and increasingly stylish — expect a 2 m × 1 m pod with a blind or door, a locker for your bag, a shared bath and a strict quiet culture; they suit solo travellers for a night or two but are not for large suitcases, couples, or light sleepers. Manga/internet cafés (Kaikatsu Club, Jiyu Kukan) rent private booths with a reclining chair or flat mat, free drinks, showers and thousands of comics by the hour or in "night packs" (8–12 hours, ¥2,000–3,500); they are legal to sleep in and used by locals who missed the last train, but a mat on the floor is not a bed. Business hotels (APA, Toyoko Inn, Dormy Inn) at roughly ¥8,000–13,000 in the big cities are the real value layer for anyone who wants a door and a private bathroom. Hostels sit between.

## The options compared

- **Capsule etiquette**: **silence after 22:00** (no phone calls, no rustling bags), **use the locker key on a wristband**, **pyjamas/room wear provided — wear them in common areas**, **no eating in the pod**, **the bath is used before bed** (see the onsen article), **check out by 10:00 and re-check-in if staying two nights** (some let you keep the locker).
- **Manga café practicalities**: **sign up at the counter with ID** (passport works), **choose "flat seat" for sleeping**, **the night pack is cheapest when started after 20:00–23:00**, **towels and toiletries sold at the counter**, **lockers are small**, **some branches are men-only after midnight — ask**. **Kaikatsu Club** is the largest chain and English-friendlier.
- **Who lives there**: manga cafés are also used by **"net café refugees"** — people without housing — a fact of the cities that visitors should understand rather than photograph.
- **Safety**: both are **very safe**; theft is rare but **use the locker**. **Women-only capsule floors** and **women's areas in net cafés** exist in most big-city branches.
- **Booking**: capsules on **Booking.com, Rakuten Travel, Jalan**; manga cafés are **walk-in** (peak nights — Friday, festivals — fill by 01:00).
- **Luggage**: capsule lockers are **~40 × 40 × 60 cm**; **send big suitcases ahead** (see the luggage-forwarding article) or use the front-desk luggage room.
- **Alcohol**: after-drink guests are the norm; **vomiting in the pod costs a cleaning fee (¥10,000+)**.
