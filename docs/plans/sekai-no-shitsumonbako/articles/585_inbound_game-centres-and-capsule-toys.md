# Q. Game centres, crane games and gachapon: how do visitors play without wasting money?｜Japan Questions #585

- Shelf: `inbound` ／ URL: `/en/questions/game-centres-and-capsule-toys` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan's 4,000 game centres and 300,000-plus capsule-toy machines are a genuine attraction, and also a place where visitors drop ¥5,000 on a plush they could buy for ¥1,500 next door. The machines are not rigged in the Western sense — they are regulated — but the skill and the settings are real.

## A. Crane games (UFO catchers) are legal amusement machines, not gambling; prizes are limited to about ¥1,000 in value under National Police Agency guidance (raised from ¥800 in 2022), and staff are expected to help — ask "ちょっと手伝ってもらえますか" and they will often reposition a prize or show the trick for that machine. Set a budget per prize (¥500–1,000), watch the machine for a few plays first, learn the two or three standard techniques (pushing, hooking a tag, sliding a box off a ledge), and stop when you hit the budget. Capsule toys (gachapon) are ¥300–500 a turn with no skill involved; buy where the set you want is, not from the first machine you see. Photo booths (purikura) and rhythm games are the other visitor favourites; note the smoking rules and closing times (most centres shut 23:00–24:00, minors out by 18:00–22:00 depending on age).

## Crane games (UFO catchers)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Legal status** | Amusement machines under the Entertainment Business Act; **prizes must be worth about ¥1,000 or less** (industry guidance on the legal limit) — so the plush in the machine is not a ¥5,000 item |
| **Cost** | **¥100–200 per play**, often **¥500 for 6 plays**; big machines ¥200–300 |
| **Are they "rigged"?** | **Claw strength and settings are adjustable and vary by machine**; operators set a target payout rate. **Not random** — a given machine is consistently strong or weak, which is why watching helps |
| **Staff help** | **Ask staff** (店員さん). It is normal for them to **move the prize to a winnable position** after a few plays or **demonstrate the intended technique**. Some chains have a "**we'll help after ¥X**" sign |
| **Techniques** | **Push (押し)**: nudge a box over the edge. **Hook (フック)**: catch the tag/loop with one claw tip. **Slide (ずらし)**: move a box across the ledge in steps. **Bridge (橋渡し)**: a box across two bars — tilt one end at a time |
| **Budget rule** | **Decide ¥500–1,000 per prize; if you've spent that and it's not visibly closer, stop** — or ask staff, who can often finish it |
| **Where the prizes are cheaper** | **Shops next door (Don Quijote, secondhand shops) sell the same prizes for ¥800–2,000** |

## Other machines

| Machine | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gachapon (capsule toys)** | **¥300–500 per turn** (¥100–200 for older machines) | **No skill**. Sets have 5–8 variants; **duplicates are the trade-off**. Big halls (Gashapon Department Store in Sunshine City Ikebukuro, Gachapon Kaikan in Akihabara) have thousands of machines. Coins only — **change machines** nearby; some take IC cards now |
| **Purikura (photo booths)** | **¥400–500** for 4–6 shots printed as stickers; digital download | **Booth areas are often women/couples only** in Tokyo (solo men barred); **shoes stay on**; **heavy skin filters** are the aesthetic |
| **Rhythm games (Taiko, maimai, Chunithm, DDR)** | **¥100–200 per credit** | **Wait your turn (queue by placing a coin or card)**; **wipe the machine after** (wipes provided). Amusement IC cards (Aime, Banapass) save scores |
| **Medal games** | Buy medals ¥100 = 10–20 medals | **Cannot be exchanged for money or prizes** — pure time-killers. That's the law |
| **Retro cabinets (Mikado Takadanobaba)** | ¥50–100 | Fighting-game culture; watch the etiquette (don't join a stranger's match without a nod) |

- **Hours and minors**: most centres **10:00–24:00**; **under-16s out by 18:00, under-18s by 22:00** under prefectural youth ordinances (parents accompanying changes some rules). **Smoking**: separate rooms or banned indoors since 2020.
- **Etiquette**: **don't hog a machine while friends watch**, **no photos of other players**, **don't lean on cabinets**, **return baskets/mallets**.
- **Big chains**: **GiGO (ex-Sega), Taito Station, Round1 (with bowling), Namco**. **Akihabara, Ikebukuro (Sunshine City), Osaka Nipponbashi, Nagoya Osu** are the hubs.
- **Prizes home**: **plush toys are bulky** — the forwarding counter at big centres will **ship prizes domestically**; for abroad, use Japan Post.
- **Value check**: a **¥1,000 budget for one prize** is roughly the item's retail cap; **beyond that you're paying for the game, which is fine if you enjoy it**.

> **[Verify]** The prize-value guidance figure (approx. ¥1,000) and its legal basis; youth curfew hours by prefecture; current play prices at major chains.
