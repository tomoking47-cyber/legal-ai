# Q. What do people get wrong about fūrin, the Japanese summer wind chime?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #591

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/furin-wind-chimes` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

A glass or iron bell with a strip of paper hanging from the clapper, tinkling under the eaves: the fūrin is Japan's sound of summer, sold at July fairs and hung at the first hot week. It looks like decoration. It is a cooling device that works on the mind, a reminder of air movement, and — in a modern apartment block — a noise-complaint waiting to happen.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a fūrin cools nothing. Studies in Japan have measured a small drop in perceived temperature and skin temperature when people hear the chime — the sound signals "wind" and the brain responds — which is why it hangs where the breeze is, not where it looks pretty. The second misunderstanding is that everyone loves the sound: in dense housing the neighbour's chime at 03:00 is a classic complaint, and many buildings ask residents to take them in at night or in typhoon season. Hang it in a windy spot you can hear from inside, bring it in when the wind picks up, and put it away in September — the Edo tradition is that a fūrin out of season is bad manners.

## What it is and how to use it

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Types** | **Edo fūrin**: hand-blown glass, painted inside, **¥1,500–4,000**; **Nambu tekki**: cast iron (Iwate), long clear ring, ¥2,000–5,000; **ceramic, bronze (Takaoka), bamboo**. Each has a distinct pitch and decay |
| **The paper (tanzaku)** | Catches the wind and moves the clapper; **often a poem or wish**. Replace when torn — a fūrin without a tanzaku barely rings |
| **The "cooling"** | A 2010s Japanese experiment found **skin temperature fell ~0.5–1°C** in subjects who heard a fūrin in a warm room; **perceived temperature dropped** more. **Association, not physics** — but real enough that air-conditioner makers have used chime sounds |
| **Where to hang** | **Under the eaves or at a window that gets the evening breeze**, on the **south or east side**; **inside the window in a flat**; **never over a neighbour's balcony** |
| **When** | **Late June/July (after the rainy season) to the end of August**; **put away in September** — leaving it up through autumn reads as neglect |
| **Wind** | **Take it in when winds exceed a gentle breeze**: a fūrin in a strong wind rings continuously (the source of complaints) and **glass ones break** against the frame |
| **Night** | **Bring it in or stop the clapper after 22:00** in dense housing; many condominium rules mention fūrin explicitly |
| **Care** | **Wipe glass with a dry cloth**; **Nambu iron rusts if wet — dry after rain**; store wrapped in paper |

- **Noise etiquette**: **the sound carries 20–30 m at night**; **a chime that rings through a still night is a wind of 1–2 m/s hitting your balcony and nobody else's** — check if it is yours. **Complaints about fūrin appear in management-association newsletters every July.** The compromise many use: **hang it only when you're home and awake**.
- **Origins**: **Buddhist temple bells (fūtaku)** hung at pagoda corners to ward off misfortune; **Edo-period glass-blowing** made them a household item; **fūrin sellers (fūrin-uri)** carried racks through the streets — the sound advertised summer.
- **Where to buy**: **Kawasaki Daishi Fūrin Market (July, 30,000 chimes from across Japan)**, **Nishiarai Daishi (Tokyo, July)**, **Shinobazu and Asakusa shops**, and the **Edo Fūrin workshops (Shinohara) that let you paint your own (¥1,500–2,500)**.
- **Modern stand-ins**: **solar-powered chimes, apps** — the point is the association with breeze, so a window-mounted chime that only rings when there is real wind does the job best.
- **Children and pets**: hang **above 1.8 m**; glass shards on a balcony are the usual accident.
