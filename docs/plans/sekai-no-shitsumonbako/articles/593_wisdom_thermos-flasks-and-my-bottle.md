# Q. What do people get wrong about the Japanese "my bottle" habit and vacuum flasks?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #593

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/thermos-flasks-and-my-bottle` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Japan did not invent the vacuum flask — James Dewar built the first one in 1892 and the Thermos brand started in Germany in 1904 — but it made it a household object (Thermos has been Japanese-owned since 1989; Zojirushi and Tiger are Osaka companies) and carries it everywhere: office workers with 350 ml of hot tea, children with 800 ml of cold barley tea, hikers with 500 ml of boiling water for cup noodles. The habit saves money and plastic, and it also has a hygiene side that most owners ignore.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a flask is a set-and-forget object. A stainless vacuum bottle keeps drinks hot (over 60°C) for 6 hours or cold (under 10°C) for 6–8 hours, which is exactly why it is dangerous for the wrong contents: milk, soup, juice or sports drinks left at lukewarm temperatures grow bacteria, and carbonated or fermenting drinks build pressure. Use it for water, tea, coffee and plain hot water; wash the lid gasket and inner surfaces daily with a bottle brush; soak in diluted oxygen bleach weekly (never chlorine — it corrodes steel); and replace the gasket every year or two. Sports drinks and salt-containing drinks need a "sports drink OK" model, and children's bottles need the straw parts taken apart every day.

## The flask and its limits

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Performance** | Typical 500 ml stainless flask: **hot 95°C → above 70°C after 6 h; cold 4°C → below 8°C after 6 h** (manufacturer ratings). Pre-heat/pre-chill with water for better results |
| **Sizes** | **200–350 ml (office, handbag), 500 ml (standard), 750–1,000 ml (school, sport, hiking)**. **1 L of water weighs 1 kg** — children's bags get heavy |
| **What to put in** | **Water, tea (barley tea/mugicha, green tea), coffee, hot water for noodles**; **sports drinks only in models labelled "スポーツドリンク対応"** (coated interior — salt corrodes plain steel) |
| **What NOT to put in** | **Milk, dairy, soup, miso soup, juice, smoothies** (bacteria at lukewarm temperatures; some pressure) — unless a **food-jar (スープジャー) model** and consumed within **6 hours**, filled boiling hot; **carbonated drinks, alcohol, fermenting drinks** (pressure, lid can shoot off); **dry ice** |
| **Daily cleaning** | **Take the lid apart** (gasket, packing, straw parts), **wash with neutral detergent and a bottle brush**, **dry upside down open**. The **gasket groove** is where black mould lives |
| **Weekly** | **Soak body and parts in oxygen bleach (40°C, 30 min)** to remove tea stains and smell. **Never chlorine bleach** (pits stainless steel) or **metal scrubbers** (scratches the interior → bacteria) |
| **Gaskets** | **Replace every 1–2 years** or when discoloured/cracked; makers sell parts (¥300–800) by model number |
| **Dishwasher** | **Most flasks are not dishwasher-safe** (vacuum seal and paint); lids often are — check the manual |
| **Dents** | A dented flask may have **lost its vacuum** — if the outside gets warm with hot contents, the insulation is gone |

- **The "my bottle" culture**: cafés give **¥20–50 off** for your own cup (Starbucks Japan ¥22); **offices have hot-water dispensers and tea corners** for refilling; **schools require a labelled bottle** and specify what may go in (water or barley tea, no juice). **Mugicha (barley tea)** is the summer default because it is caffeine-free and cheap to brew.
- **Hydration and heatstroke**: **500 ml per 2–3 hours outside in summer**, more when working; a **cold flask keeps water drinkable** where a plastic bottle reaches 30°C in an hour (see the heatstroke article).
- **Hot water for the mountain**: **a 500 ml flask filled at 95°C is enough for a cup noodle plus a coffee after 4–5 hours** — hikers' standard.
- **Kids' bottles**: **straw types trap milk and juice residue — daily disassembly**; **replace straws every few months**; **the shoulder cover protects the vacuum from drops**.
- **Smell**: tea and coffee smell comes out with **oxygen bleach or a paste of baking soda**; **citric acid** removes mineral scale (white deposit) — **never at the same time as bleach**.
- **Recycling**: **stainless flasks are metal waste (不燃 or 金属)**, lids plastic — separate; some makers run take-back schemes.
