# Q. What do people get wrong about the chōnaikai, Japan's neighbourhood association?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #594

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/chonaikai-neighbourhood-associations` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Within weeks of moving into a Japanese neighbourhood — a house or a low-rise block — someone knocks with a clipboard: would you join the chōnaikai (or jichikai)? A few hundred yen a month, a rota for rubbish-station cleaning, a festival in autumn, a disaster drill in September. New residents, and foreigners especially, misread it as either a compulsory tax or a social club they can ignore. It is neither.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that membership is mandatory. Chōnaikai are voluntary associations (the Supreme Court confirmed the right to leave in 2005), and the fee — typically ¥200–1,000 a month — pays for streetlights in some areas, festival costs, the notice board, disaster supplies and the neighbourhood's share of rubbish-station upkeep. Joining is optional; what is not optional is the goodwill you get from it: the association is the channel for disaster information, the rubbish-station rules, the kairanban circular, and the neighbours who will check on you in an earthquake. For a renter staying a year or two, pay the fee and skip most events; for a homeowner, join and take a light role when your turn comes. Refusing outright is legal and increasingly common in cities, but it costs you information and, in a crisis, a network.

## What it is

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Names** | **Chōnaikai (町内会)** in towns and cities, **jichikai (自治会)** in newer suburbs and housing estates; about **300,000 associations** nationwide |
| **Membership** | **Voluntary** (Supreme Court, 2005: a resident may withdraw at will). **Participation rates: ~70% nationally, under 50% in central Tokyo and Osaka, 90%+ in rural areas** |
| **Fee** | **¥200–1,000/month** (¥2,400–12,000/year), sometimes collected yearly or with condo management fees. **Some associations also collect Red Cross and community-chest donations — those are separately optional** |
| **What the money does** | **Streetlights** (in many municipalities the association pays the electricity), **rubbish-station nets and cleaning**, **festival (matsuri) and shrine contributions**, **disaster stockpile (water, tents, generators)**, **notice board, kairanban, children's and seniors' events**, **crime-prevention patrols** |
| **Roles** | **Han-chō (block leader) rotates yearly** among households — passing the kairanban, collecting fees, attending a monthly meeting; **officers (kaichō etc.)** are usually retirees. **Renters are often exempted from rotation**, sometimes not |
| **Duties expected** | **Rubbish-station cleaning rota** (once every few months), **the autumn festival**, **the September disaster drill**, **New Year decorations**, **occasional street cleaning** |

## Why it matters in practice

- **Disaster**: in the 1995 Kobe and 2011 Tōhoku earthquakes, **most rescues in the first hours were by neighbours**; associations run the **evacuation-centre setup**, hold **name lists of residents needing help**, and manage the **stockpile**. **Non-members can use evacuation centres — but the association knows who lives where.**
- **Information**: the **kairanban** (see that article) and notice board carry **rubbish-collection changes, water outages, road works, crime alerts, school events**. Non-members often learn of a rubbish-day change from the bag left behind.
- **The rubbish station**: **in many areas the station is association property**; a few associations restrict non-members' use, which municipalities discourage but do not always prevent. **Ask before assuming**.
- **Foreign residents**: **many municipalities publish multilingual guides and encourage associations to welcome newcomers**; the knock on the door is an invitation, not a demand. **"I'm renting, I'll pay the fee but can't take a role"** is a normal answer.
- **Declining**: **"Kekkō desu" (no thank you)** with a bow is enough; **you may be asked to still contribute toward streetlights** in some areas — decide case by case.
- **Condominiums**: the **management association (kanri kumiai) is mandatory by law** (you own a share) and is different from the chōnaikai, which the condo may join collectively — check whether your management fee includes it.

## The tradeoffs (editorial)

| Join | Don't join |
|---|---|
| Disaster network, information, festival access, the neighbours who watch your house | Save ¥2,400–12,000/year and some Sundays |
| Occasional duties (a few hours a year for most members; a heavier year when block leader) | Possible friction over the rubbish station; being the household nobody knows |
