# Q. What do people get wrong about sweeping the street in front of your house?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #599

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/sweeping-the-front-of-the-house` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

Early in the morning across Japan, shopkeepers and householders are outside with a broom, sweeping the pavement in front of their own property — leaves, cigarette butts, the neighbour's fallen blossom — and sometimes sprinkling water. It is not a municipal job, it is not required by law, and it explains more about Japanese streets than the number of rubbish bins does.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that Japanese streets are clean because someone is paid to clean them. Municipal crews sweep main roads and stations; residential streets and shopping streets are kept by the people whose frontage they are, by convention (monzen-baraki, "sweeping before the gate"), with the unspoken rule that you sweep to the property lines on either side and a little beyond, and never push your leaves onto the neighbour's stretch. Do it in the morning, before 08:00 if there is a shop; use a soft outdoor broom and a dustpan; bag the sweepings for burnable rubbish (leaves are burnable in most towns; some collect them separately in autumn); and in summer follow with uchimizu (a splash of water). It takes five minutes, it is the most visible neighbourly act you can perform, and in a new neighbourhood it is how people learn your face.

## The convention

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who** | **The household or shop whose frontage it is**; in shopping streets, **each shop before opening**; in apartment blocks, **the management company or a rota** |
| **What area** | **Your frontage, to the boundary on each side, plus the gutter and the strip of road edge**; **a little into the neighbour's side is polite, a lot is intrusive** |
| **When** | **Morning** (shops **before 08:00**, homes on getting up or after breakfast); **after wind or rain**; **daily in leaf season (Nov–Dec) and blossom season (April)**, weekly otherwise |
| **Tools** | **Outdoor broom (soft bristle or bamboo take-bōki, ¥500–1,500)**, **dustpan with a long handle (¥300–800)**, **bags**, **gloves**; **a leaf blower is considered noisy and lazy** in residential streets |
| **What you sweep** | **Leaves, blossoms, litter, cigarette butts, gravel**; **not** dog mess (the owner's job, and you'd report a habitual one), **not** hazardous things (glass — pick up carefully) |
| **Disposal** | **Leaves and litter → burnable rubbish** in most municipalities; **some towns run autumn leaf collection or accept leaves in clear bags on a set day**; **never into the storm drain or gutter** (blocks it, and it's illegal dumping) |
| **Water** | **Uchimizu after sweeping** in summer (see that article) — cools and holds dust down |
| **Snow** | The same convention applies to **clearing snow from your frontage** in snow country, **early morning before commuters** |

- **Why it works**: **clean streets stay clean** — litter attracts litter (the broken-windows effect, in reverse); **the morning presence of neighbours deters crime**; **the shopping-street association may have a rule** ("sweep by 09:00").
- **The neighbourly etiquette**: **greet anyone passing ("ohayō gozaimasu")**; **if the elderly neighbour can no longer sweep, quietly do their stretch too**; **don't sweep your leaves onto the next frontage or into the road**; **don't sweep during a neighbour's funeral or early on Sunday if it's noisy**.
- **New arrivals**: sweeping your frontage in the first week is **the fastest way to be recognised as "the new people at number 12"**, and to be told where the leaf bags go.
- **Apartment dwellers**: **the entrance and the rubbish station are the shared frontage** — the association rota covers them; **picking up a piece of litter on the way in** is the individual version.
- **Municipal side**: **road sweepers do main roads**; **volunteer "gomi-zero" (30 May, "530" = gomi zero) litter days** and **school clean-ups** cover parks; **adopt-a-road programmes** exist in cities.
- **The broom**: **bamboo brooms (take-bōki) for leaves on rough ground**, **soft palm or plastic bristle for pavement**; **store upright or hanging** so the bristles keep shape; **rinse after wet leaves**.

> **[Verify]** Leaf-disposal rules vary by municipality — cite the relevant town; the 530 day and adopt-a-road programmes with the MOE/MLIT pages.
