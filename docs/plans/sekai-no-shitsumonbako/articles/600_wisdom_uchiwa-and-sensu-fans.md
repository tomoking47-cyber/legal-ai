# Q. What do people get wrong about uchiwa and sensu, the Japanese hand fans?｜Japanese Everyday Wisdom #600

- Shelf: `wisdom` ／ URL: `/en/wisdom/uchiwa-and-sensu-fans` ／ Status: **Draft — sources unverified**

The flat paddle fan (uchiwa) handed out free outside stations in July, and the folding fan (sensu) tucked into a kimono sash or a businessman's bag: both are still everyday summer objects in Japan, not costume. They are cheap, they work, and there are rules of use — where a fan is fine, where it is rude, and why the free ones are the advertising medium they are.

## A. The common misunderstanding is that a hand fan is decorative or old-fashioned. Moving air across sweaty skin at 1–2 m/s makes 30°C feel like 27–28°C by increasing evaporation, which is why Japanese commuters still fan themselves on platforms and why 100 million promotional uchiwa are printed each summer. The rules: fan yourself, not others; fan quietly and below face level in meetings and at ceremonies; the folding sensu is the formal one (carried closed, opened with the right hand, never used to point); the paddle uchiwa is the casual one for festivals, the kitchen and the bath. Both are useless against humidity alone, so they pair with a damp towel or a cooling wipe, and both are outperformed at rest by the small electric neck fan — which has its own etiquette problem (noise, hair) on trains.

## Uchiwa vs sensu

| Item | Uchiwa (団扇) | Sensu (扇子) |
|---|---|---|
| **Form** | **Flat, rigid paddle** — bamboo frame and paper/plastic, or all-plastic promotional | **Folding** — bamboo or wood ribs, paper or cloth |
| **Airflow** | **More air per stroke** (larger area) | Less, but controllable |
| **Formality** | **Casual**: festivals, yukata, kitchen, veranda, fanning a barbecue | **Formal and portable**: kimono, business, tea ceremony (as a ritual object), theatre (rakugo props) |
| **Price** | **Free (promotional) to ¥500**; craft uchiwa (Marugame, Kyoto, Bōshū) **¥1,500–5,000** | **¥1,000–3,000 for everyday; ¥5,000–30,000 for Kyoto craft (Kyō-sensu)** |
| **Where made** | **Marugame (Kagawa) makes ~90% of Japan's bamboo uchiwa** | **Kyoto** for high-grade; mass-produced in China |
| **Carry** | In hand or bag; **stuck in the back of a yukata sash** at festivals | **Closed, in a sleeve or bag; a sensu case (¥500) protects it** |
| **Care** | Wipe; keep out of rain | **Open and close gently from the pivot**; **don't flick open**; **keep dry**; **store closed in the case** |

## Using a fan without offending anyone

- **Fan yourself, not others** (unless caring for a child or elderly person, or asked).
- **Below the chin, small movements, quietly** in offices, trains and ceremonies; **big arm-swinging fanning is for festivals**.
- **Never point with a sensu, tap someone, or gesture at people**; **don't use it to hide a whisper** (that's a stage convention, not real life).
- **At a tea ceremony or formal seated occasion, a closed sensu placed in front of you marks a boundary (kekkai)** — it's not for fanning there.
- **In the theatre and at rakugo**, the sensu is the performer's prop (chopsticks, pipe, sword) — the audience fans **sparingly** so as not to distract.
- **Sumo referees (gyōji) hold a war fan (gunbai)** — nothing to do with cooling.
- **Fan someone else's food**: fine in the kitchen (cooling rice for sushi with an uchiwa is standard); rude at the table.

## Does it actually cool?

- **Air movement of 1–2 m/s** over damp skin **lowers perceived temperature by 2–3°C** by speeding evaporation; **in still 30°C air a fan is the difference between sweating and not**. **Above ~35°C, or at very high humidity, the effect shrinks** — the air is too hot/wet to take moisture off the skin; then **shade, water and a damp towel matter more** (see the heatstroke article).
- **Pair it**: **a damp cooling towel on the neck + fanning** is the traditional combination; **cooling wipes (menthol) + fan** is the convenience-store version.
- **Promotional uchiwa**: **stations, festivals and shopping streets hand out plastic uchiwa printed with ads (about ¥30–50 each to make)**; they are **burnable or plastic waste by municipality**; **collect a few for the bath-time fanning of children**.
- **Electric neck fans and handheld fans (¥1,000–3,000)** now dominate teenagers' summers; **train etiquette: keep the noise low and hair away from the blades**; **charging**: USB, 3–6 hours' use.
- **Kids and festivals**: a **kid-size uchiwa** is a standard summer-festival souvenir; **paper ones tear in the rain**.
