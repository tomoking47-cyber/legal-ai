# Q. How do I read a Japanese sunscreen label?｜Japan Questions #2

Sunscreens sold in Japan carry a rating you will not find on most bottles at home: PA++++.
It sits next to the SPF number, and travellers all over the world ask what it actually means.

## A. SPF and PA measure two different kinds of ultraviolet light. Read them as two separate scales.

Once you see them as two scales rather than one score, choosing becomes straightforward.

## SPF and PA measure different things

Two kinds of ultraviolet light reach the ground.

| | What it does | Shown in Japan as |
|---|---|---|
| UVB | Higher energy. The light that reddens skin | **SPF** (a number) |
| UVA | Longer wavelength. Reaches deeper into the skin | **PA** (plus signs) |

Products sold outside Japan often fold UVA protection into a single phrase such as "broad spectrum."
Japan instead grades it in four steps, from PA+ to PA++++. **More plus signs indicate a higher degree of UVA protection.**

The PA rating comes from a measurement known as the PPD method, which compares how long the skin takes to darken after exposure.

## "Lotion" on a Japanese label does not mean lotion

The second thing that catches people out is the product name.

| Japanese term | English on the label | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| 化粧水 | lotion | A thin, watery liquid — not the creamy lotion you may expect |
| 乳液 | milk | A light moisturiser |
| 美容液 | serum / essence | — |

**English words printed on Japanese packaging do not always carry their usual English meaning.**
A bottle marked "lotion" is not a thick cream.

## Why there is often no expiry date

Many Japanese cosmetics carry no printed expiry date. This is not an omission.

Under Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act, **a cosmetic that keeps its quality for more than three years unopened is not required to display an expiry date.**
Instead you will usually find a batch, or lot, number stamped on the base or the back.

That applies to unopened products. **Once a product is opened, use it up reasonably soon regardless of what the label says.**

## Labels people with sensitive skin look for

| Japanese term | What it indicates |
|---|---|
| 紫外線吸収剤フリー | Made without ultraviolet-absorbing agents |
| ノンケミカル | Commonly used to indicate the same thing |

**What these terms cover varies between products.** If it matters to you, read the ingredient list, or ask a pharmacist or registered seller in the shop.

## A rough guide to choosing

- Everyday errands: first check that a PA rating is shown at all
- Long periods outdoors: look at both SPF and PA
- If your skin reacts easily: test on a small, inconspicuous area first

**A higher number is not automatically a better choice.** Choose for the situation you are in and for the condition of your own skin.
